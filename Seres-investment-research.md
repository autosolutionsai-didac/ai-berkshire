## Step 3: Moat assessment -- Buffett's "economic moat"

| Moat type | Assessment | Strength | Detailed analysis |
|-----------|------|------|---------|
| **Brand / pricing power** | Yes, but belongs to Huawei | Medium-weak | The AITO brand's average transaction price is CNY 40 ten thousand, with strong pricing power. But in consumers' minds "AITO = Huawei", and Seres brand awareness is extremely low. The launch of the Saidou brand precisely proves the dispute over who owns the "AITO" brand value |
| **Switching costs** | Low | Weak | Switching costs in the auto industry are inherently low. The HarmonyOS ecosystem has some stickiness, but that is Huawei's moat, not Seres's |
| **Network effects** | None | None | Cars are not a network-effect business |
| **Economies of scale** | Limited | Medium | Annual output of 47 ten thousand units gives some scale advantage, and the super factory is highly automated. But currently expense growth > revenue growth, so scale effects are not yet fully realized |
| **Technology / patent barriers** | Mixed | Medium | Range-extender technology holds 37.5% market share (No. 1), with 8,046 patents. But core differentiating technologies such as intelligent driving / cockpit depend on Huawei |

### Moat trend assessment

**Past 5 years**: the moat went from "none" to "medium" -- the AITO brand went from zero to one million units, range-extender technology built a lead, but the exclusivity of the core moat (the Huawei partnership) is disappearing.

**Next 5 years outlook**: the moat risks **narrowing** --
1. Huawei's "five realms in parallel" dilutes AITO's exclusive advantage
2. Intelligent-driving technology is trending toward democratization (with third-party solutions such as Momenta rising), so Huawei ADS's technology premium may decline
3. Seres's proprietary technology (range extender, Magic Cube platform), though accumulating, is not yet enough to independently support a brand premium

### Buffett-style probing question

**Will this moat still exist in 10 years? What could destroy it?**

> The key variable 10 years out is whether Seres can evolve from a "Huawei partner" into a "self-owned brand". If range-extender technology keeps leading + it successfully catches up on proprietary intelligent driving (the Saidou route), the moat could shift from "Huawei dependence" to "technology autonomy + manufacturing advantage". But if Huawei chooses to more deeply back other partners, or its intelligence technology is matched by other suppliers, Seres could become an ordinary automaker and the moat could vanish. **The most likely path to destroying it is a shift in Huawei's strategy -- not cutting off cooperation, but no longer granting "eldest son" treatment.**

### Historical analogies

**Analogy 1: HTC and Google (the most direct cautionary precedent)**

HTC was once the world's largest Android phone maker (No. 1 US market share in 2011), heavily dependent on Google's Android platform. But after Google launched its own Pixel brand, HTC fell from its peak to under 1% market share and, in 2017, was forced to sell half its R&D team to Google for USD 11 hundred million.

Similarities between Seres and HTC:
- Both depend on a platform owner for the "soul" (HTC on Android, Seres on HarmonyOS)
- The platform owner expands its partners (Google backing Samsung et al.; Huawei backing the "five realms")
- Each lacks irreplaceable, core differentiating technology

**Differences**: Seres holds a 10% stake in Yinwang, giving it deeper interest alignment with Huawei; AITO's cumulative one-million-unit partnership depth far exceeds that of the other "realms".

**Analogy 2: PC OEMs under the Wintel regime**

PC makers like Lenovo and HP, under the Microsoft + Intel "Wintel" regime, long sat at the bottom of the "smile curve" with gross margins of just 3-5%. Brand premium and technology profit were captured by the platform owners. Although Seres's current 29% gross margin is far higher, as Huawei procurement costs keep rising (already 29% of revenue), the reality of a mere 3.6% net margin is already showing early signs of "PC-OEM-ification".

### Interdisciplinary analysis

| Model | Application | Conclusion |
|------|------|------|
| Technology adoption curve | Smart EVs are in the transition from early to late majority | Slowing growth is inevitable; Seres's 2025 +13.7% growth fits the S-curve |
| Network-effect theory | The beneficiary of the HarmonyOS ecosystem's (phone-car-home) network effect is Huawei, not Seres | Seres cannot independently enjoy the ecosystem network effect |
| Competitive game theory | As the platform owner, Huawei's rational strategy is to keep multiple OEMs competing to maximize bargaining power | Running "five realms in parallel" is not a temporary tactic but the platform owner's optimal game equilibrium |
| Principal-agent theory | Conflicts of interest in family-business governance | The Zhang family controls core posts, but compensation is currently restrained (Zhang Zhengping's CNY 203 ten thousand salary is below that of externally hired executives) |

### Bias self-check

- **Narrative bias**: AITO's "one million units" and "domestic premium" success story is extremely compelling, making it easy to overlook the harsh reality that "Huawei is the real protagonist"
- **Anchoring effect**: the share price fell from CNY 174 to CNY 61, and the anchor of "it's halved, so it must be cheap" can mislead -- one must look at intrinsic value, not the historical high
- **Survivorship bias**: seeing AITO's success, it is easy to overlook the many automakers that partnered with Huawei but failed (e.g., Seres's own SF5 was a failed product)

### Munger-style probing question

**Where am I most likely to be wrong? Why would smart people not buy / short this company?**

> Citi's logic is clear: this is a manufacturer with a 3.6% net margin, whose core technology is not in its own hands, and whose growth has slowed to single digits (Q1 2026 net profit ex-nonrecurring items was only CNY 1 hundred million); at 18x PE there is no reason for a tech premium. What smart bears see is: **Huawei takes the thickest part of the value chain, and Seres gets the thinnest manufacturing profit.** As the "five realms" expand, Seres's bargaining power will only weaken.
>
> But where I might be wrong is: **underestimating the strategic execution of Zhang Xinghai's team** -- a 40-year entrepreneurial history from an CNY 8000 spring factory to CNY 1650 hundred million in revenue shows this is an extremely resilient team, good at seizing the opportunities of its era. The Saidou brand looks aggressive, but if executed well, the dual-track strategy of "premium tied to Huawei + mid/low-end self-owned" could open a second growth curve.

## Step 6: Industry and civilizational trends -- Li Lu's "civilizational-evolution framework"

### Assessment of a civilization-level paradigm shift

China's NEV industry is indeed undergoing a "civilization-level paradigm shift" -- from the internal combustion engine to electrification + intelligence, another industrial revolution following the steam engine, electricity, and the internet.

| Metric | Data | Stage assessment |
|------|------|---------|
| NEV penetration | 2024 47.9% -> 2025 53.9% | Past the 50% inflection; moving from "growth" into "maturity" |
| Annual sales | 2025: 1,649 ten thousand units | World's largest NEV market, far ahead of No. 2 Europe |
| Projected 2030 penetration | 65%-80% | Approaching full replacement; the field will consolidate to 5-8 players |
| Intelligent-driving penetration | L2+ 66%, urban NOA 15% | Intelligence still early-stage; the focus of next-phase competition |

### Historical technology-revolution analogies

| Analogous period | Current parallel | Takeaway |
|---------|---------|------|
| Cars replacing carriages, 1900-1910 | EVs replacing ICE vehicles, 2020-2026 | 50% penetration is the "irreversible" inflection; the counterattack window for legacy automakers has closed |
| PC industry consolidation, 1990s | NEV industry consolidation, 2026-2030 | After penetration passes 50%, a brutal consolidation phase begins, leaving 5-8 survivors |
| Smartphone landscape convergence, 2010s | Smart-car landscape convergence, 2027-2030 | Platform owners (Huawei/Apple/Google) take most of the profit; OEM margins are razor-thin |

### TAM growth curve and ceiling

| Year | NEV sales (ten thousand units) | Penetration | Market size (est.) |
|------|---------------|--------|--------------|
| 2024 | 1,287 | 47.9% | ~CNY 3.5 trillion |
| 2025 | 1,649 | 53.9% | ~CNY 4.3 trillion |

**Ceiling analysis**: China's annual auto sales are about 3,400 ten thousand units; at 80% NEV penetration, about 2,700 ten thousand units. At an average price of CNY 20 ten thousand, TAM is about CNY 5.4 trillion. **Incremental room is narrowing; stock competition will intensify.**

### Seres's position in the industry value chain

```
Upstream raw materials -> Batteries/chips -> Intelligence solution (Huawei Yinwang) -> Vehicle manufacturing (Seres) -> Sales channels (Huawei + self-built) -> Consumers
                                              ^ Thickest part of value chain              ^ Midstream of value chain
```

Seres sits at the **manufacturing link** of the value chain -- capital-intensive, asset-heavy, but not the highest value-added. The thickest part of the value chain (the intelligence solution) is controlled by Huawei. Seres's strategic goal is to extend upstream (proprietary intelligent driving, the Saidou brand) and control downstream (self-built channels), but for now it remains highly dependent on Huawei.

### Technology-route risk

| Pure electric | Compatible via the Magic Cube platform | Already has BEV capability; risk manageable |
| Intelligent driving | Depends on Huawei ADS | If Huawei ADS's edge is matched, AITO's differentiation disappears |
| Solid-state battery | No current layout | If solid-state batteries reach mass production, they could disrupt the current powertrain route |

### Customer/supplier concentration

| Dimension | Data | Risk |
|------|------|------|

| Customer concentration | C-end consumers; no single large customer | Low risk |

### Li Lu-style probing question

**Looking back 20 years from now, is Seres "the Standard Oil of this era" or "a flash-in-the-pan 3Com"?**

> The more likely analogy is **Lenovo/HP** -- becoming the world's largest PC maker in the Wintel era, yet always at the manufacturing link of the value chain with thin profits. Seres's fate depends on whether it can evolve from "Huawei's Lenovo" into "the Apple of self-owned brands". For now, that road is still very long. The Saidou brand is a brave attempt, but the leap from Lenovo to Apple has almost no successful precedent in history. A more realistic path may be: becoming a steady premium manufacturer with modest profit but solid cash flow, similar to Foxconn to Apple.
