### 2.3 Chokepoint Links

**First chokepoint: the logistics/delivery network (most critical)**
- 7.45 million riders make up the world's largest instant-delivery network, delivering 79.5 million+ orders per day on average
- Supply is tightest (rider churn rate over 50%, costs up 18%)
- Hardest to replace (JD has poured in tens of billions of yuan and still cannot replicate it)
- Is the fulfillment foundation of both food delivery and instant retail

**Second chokepoint: the in-store platform (highest profit margin)**
- Operating margin as high as 43%, gross margin 55%-65%
- Dianping's 20 years of UGC review data form an extremely strong content moat
- Currently facing a structural challenge from Douyin's content-driven traffic

**Third chokepoint: the food-delivery platform (strongest value capture)**
- Controls the merchant traffic entry point, the consumer reach channel, and delivery fulfillment capability
- Once a three-sided network effect is established, it is extremely hard to displace

## IV. In-Depth Analysis of Core Companies

### 4.1 Meituan (3690.HK) — Full-Chain Leader

#### Operating Dimension

**One-sentence definition:** China's undisputed leader in local-life services, connecting 800 million users and 14.5 million merchants, and leading in all three core tracks: food delivery (65-70% share), instant retail (45-60% share), and in-store (50%+ share).

**Financial data:**

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026Q1 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | CNY 337.6bn (+22%) | CNY 364.9bn (+8%) | CNY 91.0bn (+5.6%) |
| Net profit | CNY 35.8bn (historic best) | -CNY 23.4bn | -CNY 6.8bn (loss narrowing q/q) |
| Gross margin | ~38.4% | ~30-32% | — |
| Operating cash flow | ~CNY 50bn+ | Q1 CNY 10.1bn → Q2 CNY 4.8bn | — |
| Cash reserves | — | CNY 166.9bn | CNY 180.4bn |

**Revenue mix:** Core local commerce CNY 260.8bn (71.5%, including food delivery/in-store hotel & travel/Flash Purchase); new initiatives CNY 104.0bn (28.5%, including Xiaoxiang Supermarket/Kuaidilu/overseas Keeta, etc., +19%).

**Business breakdown:**

| Segment | Revenue/scale | Growth rate | Margin | Market share | Main competitors |
|------|----------|------|--------|---------|-------------|
| Food delivery | ~65 million orders/day on average | Low-to-mid single digits | 2024: 15-20% → 2025: swung to a loss due to the subsidy war | 65-70% | Ele.me/Taobao Flash Purchase, JD Food Delivery, Douyin food delivery |
| In-store/hotel & travel | ~CNY 56.6bn; operating profit ~CNY 19.5bn | 15-20% | Gross margin 87%, operating margin ~35% | Down from ~70% to 50%+ | Douyin local life (biggest threat), Ctrip |
| Instant retail/Flash Purchase | GMV ~CNY 262bn; 50,000+ flash warehouses | 30-65% | First turned profitable in 2024Q3 → lost money again in 2025 due to the war | 45-70% | Taobao Flash Purchase, JD Daojia, Dingdong Maicai |
| New initiatives (overseas/Select, etc.) | CNY 104.0bn (+19%) | ~20% | Operating loss CNY 10.1bn | Keeta #1 in Hong Kong with 44% | Duoduo Grocery, Uber Eats, Deliveroo |

**Is this a good business?**

At its core, Meituan is a "high-frequency, non-discretionary + three-sided network" platform business. The good side: eating is a non-discretionary need, with tens of millions of orders per day; once scale is achieved, the platform's monetization potential is large (commission + advertising + delivery fees), and 2024 already proved it can generate CNY 35.8 billion in profit. The bad side: user loyalty is not high — JD reached tens of millions of orders per day within just one year, showing that switching costs are low and the competitive landscape can easily be broken by subsidies.

#### Competitive Dimension

| Moat dimension | Strength | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★☆ | "Meituan = local life services" is deeply ingrained, with 770 million annual transacting users. But the subsidy war proves that price-sensitive users can easily be poached |
| Switching costs | ★★★☆☆ | Switching costs for C-end users are low (subsidies alone can pull them away); merchants (B-end) multi-home across platforms simultaneously. The Shenhui membership system boosts in-ecosystem stickiness via cross-redeemable points |
| Network effects | ★★★★☆ | A positive flywheel from 800 million users → 14.5 million merchants → millions of riders. Cross-scenario network effects across "eating, drinking, entertainment." But partly offset by Douyin's traffic network with 600 million daily active users |
| Scale effects | ★★★★☆ | Tens of millions of orders per day give the industry's lowest per-order delivery cost. Marginal cost of the in-store business is near zero. But deep-pocketed rivals are willing to burn cash to build a parallel network |
| Technology/data moat | ★★★★☆ | Over a decade of local-life data accumulation; the world's most complex instant-delivery dispatch system. Drones received an all-region operating permit. R&D spend of CNY 26 billion/year |

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** The physical moat of the delivery network is very likely to persist, but in-store information matching faces the risk of being eroded by content platforms and AI agents — ironically, in-store is precisely Meituan's highest-margin business.

#### Contrarian-Thinking Dimension

**Most likely failure modes:**
1. The food-delivery subsidy war persists/escalates (probability 55%, impact extremely high): Alibaba says it is "not afraid of losses for three years"; the three parties together have burned over CNY 150 billion
2. Douyin erodes in-store share (probability 70%, impact high): in-store share has fallen from 70% to 50%+; Douyin's 2026 target GMV exceeds CNY 1.2 trillion
3. Rider costs rise rigidly (probability 75%, impact medium): if 50% of riders need social-insurance contributions, annual additional expense of roughly CNY 56 billion

**Worst-case valuation:** If the subsidy war persists until 2028 and normalized annual profit is only CNY 15-20 billion, that implies a P/E of 22-28x — the current price is not cheap.

**Why smart people might not buy:** (1) The CNY 35.8 billion profit of 2024 may be a peak that is never revisited; (2) Alibaba reaching 77% of Meituan's daily order volume within a year shows the moat is not as deep as imagined; (3) "the stock is down 83%" does not mean "it has bottomed" — the high corresponds to the 2021 bubble valuation; (4) there is risk in anchoring to analyst price targets.

#### Management

**Wang Xing:** Born in 1979, a serial entrepreneur (Xiaonei → Fanfou → Meituan), who has won two industry-level battles — the "thousand group-buying-sites war" and the food-delivery war. Holds roughly 9.3% of shares and, through dual-class shares, controls roughly 45.6% of voting rights. In 2025, facing a three-way siege, he held share with far lower losses than rivals, but the swing from CNY 35.8 billion in profit to a CNY 23.4 billion loss shows the intensity of competition was underestimated.

**Management rating: A-.** Top-tier strategic vision and execution, validated by two industry-level battles. Pragmatic and rational — expanding when it should expand and retreating when it should retreat (Meituan Select).

#### Valuation Snapshot

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Current share price | HKD 65.45 |
| Market cap | ~HKD 418bn (~USD 53.8bn) |
| P/S (2025 revenue) | ~1.06x (historical range 1-8x, currently extremely low) |
| Static P/E (based on 2024 profit) | ~13.7x |
| Cash reserves | CNY 166.9 billion (enough to fund a protracted war) |

**Brief comment:** A P/S of about 1x sits at an extremely low point historically. If the subsidy war cools in H2 2026, Meituan could return to a path of profitability in 2026H2-2027. But "cheap" doesn't mean "will rise" — close attention is needed to Alibaba's subsidy intensity, the quarterly loss-narrowing trend, and regulatory enforcement.

**Recommendation: ★★★★☆ (core-position candidate)**

### 5.2 Historical Analogies

**Analogy 1: The US food-delivery war (2015-2020)**
- Grubhub once had a market cap over USD 80 billion, then crashed 91% before being acquired; DoorDash came from behind to become the leader
- Lesson: first-mover advantage does not equal ultimate advantage; once competition ends, the leader's valuation can recover very sharply

**Analogy 2: China's ride-hailing war (2014-2016)**
- Didi and Kuaidi/Uber China together lost more than CNY 20 billion before merging, achieving 90%+ combined market share
- But cumulative losses over 7 years exceeded CNY 50 billion, and the company only turned annually net-profitable in 2023
- Lesson: "winning the war" does not equal "making money"; regulation is the biggest unpredictable variable

**Analogy 3: The "thousand group-buying-sites war" (2010-2013)**
- 5,000+ group-buying sites entered the fray, and 99% of investors lost everything. Meituan won by controlling costs and prioritizing merchant operations
- Lesson: management has indeed been validated by extreme competition; but the path from fierce competition to industry consolidation can take up to 8 years

**Common patterns across the three analogies:**
1. Winners do exist, but most investors lose money even when they pick the eventual winner (overpaying in the later stages)
2. "Everyone loses money" is the norm in platform wars — not just the losers; most investors in the winner also lose
3. Regulatory intervention is the key variable that ends the war
4. Post-war, the leader's profitability is typically lower than before the war

### 5.3 Bias Self-Check

**Narrative bias — is Meituan's story too perfect?**

"An unbreakable moat + the darkest hour about to pass + a historically low valuation = a great buying opportunity" is a classic turnaround narrative. But be wary:
- Alibaba reaching 77% of Meituan's daily order volume within a year shows the entry barrier to the delivery network is not as high as imagined
- The "darkest hour" may last 3-5 years rather than 6-12 months (per the historical analogies)
- The trillion-yuan instant-retail figure is a classic TAM trap — a trillion-yuan GMV does not equal a trillion-yuan profit pool

**Anchoring effect — are we anchored to recent data?**
- Anchored to 2024's CNY 35.8 billion profit: a "static P/E of 13.7x" looks cheap, but that was abnormally high profit during a competitive lull
- Anchored to "the stock is down 83%": the high corresponds to the 2021 bubble valuation, making it a meaningless anchor
- Anchored to analyst price targets: sell-side price targets have historically had extremely low accuracy

**Herding effect — is this a case of "everyone is buying"?**
- "The subsidy war will eventually end" is market consensus; if that's already fully priced in, there's no excess return
- Real excess returns come from being correct where you differ from consensus

## VI. Civilizational-Trend Judgment

### Underlying Trend Attributes

**Judgment: the underlying demand for local-life services is part of a "civilizational-scale paradigm shift," but the platform itself is only an application layer, not an infrastructure layer.**

Three underlying trends:
1. **Urbanization (67% → 75-80%)** — irreversible, but decelerating
2. **Rising share of services in consumption (46% → 55-60%)** — irreversible, a structural trend as per-capita GDP moves from USD 10,000 toward USD 20,000
3. **Mobile internet penetration (1.276 billion MAU)** — already near its ceiling

Key distinction: people need to eat, get a haircut, see a doctor — this is a civilizational-level need. But whether that need has to be met via "Meituan" specifically — that's only a stage-specific technological implementation.

### Closest Historical Analogy: Department Stores During the Electricity Revolution (1890-1950)

Department stores (Sears, Macy's) were core beneficiaries of the three major trends of urbanization, railroads, and electricity. From the 1920s to the 1960s they were retail's "infrastructure." But they were then disrupted, layer by layer, by shopping malls → Walmart → Amazon.

**Lesson:** Meituan resembles the department store's role in its golden age — the optimal intermediary connecting supply and demand under current technological conditions. But the technological implementation of that intermediary layer will keep being disrupted.

### 10-20 Year Endgame Scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Meituan evolves into the "AWS of local life" | 40% | The delivery network shifts from proprietary to open infrastructure; value shifts from traffic gateway toward fulfillment infrastructure |
| Unmanned delivery reshapes the industry landscape | 30% | L4-level unmanned delivery scales up 2030-2035; the moat shifts from "rider scale" to "fleet + algorithms + right-of-way" |
| AI agents dissolve the platform's intermediary value | 20% | Large models make consumption decisions directly for users, shrinking the value of the platform's traffic gateway |
| Regulation-driven utility-ification | 10% | Rider social insurance + commission caps compress margins to 3-5% |

### Winner-Take-All vs. Disruption

- **Most likely to be winner-take-all:** instant-delivery infrastructure — extremely strong scale effects, a data flywheel, high switching costs
- **Most likely to be disrupted:** in-store information matching (the Dianping model) — Douyin has already shown that "content-based recommendation" can substitute for "active search," and AI agents may further substitute for it in the future. Ironically, in-store is precisely Meituan's highest-margin business (87% gross margin)

### Four-Dimension Simulated Commentary

#### Operating Dimension

> Meituan's two truly valuable assets are: first, Dianping's content ecosystem — an 87% gross margin shows this is a good business that can make money without subsidies; second, the scale effect of the instant-delivery network — 65 million orders per day on average means the marginal cost per order keeps falling.
>
> But the problem with food delivery itself is: users are extremely price-sensitive, and switching costs are close to zero. JD reaching 25 million orders/day within a few months shows that "ordering food delivery" is not locked to any one platform. Meituan relies on being "fast" and "comprehensive," not on being "irreplaceable."
>
> Uber looks more like "the right business" than Meituan does — mobility is a non-discretionary need among non-discretionary needs, and its global footprint diversifies single-market competitive risk. USD 10 billion in free cash flow shows the business model already works.
>
> **The most important point: don't buy Meituan just because it's cheap — buy it because its business model can recover after the subsidy war. A cheap bad business is never worth investing in.**

#### Competitive Dimension

> If you gave a smart competitor CNY 100 billion, could they replicate Meituan within 5 years? The answer is: partly yes, partly no. JD has already proven that food-delivery logistics can be bought with money. But Dianping's 20 years of review data, delivery density covering 2,800 districts and counties, and an operating system spanning 14.5 million merchants — these are not things money alone can buy.
>
> In in-store, Douyin's content-traffic advantage is a real threat (share rising from 30% to 40%+); but in food delivery, Meituan's delivery-efficiency edge makes it very hard for competitors to gain share without sustaining losses.
>
> I don't like DoorDash's current valuation. A 72x P/E implies an expectation of it becoming "America's Meituan," but Western markets lack a high-margin monetization layer like in-store hotel & travel. A good company is not the same as a good investment — **price is always the most important margin of safety**.

#### Contrarian-Thinking Dimension

> The first easy mistake: equating "the trend is correct" with "the investment is correct." Instant delivery is an irreversible trend, but during the 2000 internet bubble, "the internet will change the world" was also a correct trend — and most investors still picked the wrong names or paid too much.
>
> The second mistake: underestimating the chance that "a rational competitor turns irrational." Alibaba says it's "not afraid of losses for three years"; JD's Richard Liu personally leads the charge into food delivery — these look "irrational" in a textbook, but they've happened repeatedly throughout China's internet competitive history.
>
> The third mistake: overlooking the possibility of "winning the war but not making money." Suppose Meituan holds 60% share, but the cost is that users form a habit of comparing prices across platforms, commission rates get permanently compressed, and rider costs rise rigidly — then Meituan might "win the war but lose the profit."
>
> **Suggestion: bet with money you're willing to lose, not money you can't afford to lose. The local-life theme should be no more than 20% of the total portfolio, and Meituan no more than 8%. Watch out for the "endowment effect" — don't feel you must buy just because you've spent a lot of time researching it.**

#### Long-Term Trend Dimension

> From the perspective of civilizational evolution, the time cost of obtaining goods and services is being systematically compressed. The first compression was e-commerce (from "going to the mall" to "next-day delivery"); the second compression is happening now (from "next-day delivery" to "delivered in 30 minutes"). This is not just "faster shipping" — it's a redefinition of the retail format.
>
> China has gone furthest along this trend (food-delivery penetration roughly 30%), the US roughly 15%, and Southeast Asia and India under 10%. The gap means Chinese companies have accumulated the richest experience, and it also means overseas markets have huge room to grow.
>
> But there's a huge gap between value creation and value capture in this industry. Meituan earned CNY 35.8 billion in 2024 and then lost CNY 23.4 billion in 2025 — a swing of CNY 59.2 billion in profit within a year shows that profit stability is far lower than social value.
>
> I prefer two types of names: first, platforms that have already built multi-layered monetization capability (Uber's mobility + delivery + advertising + finance), because a single monetization layer is too easily eroded by competition; second, early-stage leaders in emerging markets (Grab), because they are where Chinese companies stood 10 years ago.
>
> **Ultimately, "everything delivered home" will become infrastructure, just like e-commerce did. But you must distinguish "certainty of the trend" from "certainty of the individual stock" — the former scores 90, the latter perhaps only 60. Buy the most likely winner with a reasonable position size at a reasonable price, and then give it time and patience.**
