# Jensen Huang says AI is a five-layer cake — who's making money at each layer?

This year at Davos and GTC, Jensen Huang has repeatedly laid out one framework: AI is not a product — it's an entire new industrial system, with five layers from bottom to top.

He calls these five layers the "AI cake." I spent a week pulling each layer apart, and found the investment logic inside is far clearer than I expected.

## First, let's lay out the five layers

```
Layer 5  Application layer      AI agents, autonomous driving, robotics
Layer 4  Model layer            Large language models, multimodal models
Layer 3  Infrastructure layer   Data centers, networking, cooling
Layer 2  Chip layer             GPUs, memory, advanced packaging
Layer 1  Energy layer           Power, transformers, nuclear energy
```

In Huang's own words: "In the future, every company will have two factories — one that makes products, and one that is an AI factory. The AI factory does just one thing every day: produce tokens."

He offered one core metric for measuring an AI factory's efficiency: tokens produced per watt. The point of this metric is that the AI business will ultimately resemble manufacturing — the competition will come down to how much intelligence can be produced per unit of electricity.

## Layer 1: Power — the most boring but most certain opportunity

Global data center electricity consumption reached 485 terawatt-hours in 2025 and is set to double within five years. But nearly half of planned U.S. data center projects have already been delayed or canceled — not for lack of GPUs, but for lack of power and transformers.

Lead times for large power transformers have stretched to four years. That means 2026 capacity was effectively locked in back in 2022 — an order placed today won't be delivered until 2030 at the earliest.

This layer is the least glamorous, but the most certain. The transformer shortage is unlikely to ease before 2028. Power-equipment companies like Eaton and GE Vernova are the most reasonably valued beneficiaries in the entire AI value chain.

## Layer 2: Chips — the $5 trillion monopoly king

Nvidia remains the "standard unit of measurement" for AI compute. FY2027 Q1 revenue was $81.6 billion, up 85% year over year; data center revenue was $75.2 billion, up 92%; gross margin was 75%. Market cap: $5.2 trillion, the largest in the world.

But the change most worth watching is this: custom-chip growth (+45%) has for the first time overtaken general-purpose GPU growth (+16%). Google, Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft are all either designing their own chips in-house or having Broadcom/Marvell build custom AI chips for them, all with the goal of reducing dependence on Nvidia.

Broadcom's AI revenue this quarter was $10.8 billion, up 143% year over year — growing even faster than Nvidia. CEO Hock Tan said he is "confident AI chip revenue will exceed $100 billion in 2027," with $73 billion in backlog already on the books.

The five key players at the chip layer: Nvidia (standard-setter), Broadcom (king of custom silicon), AMD (the only general-purpose alternative), TSMC (manufacturing monopoly), and SK Hynix (62% share of HBM memory).

## Layer 3: Infrastructure — the $725 billion arms race

The combined 2026 capital expenditure of the four major cloud providers is $725 billion, double the prior year. Not one of them is cutting back.

| Company | 2026 capex | YoY |
|------|--------------|------|
| Amazon | $200 billion | +60% |
| Microsoft | $190 billion | +138% |
| Google | $180 billion | +97% |
| Meta | $135 billion | +80% |

Where does the money go? Roughly 40-60% on GPUs/chips, 20-25% on building data centers, 15-20% on server components, and 10-15% on networking equipment.

That $725 billion is real cash, not a slide deck. As it flows down the supply chain, it sustains companies in optical modules (Innolight's 2025 net profit was 10.8 billion yuan), networking equipment (Arista), liquid cooling (Vertiv), and servers (Dell).

Worth noting: Innolight is the one point in the entire AI value chain where a Chinese company holds the strongest pricing power. It supplies roughly half of the world's 800G optical modules, and 35% of Nvidia's orders go to it.

## Layer 4: Models — the fastest revenue growth in history

Anthropic's annualized revenue went from $1 billion to $47 billion in just 14 months. This may be the fastest revenue growth of any company in business history.

| Company | Valuation | Annualized revenue |
|------|------|----------|
| Anthropic | $965 billion | $47 billion |
| OpenAI | $852 billion | $24 billion |
| xAI (merged with SpaceX) | $1.75 trillion target | $3.2 billion (xAI portion) |
| DeepSeek | $51.5 billion | undisclosed |

The second half of 2026 is shaping up to be a landmark year for AI company IPOs — Anthropic has confidentially filed, and OpenAI is targeting a September listing. This will be the moment that tests whether these sky-high valuations can withstand public-market scrutiny.

DeepSeek is the most interesting variable at this layer. V4-Pro already outperforms Claude on math reasoning, and its API pricing is just one-tenth to one-seventy-fifth that of Western competitors. It proves one thing: the gap in model capability is narrowing, but the gap in commercialization is still an order of magnitude wide.

## Layer 5: Applications — few companies are actually making money from AI

The brutal reality at this layer: for most "AI concept stocks," AI still accounts for only a small share of revenue. Yet valuations have diverged to extremes.

| Company | AI revenue growth | P/E |
|------|-----------|-----|
| Palantir | +85% | 105x |
| Tesla | FSD subscriptions +51% | 317x |
| ServiceNow | AI cross-sell up 10x | 25x |
| Salesforce | Agentforce +200% | 14x |
| Adobe | Firefly credit consumption up 3x | 10x |

Palantir is the purest AI-application play, with 85% growth crushing its peers, but a 105x P/E has already priced in a great deal. Salesforce and Adobe offer the best value — 14x and 10x P/E respectively, with fast-growing AI products but low valuations from the market, because overall revenue growth is only 10-12%.

If you believe monetization of the AI application layer is still in its early innings, then Salesforce's 14x P/E may be the most undervalued name in the entire AI value chain.

## Who's the strongest player in China at each layer?

| Layer | China's strongest player | Gap vs. global leader |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Chips | Huawei Ascend | One generation behind on hardware; an entire era behind on the CUDA ecosystem |
| Infrastructure | Innolight | Holds global pricing power in optical modules — the exception |
| Models | DeepSeek | 3-6 months behind on technology; 100x behind on commercialization |
| Cloud | Alibaba Cloud | 6x behind on revenue; a huge gap in globalization |
| Applications | iFlytek | Solid in vertical/industry deployment, far behind in general-purpose applications |

China's biggest progress in AI is at the model layer (DeepSeek); its biggest bottleneck is at the chip layer (the CUDA ecosystem); and its most underappreciated gap is in commercialization — the technology has caught up, but the ability to turn "technology into money" is still an order of magnitude behind.

## A final word

Huang's five-layer cake framework offers a very clear way of thinking: the lower the layer, the more physical it is, and the harder it is to route around, the higher the investment certainty.

The sexiest layers are models and applications — Anthropic's $47 billion in revenue, Palantir's 85% growth. But these are also the layers with the lowest certainty: valuations have already flown to the moon, and once growth slows, the drawdown will be brutal.

The most boring layers are energy and infrastructure — transformers, liquid cooling, optical modules. But these are the true chokepoints of the AI industry. With four-year lead times on transformers, this is a bottleneck nobody can get around.

Along the AI value chain, the shovel-sellers are always more certain bets than the gold-diggers. It's just that, these days, even the best shovels aren't cheap anymore.
