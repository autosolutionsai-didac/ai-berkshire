# Valuation judgment: option or trap?

> "Understanding Baidu" series · Article 05 · Final chapter
> Reading time: about 10 minutes

---

## First, a recap: what the four earlier articles found

**Baidu is a company with RMB 239.4 billion in net cash (~70% of market cap) and a P/B of just 1.16x. Its old engine (search advertising) is declining at 14% a year, while its new engines (AI cloud +34%, AI applications +48%) are accelerating — but not one of its AI tracks has had its profitability fully validated. Free cash flow turned negative for the first time in 2025 (-RMB 15.1 billion), while 2026Q1 profit rebounded sharply (+48%). The consumer AI entry point is already lost (Ernie Bot's 4.79 million MAU vs. Doubao's 345 million), but the enterprise-side AI cloud and autonomous driving businesses have genuine competitiveness. Management shows excellent technical foresight, but its execution track record is concerning.**

Now let's answer the core question: **what is Baidu actually worth?**

---

## Sum-of-the-parts valuation

Baidu cannot be valued on a single P/E — because it isn't one business, but a composite of "old business + new business + cash + options." It has to be broken apart.

### Valuation breakdown

| Asset | Valuation method | Valuation range (RMB billion) | Notes |
|------|---------|----------------|------|
| **Search advertising** | Declining-phase business, 8-10x profit | 50-70 | ~RMB 63 billion revenue in 2025, assuming ~RMB 7 billion operating profit, applying 8-10x |
| **AI cloud** | High-growth business, 3-5x P/S | 60-100 | ~RMB 20 billion revenue in 2025, growing 34%, benchmarked against peers |
| **Apollo Go** | Option pricing | 0-50 | Profitability unvalidated, extremely wide range |
| **Kunlun chips (57.67% stake)** | Benchmarked against IPO valuation | 12-46 | 57.67% of RMB 21 billion (funded) to RMB 80 billion (J.P. Morgan forecast) |
| **iQIYI (holds ~70%+)** | Benchmarked against market cap | 10-15 | iQIYI's market cap is about RMB 20 billion |
| **Net cash** | Face value | **239.4** | This part is certain |
| **Total** | — | **371.4-520.4** | — |

> Note: The above are rough range estimates; each segment carries significant uncertainty. Sources: Baidu annual report, Omdia, J.P. Morgan, public market data.

Baidu's current market cap is about **RMB 340 billion** (~$46.6 billion, based on ~$141/ADS in May 2026 × 340 million ADS).

| Scenario | Sum-of-the-parts total | vs. current market cap |
|------|------------|------------|
| Pessimistic (search keeps deteriorating + AI doesn't make money + Apollo goes to zero) | ~RMB 371.4 billion | +9% |
| Neutral (search stabilizes + AI cloud keeps growing + Kunlun IPO) | ~RMB 440 billion | +29% |
| Optimistic (AI fully monetizes + Apollo internationalization succeeds + Kunlun at a high valuation) | ~RMB 520.4 billion | +53% |

**Key finding: even in the pessimistic scenario, Baidu's sum-of-the-parts valuation is slightly above its current market cap.** This is largely because **RMB 239.4 billion in net cash** provides a hard floor — it accounts for 65% of the pessimistic-scenario valuation.

---

## Where is the hard floor?

Baidu's valuation floor is set by three "hard assets":

| Hard asset | Amount (RMB billion) | Certainty |
|--------|------------|--------|
| Net cash | **239.4** | ★★★★★ |
| Kunlun stake (at its already-funded valuation) | **~12** | ★★★★ |
| iQIYI stake | **~10-15** | ★★★ |
| **Total** | **~261.4-266.4** | — |

**A hard-asset floor of about RMB 260 billion** corresponds to an ADS price of roughly **$107-110**.

That means: **the current share price of $141 is only about 28% above the hard floor price that counts only cash and realizable assets and assigns zero value to the search and AI businesses.**

But note two risks:
1. If free cash flow stays negative (-RMB 15.1 billion/year), the hard floor price shrinks by roughly 6% a year
2. The $5 billion buyback plus dividends also consume cash — if the company isn't making money while handing out cash, it is essentially "moving money from one pocket to the other"

---

## Three key catalysts

What events could move Baidu's valuation up a level?

### Catalyst one: free cash flow turning positive

The 2025 FCF of -RMB 15.1 billion is the biggest drag on Baidu's valuation. If FCF turns positive in 2026, it would mean:
- The "cash burn" narrative reverses
- The $5 billion buyback becomes a genuine shareholder return (rather than a drawdown of reserves)
- The market becomes willing to value the business again

**Metric to watch**: Operating cash flow positive for 2 consecutive quarters. 2026Q1 core net income of RMB 7.6 billion (+48%) is a good sign, but it needs to be sustained.

### Catalyst two: AI revenue share exceeding 50%

AI revenue's share already reached **43%** in 2025Q4; at the current growth rate, it could break 50% in 2026. Once it crosses that line, Baidu shifts from a "search advertising company" to an "AI company" — and the valuation logic would change fundamentally.

**Counterargument**: "A share above 50% doesn't mean profitability. If AI revenue's gross margin is far below that of search advertising, then the higher its share, the lower the profit. What matters is AI's share of profit, not its share of revenue."

### Catalyst three: the Kunlun IPO

Kunlun has already filed confidentially with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange and has started STAR Market coaching — a dual "A+H" listing is a concrete plan.

The value of the IPO lies in:
1. Giving Kunlun a market-set price (confirming or refuting the RMB 80 billion valuation)
2. Turning Baidu's 57.67% stake into a "realizable asset"
3. Unlocking the single most independently valuable piece of Baidu's "full-stack AI" story

## Three reduce-position / exit signals

Under what conditions should the thesis be reassessed?

### Signal one: search advertising decline accelerates

If quarterly online-marketing revenue falls more than **20%** year-over-year (versus -14% for full-year 2025), it means the old engine is running out of fuel faster than expected, and the "gap between the old and the new" window will stretch longer.

### Signal two: AI cloud growth collapses

If Baidu AI Cloud's quarterly growth drops from 34% to the single digits, it means Volcano Engine's pursuit is working and Baidu's enterprise-side moat isn't solid.

### Signal three: another out-of-circle-of-competence gamble

If management launches another large non-AI investment (similar to the earlier O2O or Jidu Auto ventures), it means capital-allocation discipline hasn't improved — this is the most dangerous signal.

## What this means for different types of investors

Baidu is not a stock suited to everyone. Depending on your risk appetite:

### Aggressive investors

**Stance**: Can hold it as an AI option.

**Logic**: The hard floor price of $107-110 provides a cushion (the current $141 is only 28% above it), and any one of the three AI tracks proving out would mean significant upside. The $5 billion buyback and the Kunlun IPO are near-term catalysts.

**Risk**: If FCF stays negative, the cushion will shrink. This is not a "buy it and sleep easy" stock.

### Moderate investors

**Stance**: Wait and see for two signals.

**Logic**: Wait for FCF to turn positive for 2 consecutive quarters plus AI revenue share to exceed 50%. Until both conditions are met, Baidu's valuation anchor is unstable. "Cheap" does not equal "safe" — the combination of negative FCF plus a profit cliff does not meet traditional value-investing standards.

### Conservative investors

**Stance**: Avoid.

**Logic**: The Buffett pre-purchase checklist pass rate is **1.5/10**. ROE of only 7.2%, negative FCF, unpredictable profit, and questionable management execution fail to satisfy any of "good business, good price, good management." Even though a P/B of 1.16x looks cheap, the combination of "low P/B + negative FCF + declining core business" has historically more often been a value trap than an undervaluation.

## Baidu vs. Tencent vs. Alibaba: three fates within China's internet sector

| Dimension | Tencent | Baidu | Alibaba |
|------|------|------|------|
| Core asset | Weixin (irreplaceable) + gaming | Search (being replaced) + AI | E-commerce (squeezed by Pinduoduo/Douyin) + cloud |
| Valuation | ~18x P/E, reasonable to slightly cheap | ~16x P/E, cheap but uncertain | ~12x P/E, cheap but growth is sluggish |
| Cash flow | Extremely strong (FCF RMB 190 billion+) | **Turned negative** (-RMB 15.1 billion) | Positive but volatile |
| Management | Highly transparent + generous buybacks | Low transparency + buybacks just starting | Highly transparent + generous buybacks |
| Suited to whom | Moderate investors who want to "sleep easy" | Aggressive investors willing to bet on an AI option | Mean-reversion investors bullish on an e-commerce recovery |

**Tencent is like a Mercedes cruising smoothly down the highway — you pay a reasonable price for certainty.**
**Baidu is like a race car swapping its engine mid-race — the old engine is still running but smoking, and the new engine is half-installed and not yet firing.** If the swap succeeds, this car will be faster than anyone expects; if it fails, it will stall on the roadside.

**Are you willing to be a passenger in this car while its engine is being swapped? That is the real question of investing in Baidu.**

## Summary

Back to the question posed at the start of the series: **is Baidu ultimately "an option with a floor" or "a value trap"?**

The answer depends on how you view the following three things:

| Question | Optimistic view | Pessimistic view |
|------|---------|---------|
| Can AI monetize? | 2026Q1 profit +48% — the inflection has arrived | AI gross margin undisclosed — revenue may be rising without profit |
| Is the cash safe? | RMB 239.4 billion + a $5 billion buyback | FCF is negative — the buyback is drawing down reserves |
| Can management execute? | AI is a core competence — this time is different | History has repeatedly shown "seeing it but not achieving it" |

**If your answer to all three questions leans optimistic — Baidu's current valuation is indeed cheap, with 30-50% upside from $141.**
**If your answer to all three questions leans pessimistic — Baidu could be a story of "RMB 239.4 billion in cash slowly being burned away," ultimately becoming "the Yahoo of the AI era."**
**If you're unsure — then wait. Wait for FCF to turn positive, wait for AI's share to pass 50%, wait for the Kunlun IPO to be priced.** These signals should emerge one by one over the next 6-12 months.

The biggest risk in investing in Baidu is not that AI fails — AI capability is already an established fact. **The biggest risk is that even if the AI technology succeeds, the profit could still be eroded by competition and commercialization difficulties, ultimately "winning on technology, losing on the investment."**

This is an open question with no standard answer.
