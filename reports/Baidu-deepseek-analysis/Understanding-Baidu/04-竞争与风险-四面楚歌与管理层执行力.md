## Baidu's Four Battlefields, Besieged on All Sides

**Search is being carved up by Douyin, its AI app ranks 11th, its AI Cloud share is only 6%, and autonomous driving has never proven profitable — Baidu is fighting on four battlefields simultaneously, with no assurance of victory on any of them.**

Understanding Baidu's competitive landscape requires examining the four battlefields separately — because the opponents differ on each front, and so does the manner of potential defeat.

### Battlefield One: Search Advertising — Being Carved Up by Multiple Rivals

The competitive landscape for Baidu Search has shifted from "one dominant player" to "carved up among many":

| Competitor | Weapon | Use Case Taken | Threat Rating |
|--------|------|-----------|---------|
| Douyin Search | 700 million DAU + video content | "Quick search" (5.3 billion searches/day) | ★★★★★ |
| Xiaohongshu | 300 million MAU + purchase-decision content | "Guide search" (600 million searches/day) | ★★★★ |
| WeChat Search | 1.3 billion WeChat users + Yuanbao AI | "In-ecosystem search" | ★★★★ |
| DeepSeek | 127 million MAU + open source | "Deep Q&A" | ★★★ |

> Data sources: QuestMobile (March 2026), TMTPost.

**The most lethal factor isn't any single competitor — it's "the fragmentation of search demand"** — users no longer route all their searches through Baidu, but instead distribute them across different platforms depending on the use case. This fragmentation is irreversible.

**Counter-argument**: "Fragmented search doesn't mean search is disappearing. Users search for videos on Douyin and for guides on Xiaohongshu — but 'serious search' (academic, technical, news, legal) is still Baidu's territory. Baidu Search won't 'die'; it's just moving from being a 'universal entry point' to a 'specialized search engine.'"

**Rebuttal**: "The advertising value of 'specialized search' is far lower than that of a 'universal entry point.' The bulk of Baidu's ad revenue comes from high-CPC verticals like healthcare, education, and finance — and search demand in these verticals is being consumed by AI conversational tools (Doubao, DeepSeek). Even if Baidu holds onto 'specialized search,' ad revenue will keep declining."

### Battlefield Two: Consumer-Facing AI Applications — Already Left Behind

This is the battlefield where Baidu has lost most completely.

| Rank | App | MAU | Gap vs. Baidu |
|------|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Doubao (ByteDance) | **345 million** | 72x |
| 2 | Qwen (Alibaba) | **~170 million** | 35x |
| 3 | DeepSeek | **127 million** | 26x |
| 4 | Yuanbao (Tencent) | **~100 million+** | 21x |
| **11** | **Ernie Bot** | **4.79 million** | — |

> Data source: QuestMobile (March 2026).

**Baidu was the earliest company in China to build a large-model conversational product at scale (Ernie Bot, March 2023) — yet 2 years later it ranks 11th.** This isn't a problem of starting late; it's a structural weakness in distribution capability and product strategy.

ByteDance has Douyin and Tencent has WeChat — they can "embed" AI products into the home screens of over a billion users. Baidu has no equivalent super-app. Baidu App has 679 million MAU, but users come there to search, not to "chat."

**This gap is nearly impossible to close in the short term.** In the battle for the consumer-facing AI entry point, Baidu is essentially out of the running.

### Battlefield Three: AI Cloud — Growing Fast but Small Share

Baidu AI Cloud has found a differentiated positioning in the AI era (foundation-model capability + government/enterprise customers), but faces growing competitive pressure:

| Threat | Specifics |
|------|---------|
| Alibaba Cloud (36% share) | Clear leader, most complete ecosystem |
| Volcano Engine (14.8%, +60%) | Fastest-growing, combining internal ByteDance compute demand with external expansion |
| Huawei Cloud (19%) | Stronger government/enterprise relationships, self-sufficient Ascend chips |
| Price war | AI Cloud price competition intensified in 2025, broadly pressuring gross margins |

> Data source: Omdia 2025H1.

**Baidu AI Cloud's biggest competitive risk is Volcano Engine** — ByteDance has both the advantage of training models on massive consumer-side data and the ability to absorb compute internally through its own businesses, plus an aggressive pricing strategy. Baidu's 6.1% share could be squeezed further as Volcano Engine's 60% growth rate catches up.

**Counter-argument**: "Government and enterprise clients don't shop on price — they shop on trust. Baidu's 65% coverage of central state-owned enterprises and every systemically important bank — these clients won't migrate just because Volcano Engine is 10% cheaper. The competitive barrier in government/enterprise AI cloud lies in security review, compliance capability, and industry know-how, not price."

### Battlefield Four: Autonomous Driving — Globally Leading Scale, Commercialization Unproven

Apollo Go leads globally in scale (weekly orders of 250,000, on par with Waymo), but commercialization still faces multiple challenges:

| Challenge | Description |
|------|------|
| Regulatory limits | Full driverless operation requires city-by-city approval from local governments, with uncontrollable timelines |
| Profitability model | Full P&L has never been disclosed; "unit-economics breakeven" excludes R&D |
| Competitors | Huawei ADS empowering automakers, Tesla FSD, Xpeng, and others |
| Public acceptance | The Wuhan incident sparked social controversy; public trust still needs time to rebuild |

## Management: The Biggest Uncertainty

Any investment judgment on Baidu ultimately comes down to one person — **Robin Li**.

### Technical Judgment: Excellent

Robin Li's foresight on technology direction ranks among the best of China's internet entrepreneurs:

- **2013**: Founded the Institute of Deep Learning (IDL), 3-4 years ahead of Alibaba and Tencent
- **2017**: Went "all in on AI," launched the Apollo autonomous driving platform
- **2019**: Spun off Kunlunxin, positioning in AI chips
- **2023**: Launched China's first large-model conversational product (Ernie Bot)

**Every time, Robin Li has been the earliest among China's internet tycoons to spot the direction AI was heading.** This is beyond dispute.

### Execution: Cause for Concern

But between "seeing" and "achieving" lies a chasm of execution:

| Investment Failure | Investment | Outcome |
|---------|------|------|
| O2O (Baidu Nuomi) | Burned **20 billion yuan** | Shut down in 2018, written off to zero |
| 91 Wireless | Acquired for **$1.9 billion** | Completely written off |
| Baidu Waimai (food delivery) | Tens of billions of yuan | Sold to Ele.me at a loss |
| Jidu Auto | Baidu-Geely joint venture | **Collapsed in 2024** |
| iQIYI | Continuous cash burn | Marginally profitable, strategic value questionable |

> Data sources: Baidu annual reports, public reporting.

**20 billion yuan burned on O2O, $1.9 billion lost on 91 Wireless, and the collapse of Jidu Auto — three major losses, each a gamble outside the circle of competence.**

The most unsettling pattern is: **Robin Li saw the correct direction (mobile internet, app distribution, smart cars), but chose the wrong execution path, or committed mismatched resources.**

Will this pattern repeat in the AI era?

**Counter-argument**: "The past failures were mistakes made in 'non-core businesses.' AI is Baidu's core circle of competence, where Robin Li has a decade of accumulated depth — execution will be different this time. The profit rebound in 2026Q1 (+48%) shows execution is already improving."

**Rebuttal**: "The issue was never Robin Li's judgment on AI technology — that has been correct. The issue is: can Baidu convert its technical edge into commercial results? Ernie Bot's MAU of 4.79 million vs. Doubao's 345 million — this is already evidence of 'seeing it but not achieving it.'"

### Governance Structure

Through a dual-class share structure, Robin Li holds **59% of voting power**, giving him absolute control of Baidu. This means:

- All major decisions are made by one person
- There is no effective external check
- If Robin Li's judgment is wrong, no one can stop it

For a controlling shareholder with an uneven execution track record, **absolute control is a risk, not a safety cushion**.

## Six Core Risks Checklist

| # | Risk | Severity | Probability |
|---|------|---------|------|
| 1 | Accelerating decline in search advertising (annual decline >20%) | **Fatal** | Medium |
| 2 | AI investment fails to monetize ("winning the technology, losing the investment") | **Fatal** | Medium |
| 3 | Free cash flow remains negative (depleting net cash) | **Severe** | Medium-to-high |
| 4 | Management investing outside its circle of competence (repeating the O2O mistake) | **Severe** | Low |
| 5 | AI Cloud share squeezed by Volcano Engine | **Medium** | Medium |
| 6 | Deterioration in US-China relations affecting chip supply | **Severe** | Low |

### The Biggest Risk: Winning the Technology, Losing the Investment

This is the single thing most worth thinking through clearly before investing in Baidu.

Baidu's AI technical capability is real — Ernie 5.0 with 2.4 trillion parameters, Kunlunxin as China's No. 2 chip by shipment volume, Apollo Go leading globally in scale. But:

- Ernie foundation model is strong → yet the consumer entry point was lost to Doubao (a distribution problem)
- AI Cloud is growing fast → yet its share is only 6.1% (a scale problem)
- Apollo Go has massive scale → yet it has never proven it can be profitable (a commercialization problem)

**History has seen too many companies that were "technologically the strongest but the worst at investing"** — Xerox invented the graphical user interface but Apple took it away; Nokia had the best phone technology but was disrupted by the iPhone; Yahoo was the earliest search engine but was replaced by Google.

**Will Baidu become "the Yahoo of the AI era" — the earliest starter and biggest spender, only to have the fruits harvested by those who came later?** There's no answer to this question yet, but it's worth every investor thinking about carefully.

| Dimension | Conclusion |

| Search competition | Carved up by four rivals; fragmentation is irreversible; ad revenue will keep declining |

| Autonomous driving | No. 1 globally by scale, but commercialization remains unproven |
| Management | Excellent technical foresight; execution and capital allocation are concerning |

**In one sentence: Baidu doesn't lack technology, cash, or direction — what it lacks is the execution to turn these into money. This was the problem of the past 10 years, and it's also the most important variable to watch over the next 5.**
