## I. The 2025-2026 Hong Kong IPO Market Environment

### 1.1 Fundraising scale: accelerating further after a decade-high

The Hong Kong IPO market is going through a rare boom cycle:

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 | Full-year 2026 forecast |
|------|--------|--------|----------|---------------|
| Number of new listings | ~70 | 117 | 40 | 150-160 |
| Fundraising scale | ~HK$87 billion | ~HK$285.8 billion | ~HK$109.9 billion | HK$300-440 billion |
| YoY growth rate | - | +228% | +489% (Q1 YoY) | +5% to +54% |

In Q1 2026, Hong Kong's IPO market took just 79 days to reach the HK$100 billion fundraising mark, whereas it took nearly half a year to hit that figure over the same period in 2025. Deloitte forecasts about 160 new listings for the full year, raising no less than HK$300 billion; CICC is even more optimistic, forecasting a fundraising scale of about HK$440 billion.

**Core drivers:** dual A+H listings (Muyuan Foods at HK$12.1 billion, Eastroc Beverage at HK$11.1 billion, Montage Technology at HK$8.1 billion) and a wave of tech-company listings (the information-technology sector accounted for 67.79% of Q1 fundraising).

### 1.2 Post-listing performance: sharpening structural divergence

Behind the booming fundraising figures, the problem of stocks trading below their IPO price is becoming more severe:

| Period | Below-IPO-price rate | Notes |
|------|--------|------|
| Full-year 2025 | 27.6% (as of now) | 49 of 117 fell below their offer price |
| January 2026 | 0% (first-day) | All 11 new listings closed up on debut, with an average first-day gain of 33.7% |
| Q1 2026 overall | ~48-62% | Statistics vary by methodology; nearly half traded below offer price |

**The divergence is extremely pronounced:**
- Leading tech stocks have performed remarkably: Zhipu is up 595% since listing, MiniMax is up 542%, Biren Technology gained 76% on its first day, and SICC gained 60% on its first day
- Widespread declines among smaller offerings: Master Tong fell by half (-49%) on its first trading day, and Youlesai Sharing dropped 43% on its first day
- The "three highs" problem stands out: high pricing (some new listings trade at over 40x P/E, far above comparable companies), high embellishment (selectively exaggerated market positioning), and a high rate of trading below offer price

**Implications for Dongchedi:** the market still shows strong demand for large-cap tech stocks with a compelling story, but overpricing will be severely punished. Dongchedi, as ByteDance's first IPO, comes with built-in buzz and traffic, but if its valuation departs from fundamentals, its risk of trading below offer price is not low either.

### 1.3 Investor sentiment: structural optimism amid caution

Current Hong Kong investor sentiment shows several characteristics:

1. **Near-fervent enthusiasm for AI and hard tech** — companies like Zhipu and MiniMax have risen several-fold since listing, even though these companies are not yet profitable
2. **Valuation compression for traditional internet and consumer platforms** — Autohome's market cap has shrunk from over $10 billion to about $2.05 billion, with its share price down more than 25% over the past year
3. **Institutional capital is abundant but highly selective** — large offerings see active oversubscription, while smaller ones see tepid demand
4. **Southbound capital keeps flowing in but concentrates on leaders** — capital is concentrated in leading tech names and high-dividend stocks

### 1.4 The lurking concern of a trillion-yuan lock-up expiration wave

Hong Kong faces roughly HK$1.7 trillion in share-lock-up expirations in 2026 (nearly triple the HK$600 billion in 2025), with September alone accounting for as much as HK$530 billion. The main source is the expiration of lock-up periods for cornerstone investors and pre-IPO shareholders of the tech and biomedical companies that listed in large numbers in 2025.

This poses potential pressure on the new-listing environment for the second half of the year:
- The trillion-yuan lock-up expiration combined with dense IPO activity creates "dual pressure" on market liquidity
- Tech growth stocks that have already risen sharply, carry high valuations, and have a thin profit base will be hit hardest
- Liquidity pressure will be especially pronounced among small and mid-cap names

## II. Hong Kong Listing Case Studies: ByteDance and Chinese Internet Companies

### 2.1 ByteDance's listing history

ByteDance has repeatedly been rumored to be preparing for an IPO, but this has never been confirmed:

| Time | Event | Official response |
|------|------|---------|
| 2020 | Rumors that Douyin planned a separate Hong Kong listing | "Considering listing some businesses, not yet finalized" |
| April 2021 | Listing rumors | "Not yet ready for listing, no listing plans" |
| May 2022 | ByteDance renamed itself "Douyin Group" | Interpreted as a signal of an impending listing |
| April 2025 | Rumors that Moonton and Nuverse would bundle a US IPO | "Currently no listing plans" |
| September 2025 | Hong Kong listing rumors | VP Li Liang denied it, saying "cannot rule out that someone is stirring up speculation" |

ByteDance's latest valuation is around $480-550 billion (based on secondary-market trades such as those via Today Capital), making it one of the highest-valued unlisted companies in the world.

### 2.2 Reference paths for major-company spinoff listings

Dongchedi's spinoff-listing model has clear precedents:

| Company | Spinoff entity | Listing time | Performance |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| JD.com | JD Health | December 2020 | Surged on debut, later pulled back |
| JD.com | JD Logistics | May 2021 | Slight first-day gain, long-term decline afterward |
| Alibaba | Cainiao | 2023 (withdrawn) | Application withdrawn on the eve of listing |
| Meituan | - | - | Listed as a whole, never spun off |

The core logic of spinoff listings: giving the subsidiary independent fundraising capability, providing early investors with an exit path, and creating a replicable template for the parent company to spin off other businesses.

### 2.3 Benchmark cases of Hong Kong tech IPOs in Q1 2026

| Company | Sector | Gain since listing | Feature |
|------|------|-------------|--------|
| Zhipu | AI large models | +595% | Market cap surpassed HK$400 billion |

**Key observation:** these high-gain stocks all belong to the AI and semiconductor tracks. Automotive internet platforms are not among the current hot tracks in Hong Kong, so Dongchedi is unlikely to replicate this level of buzz.

## III. Dongchedi's Possible Fundraising Scale and Use of Proceeds

### 3.1 Fundraising scale forecast

According to multiple media reports, Dongchedi's IPO fundraising target is **$1.0-1.5 billion** (approximately HK$7.8-11.7 billion).

**Scale positioning analysis:**
- At $1.0 billion raised, it would rank as a large-to-mid-size offering within the 2026 Hong Kong IPO market (the top three Q1 offerings each exceeded HK$8 billion)
- At $1.5 billion raised, it would rank among the year's top ten IPOs
- Based on a pre-IPO valuation of $3 billion, a $1.5 billion raise implies dilution of about 33%, corresponding to a post-IPO valuation of about $4.5 billion
- At a $1.0 billion raise with 25% dilution, the corresponding post-IPO valuation is about $4.0 billion

### 3.2 Presumed use of proceeds

Referencing precedents from similar internet-platform-company IPOs, Dongchedi's proceeds may likely be used for:

| Use | Estimated share | Notes |
|------|---------|------|
| Technology R&D and AI investment | 30-35% | Recommendation algorithms, AI vehicle inspection, smart matching |
| Business expansion and marketing | 25-30% | Expanding user scale, dealer-network coverage |
| Automotive aftermarket layout | 15-20% | Used cars, car care, insurance, and other asset-heavy businesses |
| Strategic investment and M&A | 10-15% | Upstream/downstream industry-chain integration |
| General working capital | 5-10% | Day-to-day operations |

**Special note:** the automotive-aftermarket business (used cars, car care, etc.) carries a gross margin of only 6-24%, far below the roughly 80% gross margin of the media-advertising business. If a large share of proceeds is directed to the aftermarket, it would pull down overall profitability — a risk factor worth watching.

These pre-IPO investors are highly likely to participate as cornerstone investors in the IPO, to demonstrate confidence in the company's long-term value.

### 4.2 Potential new cornerstone investors

Based on ByteDance's network of relationships and Dongchedi's business characteristics, potential new cornerstone investors could include:

**Strategic cornerstones:**
- Major automakers (such as BYD, NIO, Li Auto) — as Dongchedi's largest group of advertising clients, a strategic investment could deepen the partnership
- Auto-dealership groups (such as Zhongsheng Group, Grand Auto) — the core partners of Dongchedi's CPS model

**Financial cornerstones:**
- Sovereign wealth funds (Singapore's GIC, China Investment Corporation, etc.)
- Long-term institutions (BlackRock, Fidelity, etc.)
- Chinese-capital institutions (CICC Capital, China Merchants Capital, etc.)

### 4.3 Cornerstone investor lock-up periods

Under Hong Kong Stock Exchange rules:
- **Cornerstone investor lock-up: 6 months** (from the listing date)
- **Controlling shareholder lock-up: 12 months** (6 months of no sales plus 6 further months during which control cannot be relinquished)
- Pre-IPO investors typically also have 6-12 month lock-ups, depending on their specific agreements

## V. IPO Pricing-Range Forecast

### 5.1 Comparable-company valuations

| Company | Market cap | 2025 revenue | Net profit | P/S | P/E | Notes |
|------|------|-----------|--------|-----|-----|------|
| Autohome (2518.HK / ATHM) | ~$2.05 billion | 6.45 billion yuan (~$920 million) | 1.44 billion yuan (~$210 million) | 2.2x | ~10x | Business continues to decline, DAU ~77 million |
| Dongchedi (pre-IPO) | $3 billion | Undisclosed | Undisclosed | - | - | DAU over 10 million, younger user base |

**Autohome's decline trajectory deserves attention:**
- 2025 revenue down 8.4% year-over-year, net profit down 14.2%
- Q4 revenue of 1.46 billion yuan, down 18% year-over-year; net profit of 220 million yuan, down 28%
- Media-services revenue down 24.3%
- Gross margin fell from 78.9% to 72.4%
- Operating cash flow fell from over 2 billion yuan to 890 million yuan
- Market cap has shrunk from a peak of about $10 billion to about $2.05 billion

### 5.2 Pricing-logic analysis

**Scenario one: conservative pricing (post-IPO valuation of $3.5-4.0 billion)**
- Corresponds to a raise of about $0.8-1.0 billion (dilution of 20-25%)
- A premium of about 75-95% relative to Autohome's $2.05 billion market cap
- Rationale for the premium: a younger user base, ByteDance's traffic ecosystem, CPS-model innovation, and growth potential
- This range corresponds to a P/S of about 4-5x (assuming Dongchedi's revenue is around $800 million)

**Scenario two: neutral pricing (post-IPO valuation of $4.5-5.0 billion)**
- Corresponds to a raise of about $1.2-1.5 billion (dilution of 25-30%)
- A premium of about 120-145% relative to Autohome
- Requires proof of: revenue growth significantly outpacing Autohome, a clear path to profitability, and substantive progress in the aftermarket business
- This range corresponds to a P/S of about 5.5-6.5x

**Scenario three: aggressive pricing (post-IPO valuation of $5.5-6.5 billion)**
- Would require an AI-driven narrative boost or an extremely optimistic market mood
- Risk of trading below offer price rises significantly
- Unless the company can demonstrate revenue growth or profitability figures far beyond expectations

### 5.3 Core pricing recommendation

**A reasonable post-IPO valuation range: $4.0-5.0 billion**

Rationale:
1. With a pre-IPO valuation of $3 billion, an IPO typically requires a 30-60% valuation uplift to attract investors
2. Autohome, as the anchor comparable, continues to decline, making Dongchedi's "replacement" narrative appealing
3. A scarcity premium as ByteDance's first IPO
4. However, the automotive-internet track overall is not at the core of the current tech boom (AI/chips/robotics)
5. Dongchedi's DAU (10 million) remains far smaller than Autohome's DAU (77 million), even though its users are younger

**Key risk factors:**
- If the prospectus's disclosed financials show a loss or a widening loss, the valuation will come under pressure
- If the CPS transition causes a short-term revenue decline (similar to Autohome's 24% drop in media revenue), the market may apply a discount
- If Hong Kong's trillion-yuan lock-up expiration wave in the second half of the year triggers a market pullback, the IPO window could narrow

## VI. Post-Listing Free Float and Lock-Up Expiration Schedule

### 6.1 Estimated shareholding structure

| Shareholder type | Estimated shareholding | Lock-up period |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| ByteDance (controlling shareholder) | 55-65% | 12 months |
| Pre-IPO investors (HongShan, KKR, etc.) | 15-20% | 6-12 months |
| Management and employee stock options | 5-8% | Typically 12 months |
| Cornerstone investors (new) | 5-8% | 6 months |
| Public offering (freely tradable) | 10-15% | No lock-up |

### 6.2 Free-float estimate

Assuming a post-IPO total market cap of $4.5 billion:
- **Freely tradable float on the first trading day**: about $450-675 million (10-15% of total market cap)
- This float size is at a middling level for the Hong Kong market — liquidity will not be too poor, but it will not be abundant either

### 6.3 Lock-up expiration schedule (assuming a Q3 2026 listing)

| Time point | Lock-up expiring | Estimated expiration size |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| 6 months post-listing (~Q1 2027) | Cornerstone investors | ~$225-360 million |
| 6-12 months post-listing (Q1-Q3 2027) | Pre-IPO investors, gradually | ~$675 million-$900 million |
| 12 months post-listing (~Q3 2027) | Controlling shareholder and management | ~$2.475-2.925 billion |

**Key risk:**
- The first wave of lock-up expiration at 6 months post-listing (cornerstone investors) typically has limited impact
- The real pressure comes at 12 months post-listing, when the massive lock-up expiration of the controlling shareholder and pre-IPO investors could put significant pressure on the share price
- If financial investors such as HongShan choose to exit in scale after their lock-up expires, selling pressure will be substantial
- Whether ByteDance reduces its stake after the lock-up expires is an important signal to watch — a reduction would suggest limited long-term commitment from the parent company to Dongchedi

## VIII. Conclusions and Key Judgments

### Assessment for IPO subscription

1. **If priced below $4.0 billion (conservative range)**: worth actively participating in. ByteDance's first-IPO scarcity, the substitution opportunity created by Autohome's decline, and the currently buoyant sentiment toward Hong Kong tech stocks are all favorable to first-day performance.

2. **If priced at $4.5-5.0 billion (neutral range)**: requires careful evaluation of the financial data in the prospectus. If revenue growth and profit margins can support this valuation, moderate participation remains reasonable.

3. **If priced above $5.0 billion (aggressive range)**: recommend avoiding. Given the nearly 50% below-offer-price rate in Hong Kong in 2026, the risks of a richly priced new listing far outweigh the potential reward.

### Optimal listing window

- **Q3 2026 (July-September)** is likely the best window: it avoids the dense first-half issuance period of large A+H share listings, while still allowing the listing to be completed before the full impact of the September trillion-yuan lock-up expiration wave is felt
- If delayed to Q4, market sentiment could weaken due to lock-up-expiration pressure

### Signals to keep monitoring

1. The timing of the prospectus filing and financial-data disclosure — the only reliable basis for judging valuation reasonableness
2. The cornerstone investor list and subscription scale — reflecting how well institutions accept the pricing
3. Whether the underwriting syndicate expands (currently only Citigroup and Goldman Sachs) — more banks joining usually signals a larger offering
4. ByteDance's role in the IPO — whether it provides a performance guarantee or a strategic-support commitment
