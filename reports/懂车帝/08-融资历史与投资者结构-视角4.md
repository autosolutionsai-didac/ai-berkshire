## 2. Financing History

### 2.1 Core fact: only one round of external financing

**This is a highly unusual case: from its founding in 2017 through 2024, Dongchedi never conducted any external financing.** For a span of seven years, Dongchedi grew entirely on ByteDance's internal resources — traffic, technology, and capital.

Not until 2024, in preparation for an independent IPO, did it conduct its first — and to date only — round of external financing:

### 2.2 Series A financing details (June 2024)

| Item | Detail |
|------|------|
| Round | Series A (first and, to date, only round of external financing) |
| Amount raised | Roughly $600 million (roughly RMB 5.8 billion); early reports had cited a target of $700-800 million |
| Post-money valuation | Roughly $3.0 billion (roughly RMB 21.7 billion) |
| Lead investor | HongShan (formerly Sequoia Capital China), contributing $400-500 million |
| Co-investors | General Atlantic, KKR, Gaorong Capital |

### 2.3 Deep dive on investors

**Key finding: every investor in this round is a longstanding ByteDance shareholder.**

| Investor | Relationship with ByteDance | Role in Dongchedi's Series A | Estimated contribution |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|-----------|
| HongShan | Participated in ByteDance's Series C in 2014, one of ByteDance's major shareholders | Lead investor | $400-500 million |
| KKR | Participated in ByteDance's pre-IPO round in 2018 | Co-investor | Undisclosed (estimated tens of millions of dollars) |
| General Atlantic | Participated in ByteDance's Series E in 2017 | Co-investor | Undisclosed (estimated tens of millions of dollars) |
| Gaorong Capital | Early-stage investor in ByteDance | Co-investor | Undisclosed |

**Interpretation (opinion):** It is widely believed in the industry that a deeper motive behind this financing round was that, with ByteDance itself yet to go public, an independent listing for Dongchedi offers longstanding shareholders who have supported ByteDance a channel for exit or liquidity. This is both a validation of Dongchedi's independent value and, in effect, a "reward arrangement" from ByteDance to its longtime shareholders.

## 3. Deep Dive on Equity Structure

### 3.1 Domestic equity structure (per corporate registration records)

```
Xiamen Dongchezu Technology Co., Ltd. (established December 8, 2023)
├── He Jian: 60% (President of Dongchedi)
└── Zhao Jing: 40% (Dongchedi's Head of HR)
        │
        │ 100% holding
        ▼
Beijing Dongchedi Technology Co., Ltd. (registered capital RMB 10 million)
        │
        │ Pledged to
        ▼
Beijing Dongchezu Technology Co., Ltd. (pledged equity of RMB 10 million, i.e., the entire stake)
        │
        │ 100% holding
        ▼
Hong Kong Dongchedi Technology Co., Ltd. (formerly "Hong Kong Xingfuli Technology Co., Ltd.," established 2021)
```

### 3.2 ByteDance's actual control mechanism

**On the surface:** In December 2023, Toutiao exited its equity stake in Dongchedi, and the controlling shareholder changed to Xiamen Dongchezu, held by two individuals, He Jian and Zhao Jing. On the surface, Dongchedi appears fully independent.

**In reality:** ByteDance retains actual control through the following mechanisms:

1. **Equity pledge**: Xiamen Dongchezu has pledged 100% of Beijing Dongchedi's equity to Beijing Dongchezu — which is itself 100% held by Hong Kong Dongchedi, whose equity chain very likely ultimately points back to ByteDance's offshore structure.

2. **Personnel control**: Both He Jian and Zhao Jing are core employees within the ByteDance system. He Jian is Dongchedi's president, and Zhao Jing is its head of HR. Both show clear characteristics of acting as "nominee holders."

3. **A textbook VIE structure**: Hong Kong Dongchedi (established 2021) -> Beijing Dongchezu -> pledge-based control of Beijing Dongchedi -> operation of the Dongchedi app. This is the standard VIE structure used by Chinese internet companies for overseas listings.

### 3.3 Presumed ultimate control structure (opinion)

Based on the customary practice of Chinese internet companies going public in Hong Kong, the full structure is presumed as follows:

```
Cayman Islands listing entity (Dongchedi Inc. or similar name)
        │
        ▼
BVI intermediate holding company
        │
        ▼
Hong Kong Dongchedi Technology Co., Ltd.
        │
        ▼
Beijing Dongchezu Technology Co., Ltd. (WFOE)
        │ VIE agreement control
        ▼
Beijing Dongchedi Technology Co., Ltd. (domestic operating entity)
        │
        ▼
Chongqing Dongchedi Technology Co., Ltd. (actual operations)
```

**Where data is insufficient:** the shareholding ratio at the Cayman level (ByteDance vs. external investors vs. management) is currently not publicly disclosed. Given the Series A financing of $600 million at a post-money valuation of $3.0 billion, external investors hold roughly 20%, and ByteDance is estimated to hold more than 70%.

### 3.4 Estimated shareholding ratios (projection)

| Shareholder | Estimated stake | Basis |
|------|-------------|------|
| ByteDance | 70-80% | Series A diluted only about 20%; ByteDance was the original 100% holder |

**Note: the above are projections based on public information; the actual ratios must await prospectus disclosure.**

## 4. Management Shareholding and Employee Incentives

### 4.1 Core management

| Name | Position | Known shareholding |
|------|------|-------------|
| He Jian | President of Dongchedi | Holds 60% of Xiamen Dongchezu (nominee in nature, not the ultimate beneficial interest) |
| Zhao Jing | Head of HR at Dongchedi | Holds 40% of Xiamen Dongchezu (nominee in nature) |

**Note:** He Jian's and Zhao Jing's stakes in Xiamen Dongchezu are most likely a nominee-holding arrangement set up as part of the VIE structure, rather than genuine economic interests. In a standard VIE structure, the nominal shareholders of the domestic operating entity are typically individual members of management, while economic interests and control are assigned via contract to the offshore listing entity.

### 4.2 Employee option pool

**Publicly available information is extremely limited.** No public disclosure has been found regarding an employee equity incentive plan since Dongchedi's independence.

**Reference information available:**

- ByteDance's own option mechanism: options within total compensation vest over a 4-year schedule (20%-25%-25%-30%), and after vesting there is a 5-year wait before shares can be sold in stages, for a total cycle of 9-10 years.
- In April 2025, ByteDance's option buyback price for active employees was $189.90 per share, and $161.42 per share for departed employees.
- When employees were transferred from ByteDance to Dongchedi as it became independent, there is no public information on how their existing ByteDance options were handled (continuing to hold ByteDance options, or converting them into Dongchedi options).

**Projection (opinion):** Following the customary practice for internet companies pursuing a Hong Kong IPO, Dongchedi will likely establish an independent Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP) before the IPO, typically accounting for 10-15% of total share capital. But the specific plan awaits prospectus disclosure.

## 5. Analysis of Pre-IPO Investment Channels

### 5.1 Currently possible investment channels

#### Channel one: transfer of stakes held by Series A investment institutions

| Item | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Feasibility | Low to medium |
| Description | HongShan, KKR, General Atlantic, and Gaorong Capital are the Series A investors. In theory, one could gain indirect exposure to Dongchedi through LP-interest transfers or co-investment funds affiliated with these institutions |
| Threshold | Extremely high. LP admission thresholds for these top-tier funds typically run from several million to tens of millions of dollars, and require qualified-investor status |
| Estimated entry price | Based on the $3.0 billion valuation, i.e., the Series A price |

#### Channel two: dedicated Pre-IPO funds or secondary (S) funds

| Item | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Feasibility | Medium |
| Description | There may be Pre-IPO funds in the market focused on the ByteDance ecosystem currently fundraising. In addition, a secondary fund (S fund) might acquire part of a Series A investor's stake on the secondary market |
| Threshold | High. Typically requires qualified-investor status, with a minimum investment in the millions of dollars |
| Estimated entry price | Likely at a premium to Series A, given IPO expectations, in the $3.0-5.0 billion valuation range |
| Risk note | Requires rigorous scrutiny of whether the fund's underlying assets actually hold Dongchedi equity |

#### Channel three: transfer of old shares (secondary interests)

| Item | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Feasibility | Low |
| Description | In theory, early Dongchedi Series A investors or individuals holding economic interests in Dongchedi might transfer old shares |
| Threshold | Requires a personal network to reach sellers. Also, the Pre-IPO stage typically carries transfer restrictions |
| Legal risk | High. Involves transfer of economic interests under a VIE structure, requiring highly specialized legal structuring |

#### Channel four: IPO cornerstone investors

| Item | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Feasibility | Medium to high (for institutional investors) |
| Description | Hong Kong IPOs typically bring in cornerstone investors, who subscribe at the IPO offer price and lock up for 6 months. Citigroup and Goldman Sachs, as underwriters, will be responsible for recruiting cornerstone investors |
| Threshold | Usually institutional investors or ultra-high-net-worth individuals; single subscriptions typically range from tens of millions to hundreds of millions of dollars |
| Estimated entry price | IPO offer price (expected to carry a significant premium over Series A) |

#### Channel five: Hong Kong IPO public offering

| Item | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Feasibility | High |
| Description | If Dongchedi lists in Hong Kong, both retail and institutional investors can subscribe through the public offering |
| Threshold | Lowest — only requires a Hong Kong securities account |
| Estimated entry price | IPO offer price |
| Note | For a hot new listing (especially ByteDance's first IPO), the oversubscription multiple could be extremely high, and allocation ratios may be very low |

### 5.2 Risk notes

1. **IPO timing uncertainty**: although underwriters have been selected, the listing timing and valuation still carry variables, subject to market conditions, regulatory approvals, and other factors.
2. **Valuation-premium risk**: from a $3.0 billion Series A valuation to a possible $5.0-7.0 billion at IPO (projected), whether a doubling of valuation in such a short period is reasonable warrants careful assessment.
3. **VIE structure risk**: as the standard structure for Chinese internet companies listing in Hong Kong, the VIE structure always carries legal-level uncertainty.
4. **ByteDance-dependency risk**: Dongchedi's core competitiveness (traffic, algorithms, data) is highly dependent on the ByteDance ecosystem, and how this relationship will be maintained after independence is a key risk.

## 6. The Possibility of ByteDance Employees Obtaining Dongchedi Equity

### 6.1 Current known situation

**There is no public information indicating that ByteDance employees can obtain Dongchedi equity directly through internal channels.**

### 6.2 Possible paths (projection)

| Path | Likelihood | Description |
|------|--------|------|
| Indirect benefit via ByteDance options | Low | ByteDance holds roughly 70-80% of Dongchedi's equity, but the value reflected in ByteDance options is tied to ByteDance as a whole; Dongchedi's impact on ByteDance's overall valuation is limited ($3.0 billion vs. ByteDance's roughly $300 billion valuation, about a 1% share) |
| Dedicated Dongchedi employee options | Medium | Employees who have transferred to the new Dongchedi entity may receive equity incentives tied to independent Dongchedi, but there is currently no public information on this |
| Internal subscription | Insufficient data | Some internet companies offer employees an internal subscription opportunity before listing, but whether Dongchedi will do so is unknown |
| IPO priority employee allocation | Possible | In Hong Kong IPOs, issuers sometimes set up a priority employee-allocation program; employees who have transferred to the new Dongchedi entity may have this opportunity |

### 6.3 A key distinction

Two categories of employees need to be distinguished:

1. **Employees who have already transferred to the new Dongchedi entity**: these employees' labor relationships have already moved from ByteDance to the independent Dongchedi entity. They are most likely to receive independent Dongchedi options.
2. **Employees who remain at ByteDance but serve the Dongchedi business**: some technical and platform-support staff may remain on ByteDance's payroll while supporting Dongchedi; this group is less likely to obtain Dongchedi equity.

## 8. Core Conclusions and Investment Recommendations

### 8.1 Key findings

1. **Extremely simple financing history**: Dongchedi conducted only one round of external financing in seven years, and every Series A investor was a longstanding ByteDance shareholder. This shows that Dongchedi's growth relied entirely on ByteDance's internal resources, while external capital served mainly to prepare for the IPO and to provide an exit channel for longtime shareholders.

2. **ByteDance's actual control**: although independent at the level of corporate registration, ByteDance retains actual control through the VIE structure and equity pledges. ByteDance is expected to remain the controlling shareholder after the IPO.

3. **Rapidly inflating valuation**: from a $3.0 billion Series A valuation to a projected IPO target of $5.0-7.0 billion, the valuation could roughly double in under two years. Attention should be paid to whether the specific financial data at IPO time can support this valuation.

4. **Low information transparency**: as an unlisted company, key information about Dongchedi — such as financial data (revenue, profit, cash flow), employee option plans, and specific equity ratios — has not been publicly disclosed. The informational basis for investment decisions is thin.

### 8.2 A rational assessment of Pre-IPO investment

**Advantages:**
- ByteDance's first IPO, generating extremely high buzz and attention
- Strong user growth, and a large addressable market in auto transactions
- Backing from top-tier underwriters and investment institutions

**Risks:**
- The valuation may already be fully priced in, with post-IPO upside dependent on market sentiment
- Core competitiveness (traffic) is highly dependent on the ByteDance ecosystem, and its sustainability post-independence is questionable
- The automotive vertical-media sector has a fairly limited ceiling (Autohome's market cap has long hovered around $3.0-4.0 billion, for reference)
- Severe information asymmetry at the Pre-IPO stage puts retail investors at an informational disadvantage

**Recommendation:** for individual investors, the most practical way to participate is to wait for the Hong Kong IPO public offering rather than pursue high-threshold Pre-IPO channels. The information asymmetry and liquidity risk of Pre-IPO channels far outweigh the potential additional return.
