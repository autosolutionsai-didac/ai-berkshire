# Dongchedi Dealer Relations and B2B Ecosystem: An In-Depth Analysis

> Research date: May 2026
> Research purpose: To examine Dongchedi's business-model moat, customer stickiness, and growth sustainability from the B2B (dealer) perspective

---

## I. Dealer Coverage Scale and Market Penetration

### 1.1 Core Coverage Data

| Metric | Data | Time Point |
|------|------|----------|
| Maichetong (Sell-a-Car) partner dealers | Over 30,000 | October 2025 |
| Auto brands covered | Over 110 | 2025 |
| CPS-model merchants | Over 28,500 | End of 2024 |
| Merchant-reported completed transactions | Surpassed 1 million units | January-October 2025 |

### 1.2 Coverage Rate Estimate

The nationwide total of 4S dealerships is approximately 31,400 (2025 data), and Dongchedi's Maichetong covers over 30,000 dealers. However, it should be noted that Dongchedi's definition of "dealer" includes 4S stores, second-tier dealers, used-car dealers, and other formats — not limited to 4S stores alone. Measured against the 4S-store count alone, Dongchedi's coverage of traditional 4S stores is close to 80%-90%.

Compared with Autohome's claim of "covering over 90% of OEMs and dealers," the two platforms have essentially achieved full coverage of the dealer base, so the competitive focus is no longer "whether coverage exists" but "who can deliver more completed sales."

### 1.3 Coverage Quality Issues

Coverage count does not equal deep cooperation. Survey data shows that dealers generally adopt a "multi-platform placement, favor the best performer" strategy. Some dealers retain only basic membership status, so the actual proportion of genuinely active, operating merchants must be discounted.

## II. Dealers' Genuine Assessment of Dongchedi

### 2.1 Lead Quality

**Industry pain-point background:** Over the past four years, dealer customer-acquisition costs have risen 20% and operating costs 35%, while lead conversion rates have actually fallen 14%. In 2021, industry-wide online auto lead volume reached 660 million, versus just 10 million a decade earlier (a 66x increase), while passenger-vehicle sales over the same period grew only 1.4x. This points to a severe "lead bubble" across the industry.

**Dongchedi's reported performance:**

- 65.9% of surveyed dealers said Dongchedi's lead quality is clearly above the industry average
- 92.9% of dealers said they would recommend the "Maichetong" service to peers
- 94.8% of dealers said they intend to continue the partnership
- The platform claims its lead-to-sale conversion rate exceeds that of industry competitors

**A bias worth flagging:** the survey data above comes from a report Dongchedi itself published (jointly with the China Automobile Dealers Association), so self-selection bias in the sample is a real possibility. Third-party surveys and dealer-community feedback paint a more divided picture.

### 2.2 Conversion Rate Data

| Metric | Value | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Chongqing pilot average close rate | 46.9% above the national average | H1 2022 pilot |
| Top-tier merchants (conversion rate >2.5%) | Rose from 142 to over 200 | Chongqing pilot |
| Low-conversion merchants (<1%) | Fell from 200 to under 100 | Chongqing pilot |
| Showroom-visit rate for data co-construction brands | Up 29% | 15-city pilot |
| Lead close rate (trial run) | Up 21% on average | 3 cities, 4 brands |
| Incremental showroom visits from customized lead distribution | Over 20% | 1,000+ stores across 50+ cities |

**Industry-wide lead conversion efficiency:** the industry generates 1.2-1.5 million leads per day on average; at a 50% online-penetration assumption, daily completed sales attributable to leads do not exceed 35,000 units. In other words, 1.5 million leads per day convert into only 35,000 cars sold, an overall conversion rate of roughly 2.3% — indicating substantial inflation in the headline numbers.

### 2.3 Dealers' Real Pain Points

1. **Highly volatile fees:** Under the CPS model, budgets are unpredictable — fees spike in peak season when more deals close
2. **Data black box:** Dealers have no independent way to verify the accuracy of platform data
3. **Passive acceptance of rule changes:** The platform revises its service-score and evaluation rules every year, leaving dealers with limited say
4. **Duplicate leads:** Overlap across multiple platforms is severe; after deduplication, the number of genuinely valid leads shrinks sharply

## III. In-Depth Comparison of Pricing Models: CPS vs. CPT vs. CPL

### 3.1 Definitions of the Three Models

| Model | Full name | Billing method | Representative platforms |
|------|------|----------|----------|
| CPT | Cost Per Time | Paid by time period (annual membership) | Autohome, Yiche (traditional model) |
| CPL | Cost Per Lead | Paid per number of leads | Early-stage industry model |
| CPS | Cost Per Sales | Paid per actual completed sale | Dongchedi (since 2022) |

### 3.2 Evolution of Dongchedi's CPS Model

**Timeline:**

- **2021**: Chongqing pilot of CPS, covering 523 of 530 stores (over 95%)
- **August 2022**: CPS formally launched, marking the shift from CPT to CPS
- **2023**: Nationwide rollout, triggering widespread dealer pushback
- **2024**: CPS upgraded under the banner of "greater efficiency, lighter burden," with a 20% reduction in evaluation requirements
- **2025**: Overall service-fee burden cut by 25%; merchant count surpassed 30,000

### 3.3 Fee Comparison (2023, the Key Turning Point)

| Platform | 2022 membership fee | 2023 membership fee | Increase |
|------|-------------|-------------|------|
| Dongchedi | ~RMB 132,000 | ~RMB 256,000 (CPS) | +93% |
| Autohome | RMB 162,000 (Deluxe) | RMB 234,000 (Tech edition) | +44% |
| Yiche | RMB 171,000 (Smart Enjoy edition) | RMB 239,000 | +39% |

**Per-vehicle cost change:** One joint-venture-brand dealer calculated that, based on Dongchedi's lead-generation service, the per-vehicle cost was about RMB 940 in 2022; after the 2023 price increase (even net of discounts), per-vehicle cost jumped to roughly RMB 6,000.

**Example of dealer burden:** A survey of 79 4S dealerships in Hunan found that in 2023 they collectively had to pay over RMB 25 million — an average of more than RMB 300,000 per store, with the highest reaching RMB 640,000.

### 3.4 The Price-Hike Backlash and the Compromise

In early 2023, the rollout of the CPS model triggered a sharp backlash:

- **BYD** pulled its cooperation with Dongchedi entirely
- **Huitong Luhua Group** suspended its contract
- **Automobile dealers associations in multiple provinces** issued a joint statement calling on the three major platforms to halt the price increases
- Dongchedi ultimately compromised, introducing a **"fee cap"** system — once a dealer reaches a certain sales level, no additional fees are charged

### 3.5 The Underlying Logic of the CPS Model

The CPS model appears to be about "being accountable for closed deals," but in substance it shifts Dongchedi's revenue from a fixed membership fee to a floating commission tied to dealer sales volume. Objectively, this achieves:

1. **A higher revenue ceiling for the platform:** in a strong sales season, revenue far exceeds what a fixed membership fee would have generated
2. **Partial risk transfer to dealers:** in the off-season, fewer sales mean lower fees, but in peak season the fees become unpredictable
3. **A tighter data loop:** dealers are required to report transaction data back to the platform, giving it more data assets
4. **Deeper competitive moat:** the more transaction data accumulates, the more precise the algorithms become, reinforcing a Matthew-effect advantage

## IV. Dealer Return on Investment (ROI)

### 4.1 Platform Share of Sales Contribution

This is the core premise for assessing ROI, and the data show that vertical platforms' contribution is declining:

| Channel type | Share of sales contribution | Notes |
|----------|-------------|------|
| The three major vertical media (Dongchedi + Autohome + Yiche) | Under 20% | Survey data from new-force EV brands |
| Huawei AITO (vertical-media contribution) | Under 5% | Only 4,000-5,000 of the 100,000 units sold at retail in 2023 |
| Douyin Local Life (Bendi Tong) | Up to 60% for some dealers | Effectiveness rising rapidly |
| Organic showroom visits + repeat-customer referrals | The remainder | Varies widely depending on brand strength |

### 4.2 Quantifiable ROI Data Points

- **Harmony Auto Group:** after adopting Dongchedi, its lead cost ran 15.4% below the platform average and its order cost ran 21.7% below the platform average
- **Shanghai Ningfu Volkswagen:** the share of sales closed via Dongchedi rose from 50% in 2023 to 68.6% in January-November 2024
- **Top-tier merchants:** overall sales contribution from the Dongchedi channel grew more than 50%
- **57% of CPS merchants** posted positive year-over-year sales growth in 2024 versus 2023

### 4.3 Hidden Costs Behind the ROI

Dealers' investment in the platform is not limited to membership fees or commissions — it also includes:

1. **Labor costs:** a dedicated DCC team (online-sales managers, customer-service staff) is needed to handle leads
2. **Content-production costs:** manpower and equipment investment in shooting short videos and livestreams
3. **Training costs:** the learning curve required to become familiar with the platform's tools and rules
4. **Data feedback costs:** the technical investment in system integration and maintaining data accuracy
5. **Opportunity costs:** resources devoted to Dongchedi cannot be used for other channels

Once these hidden costs are factored in, actual ROI is far lower than the figures the platform publishes.

## V. Dealer Dependence on the Platform and Alternatives

### 5.1 Dependence Is Declining

Data from multiple sources show that dealer dependence on the three major vertical media platforms is declining systematically:

**Structural reasons:**

1. **NEV direct-sales model:** brands such as Tesla, NIO, Xpeng, and Li Auto acquire their own traffic through directly-operated stores in shopping districts plus official apps; data from Huawei's experience stores show that "over 90% of customers come through the brand's own draw, while under 10% arrive via platform referral"
2. **Rise of Douyin Local Life (Bendi Tong):** some dealers report that Douyin Local Life now contributes 60% of sales, far surpassing the three major vertical media platforms
3. **New platforms such as Xiaohongshu (RedNote):** auto-related content volume is growing 1,318% year over year, with search volume up 115.4% year over year
4. **Brands building their own private-domain traffic:** private-domain channels such as WeChat groups, WeCom (Enterprise WeChat), and branded apps are steadily maturing

### 5.2 Alternative Channel Matrix

| Alternative channel | Advantages | Disadvantages | Typical use case |
|----------|------|------|----------|
| Douyin Local Life | Large traffic, strong conversion, broad coverage | Bidding costs rising quickly | Applicable to all brands |
| Xiaohongshu (RedNote) | High-net-worth users, strong "seeding"/discovery effect | Limited scale, long conversion path | Mid-to-high-end brands |
| Official brand app | Data ownership, high user loyalty | Serves only a single brand | Leading NEV brands |
| WeChat private domain | Zero cost, high repeat-purchase rate | Hard to acquire new customers, limited scale | Retaining existing customers |
| Organic walk-in traffic | No extra cost | Passive, uncontrollable | Stores in core commercial districts |
| Autohome/Yiche | Deep content accumulation, strong user habits | Aging model, high fees | Traditional gasoline-vehicle brands |

### 5.3 Tiers of Dependence

- **High dependence (40%+ of leads):** small and mid-sized traditional gasoline-vehicle dealers, and second/third-tier brands lacking brand strength
- **Moderate dependence (20%-40%):** mainstream joint-venture brands and some domestic brands
- **Low dependence (<10%):** leading NEV brands (Tesla/NIO/Xpeng/Li Auto) and Huawei-affiliated brands
- **Zero dependence:** pure direct-sales brands that have fully exited the vertical-media system

## VI. NEV Brands' Cooperation Model with Dongchedi

### 6.1 The "De-Platforming" Trend Among Direct-Sales Brands

NEV makers and traditional automakers diverge fundamentally on channel strategy:

**Traditional model (dealers depend on the platform):**
Consumer browses the platform → leaves a lead → dealer follows up → deal closes in-store

**NEV direct-sales model (bypasses the platform):**
Consumer encounters the brand via social media or an experience store in a shopping district → places an order through the official app → takes delivery at a directly-operated store

### 6.2 A Case in Point: Huawei's Complete Halt of Cooperation

In January 2024, Huawei's HarmonyOS Smart Mobility Alliance completely terminated its sales cooperation with all three major platforms — Autohome, Dongchedi, and Yiche. The immediate trigger was that in Dongchedi's December 2023 winter range test, the AITO M7 range-extended EV ranked last on range-achievement rate, which Yu Chengdong (Richard Yu) publicly criticized as "a rigged test that misleads people."

**The deeper logic:** relying on its own brand pull and offline channel network, Huawei judged that the traffic value the platforms delivered no longer justified the cost of cooperating with them. This episode stands as a landmark case of "disintermediation" by a leading NEV brand.

### 6.3 Dongchedi's Response: From "Selling Leads" to "Building an Ecosystem"

Facing the challenge that direct-sales brands no longer need leads, Dongchedi is adjusting its strategy:

1. **Retaining influence through content and testing:** standardized evaluations such as the winter and summer range tests preserve Dongchedi's voice in shaping public opinion about NEV brands
2. **Used-car transactions:** anxiety over NEV resale-value depreciation is generating transaction demand, and Dongchedi is integrating with Douyin's used-car business
3. **Trade-in services:** as of 2026, subsidy-application services now cover 17 provinces and municipalities
4. **Services for dealership-model brands:** NEV brands that don't sell direct (such as BYD's dealer network) still need platform services

### 6.4 NEV Lead Penetration Rate

On the Dongchedi platform, the penetration rate of NEV-related leads has already exceeded 30%, but this mainly reflects a shift in consumer interest rather than the depth of NEV brands' cooperation with the platform. The number of independent NEV channel outlets has reached 21,220 (growing 12.4%), and how many of these new outlets choose to partner with Dongchedi is the key variable to watch.

## VII. The Concrete Impact of the Dealer Exit Wave on Dongchedi

### 7.1 Scale of Dealer Exits

| Year | Number of 4S stores that exited | Notes |
|------|-------------|------|
| Cumulative, 2022-2025 | 14,410 | China Automobile Dealers Association data |
| 2024 | 4,419 | First-ever negative growth in the total number of 4S stores |
| 2025 | 4,961 | Exit pace accelerating |
| Share of dealers reporting a loss, H1 2025 | 52.6% | More than half of dealers are unprofitable |
| Total 4S stores in 2025 | ~31,400 | Down several thousand from the peak |

### 7.2 The Multifaceted Impact on Dongchedi

**Negative effects:**

1. **Shrinking customer base:** the total pool of paying dealers is contracting, with a net decline of several thousand per year
2. **Declining ability to pay:** 80% of dealers are selling cars at a loss (2026 data), squeezing platform advertising budgets
3. **Collapse among joint-venture brands:** luxury and joint-venture dealers — traditionally high-value customers — account for the most concentrated exits (Porsche plans to retain only 80 core dealers in 2026)
4. **Weak industry confidence:** when dealers are under heavy operating pressure, lead-generation spending is the first budget line they cut

**Positive offsetting factors:**

1. **Rising ARPU among remaining customers:** as competition intensifies among surviving dealers, they may actually increase their platform spending
2. **Expansion of NEV channels:** the independent NEV channel network is still growing at 12.4%
3. **Elasticity of the CPS model:** under a pay-per-sale model, fewer dealers but higher conversion efficiency could actually raise per-customer value
4. **Extension into the aftermarket:** aftermarket services such as maintenance and used-car sales are unaffected by dealer exits

### 7.3 Net Impact Assessment

The impact of dealer exits on Dongchedi is **structurally negative but manageable**. In the near term, customer attrition from exits will be partially offset by rising ARPU under the CPS model and the expansion of NEV channels. However, if the exit wave keeps accelerating past 6,000 stores per year, the room for offsetting it will narrow.

Autohome's financial reports already show how serious this trend is: in 2025, its lead-generation service revenue fell 13.6% year over year to RMB 2.71 billion, and the number of paying dealers kept declining. Dongchedi, being unlisted, does not disclose financial data, but it faces the same structural pressure.

## VIII. Digital Tools Dongchedi Provides to Dealers

### 8.1 Product Matrix

The tools Dongchedi provides to dealers are mainly distributed across two platforms:

**Maichetong (on the Dongchedi app):** focused on converting precise, vertical traffic into closed sales

**Dongchedi Cloud Store (on Douyin):** focused on acquiring customers at scale from broad-reach traffic

Together, the two sides form the "Giant Dongchedi" (Ju Dong Che) system.

### 8.2 Front-End Customer-Acquisition Tools

| Tool name | Function | Reported results |
|----------|------|----------|
| Smart Content Workbench | AI-assisted production of short videos and image/text content | Effective-content rate up 317%; volume-generation efficiency up 960% |
| AI Livestream Module | Intelligent assistance for livestreaming (scripts, engagement) | Lowers the barrier to livestreaming and boosts staff productivity |
| Smart IM | AI-assisted instant-messaging customer service | Speeds up response to customer inquiries |
| Matrix Account Design | Strategy for operating multiple accounts as a matrix | Delivers an all-round brand image |

### 8.3 Back-End Conversion Tools

| Tool name | Function | Reported results |
|----------|------|----------|
| DCC Workbench | Middle-office platform for lead allocation and management | Shifts from "one lead per person" to "one lead per store" |
| Smart Lead Distribution | AI matches prospective customers to the best-fit store | Showroom-visit rate up 19% |
| Smart DCC Module | Industry knowledge base + auto-response + intelligent customer service | Speeds up communication |
| Smart Outbound Calling | AI-assisted outbound phone calls | Boosts DCC team productivity |
| AI Diagnostic Console | Covers over 80 operating metrics | Precisely pinpoints operational weaknesses |
| BuyerHunter customer LLM | Precise lead grading and merchant tiering | Improves lead-matching precision |

### 8.4 Operations Management Tools

- **Merchant Diagnostic Console:** diagnoses 80+ operating metrics to pinpoint problem areas
- **Service-score system:** quantifies dealer service capability and ties it to traffic allocation
- **Training and certification platform:** certifies the professional competence of dealer staff
- **OEM back-office:** lets brand headquarters monitor operating data across all stores in real time

### 8.5 AI Capability Upgrades (2025-2026)

Leveraging the AI capabilities of ByteDance's Volcano Engine and Coze platform, Dongchedi is advancing:

- **AI car selection:** understanding fuzzy customer requirements plus intelligent multi-vehicle comparison
- **AI online sales:** agent-assisted online sales consultation
- **AI search:** automotive information retrieval powered by natural-language understanding
- **AI content generation:** automated generation of car reviews and comparison articles

The platform's goal is to achieve a "full-chain intelligent upgrade" by 2026, using AI to free up dealer manpower and systematically raise staff productivity.

### 8.6 The Lock-In Effect of These Tools

The strategic significance of these digital tools lies in:

1. **Raising switching costs:** once dealers are deeply embedded in the DCC system, migrating to another platform becomes very costly
2. **A data-accumulation moat:** historical operating data, customer profiles, and conversion models all accrue on the platform
3. **Staff dependence:** teams become accustomed to the platform's workflows, and relearning new tools takes time
4. **A network effect on results:** the longer a dealer uses the platform, the more data accumulates, the more precise the algorithms become, and the better the results

## IX. Key Conclusions and Investment Implications

### 9.1 Core Strengths of Dongchedi's B2B Ecosystem

1. **First-mover advantage in the CPS model:** the first to shift from "selling leads" to "selling closed deals," redefining the industry's billing standard
2. **A dual-engine ByteDance traffic advantage:** the synergy between Dongchedi (precise, vertical) and Douyin (broad-reach, at scale) — something Autohome and Yiche lack
3. **An AI tool matrix:** leveraging Volcano Engine's technical capabilities to lead competitors on intelligent tools
4. **A closed data loop:** end-to-end data connectivity across content exposure → lead acquisition → showroom conversion → transaction feedback

### 9.2 Risks That Warrant Continued Attention

1. **Accelerating dealer exits:** if the pace of nearly 5,000 exits per year continues, erosion of the customer base cannot be ignored
2. **"Disintermediation" via NEV direct sales:** leading NEV brands don't need vertical-media leads, and this trend is irreversible
3. **CPS fee disputes:** dealer resentment over the price increases has not fully dissipated, and repairing the relationship will take time
4. **Internal competition within ByteDance:** as Douyin Local Life's performance keeps improving, it could cannibalize Dongchedi's own dealer budgets
5. **Autohome's counterattack:** after being acquired by Ping An, Autohome is pushing a new-retail transformation, and the competitive landscape remains unsettled

### 9.3 Key Financial Metrics (Known Information)

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Valuation | ~USD 3 billion (2024 funding round) |
| IPO plan | Plans to list in Hong Kong in 2026, targeting USD 1.0-1.5 billion in proceeds |
| Daily active users | Surpassed 10 million (August 2025) |
| Auto-interested users | Over 500 million |
| Content creators | ~7.5 million |
| Dealer coverage | Over 30,000 |
| Annual revenue | Not disclosed (unlisted ByteDance subsidiary) |

**For comparison:** Autohome's full-year 2025 revenue was RMB 6.45 billion (down 8.3% year over year), adjusted net profit was RMB 1.61 billion (down 21.6% year over year), and its market cap was approximately USD 2.05 billion. Dongchedi's USD 3 billion valuation, by comparison, implies the market is assigning it a considerably higher growth premium.

### 9.4 Bottom-Line Assessment

Dongchedi's core competitive strength on the B2B side is not "how many dealers it has" but "whether it can help dealers sell more cars." The CPS model directly ties the platform's interests to dealer transactions — the right direction from a business-logic standpoint. But the ceiling on this path is constrained by two external variables: first, whether the dealer exit wave can stabilize before 2027; second, whether the NEV direct-sales model spreads from leading brands to mainstream brands. If both variables move in the wrong direction, Dongchedi could end up "winning in a shrinking market" even if it becomes the undisputed industry leader.
