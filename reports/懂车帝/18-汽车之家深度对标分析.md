## I. Company Overview

Autohome Inc. (NYSE: ATHM / HKEX: 02518) was founded in 2005 and is one of China's earliest and largest vertical automotive media platforms. It listed on the NYSE in 2013 and completed a secondary listing on the HKEX in 2021.

**Ownership history:**
- 2016: Ping An Insurance of China acquired a 47.4% stake in Autohome for approximately $1.6 billion, becoming the controlling shareholder
- February 2025: Ping An sold its 41.91% controlling stake to Haier Group for approximately $1.8 billion
- August 2025: Haier Group completed the deal closing and board reorganization, formally becoming the controlling shareholder

3. **Online marketplace and others (the sole growth line)**: this segment includes new retail (space stations/satellite stores), used-car transactions, data products, and more. It has grown roughly 8-9% for two consecutive years, with its share rising from 31% to 40% — approaching leads services to become the largest revenue source.

## IV. Strategic Shifts and Execution Results During the Ping An Era

### 4.1 The Three Phases of Ping An's Control (2016-2024)

**Phase one (2016-2018): the honeymoon period**
- Planned a "4+1" ecosystem: auto media, auto e-commerce, auto finance, and auto lifestyle
- Rapid business growth; the share price rose 2.5x from the acquisition price to $119
- Partner OEMs increased from 87 to 103, and mobile average daily UV rose from 13.8 million to 29.1 million

**Phase two (2019-2021): tensions emerge**
- Ping An tried to deeply integrate the user base with its financial-services business
- The platform became saturated with financial-services ads, and content quality and user experience deteriorated sharply
- Media-services revenue posted its first negative growth in Q4 2019, with the decline widening to 41.78% by 2021

**Phase three (2022-2024): across-the-board decline**
- Quarterly media-services revenue fell to as low as RMB 267 million, less than 20% of the mix
- Market share was continuously eroded by Dongchedi and Yiche
- In February 2025, Ping An "sold off" its controlling stake to Haier for $1.8 billion

### 4.2 Lessons from the Ping An Era

1. **Financial synergy failed**: attempting to turn a media platform into a lead-generation funnel for financial products damaged the content ecosystem
2. **Severe talent attrition**: repeated changes of CEO led to a wavering, inconsistent strategy
3. **Missed the two great waves — NEVs and short-form video**: Ping An focused more on financial monetization than on product innovation
4. **Valuation shrank from $12 billion to roughly $2 billion**: over 80% of market value evaporated

### 4.3 Early Changes in the Haier Era (2025-present)

- Haier veteran Liu Chi was parachuted in as Chairman and CEO
- Strategic direction has shifted toward "connected home and car": integrating Autohome into Haier's smart-home ecosystem
- Launched an O2O mall business (trial operation began September 2025)
- Rolled out an AI assistant built on the DeepSeek large language model
- The new-retail space-station and satellite-store network has surpassed 200 locations

**Haier's challenge:** can a home-appliance company execute better than a financial conglomerate did? The cross-industry synergy logic from appliances to automobiles is no clearer than the financial-synergy logic was.

## V. In-Depth Analysis of the Four Business Segments

### 5.1 Leads business (42% of revenue, the core cash cow)

**Business model:** charges dealers an annual fee (CPT model) in exchange for car-buyer leads.

**Current difficulties:**
- Industry-wide lead volume has surged 66x over 10 years (from 10 million to 660 million leads), while passenger-vehicle sales have grown only 1.4x
- Lead quality has become severely diluted; dealer customer-acquisition cost has risen 20% over four years while conversion rates have fallen 14%
- The number of paying dealers fell roughly 20% in 2025
- Dongchedi's CPS model (pay-per-transaction) is disrupting the CPT model from below

**Trend assessment: the leads business is a sunset model. Time-based CPT billing cannot demonstrate results, and dealers are voting with their feet.**

### 5.2 Media services (18% of revenue, accelerating shrinkage)

**Business model:** sells display ad inventory and content-marketing services to automotive brands.

**Current difficulties:**
- ICE-brand budgets continue to shrink
- NEV brands increasingly favor social platforms such as Douyin and Xiaohongshu
- Cumulative decline of 38% over two years, with share falling from 26% to 18%
- The content format (predominantly text and images) has fallen behind the short-video era

**Trend assessment: traditional brand advertising on automotive vertical platforms will continue to shrink — this is a structural trend.**

### 5.3 Online marketplace and others (40% of revenue, the sole growth pole)

**Included businesses:**
- New retail: offline space stations and satellite stores offering one-stop viewing, test-driving, and transaction services
- Used cars: the Tiantian Paiche (Guazi-style) platform, serving both buyers and sellers
- Data products: data-analytics and decision-support tools for automakers
- Transaction commissions: service fees for facilitating new- and used-car transactions

**Growth drivers:** the segment has grown roughly 8-9% for two consecutive years, though the growth rate is not particularly fast, and the asset-heavy new-retail business is dragging down gross margin.

### 5.4 AI and Data Products

Five AI/data product lines launched in 2025:
1. AI Marketing Brain — intelligent decision support
2. AI Lead-Generation Pioneer — automated content production
3. AI Lead Master — precision user targeting
4. AI Sales Champion Tool — sales-capability enablement
5. AI Car-Inspection Expert — vehicle-condition assessment

**Assessment:** productizing AI is the right direction, but current revenue contribution is limited, and the company relies on an external large language model (DeepSeek) rather than deep proprietary technology barriers.

| P/B | ~0.65x |
| Total cash and investments | RMB 21.36 billion (approx. $2.95 billion) |
| Enterprise value (EV) | **Negative** (market cap is well below net cash) |

### 6.2 Valuation Interpretation

**Autohome currently sits in a state of extreme undervaluation — or arguably a "value trap":**

1. **Net cash exceeds market cap**: the company holds RMB 21.36 billion (approx. $2.95 billion) in cash and investments, while its market cap is only $2.05 billion — meaning the market is pricing the underlying business itself at a **negative** value.

2. **P/E of 10.9x**: this looks cheap, but profit is declining at an accelerating rate (-14.2%); if the trend continues, the forward P/E will rise.

3. **P/B of 0.65x**: trading well below book value, reflecting the market's pessimistic expectations for the company's future value-creation ability.

4. **Share price down 33.8% over the past year**: the market continues to vote with its feet.

5. **A $200 million buyback program**: management is trying to signal confidence to the market, but the buyback size is small relative to the net-cash pile.

**The core question: is this a value trap or a value opportunity?**

From a value-investing standpoint, the key judgment points are:
- Can the revenue decline be halted now that Haier is in control?
- Can the new-retail business grow fast enough to offset the shrinkage of the legacy businesses?
- Can the dealer-attrition trend be reversed?

## VII. Core Challenges Facing Autohome

### 7.1 Share Erosion by Dongchedi

| Dimension | Autohome's disadvantage |
|------|---------------|
| Content format | Predominantly text and images; low share of short-video content |
| Traffic source | Mainly its own app; lacks external traffic channels |
| User profile | Skews toward older, existing users; losing younger users |
| Lead model | Annual CPT fee; cannot charge based on performance |
| Technical capability | Relies on an external large language model; recommendation-algorithm ability trails ByteDance's platforms |
| User experience | Damaged reputation from financial-ad saturation during the Ping An era |

### 7.2 Structural Industry Shifts

1. **NEV penetration has surpassed 50%**: NEV brands place greater emphasis on direct sales and social-media marketing, and rely less on traditional vertical media
2. **Dealer-industry consolidation**: large numbers of ICE 4S dealerships are closing, shrinking the base of paying dealers
3. **Changing user decision paths**: from "search — read — decide" to "scroll video — watch livestream — order"
4. **Full-scale encroachment by short-video platforms**: Douyin and Kuaishou are not just content platforms — they are also moving into automotive transactions

### 7.3 Risk of Frequent Management Turnover

- Multiple CEO changes during the Ping An era left strategy lacking continuity
- Another change of leadership followed Haier's takeover; whether the new management team can execute steadily remains unknown
- As a home-appliance company, Haier's depth of understanding of the automotive industry is questionable

| **Brand coverage** | 96 automakers | 110+ auto brands |
| **Content creators** | 2,500+ premium creators | 7.5 million+ creators |
| **Core content format** | Predominantly text and images, video secondary | Predominantly short video and livestreaming, text and images secondary |

| **Technical moat** | Traditional search + content systems | ByteDance recommendation algorithms + AI |
| **User-acquisition logic** | "People find cars" (search-driven) | "Cars find people" (recommendation-driven) |

| **Cash reserves** | RMB 21.36 billion | Ample funding post-financing |
| **Growth trend** | Revenue and profit both declining for two consecutive years | High-growth phase |

### Summary of Key Differences

1. **Fundamentally different traffic sources**: Autohome's traffic is "isolated" — users must actively download the app or visit the website; Dongchedi is backed by Douyin's 600 million+ DAU traffic pool, with "daily auto-content consumption reaching 500 million views," giving it near-zero traffic-acquisition cost.

2. **A generational gap in content production methods**: Autohome relies on its editorial team and a small number of contracted creators (2,500+) to produce standardized text-and-image content; Dongchedi has a UGC ecosystem of 7.5 million creators, with 2 million creators posting content daily — an enormous gap in content-supply scale.

3. **An evolutionary gap in business models**: Autohome's CPT model follows a "rent collection" logic (charging regardless of results), leading to low dealer satisfaction; Dongchedi's CPS model follows a "revenue share" logic (pay per transaction), aligning its interests more closely with dealers. Surveys show 65.9% of dealers believe Dongchedi's lead quality is higher, and 60% report significantly improved conversion rates after adopting CPS.

4. **A generational gap in technical capability**: Autohome's technical foundation is a Web 2.0-era search-and-classified-listings system; Dongchedi has inherited ByteDance's recommendation-algorithm engine, enabling "one thousand faces for one thousand users" content and lead distribution.

## IX. Investment Decision Analysis: Buy Autohome, or Wait for Dongchedi's IPO?

### 9.1 The Investment Case for Autohome

**Bull case:**
- Net cash exceeds market cap, meaning the business itself is theoretically obtained "for free"
- P/E of 10.9x and P/B of 0.65x put the valuation at a historical low
- The $200 million buyback program provides a degree of margin of safety
- Haier's control could bring a new "connected home and car" narrative
- Even if the business keeps shrinking, the large cash reserve buys time

**Bear case:**
- Revenue and profit have both declined for two consecutive years, with the decline accelerating
- The core businesses (leads and media) face structural decline
- The new-retail business isn't growing fast enough and is dragging down gross margin
- Haier's cross-industry integration carries enormous uncertainty
- The trend of share erosion by Dongchedi is difficult to reverse
- Paying dealers have fallen 20%, and the core customer base is eroding
- The founding team has long since departed, leaving no "soul" figure at the helm

### 9.2 The Investment Case for Dongchedi

**Bull case:**
- Backed by ByteDance/Douyin's traffic ecosystem, giving it extremely low customer-acquisition costs
- Strong user growth, with DAU surpassing 10 million and still rising
- The CPS model represents the direction the industry is evolving toward
- A UGC ecosystem of 7.5 million creators that is difficult to replicate
- A structural beneficiary of the NEV trend
- If it IPOs at a $3.0-5.0 billion valuation (implying roughly 5-8x P/S), there is still reasonable room

**Bear case:**
- Financial data is undisclosed, so profitability is unverified
- Post-IPO, it may face downward valuation pressure (the market currently values Chinese ADRs/H-shares at a discount)
- Overreliance on ByteDance/Douyin traffic
- The profitability of the CPS model needs to be validated (favorable to dealers, but platform margins may be thinner)
- The automotive vertical space itself faces encroachment from comprehensive platforms like Douyin and Xiaohongshu

### 9.3 Investment Recommendation

**Core judgment: for investing in the automotive-internet space, one should wait for Dongchedi's IPO rather than buy Autohome.**

The reasoning:

1. **Trend matters more than valuation**: Autohome may be cheap on valuation, but it is on a sustained downward trajectory. The essence of value investing is not buying cheap assets, but buying good businesses. Autohome's current predicament resembles Kodak or Nokia — not a cyclical trough, but a business model being disrupted by a new paradigm.

2. **But don't rush to buy Dongchedi either**: after its IPO, Dongchedi will need several quarters of public financial data to validate its profitability model. Whether the roughly $3.0 billion valuation implies a reasonable P/S multiple can only be assessed once revenue data is disclosed.

3. **Autohome's "net cash exceeding market cap" looks safe but is actually a trap**: unless management is willing to pursue a large-scale dividend or a take-private (which Haier could do), the cash on the balance sheet has no practical meaning for minority shareholders. Recall the Ping An era — the large cash pile never translated into shareholder returns.

4. **The best strategy is to wait:**
   - Wait for Dongchedi's IPO and 2-3 quarters of subsequent published financial data
   - Observe its revenue scale, growth rate, and profitability
   - If the profitability model proves sustainable, build a position at a reasonable valuation
   - Autohome can be watched as a hedge candidate, but building an active position is not recommended

5. **A deeper question**: is the automotive vertical platform a good sector to be in at all? With comprehensive platforms like Douyin and Xiaohongshu all moving into automotive content and transactions, how deep is the moat of an independent automotive vertical platform, really? This question applies equally to Autohome and Dongchedi.

## X. Autohome's Significance as a Valuation Reference for Dongchedi

### 10.1 Valuation Anchoring

| Metric | Autohome (actual) | Dongchedi IPO (expected) |
|------|----------------|------------------|
| Market cap/valuation | $2.05 billion | $3.0-5.0 billion |
| Revenue | RMB 6.452 billion | RMB 4.0-5.0 billion (estimate) |
| P/S | ~2.3x | ~5-8x (at a $3.0 billion valuation) |
| P/E | ~10.9x | Pending disclosure |
| Growth rate | -8.3% | Estimated 20-30%+ |

**Derivation logic:** Autohome's P/S of 2.3x reflects a mature, negative-growth business; if Dongchedi can demonstrate 20%+ revenue growth and a sustainable profitability model, a P/S of 5-8x would be reasonable. But if Dongchedi's IPO were priced above 10x P/S, that would warrant considerable caution.

### 10.2 Autohome's Value as a "Cautionary Tale"

For evaluating Dongchedi, Autohome offers several key lessons:

1. **A controlling shareholder's "synergy" can be poison**: Ping An's financial-services synergy destroyed Autohome's content ecosystem
2. **Being the industry leader doesn't guarantee permanent safety**: Autohome was once the undisputed leader, yet was overtaken by Dongchedi in just five years
3. **The power of shifting content formats is enormous**: the transition from text/images to short video is no less significant than the transition from PC to mobile
4. **The moat around the leads model is shallow**: dealers have no loyalty to the platform — they use whichever one delivers better results
