# Dongchedi Risk and Management Assessment -- A Deep Value Investing Perspective

> Analysis date: May 2026
> Analysis dimensions: risk identification, management quality, long-term certainty, investment channels
> Core method: inverted thinking -- first find the reasons "not to invest," then check whether there is a sufficient margin of safety

---

## I. Management Assessment

### 1.1 Core Managers

**He Jian** -- President of Dongchedi, the person actually running the company's operations.

- Founding shareholder of Xiamen Dongchezu Technology Co., Ltd. (the holding entity of Dongchedi's operating vehicle), holding 60%; the other shareholder, Zhao Jing (head of HR), holds 40%
- Formerly head of R&D for Baidu's healthcare division, with a technical background
- Has been involved in core management since Dongchedi launched in 2017, and has now spent nearly nine years deeply focused on the automotive sector
- Publicly emphasizes that "loving cars is the primary productive force" and "tinkering because of passion," reflecting a product-driven management style

**Assessment:**
- **Strengths**: a technical background combined with long-term focus on the automotive vertical gives him deep understanding of product and technology; sustained resource support within the ByteDance system suggests high internal recognition
- **Risks**: He Jian did not come up through the traditional automotive industry, and lacks the deep network and experience of that industry's traditional supply chain; public information is limited, making it hard to fully judge his strategic vision and capital-markets capability
- **Key question**: After an IPO, will he be capable of handling the complexity of capital markets as the CEO of a public company? Will ByteDance parachute in outside executives?

### 1.2 ByteDance's Strategic Positioning of Dongchedi

**Core judgment: not a core business, but one with strategic value.**

- Dongchedi is valued at roughly $3 billion, only about 0.5% of ByteDance's $550 billion overall valuation
- ByteDance has already completed the equity carve-out of Dongchedi (Toutiao exited the equity structure at the end of 2023), paving the way for an independent IPO
- 36Kr's analysis noted that spinning off Dongchedi is part of ByteDance's plan to "divest businesses no longer viewed as core"
- But ByteDance still tightly controls Dongchedi through its equity design, indicating this is not abandonment but rather "free-range cultivation"

**Risk signals:**
- ByteDance's strategic focus is on AI and globalization (TikTok), and Dongchedi's internal priority may continue to decline
- If performance after the IPO is weak, ByteDance may reduce its resource investment
- Dongchedi's traffic synergy with Douyin may change after it becomes independent

### 1.3 Talent and Team

- The R&D team is distributed across Beijing, Chongqing, and Hangzhou, with a front-end team of nearly 100 people
- Tech stack: React, TypeScript, Unity3D (for scenarios such as 3D car viewing)
- Compensation is aligned with the broader ByteDance system and is reasonably competitive in the hiring market
- ByteDance's OKR culture and internal "horse-race" competition mechanism continue within Dongchedi

**Assessment:** ByteDance's organizational culture is good at cultivating mid-level managers with strong execution, but in an industry like automotive that emphasizes deep industry accumulation and relationship networks, a team with a purely internet-native gene may face blind spots -- particularly in "heavy operations" segments such as offline transactions and dealer management.

## II. Competitive Risk

### 2.1 Autohome's Ability to Fight Back

**Current state:**
- Autohome's market cap has shrunk from a peak of over $10 billion to roughly $2.05 billion
- Full-year 2024 revenue was 6.45 billion yuan, down 8.3% year over year; adjusted net profit was 1.61 billion yuan, down 21.6% year over year
- Being forced to transform from an "auto information platform" into a "one-stop automotive ecosystem service platform"

**Barriers Autohome still holds:**
- More than 20 years of data accumulation, including a vehicle-model database, word-of-mouth data, and resale-value data
- Deep ties with dealers (though loosening)
- Brand recognition built in the PC era, still influential among middle-aged users
- Has already begun deploying AI content and AIGC

**Risk rating: medium.** Autohome is clearly in decline but will not disappear quickly. As a public company it faces pressure and has resources to reform. If Dongchedi runs into trouble during its IPO process, Autohome may get a breather.

### 2.2 The Risk of Douyin Itself Displacing Dongchedi -- the Most Underrated Risk

**Core contradiction:** Dongchedi's core traffic comes from the Douyin ecosystem. In July 2023, Dongchedi fully merged its automotive content operations with Douyin, Toutiao, and Xigua Video, reaching 310 million daily users interested in automotive content.

**Key questions:**
- If Douyin itself strengthens its own automotive channel and live car-selling features, will Dongchedi become a "redundant middle layer"?
- After an independent IPO, will Douyin still direct traffic to Dongchedi with the same intensity?
- Users can already watch reviews, compare models, and leave leads directly on Douyin -- why would they still open the Dongchedi app?

**What the actual data implies:** Daily automotive-content consumption across the Douyin and Dongchedi platforms combined reaches 500 million visits, but Dongchedi's mobile DAU is only 10 million. This means the vast majority of automotive-content consumption happens on Douyin itself, and the standalone value of the Dongchedi app is questionable.

**Risk rating: high.** This is the most fundamental structural risk Dongchedi faces. Sovereignty over its traffic does not lie in its own hands.

### 2.3 The Threat of New-Energy Automakers Building Their Own Channels

**Evolution of the trend (a surprising reversal):**
- 2020-2023: new-energy automakers (NIO, Li Auto, Xpeng, etc.) aggressively pushed direct-sales models, seemingly bypassing lead-generation platforms
- 2025-2026: the trend reversed. Denza closed and transferred stores, HarmonyOS Intelligent Mobility scrapped its direct-sales network, and Li Auto and NIO have been exploring dealer models
- Core reason: direct-sales costs are too high -- annual operating cost for a single store in a first-tier city reaches 5-6 million yuan, and 200 stores cost roughly 1 billion yuan a year to run

**Impact on Dongchedi:**
- Short-term positive: as automakers return to the dealer model, demand for leads rises rather than falls
- Long-term concern: new-energy automakers' user-data capabilities are far stronger than those of traditional automakers, and even under a dealer model they may still build their own digital marketing systems

**Risk rating: medium-low.** In the short term this is actually a positive, but the improvement of automakers' digital capabilities needs to be watched over the long term.

### 2.4 Price-War Risk

- Under the traditional CPT (cost-per-time-period) model, lead-acquisition costs keep rising
- Dongchedi has proactively shifted to a CPS (cost-per-sale) model, with more than 28,500 dealers already having switched
- The CPS model ties Dongchedi's revenue to dealers' actual completed sales, reducing dealers' perceived risk
- But CPS also means Dongchedi bears more pressure to "guarantee results"

**Risk rating: medium.** The CPS model is a double-edged sword -- if conversion rates fall, Dongchedi's revenue is directly affected.

## III. Regulatory Risk

### 3.1 Data and Privacy

- In August 2025, the China Cybersecurity Association named Dongchedi and other apps as having problems such as excessive collection of personal information and over-invocation of sensitive permissions; the issues were subsequently remediated
- The lead-generation business fundamentally involves collecting users' personal information (phone numbers, purchase intent) and distributing it to dealers, which inherently creates tension with the Personal Information Protection Law
- Complaints of the type "I just looked at a car on Dongchedi and now I get sales calls harassing me every day" are common

**Risk rating: medium-high.** If regulation tightens further (for example, requiring explicit user consent each time before a lead is forwarded), it would directly hit the core business model.

### 3.2 The Review-Credibility Crisis

- In July 2025, Dongchedi partnered with CCTV on an assisted-driving test, and the results drew strong pushback from multiple automakers (including HarmonyOS Intelligent Mobility)
- Dongchedi was accused of inconsistent testing standards, selective video editing, and human interference
- CCTV later removed the "co-produced" label, damaging its credibility
- Multiple posts on Zhihu allege that "Dongchedi has been caught faking results multiple times"

**Risk rating: high.** Review credibility is the foundation of Dongchedi's value as a content platform. If both users and automakers distrust its reviews, the platform's value will be significantly diminished. More seriously, if automakers reduce cooperation as a result, revenue will be directly affected.

### 3.3 Transmission of ByteDance's Overall Regulatory Pressure

- The regulatory pressure TikTok faces in the U.S. could affect ByteDance's overall strategic pace
- Antitrust scrutiny of internet platforms within China continues
- But once Dongchedi operates independently, it can, to some extent, insulate itself from the parent company's regulatory risk

**Risk rating: medium-low.** Spinning off for an independent listing objectively helps with risk isolation.

### 3.4 Auto Finance / Insurance Licensing

- After Dongchedi moves into auto transactions, it may need related financial licenses
- Information is limited at present, but if Dongchedi wants to pursue high-margin businesses like auto finance and insurance, obtaining licenses will be a key challenge

**Risk rating: medium.** If it does not pursue financial services, the risk is low; if it does, it will face a fairly high compliance bar.

## IV. Business Risk

### 4.1 Over-Reliance on ByteDance Traffic

**This is the single most fundamental business risk.**

- After Dongchedi fully merged with Douyin, Toutiao, and Xigua Video, the four platforms together have 310 million daily active automotive users
- But Dongchedi's own app DAU is only about 10 million
- This means more than 96% of user reach depends on traffic distribution from other ByteDance-family products

**Structural problems:**
- After an independent listing, will ByteDance charge Dongchedi market-rate fees for traffic?
- If Douyin's algorithm shifts its priorities (for example, giving more weight to e-commerce or local services), the recommendation weight given to automotive content may decline
- Dongchedi lacks independent customer-acquisition capability, and traditional channels such as SEO and branded search are weak

**Risk rating: extremely high.** This is analogous to a company that derives more than 90% of its revenue from a single customer.

### 4.2 Cyclical Downturn in the Auto Market

- Growth in China's auto market is slowing; a 2025 dealer-survival survey found that only 42.9% of new-energy-brand dealers were profitable
- 34.4% of dealers were operating at a loss, and a "wave of store closures" could reduce demand for leads
- The price war has left dealers with thin profits, making them more cautious with marketing budgets

**Risk rating: medium.** Industry cyclical risk is unavoidable, but the CPS model ties Dongchedi's fortunes to those of its dealers to some extent -- when dealers are struggling, it is hard for Dongchedi to do well either.

### 4.3 The Ceiling on the Lead-Generation Business

**The predicament of the traditional lead model:**
- Lead quality has deteriorated significantly: fake numbers, duplicate leads, and competitor scouts are common problems
- Industry analysis states that "the traditional auto-lead model is coming to an end"
- The CPS model is a response to the lead-quality problem, but it still fundamentally relies on the logic of "user leaves information -> dealer follows up"

**Where is the ceiling?**
- China has roughly 30,000 auto dealers (already covered by Dongchedi), leaving limited room for incremental growth
- An individual dealer's annual marketing budget is limited, and under the CPS model Dongchedi's ARPU depends on transaction volume
- If new-energy automakers continue to strengthen direct-sales capability, the number of dealers may shrink further

**Risk rating: medium-high.** The lead-generation business itself is going through a period of model transition, and whether Dongchedi's CPS innovation can truly work has not yet been proven.

### 4.4 The Transition from Media to Transactions

**Dongchedi's offline footprint:**
- Has opened "Dongchedi Auto Malls" in Chongqing, Hefei, Wuhan, Nanjing, and other cities
- The Wuhan store spans nearly 20,000 square meters and can hold 500 vehicles
- Covers new- and used-car transactions, and has developed the D-BOX smart inspection device (328-380 inspection items)
- Online used-car inventory exceeds 300,000 vehicles, with roughly 29,000 partner dealers covering more than 350 cities

**Transition risks:**
- Offline operations require a completely different competency set: inventory management, logistics, after-sales service, dispute handling
- The used-car industry is a notorious "lemons market," where building trust is extremely difficult
- Assets become heavier, which conflicts with an internet company's typical asset-light model
- Faces competition from established players such as Guazi Used Cars

**Risk rating: medium-high.** There are few successful examples of internet companies succeeding at offline transactions. Dongchedi needs to prove its offline operating capability.

## V. Long-Term Certainty Assessment

### 5.1 Dongchedi Ten Years From Now

**Optimistic scenario:**
- Successfully completes its IPO, builds independent brand recognition, and becomes China's leading automotive lifestyle-services platform
- The CPS model proves out, extending from leads into transactions, financing, insurance, and after-sales services across the full chain
- Deep application of AI technology (it has already launched the "AI Xiaodong" smart assistant), building technical barriers in car-selection and car-usage scenarios
- Valuation grows from $3 billion to more than $10 billion

**Pessimistic scenario:**
- Douyin itself strengthens its own automotive business, marginalizing Dongchedi
- The offline transaction business keeps losing money, dragging down overall profitability
- Automakers' digital capabilities improve, and they no longer need a third-party lead-generation platform
- It is ultimately sold off by ByteDance or folded into another business line

**Most likely scenario:**
- Dongchedi remains the only automotive-vertical platform within the ByteDance system, operating at a middling scale
- After the IPO it becomes a mid-sized internet company with revenue in the range of 5-10 billion yuan
- But the growth ceiling is clear, and its valuation multiple stays under pressure

### 5.2 Potential Disruptive Factors

**Autonomous driving + shared mobility:**
- McKinsey forecasts that shared-mobility penetration in China will reach 45% by 2030
- Each additional autonomous ride-hailing vehicle can replace 11-14 privately owned vehicles
- If demand for private cars falls sharply and the frequency of car-purchase decisions declines, the value of lead-generation and content platforms would shrink

**But the time dimension matters a great deal:**
- As of 2025, fully autonomous private vehicles remain a distant dream
- Driverless robotaxis mainly operate in limited zones of specific cities
- This disruption would need at least 10-15 years before it has a substantive impact on auto consumption

**Assessment:** This ultra-long-term risk is real, but it does not constitute a reason to veto the current investment case.

### 5.3 The Likelihood of ByteDance Spinning Off or Selling Dongchedi

- Current path: an independent spin-off IPO, with ByteDance retaining control
- Low likelihood of an outright sale: at $3 billion, the amount is too small for ByteDance, and not worth the negative signal a sale would send
- The more likely risk is: if performance after the IPO is weak, ByteDance gradually reduces its attention and resource investment, letting Dongchedi "fend for itself"

### 5.4 IPO Timeline

- Completed a Pre-IPO financing round in 2024 (roughly $600 million, at a $3 billion valuation)
- In February 2026, Bloomberg reported that it was considering a Hong Kong IPO to raise $1.0-1.5 billion
- In March 2026, Citigroup and Goldman Sachs were selected as underwriters
- Expected to complete its listing between the second half of 2026 and the first half of 2027
- If successful, it would become the first independently listed company within the ByteDance system

## VI. Investment Channel Analysis

### 6.1 Direct Investment (Extremely Difficult)

**Pre-IPO equity:**
- The 2024 Pre-IPO round was led by Sequoia China, with participation from KKR, General Atlantic, and GGV Capital
- Ordinary investors essentially cannot participate; the threshold is extremely high (typically tens of millions of dollars to get in)
- Some secondary marketplaces (such as UpMarket) offer Pre-IPO shares of ByteDance, but pricing is opaque and liquidity is poor

**IPO subscription:**
- If it lists in Hong Kong, retail investors could participate in the IPO subscription through Hong Kong brokers
- But the allotment rate for hot IPOs is typically extremely low

### 6.2 Indirect Investment

**ByteDance itself:**
- Buying ByteDance equity through secondary marketplaces (such as Forge, EquityZen, UpMarket)
- But Dongchedi accounts for only 0.5% of ByteDance's valuation, so the indirect exposure is minimal

**Companies in Dongchedi's ecosystem:**
- There is currently no clearly listed company with a strong tie to Dongchedi's business

**Industry benchmarking:**
- For those bullish on the auto-information-plus-transaction-platform sector, worth watching:
  - Autohome (ATHM / 02518.HK): a direct competitor, currently valued at roughly $2 billion, which could actually have "turnaround" investment value
  - But Autohome itself also faces the risk of continued declining performance

### 6.3 Investment Recommendation

**As a private company, ordinary investors are currently not advised to attempt to invest in Dongchedi.** Reasons:

1. The Pre-IPO channel is opaque, with significant risk of unreasonable pricing
2. After the IPO, at least 2-3 quarters of earnings reports will be needed to validate the business
3. Core risks (traffic dependence, credibility crisis) remain unresolved
4. Whether the $3 billion valuation is reasonable cannot be judged until financial data is disclosed

**If considering investment after the IPO, key metrics to verify:**
- The share of independent traffic that does not come from ByteDance-family products
- Per-dealer ARPU and retention under the CPS model
- Gross margin and unit-store economics of the offline transaction business
- Management's performance and communication capability in the capital markets

## VIII. Summary -- Core Conclusions From an Investment Perspective

### Dongchedi's Core Contradiction

Dongchedi is a business that is **very capable but weakly independent**. Backed by ByteDance's algorithms and traffic, it has shown astonishing growth, but once that outer layer is stripped away, its standalone survival capability is in question.

### The Biggest Risk Is Not Competition, but "Dependence on the Parent"

To use an analogy: Dongchedi is like a branch grafted onto the great tree of Douyin -- the flowers and fruit look beautiful, but if the day comes when it must "grow as its own tree," are its roots deep enough?

### The Key "Disqualifier" Test

1. **Is the moat deep enough?** -- No. Its traffic comes from Douyin, its technology has no unique barrier, and its brand recognition is weaker than Autohome's
2. **Has the business model been fully validated?** -- No. The CPS model is still being rolled out, offline transactions have only just begun, and financial data has not been disclosed
3. **Has management been tested?** -- Not sufficiently. He Jian has performed well within the ByteDance system, but has not yet been tested as the leader of an independent public company
4. **How certain is the ten-year outlook?** -- Low. Autonomous driving plus shared mobility could fundamentally change the logic of auto consumption

### Conclusion

**From a deep value investing standpoint, Dongchedi does not currently constitute an attractive investment target.** The reason is not that the business itself is bad, but that:
- Its independence is insufficient, and it cannot create value on its own
- Its core risks (traffic dependence, credibility) have no clear path to resolution
- What profitability corresponds to its $3 billion valuation is unknown
- As a private company, it lacks transparency and an exit channel

**Recommended time window to watch:** the release of the 3rd-4th quarterly earnings reports after the IPO, at which point real financial data, the performance of independently generated traffic, and management's capability in facing the capital markets can all be observed.
