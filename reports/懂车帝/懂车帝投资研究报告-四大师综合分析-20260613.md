## One-Line Conclusion

> DCar is a "good business borrowed from someone else" — it has risen quickly in the automotive vertical by relying on ByteDance's traffic ecosystem, and its CPS model points in the right direction. But the moat does not belong to it, pricing power sits with its customers, and the industry ceiling is being pushed down by the rise of NEV direct sales. A $3.0 billion valuation already prices in substantial growth expectations, and with financial data completely opaque, there is no margin of safety to speak of.

## Summary by Dimension

### Business Model (Duan Yongping's Lens)

- Charging on a pay-per-transaction (CPS) basis is the right direction, but the 2023 price hike, which triggered a collective boycott, exposed a lack of pricing power
- The flywheel's starting point (traffic) is heavily dependent on the ByteDance ecosystem — this is a "half-loop," not a fully closed loop
- Neither the 7.5 million creators nor the 30,000 dealers constitute exclusive lock-in — both operate across multiple platforms
- Of five scenarios for what this looks like in 10 years, only "becoming automotive-transaction infrastructure" (20% probability) is an ideal outcome
- Duan Yongping's summary: this is not a business that "wins simply by avoiding stupid mistakes"

### Financials & Valuation (Buffett's Lens)

- Estimated revenue of RMB 3.5-5.5 billion, midpoint RMB 4.5 billion, implying a PS multiple of about 4.8x, higher than Autohome's 2.3x
- Using Autohome's mature-stage profitability as a benchmark, the estimated margin-of-safety price is about $2.0 billion; the current $3.0 billion valuation has no margin of safety
- The IPO target of $4.0-5.0 billion requires extremely strong growth data to support it; Autohome's $2.5 billion market cap is a warning sign of the industry's ceiling
- The asset-light model is a plus, but profitability is completely unverified
- The CPS model has a low monetization ceiling (~0.3% take rate) — as an automotive information platform, it does not touch the transaction's cash flow

### Industry & Competition (Munger's Lens)

- NEV penetration has already exceeded 50% and will break through 60%; more than half of new cars now bypass dealerships entirely — the platform's "addressable market" is physically shrinking
- Lead conversion rates have collapsed from 5% to 1.5%; the industry is generating an ever-increasing volume of low-value leads
- Autohome's revenue and profit have both declined for two straight years (2025 revenue RMB 6.45 billion, -8.3%), which may be a signal of industry-wide decline
- The platform has weak bargaining power in the value chain, squeezed from both sides — upstream by traffic platforms and downstream by automakers' direct-sales channels
- Munger's summary: I don't like businesses that have to keep running just to stay in place

### Risk & Management (Li Lu's Lens)

- An "illusion of independence" — equity is pledged to ByteDance-affiliated entities; on paper He Jian holds 60%, but actual control is questionable
- CEO He Jian has almost no public information, and the management team is nearly completely opaque — abnormal for a company approaching an IPO
- Deep dependence on Douyin traffic is the single biggest risk — should ByteDance adjust its strategy or build its own closed-loop automotive transaction business
- The winter-test controversy (in which Huawei suspended cooperation) exposed the fragility of a content platform's credibility
- More than half of dealers are losing money, and the paying customer base is shrinking
- Li Lu's summary: this is not a business you can "hold comfortably for 10 years"

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
1. ByteDance's first IPO candidate, carrying built-in traffic and attention
2. The CPS model has been the first in the industry to scale, with 28,500+ dealers participating — the right direction
3. DAU growth far outpaces Autohome, with a high share of young users and substantial room for growth
4. Citi and Goldman Sachs have already been selected as underwriters — a clear signal the IPO is moving forward
5. Asset-light model, theoretically capable of high ROE and strong free cash flow
6. Some NEV makers are starting to "let go of their direct-sales obsession" and return to dealers — a near-term positive
7. Strike price of $0.001 — if the listing succeeds, the threshold to realize option value is extremely low

### Bear Case
1. The moat does not belong to DCar — its core competitiveness is ByteDance's traffic and algorithms
2. No pricing power — price increases triggered boycotts, forcing a pivot to a "lighter-touch" CPS strategy
3. The NEV direct-sales model is cutting off the platform's roots — its addressable market is shrinking
4. Financials are completely opaque; profitability is entirely unverified
5. Complex equity structure — "nominally independent, substantively controlled" — protection of public shareholders' interests is questionable
6. Rival Autohome's continuing decline hints at a limited industry ceiling
7. The $3.0 billion valuation already prices in substantial growth expectations; an IPO priced at $4.0-5.0 billion would be even more aggressive

## Summary

DCar is a fast-growing horse incubated by ByteDance, with the CPS model pointed in the right direction and a young, rapidly growing user base. But measured against value-investing standards, it has three fundamental problems: **the moat is borrowed, pricing power does not sit with the company, and the industry ceiling is being pushed down.**

The four analysts' combined score is 2.4/5 — this is not a business that "wins simply by avoiding stupid mistakes," but one that has to keep doing the right thing just to survive. With financial data completely opaque, the current $3.0 billion valuation has no margin of safety, let alone a possible post-IPO pricing of $4.0-5.0 billion.

**For option holders:** if the strike price is extremely low (e.g., $0.001), then as long as the company IPOs successfully, the options will have considerable value. The core risks are "whether it can list" and "whether the post-listing valuation can hold." Treat the options as a high-odds call option — there's a chance of a big payoff, but you should also be mentally prepared for the scenario in which it never lists.
