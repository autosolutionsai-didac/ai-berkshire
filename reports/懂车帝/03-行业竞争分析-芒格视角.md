# Dongchedi Industry Competition Analysis: A Munger Perspective

> Analytical dimensions: industry economics, competitive landscape, moat, and position in the value chain
> Updated: May 2026

---

## I. Industry Size and Growth

### 1.1 Automotive Content/Media Market Size

China's online automotive marketing market can be broken down into three core revenue pools:

| Revenue Type | 2023 Size | 2025 Estimate | Trend |
|---------|-----------|-----------|------|
| Automotive online advertising (brand + performance) | approx. 15.9 billion yuan | approx. 17-18 billion yuan | Video ads' share keeps rising past 40%+ |
| Lead generation/dealer services | approx. 8-10 billion yuan | Contracting | Dealers' willingness to pay is declining while per-lead prices rise |
| Transaction commissions/value-added services | Early stage | Rapid growth | Rise of the CPS (cost-per-sale) model |

The overall automotive digital marketing market (including SaaS, data services, and outsourced operations) had already reached approximately 26 billion yuan in 2024, growing at more than 10%. But the structural shift matters more than the aggregate figure: **traditional display advertising and CPT (cost-per-time) leads are shrinking, while content marketing, short-video ad spend, and CPS transaction commissions are growing.**

### 1.2 Growth Rate of Automotive Online Marketing

- In 2025, domestic automotive brands' new-media marketing spend reached 62% of total marketing spend, yet the overall conversion rate was only 3.7%
- Average daily views of automotive vertical content on mainstream platforms reached 2.8 billion, but there is a significant "quality content gap"
- China's AI marketing market (across all industries) is projected to reach 66.9 billion yuan in 2025, growing at a 26.2% CAGR
- Within automotive online advertising, video ads account for roughly 38% and app advertising plus other formats account for roughly 47%

### 1.3 The Impact of New-Energy Vehicles on the Marketing Industry

**This is the single most important variable for understanding the whole industry.**

New-energy vehicle (NEV) penetration surged from roughly 45% in 2024 to 59.1% in December 2025, and is expected to break through 60% in 2026. This brings three structural effects:

**First, the direct-sales model is cooling off, and hybrid channels are making a comeback.**

Entering Q4 2025, NEV makers collectively began abandoning the pure direct-sales model:
- Denza closed direct-sales stores or transferred them to dealers in core markets such as Hebei, Henan, and Zhejiang
- Hongmeng Zhixing (Huawei's smart-vehicle alliance) launched a broad cutback of its direct-sales network
- Li Auto and NIO have also begun exploring lower-cost channel-optimization paths
- Annual operating cost for a single store in a tier-1 city's core commercial district runs as high as 5-6 million yuan; for a 200-store network that is roughly 1 billion yuan a year

**The core reason: the high cost of direct sales is unsustainable amid a price war.** In 2025, only 23.5% of dealers were profitable, while 55.7% were operating at a loss.

**Second, new-energy makers' reliance on third-party platforms is declining.**

Tesla, NIO, and Xiaomi Auto reach users directly through their own apps, communities, and direct-sales systems, so their owned-traffic pools naturally need fewer leads from third-party content platforms. But penetration into lower-tier markets still requires third-party platform support: in 2025, NEV makers operated 26,260 independent sales outlets, and expansion into tier-3, tier-4, and tier-5 cities became a growth engine (tier-5 city sales grew 14.6%, nearly three times the tier-1 growth rate).

**Third, the large-scale contraction of dealers is directly hitting the foundation of the lead-generation business.**

Roughly 4,961 4S dealerships closed and exited the network in 2025 alone. Luxury brands were hit especially hard:
- BMW's dealer count has shrunk from its peak to about 600, with a target of 550
- Mercedes-Benz has fallen back to under 600 dealers
- Porsche's dealer count plunged from 150 to 114, targeting 80 by 2026
- In early 2026, western China's largest dealer group, Guizhou Tongyuan Group, and Henan's leading dealer group, Dong'an Holdings, both collapsed in succession, affecting 180+ stores

**Key Munger-lens judgment: dealers are the core paying customers of automotive vertical platforms. When this customer base is exiting the network at a rate of nearly 5,000 a year, and more than half of the remaining dealers are losing money, the traditional lead-generation business model is being eroded.**

## II. Competitive Landscape

### 2.1 Comparison of the Three Major Platforms

| Metric | Autohome | Dongchedi | Yiche (Tengyi Technology) |
|------|---------|--------|----------------|
| DAU (2025) | approx. 14 million (declining from its historical peak) | over 10 million (still growing) | approx. 8 million |
| MAU | approx. 65 million | approx. 35-40 million (app) | approx. 40 million |
| Cross-platform automotive-interest user reach | Not disclosed | 510 million (including the Douyin ecosystem) | Relies on Tencent's ecosystem-wide reach |
| Dealers covered | approx. 20,000 (paying count declining) | over 30,000 | Not disclosed |
| Brands covered | All brands | 110+ | All brands |
| 2025 revenue | 6.452 billion yuan | Undisclosed (estimated at 3.0-4.0 billion yuan) | Undisclosed (already taken private) |
| Net profit | 1.443 billion yuan (down 14% YoY) | Achieved profitability at scale | Not disclosed |
| Valuation/market cap | approx. $2.05 billion | approx. $3.0 billion valuation (2024 funding round) | Tencent subsidiary, no independent valuation |
| Background | Controlled by Ping An Group | Spun off from ByteDance | Wholly acquired by Tencent |

**Key insight: Dongchedi's app-level DAU and MAU are still lower than Autohome's, but once the 510 million auto-interested users across the Douyin ecosystem are added in, its actual reach far exceeds that of the traditional vertical platforms.** According to people inside ByteDance, Dongchedi's DAU had already overtaken both Autohome and Yiche as early as October 2021.

### 2.2 The Impact of Short-Video Platforms on Traditional Automotive Media

**This is the industry's biggest structural change of the past three years.**

The impact of Douyin/Kuaishou on traditional automotive vertical platforms can be measured along several dimensions:

- Daily automotive-content consumption across Douyin and Dongchedi reached 500 million views, with the user base up 82% year over year
- More than 2 million creators and users post automotive content every day
- Nearly 30,000 dealers have already gone live on Douyin
- Automotive brands' new-media marketing spend has reached 62% of the total, dominated by short video and livestreaming

But the disruption has its limits:
- Lead costs have risen sharply — for the same budget, the number of qualified test-drive leads has fallen from a dozen-plus to five or six
- Traffic keeps getting more expensive, adding pressure on ROI
- Content is highly homogeneous, and there is a significant gap in quality content

### 2.3 New Entrants

| Platform | Automotive Content Positioning | User Scale | Competitive Advantage | Limitations |
|------|------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Xiaohongshu (RED) | Lifestyle content + authentic owner sharing | 230 million+ auto-interested users | Strong "seeding" power — 47.8% of users make decisions based on owners' shared experiences; search volume up 100% year over year | Not transaction-oriented; long lead-to-conversion chain |
| WeChat Channels | Dealer operations + brand content | Built on the WeChat ecosystem | Social virality, owned-traffic accumulation | Professional automotive content ecosystem still under construction |
| NIO/Xiaomi communities | Brand owned-traffic | Millions of users each | Extremely high user stickiness | Closed ecosystems, not general-purpose platforms |
| Bilibili/Zhihu | In-depth reviews/technical discussion | Relatively small automotive vertical | Highly professional | Weak monetization capability |

**Xiaohongshu's rise deserves attention:** direct-message lead capture in the automotive category surged 860% between 2023 and 2025, and it is becoming an important arena for automotive "seeding." But its path from seeding to closed sale is still far less complete than Dongchedi's.

### 2.4 Core Differentiation Across Platforms

**Autohome:** its 20-year moat has been built on accumulating a "professional spec database + word-of-mouth community." Structured vehicle-model data, authentic owner reviews, and forum communities — these assets still hold value at the "rational comparison" stage of a car-purchase decision. But that value is being diluted by the "gut-feel decision-making" of the short-video era.

**Dongchedi:** at its core, this is a "data + algorithm" company. Its main advantage is ByteDance's recommendation-algorithm capability and the traffic supply from the Douyin ecosystem. Its structured-data scale exceeds 50 million records, its vehicle-model database covers 76,000+ models, and its real-world testing database covers 4,000+ models with 1 million+ data points. Its innovation is the CPS model — moving from "selling leads" to "paying per completed sale," bringing it closer to the transaction itself.

**Yiche (Tengyi Technology):** deeply tied to the Tencent ecosystem, with its core asset being social traffic from WeChat and WeChat Channels. In 2025, Tencent opened its full resource stack to Tengyi Technology, positioning it as "social + automotive." Its differentiation lies in using WeChat's owned traffic to help dealers manage customer relationships.

## III. Detailed Competitor Analysis

### 3.1 Autohome (ATHM)

**Financial performance (full-year 2025):**
- Total revenue: 6.452 billion yuan, down 8.3% year over year (the second consecutive year of decline on both revenue and profit)
- Net profit: 1.443 billion yuan, down 14.2% year over year
- Adjusted net profit: 1.607 billion yuan, down 21.6% year over year
- Gross margin: 72.4% (versus 78.9% in 2024, a decline of 6.5 percentage points)

**Revenue by segment:**
- Media services: 1.153 billion yuan, down 24.3% year over year (the steepest decline)
- Leads services: 2.709 billion yuan, down 13.6% year over year (fewer paying dealers)
- Online marketing and other: 2.589 billion yuan, up 8.8% year over year (the only growing segment)

**Q1 2026:** total revenue of 1.048 billion yuan, still under pressure.

**2026 guidance (issued in Q1):** full-year EPS projected at $0.48-$0.78, with revenue of $188-271 million.

**Market cap:** down from a peak of over $10 billion to approximately $2.05 billion (about HK$16.6 billion on its Hong Kong listing); over the past year its share price has fallen more than 25% on both the U.S. and Hong Kong exchanges.

**Strategic direction:**
- Transitioning into an O2O automotive transaction platform (offline "Space Station" showrooms + Tiantianpaiche used-car auctions)
- AI integration (AI-powered smart marketing solutions)
- Growth in the new-energy vehicle sub-segment
- Used-car business: offline, built on the "Space Station" franchise network; online, centered on Tiantianpaiche

**A Munger-lens assessment:**

Autohome's problem, at its core, is the **devaluation of its "connectivity toll."** It used to be the essential gateway through which automakers and dealers reached car buyers, letting it charge steep lead-generation fees. But once Douyin/Dongchedi achieved more efficient user reach through algorithmic recommendation, that toll's pricing power was broken. Media-services revenue has fallen nearly 50% over two years, and leads revenue keeps shrinking — evidence that Autohome's core business is being eroded.

Its 72% gross margin is still high, which shows that the underlying economics of the existing business remain decent — but the revenue base is shrinking. This is a "boiling frog" style of decline: profit is still there for now, but the trend is irreversible.

### 3.2 Yiche (Tengyi Technology)

**Basic facts:**
- In 2020, Tencent acquired it in full for approximately 11.3 billion yuan and took it private, delisting it
- It has been renamed "Tengyi Technology" and has become the core vehicle for Tencent's automotive ecosystem
- It no longer discloses financial data independently

**Integration with the Tencent ecosystem:**
- In 2025, Tencent opened its full suite of resources to Tengyi Technology
- Yiche's content and dealer content were connected to users across Tencent's entire network
- Deep collaboration with WeChat Channels through the "WeChat Channels Factory-Store Ecosystem Project"
- Providing dealers with WeChat Channels operating tools and SaaS services
- Yiche's MCN ranks among the top on WeChat Channels' institutional-influence rankings

**Competitive strategy:**
- Repositioning from a "vertical automotive platform" to a "Tencent automotive ecosystem partner"
- Leveraging WeChat's social graph to help dealers run owned-traffic operations
- No longer chasing independent DAU/MAU growth, but integrating into the broader Tencent ecosystem

**A Munger-lens assessment:**

Yiche's strategy is essentially to give up independent development and become the "automotive module" of the Tencent ecosystem. In the short run this solves its traffic-anxiety problem (by tapping into WeChat's 1.2 billion users), but it also means giving up independent pricing power and brand independence. As an internal Tencent business unit, its ceiling depends on how much Tencent is willing to invest in the automotive space.

### 3.3 The Relationship Between Douyin Auto and Dongchedi

**Ecosystem positioning:**
- Douyin is the "traffic gateway," reaching 310 million daily active auto-interested users
- Dongchedi is the "professional landing point," providing in-depth car-selection and purchase-decision services
- Together they form a closed loop of "interest generation (Douyin) → in-depth research (Dongchedi) → transaction conversion"

**Livestream car sales:**
- Nearly 30,000 dealers are livestreaming on Douyin
- Growth was rapid from 2022 to 2024, but lead costs rose sharply in 2025-2026
- For the same budget, qualified test-drive leads have fallen from a dozen-plus to five or six, and customer-acquisition difficulty has climbed sharply

**Creator ecosystem:**
- Dongchedi has announced 500 million yuan in cash, 150 billion in traffic support, and 10,000 vehicle-trips of quality inventory to support creators
- The 2025 "Dongchedi Douyin Auto Influencer Carnival" generated over 1 billion impressions across the network
- Approximately 7.5 million automotive-content creators

### 3.4 NEV Makers' Self-Built Channels

| Automaker | Channel Model (2026 status) | Dependence on Third-Party Platforms |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Tesla | Beginning to pull back direct-sales stores in core commercial districts | Low (brand carries its own traffic) |
| NIO | Mostly direct sales, starting to bring in third-party partners; sub-brand Onvo uses a hybrid model | Low-to-medium (strong community, but needs partners for lower-tier markets) |
| Xiaomi Auto | Hired away Tesla China's former CEO to strengthen sales; delivered 500,000 units in 19 months | Medium (Xiaomi's ecosystem carries its own traffic, but still needs channel expansion) |
| BYD | Mostly dealer-based | Medium-high (relies on third-party platforms for leads) |
| Li Auto | Starting to optimize direct-sales costs | Low-to-medium |

**Key conclusion: leading NEV makers (Tesla/NIO/Xiaomi) need third-party platforms less and less, but traditional automakers and second/third-tier NEV brands still depend on them heavily.** Lower-tier markets (tier-3, tier-4, and tier-5 cities) remain an area where third-party platforms still add value.

## IV. Industry Trends

### 4.1 The Shift from the "Lead Era" to the "Content Era"

**Traditional model (Lead Era):**
- Dealers purchase lead packages from platforms annually or monthly
- Platforms charge on a CPT (cost-per-time-slot) basis
- Lead quality is uneven; 80% of brands face the pain point of "high lead volume, low lead quality"

**New model (Content Era):**
- Content is traffic; short video and livestreaming have become the primary means of reaching users
- Shifting from CPT to CPS (pay per completed sale); Dongchedi made a full switch starting in 2023
- An integrated pipeline of "content planning — influencer network — AIGC production — social-media operations"
- Marketing has shifted from "buying leads" to "making content"

**The question Munger would ask: is the CPS model actually better than CPT?**

For dealers, CPS lowers up-front investment risk (no sale, no payment). But for the platform, the CPS model brings greater revenue volatility and demands stronger end-to-end transaction capability. This is really a choice between "certain, moderate income" and "uncertain, high-income potential." Dongchedi has chosen the latter, which requires stronger execution.

### 4.2 AI's Impact on the Automotive Content Industry

**Already happening:**
- AI-generated car-review content (text, video scripts)
- AI-powered car-selection assistants (Dongchedi launched its "AI Panorama" in 2026, featuring AI agents for a range of scenarios)
- AI-assisted optimization of dealer sales scripts and customer management
- Autohome has launched a full-chain AI solution spanning "content production – precision distribution – lead handling"

**Potentially industry-changing:**
- AI-powered virtual test drives (still at an early stage)
- AI-driven personalized recommendations replacing traditional editorial recommendations
- AIGC sharply lowering content-production costs, intensifying content homogenization

**A Munger-lens question: is AI an "equalizing force" for this industry, or does it make the strong stronger?**

In the short run, AI favors platforms with large-scale data and algorithmic capability (Dongchedi/ByteDance). But in the long run, once AI drives content-production costs toward zero, differentiation in the content itself will disappear, and a platform's core value will revert to its "transaction-closing capability" and "user trust."

### 4.3 Shifts in NEV Makers' Marketing Budget Allocation

- NEV makers increasingly prefer building their own owned-traffic pools and communities, cutting ad spend on third-party platforms
- When the price war intensifies, marketing budgets are the first thing to get cut
- Autohome's media-services revenue falling from roughly 1.5 billion yuan to 1.15 billion yuan over two years reflects this trend
- But marketing demand in lower-tier markets is actually increasing

### 4.4 The Impact of Shrinking Dealer Numbers on the Leads Business

This is the biggest structural risk facing the industry:

- Roughly 4,961 4S dealerships closed and exited the network in 2025
- Only 23.5% of dealers were profitable
- The average gross margin on new-car sales at luxury-brand dealers was -23.6%
- Several large dealer groups collapsed in early 2026

**The impact on the leads business is direct and lethal:**
- Autohome's leads-services revenue fell 13.6% year over year, primarily because of "a decline in the number of paying dealers"
- When dealers are losing money, the lead-purchasing budget is the first thing they cut
- Average spend per dealer is also falling (expansion into lower-tier cities is pulling down average order value)

## V. Value-Chain Analysis

### 5.1 Value-Chain Breakdown

```
Automakers (OEMs)
  ↓ Ad budgets + lead demand
Content/advertising platforms (Autohome, Dongchedi, Yiche, Douyin, Xiaohongshu)
  ↓ Leads / traffic / content
Dealers (4S stores, NEV direct-sales stores)
  ↓ Sales + service
Consumers
```

### 5.2 Changes in Bargaining Power Across the Chain

**Automakers (upstream): bargaining power increasing**
- The price war is squeezing automakers' own profits, compressing marketing budgets
- NEV makers are building their own channels, reducing reliance on third parties
- More choices available: from "only advertising on vertical automotive platforms" to "Douyin/Xiaohongshu/WeChat Channels are all viable options"
- Their demand of platforms has shifted from "give me leads" to "help me close the sale"

**Platforms (midstream): bargaining power diverging**
- Traditional vertical platforms (Autohome) have seen their bargaining power decline markedly — revenue and profit have both fallen for two consecutive years
- Short-video platforms (Douyin/Dongchedi) retain bargaining power for now, thanks to their traffic advantage
- But rising traffic costs and falling ROI show that competition among platforms is also intensifying

**Dealers (downstream): weakest bargaining power**
- 55.7% are operating at a loss, making them the most vulnerable link in the value chain
- Squeezed by automakers to cut prices and chase volume, and by platforms to pay for leads
- The large-scale exit from the network is itself a sign of lost bargaining power

**Consumers: strongest bargaining power**
- The biggest beneficiaries of the price war
- An ever-growing range of channels to choose from (online price comparison, livestream rooms, direct-sales stores, dealers)
- Information asymmetry has narrowed considerably

### 5.3 Is the Platform's Value in the Chain Increasing or Decreasing?

**Core judgment: the value of the traditional "lead intermediary" role is decreasing, but the "transaction facilitator" role has room to grow.**

**Where value is decreasing:**
- The pure lead-selling business model is dying (poor lead quality, low conversion, declining dealer willingness to pay)
- Brand ad budgets are being siphoned off by short-video platforms and owned channels
- NEV makers' direct-sales model bypasses the intermediary platforms

**Where value is increasing:**
- Used-car transactions: high information asymmetry and transaction complexity mean platforms still add intermediary value
- Coverage of lower-tier markets: tier-3, tier-4, and tier-5 cities that automakers themselves struggle to penetrate
- Content ecosystem: high-quality professional reviews and word-of-mouth still influence purchase decisions
- Transaction closed-loop: end-to-end service from lead to sale — the CPS model has platforms taking on more of a transactional role

## VI. A Munger-Lens Overall Assessment

### 6.1 Does This Industry Have a "Moat"?

**The traditional moat is being dismantled:**
- Autohome's 20 years of accumulated user reputation and structured data are losing their defensive power as user behavior shifts in the short-video era
- Its "network effect" is weaker than that of social platforms — users can use multiple automotive platforms at once
- Brand loyalty is limited — users go wherever the content quality and user experience are best

**Where the new moat lies:**
- ByteDance's algorithmic recommendation capability and traffic ecosystem are Dongchedi's biggest moat
- But this moat belongs to the ByteDance system, not entirely to Dongchedi as an independent entity
- The accumulation of transaction data (completed-sale data under the CPS model) could become a new moat

### 6.2 Industry Economic Characteristics

**Several characteristics Munger would dislike:**
- Customers (dealers) are shrinking and losing money on a large scale
- The industry is in a "price war" phase, where all participants are competing over who loses less
- A business model dependent on advertising and lead revenue is highly exposed to macroeconomic swings
- Differentiation among platforms is unclear, and competition is intense

**Several characteristics that might interest Munger:**
- China's 20 million+ annual new-car sales make it the world's largest market — the underlying base is plenty large
- A car is a high-ticket, low-frequency purchase, and users genuinely need information services during the decision process
- The used-car market (with transaction volume of over 20 million vehicles in 2025) still has low online penetration and room to grow
- Upgrading the business model from "selling leads" to "driving transactions," if successful, would produce healthier unit economics

### 6.3 Assessment of Dongchedi's Competitive Position

**Strengths:**
1. ByteDance's traffic ecosystem (510 million auto-interested users) gives it overwhelming distribution power
2. Its CPS-model innovation is closer to the actual transaction and better aligns its interests with dealers'
3. It has already achieved profitability at scale and is nearing an IPO (valuation of $3.0 billion, planning to raise $1.0-1.5 billion)
4. Its data-plus-algorithm capability leads the automotive vertical
5. A high share of young users, which fits the new-energy-vehicle buyer profile

**Risks:**
1. Dependence on ByteDance's traffic — can it maintain traffic supply after becoming independent?
2. Can transaction volume keep growing under the CPS model amid large-scale dealer exits?
3. As the automotive price war continues, automakers' and dealers' marketing budgets may shrink further
4. Numerous competitors (Autohome, Yiche, Xiaohongshu, WeChat Channels) mean its moat isn't deep enough
5. As a public company after its IPO, it will face heavy pressure to meet growth expectations

**Competition with Autohome:**

Dongchedi is eating into Autohome's market share, but this is not a "winner-take-all" dynamic. Autohome's 20 years of accumulated data and PC-era user habits still hold value in its existing installed base — it is just that all the incremental growth has gone to Dongchedi and other new platforms. Autohome's market cap has shrunk from over $10 billion to about $2 billion, while Dongchedi's valuation has gone from zero to $3 billion — this shift from one to the other is already unmistakable.

### 6.4 Projecting the Industry's Endgame

**The most likely scenario:**
- No single player will dominate. Instead, multiple platforms will coexist: "Douyin/Dongchedi (content + transactions) + WeChat/Yiche (social + owned traffic) + Xiaohongshu (seeding) + Autohome (tools + word of mouth)"
- The leads business keeps shrinking, while the transaction business becomes the new growth engine
- The used-car business could become the real battleground for differentiation
- AI will accelerate the industry shakeout, but it won't change the underlying need for "decision support" that users have

**Munger's likely conclusion:**

This is not an industry that offers a "wonderful business at a fair price." Customers are shrinking, competition is intensifying, and the moat is being eroded. Dongchedi holds clear advantages over its competitors, but the economic characteristics of the industry as a whole — dependence on advertising/lead revenue, large-scale customer losses and exits, and a price-war environment — are cause for concern. Unless Dongchedi can truly complete its transformation from a "media platform" into a "transaction platform," and build a genuine moat in high-value areas like used cars, this industry does not warrant a large position.
