## First, let's work out exactly where the ceiling is

Investors are drawn in by "global No. 3," but one more important fact is often overlooked: **third place in which market? And how big is that market?**

The global CMOS image sensor market is about **$31.2 billion** (2025). But the "pond" Gpixel operates in isn't the entire CIS market — it barely touches smartphones, automotive, or security — its pond is **industrial imaging + scientific imaging + medical imaging**.

| Sub-market | Global size (estimate) | Gpixel share | Gpixel revenue |
|---------|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Industrial imaging (machine vision) CIS | ~**$2 billion** | **~15.2%** (3rd) | ~$300 million |
| Scientific imaging CIS | ~**$0.5 billion** | **~16.3%** (3rd) | ~$80 million |
| Medical imaging CIS | ~**$0.8 billion** | <1% (just entering) | Negligible |
| **Total addressable market** | **~$3.3 billion** | ~5% | ~$120 million |

(Source: Frost & Sullivan, inferred from the prospectus)

**$3.3 billion** — that is the entirety of the market currently addressable by Gpixel.

For comparison: the smartphone CIS market is about **$15+ billion**, and the automotive CIS market is about **$4+ billion**. The track Gpixel has chosen is only one-fifth the size of the smartphone CIS market.

**This is a double-edged sword**:

- **The upside**: the pond is small, and the giants can't be bothered to fight seriously. Sony earns over RMB 100 billion a year from smartphone CIS alone — it's not worth going all-in against you in a $2 billion industrial market.
- **The downside**: the ceiling is low. Even if Gpixel captured 30% share of industrial + scientific CIS (versus 15% today), that would only be about **$700 million-$1 billion** in revenue. At a 30% net margin, net profit would be about $200-300 million (RMB 1.5-2 billion). Against the current **HKD 30 billion** market cap, you would need revenue to triple just to "grow into" this valuation.

## Five rivals, five different playbooks

Gpixel's competitors fall into two categories: **overseas giants** and **domestic challengers**.

### First tier: overseas giants

| Company | Type | Industrial CIS share | Characteristics | Threat to Gpixel |
|------|------|-------------|------|-----------------|
| **Sony** | IDM | **~35%** | Strongest technology, most complete production lines, overwhelming scale | Risk of a crushing top-down move |
| **onsemi** | IDM | **~20%** | Dual-engine of automotive + industrial, mature process | Direct competitive pressure |
| **Teledyne** | IDM | ~10% | Acquired e2v, a veteran in scientific/aerospace CIS | Direct competition in scientific imaging |

**Sony** is the biggest potential threat. Sony holds 43% of the global consumer CIS market — if it decided to migrate its consumer-grade scale advantages (more advanced processes, lower costs) into the industrial tier, both Gpixel's gross margin and share would come under pressure.

But Sony's current strategic focus remains on smartphones and automotive — its planned capital expenditure on image-sensor business for fiscal years 2024-2026 is actually **down about 30%** versus fiscal years 2021-2023. Sony is shrinking its overall CIS investment, not expanding it.

(Source: Sony's annual report, Yole Group)

**onsemi** is the more direct competitor. It holds about 20% share in industrial CIS, its product line overlaps heavily with Gpixel's (global shutter, high-speed imaging), and as an IDM — with its own fabs — it enjoys controllable costs and stable supply.

**Teledyne** entered the scientific/aerospace CIS market by acquiring the UK's e2v, and competes directly with Gpixel in scientific imaging.

### Second tier: domestic challengers

| Company | Background | Industrial CIS positioning | Threat to Gpixel |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **OmniVision (Will Semiconductor)** | The world's 3rd-largest CIS maker | Expanding from consumer into industrial grade, well capitalized | Medium-high |
| **SmartSens** | Started in security CIS | Expanding into industrial CIS | Medium |
| **GalaxyCore** | Low-end consumer CIS | Not yet in high-end industrial | Low |

(Source: each company's financial reports, industry reports)

**OmniVision (Will Semiconductor)** is the domestic rival most worth watching closely. Its annual revenue is about **RMB 25 billion** — **30 times** Gpixel's. Although its main focus is currently consumer and automotive-grade CIS, if it were to seriously commit to the industrial tier — with money, a team, and foundry resources — the impact on Gpixel could not be ignored.

**SmartSens** started in security CIS and is expanding toward industrial applications. Its advantages are lower prices and good domestic customer relationships; its weakness is that its product performance still lags Gpixel's high-end line.

## Domestic substitution: real proposition or false one?

"Domestic substitution" is the investment logic most often cited for Gpixel. But this logic needs to be unpacked.

### The case for it: domestic substitution's addressable space really is large

**Fact one**: China is one of the largest machine-vision markets globally, and its industrial automation rate is still lower than Japan, South Korea, and Germany, giving high-confidence room for penetration to rise.

**Fact two**: in industrial CIS, Chinese equipment makers have historically used almost 100% Sony and onsemi products. Gpixel is the only domestic maker capable of offering an alternative.

**Fact three**: geopolitics has accelerated the substitution process. Out of supply-chain security concerns, Chinese equipment makers are proactively seeking domestic CIS suppliers. Against a backdrop of persistent uncertainty around chip sanctions, "being able to buy it" matters more than "buying the best one."

**Fact four**: AI vision inspection (defect detection, 3D measurement) requires higher-performance CIS, driving both ASP increases and higher demand volume.

### The case against it: don't overestimate the pace of substitution

**Problem one: the substitution cycle for industrial CIS is extremely long.**

Qualifying a new sensor in a machine-vision device takes a **6-18 month** verification cycle. Equipment makers won't immediately swap out a Sony chip they've used for years just because of "domestic substitution" — the switching cost is too high (the entire production line needs to be recalibrated). Substitution is gradual, not explosive.

**Problem two: "domestic substitution" doesn't mean "only Gpixel."**

OmniVision (Will Semiconductor) is also a Chinese company, and a much larger one. If industrial equipment makers are looking for a domestic alternative, OmniVision is also an option. Gpixel has the edge at the high end, but in mid-to-low-end industrial CIS, OmniVision may be more price-competitive.

**Problem three: the ceiling on substitution depends on the end market.**

China's industrial automation investment is cyclical. In 2023, Gpixel's revenue grew almost zero (RMB 604 million → RMB 605 million), showing that when industrial investment slows, the "domestic substitution" logic cannot offset an industry downturn.

### Conclusion

**Domestic substitution in industrial CIS is a real proposition, but the pace is slower than the market expects, and the ceiling is lower than the narrative implies.**

## What if Sony starts a price war?

This is one of the "soul-searching" questions investors ask most often.

**Look at the facts first**: Sony is currently not fighting a price war in industrial CIS. The reason is simple — the industrial CIS market is too small to be worth Sony trading losses for share. Sony's strategic focus is on smartphone and automotive CIS; industrial is more of a side business.

**But what if Sony does?**

| Scenario | Impact |
|------|------|
| Sony cuts prices moderately by 10-15% | Gpixel's gross margin falls from 67% to 55-60%, still profitable |
| Sony cuts prices aggressively by 30%+ | Gpixel's gross margin could fall below 50%, profit shrinks substantially |
| Sony uses new (stacked) processes for industrial CIS | A performance generation gap emerges, Gpixel's high-end products lose competitiveness |

**Gpixel's defensive capabilities**:

1. **Customer stickiness**: switching sensors is costly for industrial equipment makers (6-18 month verification cycle), so they won't easily switch suppliers over a price gap
2. **Local service**: Chinese customers can more easily obtain Gpixel's technical support and customization services
3. **Domestic-substitution demand**: some customers must use domestic CIS for supply-chain security reasons, so price is not the primary consideration

**But defense has its limits**: if Sony uses 3D stacking to produce industrial CIS with a substantial performance lead, "stickiness" and "service" won't be enough — customers want the best product.

**Most likely scenario**: Sony won't deliberately start a price war (the market is too small, not worthwhile), but it will keep using its technology advantage to maintain high-end share. Gpixel has domestic-substitution protection in the China market, but will have to compete head-on with Sony and onsemi in overseas markets.

## An overlooked dimension of competition: fabless vs. IDM

Gpixel has a **structural difference** from its main competitors — it is fabless (no in-house wafer fab), while Sony, onsemi, and Teledyne are all IDMs (with their own fabs).

| Dimension | IDM (Sony/onsemi) | Fabless (Gpixel) |
|------|------------------|-------------------|
| Manufacturing control | Fully independent | **Subject to foundries** |
| Process optimization | Design and process co-evolve | Can only design within the process range the foundry offers |
| Capacity assurance | Own capacity, prioritized for self-use | Capacity gets squeezed by large customers when tight |
| Sanctions risk | Low (own fabs mostly domestic) | **High (foundries are overseas)** |
| Capital efficiency | Asset-heavy, lower ROE | Asset-light, high ROE potential |
| Cost | Scale advantage, controllable cost | Foundry fees + packaging fees, weak bargaining power |

Under normal operating conditions, the fabless model is the more efficient choice — no need to invest tens of billions of dollars building fabs, and it can focus on what it does best: design.

**But under the current geopolitical environment, the disadvantages of the fabless model are dramatically magnified.**

Gpixel's wafer manufacturing depends on Israel's Tower Semiconductor and South Korea's DB HiTek, and packaging depends on Japan's KFT. All of the top five suppliers are overseas companies, together accounting for more than **70%** of purchases. If sanctions escalate, these foundries could be prohibited from, or could voluntarily refuse, manufacturing for Gpixel — leaving the company facing a dire situation of **having no chips to sell**.

(Source: prospectus, National Business Daily)

**This is not a theoretical risk** — the prospectus explicitly flags it in its risk factors section.

**Mitigation measures**: Gpixel has built its own packaging line through its subsidiary Gpixel Chip Assembly (in-house packaging ratio reached **41%** in 2025), and is advancing tape-out cooperation with domestic foundries. But transferring wafer-fabrication processes is far harder than packaging — high-end CIS requires specialized image-sensor processes, and very few domestic foundries can offer this. This problem cannot be fundamentally resolved in the short term.

## Summary of the competitive landscape: Gpixel's position

A simplified map of the competitive landscape:

```
                    High-end ←————————————→ Mid-to-low-end
                    
  IDM (own fab)      Sony         onsemi
                    Teledyne
                    
  Fabless (no fab)   Gpixel       OmniVision (Will Semi)
                                  SmartSens
```

Gpixel's position is in the **bottom-left corner** — technologically close to the high end, but structurally constrained by the fabless model.

**Zone of strength**: high-end industrial/scientific CIS in the China market — domestic-substitution demand + technology barriers + customer stickiness together form a relatively secure "home base."

**Zone of risk**: head-to-head competition in overseas markets — without the protection of domestic-substitution logic, it must compete on price-performance directly against Sony and onsemi; plus the geopolitical risk in the foundry supply chain.

**Core judgment**: Gpixel is a big fish in a small pond. Within this pond, its position is solid — global No. 3, China's No. 1, 67% gross margin. But the pond itself isn't big enough, and the fish doesn't have a fully independent "respiratory system" (foundry dependence).

**Counter-argument**: there's a possibility Gpixel's supporters may not have fully considered — if global industrial CIS market growth slows to under 5% (Gpixel's near-zero revenue growth in 2023 already hinted at this), and share gains also stall, then capturing 15% of a $3 billion pond is the ceiling. At the current market cap, that is not an attractive return.
