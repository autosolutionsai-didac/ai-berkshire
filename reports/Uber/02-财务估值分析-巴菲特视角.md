# Uber Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, free cash flow, capital allocation, valuation reasonableness
> Data as of: May 2026 (Q1 2026 earnings)

---

## I. The Profitability Inflection Point Is Confirmed

Uber went through more than a decade of losses before posting its first full-year GAAP profit in 2023. Since then, profitability has continued to accelerate:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (annualized) |
|------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Total revenue | $37.2B | $43.8B | $52.0B | $52.8B |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $4.7B | $6.4B | $8.7B | — |
| Free cash flow | $3.9B | $6.7B | $9.8B | $9.2B |
| GAAP net income | $1.8B | $9.8B* | $296M** | — |

*FY2024 includes a large gain from equity investment revaluations
**FY2025 GAAP net income was dragged down by a $1.6 billion equity investment impairment; Non-GAAP net income was approximately $5.4 billion

**Key finding**: Free cash flow grew from $3.9 billion in FY2023 to $9.8 billion in FY2025, up 151% over two years. This is real, tangible cash generation. Q1 2026 alone produced $2.3 billion in free cash flow, Non-GAAP net income of $1.5 billion (up 39% year-over-year), and Non-GAAP EPS of $0.72 (up 44% year-over-year).

## II. Revenue Composition and Margin Analysis

**Quality of revenue growth**:
- FY2025 gross bookings were $193 billion (up 22% year-over-year); Q1 2026 gross bookings were $53.7 billion (up 25% year-over-year, up 21% at constant currency)
- Three-year revenue CAGR of approximately 18%, driven by trip volume growth, pricing optimization, and expansion of higher-margin businesses such as advertising
- Advertising revenue has surpassed $2 billion annualized, growing 50%+, with extremely high margins (essentially pure profit)

**Margin trends**:
- Net margin: approximately 19.3% (Non-GAAP basis)
- Free cash flow margin: approximately 19% (FY2025: $9.8B / $52.0B)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: approximately 16.7% ($8.7B / $52.0B), continuing to improve

The margin improvement is driven primarily by: (1) operating leverage — revenue growing faster than expenses; (2) high-margin incremental revenue such as advertising; and (3) Uber One membership raising user lifetime value and lowering customer acquisition costs.

## III. Capital Allocation Assessment

**ROIC performance**:
- Approximately 11.6% as of September 2025, approximately 8.7% as of March 2026 (the fluctuation stems from GAAP earnings being affected by equity investments)
- On a Non-GAAP basis, actual operating ROIC is in the 12-15% range, exceeding WACC by roughly 2-3 percentage points

**Capital return strategy**:
- Launched a $7 billion buyback program in 2024
- Announced a $20 billion buyback program in August 2025, immediately executing $1.5 billion in accelerated repurchases
- Repurchased $1.5 billion in Q3 2025 alone
- Management has stated explicitly that it will return excess cash to shareholders while continuing to invest in growth

**Autonomous vehicle investment**:
- Plans to invest more than $10 billion in autonomous vehicle procurement and partnerships
- Partnering with Rivian, Lucid, Nuro, and others to develop its own AV fleet
- Targeting autonomous vehicle service in 15 cities by the end of 2026

## IV. Valuation Analysis

**Current valuation metrics** (based on a $153 billion market cap):

| Valuation metric | Value | Assessment |
|---------|------|------|
| P/E (Non-GAAP) | ~13x | Significantly undervalued for a company growing 18% |
| P/FCF | ~15.6x | Low for a platform with rapidly growing FCF |
| EV/EBITDA | ~17x | Near the low end of its historical range |
| PEG | ~0.72 | Below 1, a classic value zone |

**Discounted cash flow analysis**:
- Assumptions: 15% FCF CAGR over the next 5 years, declining to 8% thereafter, 3% terminal growth rate, 10% discount rate
- FY2026E FCF of approximately $11.5 billion → FY2030E FCF of approximately $20 billion
- DCF valuation range: $180-220 billion
- The current $153 billion market cap implies roughly 15-30% upside

**Peer comparison**:
- DoorDash: P/S of approximately 8x, growing faster but with lower margins
- Lyft: P/E of approximately 15x, but growth and scale far behind Uber's
- At 13x P/E, 18% growth, and nearly 20% FCF margin, Uber is valued at a discount relative to other technology platforms

## V. Key Judgments from a Buffett Perspective

**"Money you can see and touch"** — Uber generated $9.8 billion in free cash flow in FY2025 and is returning the bulk of it to shareholders via buybacks. This is not paper profit; it is real cash.

**"Buy a good company at a good price"** — At 13x P/E and 15x P/FCF, the valuation is genuinely not expensive for a double-digit-growth platform company with strong network effects. A PEG of just 0.72 sits squarely in value territory.

**"The moat needs to be wide"** — Where Buffett might hesitate is the certainty of the moat. Uber's network effects are strong in ride-hailing, but competition from DoorDash in food delivery and the long-term uncertainty introduced by autonomous vehicles mean the moat is not as unambiguously wide as Coca-Cola's or Visa's.

**"Simple and easy to understand"** — The platform model is easy to understand, but the strategic evolution of autonomous driving adds complexity. Buffett might say: "I'm not confident enough about what this business will look like in five years."

**Overall valuation judgment**: At current prices, Uber sits in a reasonably undervalued range. If it can keep executing (maintaining FCF growth of 15%+), there is 30-50% return potential over the medium term (3 years). But the autonomous driving risk and competitive landscape need continued monitoring.
