## Core investment thesis

### Why it's worth watching (the bull case)

1. **The profitability inflection is now irreversible**: from years of losses to $9.8 billion in free cash flow generated in FY2025, with five consecutive years of 20%+ growth. This is not a one-off tailwind but the result of operating leverage continuing to play out.

2. **Uber One is an underrated moat**: 50 million members contribute half of total bookings, and members spend 3x as much as non-members. This turns a transactional platform into a subscription-style platform, sharply improving revenue predictability and user stickiness.

3. **Advertising is a "hidden profit engine"**: annualized revenue exceeds $2 billion, growing 50%+, at extremely high margins. Following the precedents of Meituan and Amazon, advertising could become one of the biggest contributors to Uber's future profit growth.

4. **Valuation is clearly low**: 13x PE, PEG of 0.72, near the low end among tech-platform companies. The $20 billion buyback program provides downside valuation protection.

### Key risks (the bear case)

1. **Autonomous-driving disruption**: if Waymo/Tesla succeed in building their own ride-hailing networks, Uber's middleman role could be weakened. Uber is investing $10 billion+ to build its own AV fleet, but the outcome is highly uncertain.

2. **Regulatory risk**: multiple jurisdictions worldwide are pushing to reclassify drivers as employees, which could substantially raise costs. New Jersey passed related rules in May 2026.

3. **Delivery competition**: DoorDash holds 67% of the U.S. delivery market, and Uber Eats' 23% share faces the risk of further erosion.

## Core conclusions across the four analytical dimensions

### Business model analysis

At its core, Uber is a two-sided network platform connecting supply and demand, collecting a platform commission (take rate of roughly 28%) across its three business lines — mobility, delivery, and freight. The asset-light model confers extremely high operating leverage — revenue grew from $37.2 billion in FY2023 to $52.0 billion in FY2025, while free cash flow grew from $3.9 billion to $9.8 billion, with profit growth far outpacing revenue growth.

Key insight: **the combination of Uber One membership and the advertising business is upgrading Uber from a "transaction middleman" into a "user life platform."** If this transformation succeeds, the moat will widen significantly.

### Financial and valuation analysis

FY2025: revenue of $52.0 billion, free cash flow of $9.8 billion, adjusted EBITDA of $8.7 billion. Q1 2026 continued the strong momentum: total bookings of $53.7 billion (+25%), Non-GAAP net income of $1.5 billion (+39%).

At a market cap of $153 billion, P/FCF is roughly 15.6x. The DCF valuation range is $180-220 billion. **The current price implies the market is pricing in long-term growth of only about 8-10%, well below the actual 18% growth rate** — there is an expectations gap.

### Industry and competitive analysis

Mobility market: the duopoly structure is settled, with Uber holding 76% and Lyft posing no strategic threat.
Delivery market: DoorDash holds a dominant 67% share; Uber Eats is a "strong number two."
Autonomous driving: Uber is pursuing a "hedge across multiple partners" strategy (20+ partners plus its own fleet), the biggest long-term variable.

Key insight: **Uber's true moat lies not in any single business's market share but in the cross-platform synergy of mobility + delivery + membership.** This is a structural advantage that neither Lyft nor DoorDash can replicate.

### Risk and management assessment

CEO Dara has spent eight years transforming Uber from a chaotic, loss-making company into a disciplined profit machine — one of the most successful CEO turnaround stories in modern business history. The capital-allocation strategy of a $20 billion buyback alongside continued growth investment deserves credit.

Main concerns: (1) whether the strategy of simultaneously partnering with and competing against AV players is sustainable; (2) uncertain returns on the $10 billion+ AV investment; (3) a global regulatory environment that continues to tighten.

---

## Investment recommendation

### Conclusion: **Worth adding to the watchlist; consider building a position on pullbacks**

**Suitable investor profile**:
- 3-5 year time horizon
- Able to tolerate the long-term uncertainty created by autonomous driving
- Values free cash flow over GAAP profit

**Suggested entry strategy**:
- The current price (PE ~13x) is already in a reasonable range, but given autonomous-driving and regulatory risk, the ideal entry point would be a pullback to a PE of 11-12x (corresponding to a market cap of roughly $130 billion)
- If Q2-Q3 2026 results remain strong and the AV strategy shows positive progress, building a position at the current price is also reasonable

**Key metrics to track**:
1. Uber One membership count and its share of total bookings
2. Advertising revenue growth rate and penetration
3. Number of autonomous rides and pace of city expansion
4. Regulatory developments on driver classification across regions
5. Whether free-cash-flow growth holds at 15%+

**Position sizing**: moderate position (3-5% of the portfolio), leaving room to manage volatility.
