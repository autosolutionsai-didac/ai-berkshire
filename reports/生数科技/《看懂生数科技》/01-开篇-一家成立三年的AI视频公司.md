## Start with a set of facts

In April 2026, Sora officially shut down. Burning $15 million a day in inference cost while generating only $2.1 million in total revenue, OpenAI spent two years proving one thing: being the strongest technically doesn't mean you survive.

In the very same month Sora closed its doors, a Chinese startup completed a Series B round of nearly 2 billion yuan, at a valuation of over $2 billion. It's called Shengshu Technology, founded just three years ago, with fewer than a hundred employees, and its flagship product Vidu ranks No. 2 globally on technical benchmarks.

Here's where the company stands:

Put it all together and you get this: **a startup founded just three years ago, with fewer than a hundred employees, already valued at over $2 billion and ranked No. 2 in the world on technical benchmarks.**

If that sounds impressive to you — you're not wrong, but you're only seeing half the picture.

The other half is: **its direct rival Kling (Kuaishou) already has an ARR 25 times larger, and ByteDance's Jimeng sits behind an annual capital expenditure budget of 200 billion yuan. Meanwhile the sector's most famous pioneer, Sora, officially shut down in April 2026 after burning $15 million a day.**

That's the reality Shengshu Technology faces: **being technically strong enough doesn't guarantee staying alive.**

## What exactly is Shengshu Technology

In one sentence: **Shengshu Technology is an AI video generation company built around an original Diffusion+Transformer architecture, and its flagship product is called Vidu.**

What Vidu does: you type in a text description (say, "a cat playing guitar on the moon"), and it uses AI to generate a high-quality video for you. Its real-world use cases are expanding quickly:

| Use case | Specific application | Market status |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Advertising & marketing | Generating ads for a few hundred yuan (traditional production costs tens of thousands) | Fastest-growing, already has a working commercial loop |
| Short dramas / animated comics | AI-generated animated short dramas — **122,000** titles released in Q1 2026 | Explosive growth, but the hit rate is only **0.12%** |
| E-commerce | Digital-human livestreaming, product showcase videos | Market size estimated to double year-over-year |
| Film & TV production | Storyboard previews, VFX assistance | Early exploration stage |
| Personal content creation | Social media content generation | Large user base but low willingness to pay |

**Industry consensus holds that the AI video generation market will grow from about $1.1 billion in 2025 to about $12.8 billion by 2033** (source: market research firm estimates; figures vary by methodology). The direction of the sector is settled — the question is who survives to the endgame.

## Three people from Tsinghua and a CEO parachuted in

To understand Shengshu Technology, start with its team.

| Person | Role | One-line summary |
|------|------|-----------|
| **Zhu Jun** | Chief Scientist / Co-founder | Tsinghua professor, IEEE Fellow, a globally leading figure in diffusion models. His academic reputation is Shengshu Technology's biggest talent magnet |
| **Tang Jiayu** | President / Co-founder | Master's degree from Tsinghua's Computer Science department, previously at Tencent Youtu and RealAI. Originally CEO, stepped back to President in 2025 |
| **Bao Fan** | CTO / Co-founder | Born after 1995, Tsinghua PhD, first author of the U-ViT paper (ICLR 2023 Outstanding Paper Award). The core engine of technical execution |
| **Luo Yihang** | CEO (joined March 2025) | PhD from Tsinghua's Department of Automation, formerly head of ByteDance Volcano Engine's AI application product line. Brought in from outside to lead commercialization |

This team composition sends a clear signal: **the founding team is purely academic and lacks strong commercialization ability, so they poached someone with big-tech experience from ByteDance to become CEO.**

The bulls say: this shows the team has self-awareness and can bring in complementary talent.
The bears say: the founding CEO stepped down, which suggests commercialization wasn't progressing well, and whether a parachuted-in CEO can mesh with an academically-oriented founding team is a big question mark.

**Both sides have a point.** What matters is whether Luo Yihang can grow revenue over the next 12 months.

## Fundraising cadence: five rounds in three years, and accelerating

| Round | Date | Amount | Lead investor | Post-money valuation |
|------|------|--------|--------|---------|
| Angel round | June 2023 | Nearly 100 million yuan | Ant Group | About $100 million |
| Pre-A round | June 2024 | Several hundred million yuan | Baidu, Beijing AI Fund | Undisclosed |
| Series A | September 2025 | Several hundred million yuan | Bohua Capital | Undisclosed |
| Series A+ | February 2026 | Over 600 million yuan | Zhongguancun Science City, Xinglian Capital | Undisclosed |
| Series B | April 2026 | Nearly **2 billion yuan** | **Alibaba Cloud** | Over **$2 billion** |

*Source: Bloomberg, CNBC, Cailian Press, 36Kr (cross-verified across multiple sources)*

A few signals worth noting:

1. **2.6 billion yuan raised in two months** (600 million in the A+ round plus 2 billion in the B round) — either the company is in extremely high demand, or it's burning cash extremely fast. Quite possibly both
2. **Alibaba Cloud led the Series B** — this isn't just a financial investment; the two sides signed a strategic cooperation agreement, and Vidu is already live on Alibaba Cloud's Bailian model marketplace. Alibaba Cloud is both an investor and a distribution channel
3. **20x valuation growth in three years** ($100 million to $2 billion) — not unusual for the primary market, but only if the growth is sustained
4. **Completed its corporate restructuring in March 2026** — converting from a "limited liability company" to a "joint-stock company," the standard preparatory step ahead of a Hong Kong IPO

**A counterargument**: intensive fundraising can also signal an extremely high cash-burn rate — given its current funding balance and inference costs, the company may not be able to hold out long without continuing to raise capital. A $2 billion valuation in the primary market is a VC price tag; it doesn't mean the secondary market will accept the same number.

## A key comparison: the lesson of Sora

Before studying Shengshu Technology, it's worth looking at a cautionary tale first.

On March 24, 2026, OpenAI announced it was shutting down Sora — the most famous product in the AI video generation space.

Sora's financials are startling:

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Average daily inference cost | **$15 million** | Forbes, Cantor Fitzgerald estimates |
| Total lifetime revenue | **$2.1 million** | Media reports |
| Inference cost per 10-second video | About **$1.30** | Analyst estimates |
| Outcome | **Officially shut down on April 26, 2026** | OpenAI announcement |

**Burning $15 million a day, with total revenue of $2.1 million.** That means Sora burned more money in a single day than it ever earned in its entire existence.

Sora's failure taught the whole industry a lesson: **technical leadership doesn't equal commercial viability, and inference cost is a matter of survival.**

Shengshu Technology's Vidu has lasted longer than Sora and has better cost control (its API pricing is **55%** below the industry average), but it faces the same underlying industry problem: **can AI video generation inference costs come down to a level where the business model actually works?**

We'll dig into this question in detail in Episode 4.

## Why it's worth studying

Shengshu Technology hasn't gone public yet, and most people have probably never heard the name. So why spend time studying it?

Three reasons:

**First, AI video generation is one of the most certain technology trends of 2026.** Global VCs poured **$4.7 billion** into this sector in 2025 (up 189% year-over-year). Regardless of whether Shengshu Technology itself succeeds, understanding this space is essential to understanding the future of the content industry.

**Second, Shengshu Technology is the best sample case for understanding the core question of "can an independent AI startup survive."** Of more than 800 AI video companies, fewer than a fifth have a working commercial loop. The big platforms have traffic, compute, and distribution channels — what does an independent company have to compete with?

**Third, it will very likely IPO in Hong Kong in 2026-2027.** If it goes public, investors need to understand this company's true nature ahead of time — is it a genuine technology platform, or just another "storytelling" AI fundraising bubble?
