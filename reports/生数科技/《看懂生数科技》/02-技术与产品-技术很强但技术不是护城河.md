## Starting with a paper

In September 2022, Bao Fan, a PhD student on Zhu Jun's team at Tsinghua University, published a paper introducing the **U-ViT architecture** — the world's first architectural design to deeply fuse diffusion models with Transformers.

This paper won the ICLR 2023 Outstanding Paper Award (a top-tier global AI conference, with an award rate under 1%).

Why does this paper matter? Because it came a full year before OpenAI's DiT (Diffusion Transformer, the technical foundation of Sora). In other words, the Tsinghua team arrived at the core architectural idea behind Sora earlier.

That's Shengshu Technology's technical starting point: **not improving on someone else's technology, but owning an original architectural foundation.**

```
             Vidu Technical Architecture (simplified)
```

In simple terms:

- **Traditional video generation**: trains a text-understanding model and a video generation model separately, then bolts them together. The result is like two people who don't share a native language collaborating on an essay — the pieces frequently don't line up
- **The U-ViT architecture**: unifies text understanding and video generation into a single architecture trained end-to-end. The result is like one person who understands both text and imagery at once, producing more coherent output

In early products, this architectural advantage showed up as: **strong character consistency, coherent motion, and more accurate understanding of complex scenes.**

## Vidu's product lineup

As of May 2026, Vidu's product line has expanded from a single "text-to-video" tool into multiple directions:

| Product | Function | Pricing | Target user |
|------|------|------|---------|
| **Vidu SaaS** | Text-to-video, image-to-video, reference-to-video | Free / from $9.99/month | Consumer creators |
| **Vidu API/MaaS** | Developer access to video generation capability | About $0.0375/second | Enterprise developers |
| **Vidu Agent** | AI video creation agent | Undisclosed | Professional creators |
| **Vidu Claw** | AI advertising video generation | A few hundred yuan per ad | Advertisers |
| **Vidu App** | Mobile video generation | Same pricing as SaaS | Mobile users |

**The API pricing deserves its own mention**: Vidu's API costs about $0.0375/second, which is **55%** below the industry-average $0.084/second.

The signal this pricing strategy sends is unambiguous: **trading low price for market share.** At this stage, Shengshu Technology has chosen the classic internet playbook — "chase volume first, raise prices later."

But the counterargument is equally forceful: **low prices exist because there's no pricing power.** If users come because it's cheap, they'll go wherever is cheaper still. That's not a moat — that's a price war.

## Moat analysis: breaking down five dimensions one by one

Being technically strong doesn't equal having a moat. Let's evaluate it using the classic five-dimension framework.

### 1. Brand and pricing power: ★★☆☆☆ (weak)

- API pricing is **55%** below the industry average — this shows it's **buying market share**, not **selling a brand**
- It has some recognition among professional creator circles, but its consumer-facing visibility falls far short of Kling and Jimeng
- It hasn't established a "using Vidu = professional" brand perception

### 2. Switching costs: ★★☆☆☆ (weak)

- Consumer users can switch to Kling, Jimeng, Runway, and other competitors at zero cost
- Enterprise API customers face some integration cost (connecting to documentation, adapting workflows), but this doesn't amount to strong lock-in
- The prepaid credit system creates a little stickiness, but it's fundamentally weak lock-in

### 3. Network effects: ★☆☆☆☆ (extremely weak)

- Video generation is a **tool-type product**, with almost no network effects between users
- Unlike WeChat (the more people who use it, the more indispensable it becomes) or Taobao (the more sellers, the more attractive to buyers)
- This is a common ailment of AI tool products generally, not unique to Shengshu Technology

### 4. Scale effects: ★★★☆☆ (moderate)

- Model training carries high fixed costs, so the marginal inference cost per user falls as user volume grows
- **But the scale disadvantage is stark**: ByteDance's 2025 capital expenditure was **200 billion yuan**, and Kuaishou's Kling is backed by Kuaishou's own compute — Shengshu Technology's compute scale simply cannot compare
- Scale effects exist, but in this sector, an independent startup's scale can never catch up to a big platform's

### 5. Technology / patent barriers: ★★★★☆ (relatively strong)

- The original U-ViT architecture, **65 patents**
- Bao Fan's ICLR Outstanding Paper Award, Zhu Jun's status as an IEEE Fellow
- Vidu Q3 ranks in the global top two on the Artificial Analysis benchmark

**But this is the most fragile item in Shengshu Technology's moat — because technology barriers in AI decay extremely fast.**

## The shelf life of a technology barrier: 6-12 months

There's a brutal rule in the AI field: **today's technical breakthrough gets matched within 6-12 months.**

Historical evidence:

| Technical breakthrough | First mover | Time to be matched |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| GPT-4 (large language model) | OpenAI (March 2023) | Claude 3.5 Sonnet caught up roughly 18 months later |
| Stable Diffusion (image generation) | Stability AI (August 2022) | Matched by several open-source models within 6 months |
| Sora (video generation) | OpenAI (demo released February 2024) | Kling, Vidu, and others reached comparable levels within 12 months |
| DiT architecture | U-ViT (September 2022) → OpenAI's DiT (2023) | Now adopted uniformly across the industry |

The U-ViT architecture itself is a case in point: within less than a year of its introduction, the whole industry had absorbed the idea. Nearly all mainstream video generation models now use a similar Diffusion+Transformer architecture. **Most of the originator's first-mover advantage has already been absorbed by the industry's rapid catch-up.**

Shengshu Technology's current technical edge shows up more in **engineering execution** (the speed of converting architectural advantages into product experience) and **sustained iteration ability** (the improvement each model generation delivers) than in the exclusivity of the architecture itself.

**Bulls would say**: technology barriers decay fast, but Shengshu Technology's team can keep staying ahead — Zhu Jun's academic judgment ensures the right direction, and Bao Fan's execution ensures engineering follow-through.

**Bears would say**: rapid decay of the technology barrier, combined with big platforms' compute advantage of more than 10x, means the technical lead can't hold for long. Open-source models (Alibaba's Wan 2.2, Tencent's HunyuanVideo) are rapidly closing in on closed-source quality, and once open source catches up to closed source, API pricing will collapse to zero.

**Both sides have a point.** The technology barrier is currently Shengshu Technology's strongest moat, but also its most fragile. Its value isn't in "being deep right now" but in "whether it can keep being dug deeper" — which depends on whether the team can hold onto a global top-two position in every subsequent model generation.

## 65 patents: how much protection do they actually offer?

Patent protection in the AI field deserves a question mark.

**The favorable side**:
- The 65 patents cover the U-ViT architecture and related technology
- In negotiations with major enterprise clients, the patent portfolio serves as a signal ("we have proprietary intellectual property")
- If patent licensing becomes relevant in the future, this is a potential revenue source

**The unfavorable side**:
- Patent workarounds are relatively easy in AI — tweaking a few module designs can bypass them
- Chinese AI companies rarely sue each other over patents
- Open-source models aren't bound by patents at all — Alibaba could use an entirely different architecture to achieve similar results

**Conclusion: patents function more as a defensive tool (providing legal recourse if copied) than an offensive weapon (they cannot prevent competitors from emerging).**
