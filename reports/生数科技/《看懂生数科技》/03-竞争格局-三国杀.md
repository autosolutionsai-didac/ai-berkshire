What does this gap mean? Even though Vidu's revenue reportedly grew 10x last year (per the company's own disclosure), there's still an order-of-magnitude chasm between it and Kling.

## Kling: money, traffic, and an ecosystem behind it

Kling is Shengshu Technology's most direct and most dangerous competitor. Let's break it down.

### Revenue and growth

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Q1 2026 revenue | **$75 million** | Media reports |
| Annualized ARR | **$500 million** (doubled since before Chinese New Year) | 36Kr, QbitAI |
| Expected ARR, Q1 2027 | **$1.3 billion** | Sources familiar with the matter |
| Share of overseas revenue | Majority from North America and other overseas markets | 36Kr |

### Spin-off and listing plan

Kuaishou is pushing forward a plan to spin off Kling AI and raise independent financing:

- Target valuation of **$20 billion**, close to **70%** of Kuaishou's overall market cap (about $28.8 billion)
- Fundraising size of about **$2 billion**
- Plans to begin the IPO process in Q1 2027

**$20 billion vs. $2 billion — Kling's valuation is 10 times Shengshu Technology's.** This gap is driven mainly by revenue scale, but it also reflects the market's premium for "backing by a major platform's ecosystem."

### Kling's core advantages

1. **The Kuaishou ecosystem**: Kling is deeply integrated with the Kuaishou short-video platform, forming a closed loop of "create → distribute → monetize." Creators who generate content on Kling get traffic and revenue directly on Kuaishou. This kind of closed loop is something an independent tool can't replicate
2. **Compute scale**: backed by Kuaishou's own GPU clusters, its compute costs are far lower than an independent company that has to source compute externally
3. **Data flywheel**: Kuaishou's massive short-video dataset can be used for model training, and this data advantage reinforces itself over time
4. **Revenue validation**: an ARR of $500 million has already proven the viability of the business model, while Vidu is still at an early stage

### Kling's weaknesses

1. **Brand independence**: Kling is tied to the Kuaishou brand, and its independent brand recognition in overseas markets is weaker than Vidu's
2. **Technical ranking**: it ranks below Vidu Q3 in Artificial Analysis benchmarks
3. **Organizational inertia**: spinning off a unit within a large company can run into conflicts of interest and resource competition

## Jimeng: ByteDance's unlimited ammunition

Jimeng AI is ByteDance's AI video generation product. Its threat lies not in how strong the product itself is, but in the fact that **ByteDance stands behind it.**

### ByteDance's resource commitment

| Resource | Scale | Significance for Jimeng |
|------|------|------------|
| 2025 capital expenditure | **200 billion yuan** (about $29 billion) | AI video is just a small slice of this, but the absolute figure already exceeds the total funding raised by most independent companies |
| Douyin DAU | About **800 million** | The world's largest short-video distribution platform, a natural outlet for Jimeng content |
| CapCut (Jianying) | One of the world's largest video editing tools | Jimeng's capabilities can be embedded directly into CapCut, so users don't need to use Jimeng separately |
| Volcano Engine | ByteDance's cloud computing platform | Provides enterprise-facing API capabilities, competing directly with Vidu |

### Jimeng's playbook

Jimeng's strategy differs from Kling's — it doesn't aim to become a standalone product, but instead **embeds deeply into ByteDance's ecosystem**:

- Offering AI video creation capability inside Douyin
- Integrating an AI video generation module inside CapCut
- Exporting APIs to enterprise clients through Volcano Engine

This means Jimeng's revenue isn't tallied separately — the value it creates shows up as improved user stickiness on Douyin and CapCut. **For Shengshu Technology, this kind of "invisible competition" is harder to counter than competition with a sticker price attached.**

### Jimeng's competitive advantages

1. **Unmatched distribution**: Douyin's 800 million DAU plus CapCut's global user base equal the largest short-video distribution network on Earth
2. **Unlimited ammunition**: ByteDance's 2025 revenue exceeded **$110 billion**; no matter how much money Jimeng loses, it doesn't affect the parent company
3. **Understanding of Chinese-language scenarios**: Jimeng leads in depth of understanding for Chinese-language content creation scenarios
4. **Data advantage**: the video data generated on Douyin every day is the best fuel for training AI models

### Jimeng's limitations

1. **Not a standalone product**: weak independent brand recognition among the global creator community
2. **Technical ranking**: ranks below Vidu Q3 in benchmarks
3. **Overseas expansion constraints**: the geopolitical risks facing TikTok will affect Jimeng's overseas expansion

## Global competitors: a reshuffle is underway

Beyond the domestic "big three," the global race is also changing dramatically:

| Company | Status (May 2026) | Impact on Shengshu Technology |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Sora (OpenAI)** | **Already shut down** (April 26, 2026) | Favorable: the strongest competitor has exited. But it also shows how hard it is to monetize in this sector |
| **Runway** | Series E valuation of $5.3 billion, but growth is slowing | Neutral: Runway remains one of the top choices for overseas creators, but the technology gap is narrowing |
| **Pika Labs** | Series B, valued at $900 million-$1.5 billion, projected 2026 ARR of about $130 million | Competitive: Pika's ARR already far exceeds Vidu's, and it's also an independent startup |
| **Google Veo** | Technically strong but conservative on commercialization | Neutral: Google is unlikely to aggressively cut prices to grab market share |
| **Open-source models** | Alibaba's Wan 2.2 and Tencent's HunyuanVideo are improving in quality rapidly | **Threat: free open-source models directly erode the pricing room of closed-source models** |

**The variable most worth watching is open-source models.** If open-source video generation models reach 90% of closed-source model quality — "good enough" for most users — then every closed-source company's API pricing will be squeezed. This isn't a hypothetical; it's already happening.

4. PixVerse AI (Aishi Technology)
5. Hailuo AI Video (MiniMax)
6. **Vidu (Shengshu Technology)** — ranked sixth
7. Wondershare Tianmu (Wondershare)
8. MOKI (Meitu)
9. SenseTime Miaohua
10. Tencent Zhiying

*Source: Kuaisi Manxiang Research Institute & Xinhua News Agency (January 2026)*

**Sixth domestically, but second globally on technical benchmarks** — this mismatch is quite telling.

What does a high technical ranking but low application ranking mean? It shows that **there's a huge gap between technical capability and market share.** Jimeng isn't ranked first because its technology is the strongest — it's because it has Douyin and CapCut's distribution channels behind it. Kling isn't ranked third because its technology is the strongest — it's because of Kuaishou's ecosystem user base.

**This is exactly where Shengshu Technology's predicament lies: technical rankings are earned through papers and benchmarks, but market rankings are built through channels and ecosystems. The former runs on intelligence, the latter on resources.**

## Room to survive: can an independent company make it?

This is one of the most central questions in the whole series. Let's analyze it with data.

### Historical comparison: independent AI companies vs. big platforms

| Analogy | Independent company | Big platform | Outcome |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Search engines | AltaVista, Yahoo Search | Google | Independent companies died out; the big platform swept the field |
| Cloud computing | Early independent cloud vendors | AWS, Azure, Alibaba Cloud | Independent vendors were marginalized or acquired |
| Image generation | Stability AI | Adobe Firefly, Midjourney | Stability AI declined; Midjourney survived through differentiation |
| Autonomous driving | WeRide, Waymo | Tesla | Independent companies generally struggle; Tesla leads via its data advantage |
| **Speech recognition** | **iFlytek** | **BAT (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent)** | **iFlytek found room to survive in the B2B government/enterprise market** |

**The lesson of history is: independent AI companies rarely beat the big platforms in the consumer market, but they may find room to survive in the enterprise (B2B) market.**

iFlytek's case is especially instructive — it couldn't beat BAT in consumer products (like the iFlytek input method), but it found vertical scenarios in the government and enterprise market (education, healthcare, judiciary) that the big platforms weren't willing to cultivate deeply, and it ultimately went public and has survived to this day.

### Shengshu Technology's possible paths to survival

| Path | Feasibility | Risk |
|------|--------|------|
| **Path A: Go deep on B2B** | Medium-high | Leverage the Alibaba Cloud partnership plus its base of 10,000+ enterprise customers to build irreplaceability in vertical scenarios like advertising, film/TV, and e-commerce |
| **Path B: Get acquired** | Medium | Alibaba Cloud is already the lead investor in the Series B; folding into Alibaba's ecosystem in the future is a reasonable path. This would be a decent exit for early investors |
| **Path C: IPO independently and go global** | Medium-low | Build a brand in overseas creator markets, competing with Kling/Jimeng through differentiated product experience. But the resource disadvantage is significant |
| **Path D: Decline** | Medium | Technology barrier erosion, squeeze from big platforms, and commercialization falling short of expectations lead to gradual marginalization |

**A counterargument**: some believe Shengshu Technology will ultimately end up on Path B (being acquired). Alibaba Cloud leading the Series B isn't purely a financial investment — it could be laying the groundwork for future integration. If acquired by Alibaba Cloud, it would be a decent outcome for early investors, but not really a "success" for a founding team that wants to build something big and independent.

## Where does Shengshu Technology's differentiation lie?

In such a fiercely competitive environment, Shengshu Technology isn't without cards of its own:

### 1. Technical leadership

Vidu Q3 ranks in the global top two on the Artificial Analysis benchmark, surpassing Kling and Jimeng, both of which are backed by major platforms. **Technology is the only dimension where Shengshu Technology leads the big platforms.** Its shelf life may be short, but as long as it can be sustained, it holds differentiated value.

### 2. Native synchronized audio and video

Vidu Q3 is the industry's first model to support 16 seconds of native synchronized audio-video output. Most competitors need to bolt on an external audio model and then stitch it together; Vidu's end-to-end approach has an edge in experiential coherence.

### 3. Independent overseas brand

As an independent brand, Vidu has higher recognition in overseas markets than Kling and Jimeng — it isn't tied to any Chinese big-platform brand, which is a point in its favor for overseas users who have concerns about "Chinese big-tech data security."

### 4. Neutral positioning at the model layer

Shengshu Technology doesn't do distribution (it doesn't run a short-video platform), so it can serve multiple platforms at once. For enterprises that don't want to hand their core capability to a competitor (ByteDance or Kuaishou), choosing an independent third party is a reasonable choice.

**But whether this differentiation can convert into a durable competitive advantage depends on execution speed.** If Kling also achieves native synchronized audio-video output within 12 months, and if open-source models reach similar quality — then this differentiation would be erased.

## A brutal calculation

Estimating how hard it would be to catch up:

| Metric | Kling | Vidu | Gap multiple |
|------|------|------|---------|
| Current ARR | $500 million | $20 million (estimated) | **25x** |
| Target ARR (Q1 2027) | $1.3 billion | ? | ? |
| Parent company / funding ammunition | Kuaishou market cap of $28.8 billion | Cumulative funding of about $400 million (converted from 3 billion yuan) | **About 70x** (estimated, unified in USD) |

Assuming Vidu sustains 100% annual growth (already very aggressive), its Q1 2027 ARR would be about **$80 million**. Over the same period, Kling's projected ARR is **$1.3 billion**. The gap narrows from 25x to about 16x — closing, but not closed.

**To catch up with Kling, Vidu would need to sustain 200-300% annual growth for several consecutive years, while Kling's growth rate slows sharply.** That's not mathematically impossible, but the probability of it happening in commercial reality is low.

A more realistic goal might be: **don't chase Kling — instead, find your own position in a niche segment** — for example, becoming the go-to independent third-party AI video service provider for enterprise clients.
