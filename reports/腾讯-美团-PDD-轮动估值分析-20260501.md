# China-ADR Rotation Valuation Analysis: After Tencent's Rally, Is There an Undervaluation Opportunity in Meituan/PDD?

> **Report date**: 2026-05-01
> **Data window**: 2021-05-03 ~ 2026-04-30 (261 weeks)
> **Core question**: When Tencent's share price enters the fair-value/overvalued zone, does capital "spill over" into Meituan/PDD, creating an undervalued entry point?
> **Method**: 5-year percentile comparison + Meituan/PDD 12W/26W/52W performance following Tencent high-percentile events + lead-lag analysis + "golden cross" event backtest

---

## I. The single most important conclusion (the answer first)

### The "rotation hypothesis" essentially does not hold up in the historical data

| Hypothesis | Data-based answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Does capital spill over into Meituan/PDD after Tencent finishes rallying? | **Does not hold** | Across 8 instances of Tencent entering a high percentile, Meituan's average 12W return was **-19%**, PDD's average 12W return was **0%** |
| Does Tencent lead Meituan/PDD's moves? | **No lag observed** | Lead-lag analysis shows correlation peaks at lag=0 (0.68/0.53); correlation at every other lag is close to zero |
| Is "Tencent high + Meituan low" a good entry point? | **At least not historically** | In the most recent 36-week occurrence of this state, Meituan fell a further 7-21% |

### But there IS one reverse pattern that holds — and this is the real rotation signal

> **When Tencent enters a low percentile (≤ 20%), that is the best time to buy PDD**:
> - After the 2022-07-11 Tencent-bottom event, **PDD returned +16% (12W), +77% (26W), +45% (52W)**
> - After the identical 2022-07-25 event, **PDD returned +20% (12W), +114% (26W), +82% (52W)**

**The correct rotation logic runs in the opposite direction**: when the China-ADR bellwether (Tencent) gets hammered down to a bottom, the whole sector rallies together, and **the stock with the greatest rebound elasticity is PDD (the strongest-growth name)**.

- This means: **waiting for "Tencent to finish rallying, then Meituan follows" is not a viable strategy — they react simultaneously**
- The real divergence lies in "magnitude of move" and "long-term direction," not in "timing lag"

## VI. Why doesn't the "rotation hypothesis" hold? Underlying reasons

### 6.1 The three companies' decline drivers are completely different

| Company | Primary cause of decline | Can "capital rotation" fix this? |
|---|---|---|
| **Meituan** | Douyin taking food-delivery share, unprofitable overseas cash burn, margin compression | No — this is a **fundamentals** problem that a valuation switch cannot cure |
| **PDD** | US tariff threats, uncertainty over the Temu model, margin management | No — this is a **geopolitical + business-model** problem |
| **Tencent** | Doubling AI capex, concerns over reduced buybacks | Yes — short-term sentiment, **fixable via a valuation switch** |

**Key insight**: Meituan and PDD's lows are **fundamentals-driven lows**, not **sentiment-driven oversold lows**. These two types of lows have completely different rebound mechanisms:
- Sentiment-driven lows: repaired by "risk appetite recovering" → suited to valuation switching/capital rotation
- Fundamentals-driven lows: require waiting for an actual **fundamentals inflection point** (delivery share stabilizing, tariffs becoming clear)

### 6.2 China-ADR capital isn't "internal rotation," it's "external in-and-out flow"

Capital flows into China ADRs come from **Southbound Connect flows + global emerging-market funds + China-ADR ETFs**. This money tends to move in together (the whole sector rallies) or move out together (the whole sector falls) — **there is no internal rotation mechanism**.

→ This is precisely why correlation is high at Lag=0 and near zero at every other lag.

### 6.3 The three names have different valuation anchors

- **Tencent**: a stable-earnings large-cap, valued on P/E (core business currently ~12x)
- **Meituan**: a turnaround/distressed name, valued on P/S and delivery unit economics
- **PDD**: a growth stock, valued on revenue growth and the Temu narrative

→ **Different valuation anchors mean the same "undervalued" yardstick cannot be applied across all three**
