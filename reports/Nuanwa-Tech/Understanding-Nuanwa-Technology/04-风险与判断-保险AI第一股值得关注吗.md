## Before Reaching a Verdict, Look at All the Risks First

There is a type of company that becomes harder to judge the more you study it. The numbers aren't bad, but they're not good either; the technology isn't absent, but it doesn't quite qualify as a moat; the prospects aren't nonexistent, but every growth thesis comes with a "but" attached. NuanWa Technology is exactly this kind of company.

What this piece sets out to do is straightforward: **lay every key risk out on the table, present both sides clearly, and let you form your own judgment.**

---

## Risk One: Aibang Insurance Brokerage — A Compliance Hazard That Cannot Be Ignored

NuanWa Technology's wholly owned subsidiary **Aibang Insurance Brokerage Co., Ltd.** holds an insurance brokerage license and is the entity through which NuanWa conducts its insurance distribution business.

This company's record on consumer complaint platforms looks very bad:

| Complaint Channel | Complaint Volume | Main Content |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Black Cat Complaints | **248+** cumulative | Unauthorized charges, policies purchased without customer knowledge, inducing elderly customers into buying policies |
| 315 Financial Exposure Platform | Already reported | Phone harassment, text messages with hidden policy-purchase links |
| Economic Information Daily (Xinhua News Agency) | In-depth report | Elderly customers charged without explanation, charges triggered by clicking a link |

Source: Black Cat Complaints platform, 315 Financial Exposure Platform, Economic Information Daily

Specific complaint cases include:
- Elderly customers enrolled in policies without their knowledge, with automatic charges to their bank cards
- Customer-service chatbots calling under the pretext that "your insurance is about to expire" or "your supplementary medical reimbursement has arrived," directing customers to click a policy-purchase link in a text message
- Being charged immediately after clicking the link, with a complicated cancellation process

**This is not merely a compliance issue — it reflects the possibility that the company has adopted aggressive, even boundary-crossing, marketing tactics under growth pressure.**

### Two Interpretations

**The harsh interpretation**: Aibang's complaint pattern (AI outbound calls + SMS lead generation + automatic charging) is highly systematic — not the misconduct of individual employees, but **a problem at the level of business-model design**. Jiangsu Daotai, which NuanWa acquired in 2023, specializes precisely in AI outbound-call user operations — implying that "harassment-style marketing" may not be a byproduct, but a capability NuanWa deliberately built.

**The mild interpretation**: Complaint rates are generally high across the insurance-intermediary industry, and this is not a problem unique to NuanWa. 248 complaints represent a very low proportion for a company that processes tens of millions of cases a year. Regulators have not yet imposed substantive penalties on Aibang, suggesting the problem may not be as severe as it appears.

**Key judgment**: Regardless of interpretation, **the fact that a subsidiary of a soon-to-be-listed company has been named in a complaint report by a media outlet under Xinhua News Agency** will certainly be scrutinized during the HKEX hearing. If not handled well, it could affect the listing process.

## Risk Two: Ownership Structure — Balanced to the Point of Danger

| Shareholder | Ownership Stake |
|------|---------|
| Lu Min (through trusts, etc.) | **31.65%** |
| ZhongAn Online | **31.65%** |
| Sequoia Capital | **15.90%** |
| Other investors | ~20.8% |

Source: NuanWa Technology prospectus

Lu Min and ZhongAn Online **each hold 31.65% — a completely even split**.

This kind of ownership structure can be sustained by "mutual understanding" while the company is private, but the pressures it faces after listing are entirely different:

1. **Strategic-divergence risk**. Lu Min may want to reduce dependence on ZhongAn and move toward independence; ZhongAn may want NuanWa to keep serving the ZhongAn ecosystem. The two sides' interests do not necessarily align.
2. **Board-deadlock risk**. If both sides hold 31.65% each, votes on key decisions could tie — who breaks the deadlock?
3. **Control disputes**. If more public shareholders are brought in after listing, the existing balance of power could be upset.

**Counterargument**: Lu Min and ZhongAn's partnership has already lasted eight years, and the two sides' interests are tightly aligned. Moreover, if NuanWa lists successfully, the value of ZhongAn's 31.65% stake would rise substantially, giving ZhongAn no incentive to disrupt the arrangement.

## Risk Three: IPO Uncertainty

NuanWa Technology's path to listing has not been smooth:

| Timeline | Event |
|---------|------|
| September 2025 | First prospectus filed with HKEX |
| November 2025 | CSRC requested supplementary explanation of the compliance of the ownership structure |
| March 2026 | First filing lapsed (hearing not passed within six months) |
| April 13, 2026 | Second filing, updated prospectus |
| May 2026 | Hearing still not passed |

Source: HKEX announcements, Zhitong Finance

Two filings, CSRC follow-up questions, and a hearing not yet passed — these signals suggest **NuanWa's listing is far from a foregone conclusion**.

**Core obstacles**:
1. ZhongAn Online's "four-in-one" role (shareholder + customer + channel + incubator) creates extremely high related-party-transaction complexity
2. The CSRC's questions about the compliance of the red-chip structure
3. Aibang Insurance's compliance risk could draw HKEX scrutiny
4. Insurance-technology companies have performed poorly in the Hong Kong stock market, casting doubt on investor subscription appetite

**Consequences if the listing fails**:
- The company has raised approximately **USD 59.2 million** (about RMB 400 million) in cumulative funding, while cumulative losses over three years have exceeded RMB 660 million — meaning the funds raised have already been largely consumed by losses
- Without a listing, the company cannot access new capital, yet adjusted net profit is only RMB 60 million a year — barely enough to sustain operations, with no room left over to invest in growth
- Investors (Sequoia, Longhu, etc.) would have no prospect of an exit and might pressure management to make changes

## Risk Four: The Insurance-Tech Sector's "Capital-Market Curse"

NuanWa is not the first insurance-technology company to make a run at the Hong Kong stock market. Look at how its predecessors have fared:

| Company | Listing/Filing Time | Current Status |
|------|-------------|------|
| **Sipai Health** | Listed on HKEX in 2022 | Market cap has shrunk to about **HKD 4 billion**, down more than 90% from its peak |
| **Yuanxin Technology** | Filed multiple times | IPO on hold, cumulative losses of approximately **RMB 2.5 billion** |
| **Waterdrop Inc.** | Listed on Nasdaq in 2021 | Market cap has shrunk from about USD 6 billion at listing to roughly **USD 500 million** |
| **Huize Holding** | Listed on Nasdaq in 2020 | Market cap has remained depressed for an extended period |

Source: Public data from the respective exchanges

**Insurance-technology companies' performance in the capital markets has been almost a "total wipeout."** The reason is that these companies generally face the same predicament: the sector sounds large, but any single company's revenue ceiling is limited, profitability is weak, and growth is unstable. The capital markets ultimately vote with real money.

**Counterargument**: Insurance-technology companies' poor performance in recent years is partly attributable to the macro environment (the crash in Chinese ADRs, weakness in the Hong Kong market), and cannot be entirely blamed on the business model. If insurance-AI penetration rises from 7% to over 30%, the valuations of industry leaders could be re-rated.

## Risk Five: The Possibility That Gross Margin Keeps Declining

| Year | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| Overall gross margin | 57.7% | 58.3% | 49.8% | **47.2%** |

Source: NuanWa Technology prospectus

If gross margin continues to decline at a rate of 2-3 percentage points a year:
- Gross margin could fall to **44-45%** in 2026
- Adjusted net profit could revert from a profit back to a loss
- The company would fall into the trap of "revenue growth without profit growth"

**Under what circumstances could gross margin stabilize or rebound?**
- The share of Jiangsu Daotai's outbound-call business declines, or its gross margin improves
- AI tools improve service efficiency and reduce labor costs
- The share of higher-margin claims/anti-fraud business increases
- Raising prices — though this is very difficult in an environment of intensifying competition

## The Bull Case: Why NuanWa Might Be Worth Watching

Listing all the positive factors together:

1. **High sector certainty**. Insurance + AI is a long-term, near-certain trend; China's insurance industry's technology spending is growing 14.6% a year, and with penetration at only 7%, the room for growth is enormous.
2. **Revenue has already crossed the RMB 1 billion threshold**. It ranks among the leaders in the insurance-technology sector, with broad customer coverage.
3. **Adjusted net profit has been positive for three consecutive years** (RMB 19 million -> RMB 58 million -> RMB 61 million), a favorable profitability trend.
4. **High-quality customer base**. It covers nine of the top ten insurers, with a policy renewal rate of 98.4% and a cross-selling rate of 65%.
5. **Management has extensive industry experience**. Lu Min has twenty years of experience in insurance technology, and the team grew out of ZhongAn Insurance.
6. **Backing from top-tier capital**. Sequoia China, Longhu Capital, JPMorgan (sponsor), and HSBC (sponsor).
7. **De-dependence is progressing**. Non-ZhongAn revenue has grown from RMB 75 million to RMB 581 million, nearly a sevenfold increase over three years.
8. **Overseas expansion is beginning to take shape**. Initial progress has been made in the Hong Kong and Middle East markets.

---

## The Bear Case: Why NuanWa Might Not Be Worth Watching

Listing all the negative factors together:

1. **Dependence on ZhongAn is not fundamentally resolved**. 43% of revenue comes from the major shareholder, and the fairness of related-party transactions is in question.
2. **Gross margin has declined for three consecutive years** (58% -> 47%), with profitability continuing to deteriorate.
3. **Customer retention rate has fallen** (77.7% -> 69.6%), and net revenue retention has dropped below 100%.
4. **Cumulative net loss of RMB 660 million over three years**, with an adjusted net profit margin of just 5.9% — extremely thin.
5. **Growth has slowed sharply** (44% -> 8.5%), underperforming a sector growing at 36% a year.
6. **Aibang Insurance's compliance problems**, reported by a media outlet under Xinhua News Agency, pose an obstacle to the IPO.
7. **Insurance-technology predecessors have all been wiped out** — Sipai Health down more than 90%, Yuanxin Technology's IPO on hold, and Waterdrop's market cap down 90%.
8. **Ownership structure is balanced to the point of danger**. Lu Min and ZhongAn each hold 31.65%, a potential governance deadlock.
9. **Its technology moat may be eroding**. General-purpose large models plus in-house capability-building by large insurers.
10. **Two prospectus filings, still no passed hearing** — listing uncertainty remains high.

## What Would Need to Be Seen to Justify Watching NuanWa?

Rather than concluding "absolutely stay away," here is a **checklist of conditions that need to be met**. If the following conditions are satisfied one by one, NuanWa Technology's investment value would improve markedly:

| Condition | Current Status | Target Threshold |
|------|---------|---------|
| Successfully pass the HKEX hearing and list | Second filing in progress, not yet passed | Complete the listing |
| ZhongAn's share of revenue | 43.3% | Fall below **30%** |
| Gross margin | 47.2%, declining year over year | Stabilize at **48%+** or rebound |
| Adjusted net profit margin | 5.9% | Reach **10%+** |
| Customer retention rate | 69.6%, declining | Rebound to **75%+** |
| Net revenue retention | 96.5%, below 100% | Rebound to **110%+** |
| Aibang Insurance compliance issues | Complaints ongoing, reported by media | Complaint volume drops significantly, no regulatory penalties |
| Revenue growth rate | 8.5% | Recover to **20%+** |

**At present, not a single one of these conditions has been met.** That is why the overall rating is ★★☆, not higher.

## Three Scenarios

### Scenario One: Optimistic (Lower Probability)

- Successfully lists in 2026 and obtains development funding
- Dependence on ZhongAn continues to fall below 30%
- Large AI models empower product upgrades and gross margin rebounds
- Customer retention improves and net revenue retention returns to 120%+
- Adjusted net profit margin reaches 10%+ within three years
- Breakthroughs are achieved in overseas markets

**Outcome**: Becomes a genuine leader in the insurance-AI sector, with upside room in market value.

### Scenario Two: Neutral (Highest Probability)

- Lists, but at a modest valuation
- Dependence on ZhongAn declines slowly but always remains at 35%+
- Gross margin oscillates in the 45-48% range
- Revenue growth of 10-15%, lukewarm
- Remains marginally profitable but cannot break through to scaled profitability

**Outcome**: Becomes yet another insurance-technology company that "went public but nobody cares," with a persistently depressed share price.

### Scenario Three: Pessimistic (Medium Probability)

- The listing fails again, or proceeds at a severely reduced valuation
- A change in the ZhongAn relationship causes revenue to decline
- Gross margin continues falling below 43%
- Aibang Insurance is hit with regulatory penalties
- Disruption from general-purpose large models accelerates customer attrition

**Outcome**: Follows in the footsteps of Sipai Health and Waterdrop Inc., or is reduced to just a business unit of ZhongAn Online.

## Finale Summary: A Company of Contradictions, an Ambiguous Answer

Returning to the question posed in Part 1: what kind of company is NuanWa Technology, really?

After four installments, the answer remains **contradictory**:

- It sits in a sector with high certainty (insurance + AI), yet **its own certainty is low**
- It has genuine technical capability and a customer base, yet **the independence of its business model is unproven**
- Its adjusted net profit has only just turned positive, yet **both gross margin and retention rates are deteriorating**
- It calls itself the "largest independent AI technology company," yet **more than 40% of its revenue comes from its own major shareholder**
- It has secured backing from top-tier sponsors and VCs, yet **two filings still have not passed the hearing**

**Final judgment**: NuanWa Technology is an insurance-AI company with real technical capability that has not yet proven independent commercial value. "ZhongAn dependency syndrome" is its greatest structural problem, and the decline in gross margin along with the deterioration in customer retention are near-term warning signs. Until these problems are resolved, it is better suited to **continued observation** than to **immediate investment**.

If you are bullish on the insurance-AI sector, the safest approach is not to invest in NuanWa now, but to **wait until after it lists, until it has delivered 2-3 quarters of independent financial reports, and until it has proven that both de-dependence and profitability are on the right track — and only then make a judgment**.

A good company can afford to wait. A company that isn't good enough may not become good even after waiting.
