# Competition and Commercialization — The Real Question Behind RMB 1.024 Billion in Revenue

> "Understanding Nuanwa Technology" series · Article 03
> Reading time: about 12 minutes

## How big is the insurance-AI market, really?

RMB 1 billion in revenue isn't a big-company number in most industries. But in the insurtech track, it already counts as top-tier scale — most companies on this track are still burning cash to chase scale, and few have gotten close to break-even. But "top-tier" doesn't mean "winning." Before dissecting the quality of Nuanwa's RMB 1 billion, there's a prior question to answer: **how big is this track, really?**

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|------|------|------|
| China's insurance-AI market size in 2024 | **RMB 11 billion** | Gelonghui, citing industry reports |
| Projected 2030 global insurance-AI market size | **RMB 216.1 billion** | Industry forecast |
| Global AI-in-insurance market CAGR (2024-2029) | **36.6%** | Boston Consulting Group |
| China's total 2025 insurance-industry tech spend | **RMB 67 billion+** | iResearch |
| Growth rate of insurance-industry tech spend | **14.6%** | iResearch |
| Share of insurers that have achieved AI deployment at scale | **just 7%** | Boston Consulting Group |

Source: various public reports from the cited institutions

A few key judgments:

1. **The track is real.** China's insurance industry spends more than RMB 67 billion a year on technology, growing at 14.6% annually — this isn't hype.
2. **Penetration is extremely low.** Only 7% of insurers have achieved AI deployment at scale, meaning **93% are still at the pilot or wait-and-see stage.** That implies enormous incremental headroom.
3. **But how much of that can Nuanwa actually capture?** Of the RMB 67 billion in tech spend, only a limited share goes to third-party AI vendors — most of it is absorbed by large insurers' own technology subsidiaries or traditional IT service providers (such as Sinosoft and Hundsun Technologies). Nuanwa's 2025 revenue of RMB 1.024 billion represents about **9%** of the RMB 11 billion insurance-AI market, but only about **1.5%** of the RMB 67 billion total tech-spend figure.

**Counter-argument**: a small market share is exactly what makes the growth runway large. The insurance-AI market is growing at 36.6% a year — if Nuanwa can hold or expand its share, revenue could go far beyond RMB 1 billion.

**Rebuttal**: the problem is that Nuanwa's own 2025 revenue growth has collapsed from 44% to **8.5%**. Growing only 8.5% in a track that's expanding 36% a year means **Nuanwa is losing market share.**

## Competitive landscape: who is Nuanwa fighting for market share with?

Players in the insurtech track fall roughly into four categories:

### Category one: independent insurtech companies (Nuanwa's direct competitors)

| Company | Core positioning | 2024 revenue | Status | Difference from Nuanwa |
|------|---------|------------|------|-------------|
| **Nuanwa Technology** | Full-stack AI underwriting + claims | RMB 944 million | Second HKEX filing | Full-stack coverage, deeply tied to ZhongAn |
| **MediTrust Health** | Innovative payment + pharma insurance | **RMB 2.035 billion** | Preparing to list | Revenue is 2x Nuanwa's; dual-engine "smart pharma + smart insurance" |
| **Sipai Health** | Healthcare + insurance | Undisclosed | Already listed in Hong Kong | Market cap has shrunk severely, to about **HKD 4 billion** |
| **Yuanxin Technology** | Pharma + insurance | Undisclosed | Listing on hold | Cumulative losses of about **RMB 2.5 billion** |
| **Insgeek** | Corporate group-insurance SaaS | Undisclosed | Not yet listed | Focused on corporate customers, a different track |

Source: each company's prospectus/public reporting, 36Kr

**Key findings**:

1. **MediTrust Health's revenue is 2x Nuanwa's.** This means Nuanwa is not the absolute leader of the insurtech track — at least not by revenue scale. Nuanwa's title of "China's largest independent AI technology company in insurance" hinges on the word "AI" — it's a narrower, more specific definition.
2. **Sipai Health is a cautionary tale.** Sipai Health listed in Hong Kong in 2022, and its market cap has kept shrinking since, down to about HKD 4 billion. Insurtech companies have broadly performed poorly in the capital markets.
3. **Yuanxin Technology has racked up RMB 2.5 billion in cumulative losses and has put its listing on hold** — showing just how hard it is to commercialize in this track; very few companies have found a working path to profitability.

### Category two: technology subsidiaries of large insurers

| Company | Parent | Capability |
|------|--------|------|
| Ping An Technology | Ping An Insurance | AI + big data + cloud computing; the strongest full-stack capability |
| CPIC Technology | China Pacific Insurance | Digital transformation, building its own core systems |
| PICC Technology | China's PICC | Gradually building AI capability |

These companies **are not Nuanwa's customers — they are potential replacements.** Their financial resources, data scale, and talent reserves far exceed Nuanwa's. The fact that Nuanwa can currently serve them may simply reflect that "buying is faster than building" for now — but over the long run, large insurers building their own AI capability is a near-certain trend.

### Category three: traditional insurance IT vendors

Sinosoft, Hundsun Technologies, and other core-system suppliers for insurers already hold the entry point into insurers' core systems. If they layer AI capability on top of their existing products, Nuanwa's competitive space gets squeezed further.

### Category four: general-purpose AI companies

Baidu AI Cloud, Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud are all pushing hard to promote AI solutions in the insurance industry. Their industry knowledge isn't as deep as Nuanwa's, but their compute power, model capability, and brand trust far exceed Nuanwa's.

## How is that RMB 1.024 billion in revenue actually earned?

Nuanwa's 2025 revenue was **RMB 1.024 billion**. Let's break down its structure:

### Revenue structure

| Business line | Share (estimate) | Gross-margin trend | Core drivers |
|--------|------------|-----------|---------|
| AI Underwriting Solutions | ~**75%** | Fell from 69% to ~53% | Product design, precision marketing, intelligent underwriting |
| AI Claims Solutions | ~**25%** | Relatively stable, ~35-40% | Intelligent claims processing, anti-fraud |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's prospectus (estimated based on 2022-2024 proportions)

**Underwriting is the core revenue source, but its gross margin is falling fastest.** This is because of:

1. **The effect of the Jiangsu Daotai acquisition.** In October 2023, Nuanwa acquired Jiangsu Daotai, a company focused on AI outbound calling and SMS-based user operations. Outbound-call business requires paying substantial fees to telecom carriers, which directly pushes up costs and drags down gross margin.
2. **Intensifying price competition.** More entrants into the insurtech market has strengthened customers' bargaining power.
3. **Shifting customer mix.** To reduce its dependence on ZhongAn, Nuanwa has been actively expanding into small and mid-sized insurers — but these customers typically carry lower deal sizes and lower gross margins than large customers.

### What the gross-margin decline really means

| Year | Overall gross margin | AI underwriting gross margin |
|------|-----------|------------|
| 2022 | 57.7% | 69.1% |
| 2023 | 58.3% | 66.3% |
| 2024 | 49.8% | 53.3% |
| 2025 | **47.2%** | ~53-56% (estimate) |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's prospectus, Sina Finance

A decline of more than **11 percentage points** over three years.

**A simple piece of arithmetic**: if Nuanwa's 2025 gross margin had held at the 2023 level of 58%, gross profit would have been RMB 594 million; but at the actual gross margin of 47.2%, gross profit was only about RMB 483 million — a shortfall of **RMB 110 million**. For a company whose annual adjusted net profit is only RMB 60 million, that RMB 110 million gap means **every additional point of gross-margin decline can eat up a large chunk of net profit.**

**Counter-argument**: the gross-margin decline is mainly a change in business mix caused by the Jiangsu Daotai acquisition, not a decline in the core business's competitiveness. Strip out the telecom costs, and the core AI business's gross margin remains healthy. And the Daotai acquisition brought user-operations capability that expanded the scope of services.

**Rebuttal**: whatever the cause, the result is that the company's overall earning power is weakening. And the acquisition itself is questionable to begin with — why acquire an outbound-calling business that drags down gross margin? Is this "watering down" revenue to look better ahead of the IPO?

## ZhongAn dependency: "poison" or "nourishment"?

This is the single most central question for understanding Nuanwa Technology's business model.

### The ZhongAn-Nuanwa relationship map

```
ZhongAn Online
├── Holds 31.65% of Nuanwa Technology (joint largest shareholder)
├── Is Nuanwa's largest customer (contributing 43.3% of revenue)
├── Is Nuanwa's business partner (insurance-product distribution channel)
└── Is Nuanwa's incubator (spun out of ZhongAn's health-business unit in 2018)
```

A single entity simultaneously playing **shareholder + customer + channel + incubator**. Among related-party transactions at listed companies, this "four-in-one" relationship is among the most complex and the most likely to draw scrutiny.

### Progress on de-dependence

| Year | ZhongAn revenue (RMB million) | Share | Non-ZhongAn revenue (RMB million) | Non-ZhongAn customer count |
|------|----------------|------|-------------------|------------|
| 2022 | 270 | 78.7% | 75 | ~40+ |
| 2023 | 400 | 61.8% | 255 | ~90+ |
| 2024 | 427 | 45.2% | 517 | ~140+ |
| 2025 | ~443 | 43.3% | ~581 | ~200+ |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's prospectus

**Non-ZhongAn revenue grew from RMB 75 million in 2022 to about RMB 581 million in 2025 — an increase of nearly sevenfold.** That is a genuine achievement in de-dependence.

But the problem is:

1. **ZhongAn's absolute contribution is still growing.** It rose from RMB 270 million to RMB 443 million — meaning it isn't that ZhongAn is "letting go," but that Nuanwa is "diluting" the ratio.
2. **A 43% dependency rate is still extremely high.** HKEX's usual "comfort line" for listed companies is that a single large customer should not exceed 30% of revenue. Nuanwa still has some distance to travel to get there.
3. **What is the "cost" of de-dependence?** Is the gross margin on non-ZhongAn customers lower than on ZhongAn customers? If Nuanwa is winning customers at low prices just to dilute ZhongAn's share, that kind of de-dependence is "toxic."

### Is the related-party pricing fair?

The market's biggest concern is: is the pricing on the RMB 443 million in revenue ZhongAn pays Nuanwa **actually fair market pricing?**

If ZhongAn procures Nuanwa's services at market rates, this is a normal commercial transaction. But if there is "above-market related-party procurement" — then Nuanwa's real revenue would be effectively "inflated."

Disclosure on this point in the prospectus is limited — this is a risk point investors need to watch closely.

**Counter-argument**: Nuanwa's sponsors are J.P. Morgan and HSBC, and HKEX scrutinizes related-party transactions extremely closely. If the pricing weren't fair, the sponsors would not sign off. And ZhongAn itself is a listed company (HKEX: 06060), so its related-party transactions are subject to the same compliance disclosure requirements.

## Growth logic: what will keep driving growth?

Nuanwa's 2025 revenue growth collapsed from 44% to **8.5%**. What can it still grow on?

### Growth engine one: expanding insurer-customer coverage

Currently covering 115 insurers — China has more than **230** insurance companies (life, property and casualty, reinsurance, etc. combined). Penetration is about **50%**, so there's still room.

**But the problem is**: nine of the top ten insurers are already covered, so the remaining incremental customers are small and mid-sized insurers. These customers have weaker willingness and ability to pay, and lower per-customer revenue.

### Growth engine two: raising revenue per customer

A cross-selling rate of **65%**, far above the industry average of 15%-25%, shows Nuanwa is doing well at "mining" its existing customer base. But revenue retention has already dropped below 100%, meaning the pace of existing customers buying more is slowing.

### Growth engine three: overseas markets

Nuanwa's website mentions expansion into "Hong Kong and Middle East markets." 30% of IPO proceeds are earmarked for market expansion and product diversification.

**But overseas-market uncertainty is extremely high.** Insurance is a heavily regulated industry, and regulations, product structures, and competitive landscapes vary completely from market to market. Chinese insurtech companies currently have no successful precedent for expanding overseas.

### Growth engine four: product upgrades from AI large models

If Nuanwa can integrate large-model capability into its existing products, it could improve product strength and pricing power, potentially opening up new growth space.

**Overall judgment**: Nuanwa's growth engines are shifting from a "high-growth phase" to a "plateau phase." The easiest customers to win have already been won, and further growth will require higher customer-acquisition costs and longer conversion cycles. **The 8.5% growth rate may not be an isolated slowdown, but the start of a new normal.**

**Counter-argument**: the 8.5% growth rate reflects macroeconomic conditions and the insurance industry's cyclical mood, not a ceiling on Nuanwa's growth capability. Insurance-AI penetration is only 7%, so the long-term growth runway is far from exhausted.

## This article's core conclusions

| Dimension | Judgment |
|------|------|
| Market size | The insurance-AI track is real and growing fast, but the share Nuanwa can capture has a ceiling |
| Competitive landscape | Under pressure from all sides — peers, large insurers building in-house, traditional IT vendors, and general-purpose AI companies |
| ZhongAn dependency | De-dependence is progressing but far from complete; fairness of related-party pricing remains in question |
| Growth outlook | The high-growth phase is very likely over; the company has entered a plateau |
| Gross margin | Structural decline, unlikely to reverse in the near term |

**In one line**: Nuanwa Technology's RMB 1 billion-plus in revenue isn't "fake," but the quality of that growth is deteriorating — growth is slowing, gross margin is falling, customer retention is dropping, and ZhongAn dependency hasn't been fundamentally resolved. This isn't a machine that's "running ever faster" — it's a machine that "got up to speed but is starting to wheeze."
