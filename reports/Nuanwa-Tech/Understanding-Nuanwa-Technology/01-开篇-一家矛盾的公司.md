# Nuanwa Technology — A Company of Contradictions

> "Understanding Nuanwa Technology" series · Article 01 · Opener
> Reading time: about 8 minutes

---

## A set of numbers that will confuse you

On April 13, 2026, Nuanwa Insight Technology Co., Ltd. filed its second application to list on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, with J.P. Morgan and HSBC acting as joint sponsors. The first filing, submitted in September 2025, lapsed automatically after failing to pass the hearing within six months.

The company carries a lofty title: **China's largest independent AI technology company in the insurance industry** (source: Frost & Sullivan).

But open its prospectus and you'll find a set of numbers that don't quite add up:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|------|---------|---------|---------|------|
| Revenue (RMB million) | 655 | 944 | **1,024** | Continued growth |
| Revenue growth | +89.9% | +44.1% | **+8.5%** | Growth rate collapsing |
| Net loss (RMB million) | -240 | -155 | **-269** | Losses widening |
| Adjusted net profit (RMB million) | 19 | 58 | **61** | Positive for three straight years |
| Gross margin | 58.3% | 49.8% | **47.2%** | Declining year after year |
| Number of customers | 112 | 160 | **245** | Continuing to expand |
| ZhongAn's share of revenue | 61.8% | 45.2% | **43.3%** | Slowly declining |
| Customer retention rate | 74.2% | 77.7% | **69.6%** | Fell in 2025 |
| Revenue retention rate | 160.2% | 134.0% | **96.5%** | Dropped below 100% |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's updated April 2026 prospectus, 36Kr, Sina Finance

Put these numbers together and you get: **a company whose annual revenue has crossed RMB 1 billion, whose customer count has doubled to 245, and which covers nine of China's top ten insurers — yet has racked up cumulative net losses of over RMB 660 million across three years, has seen gross margin decline for three consecutive years, and derives more than 40% of its revenue from its own controlling shareholder.**

Even more contradictory: on a GAAP basis, the company lost a cumulative **RMB 660 million** over three years; but on an adjusted-net-profit basis, it earned a cumulative **RMB 138 million** over the same period.

If you think this is just "growing-pains of a growth stock" — you may be underestimating the structural problems.
If you think this smells like "financial fraud" — you may be misunderstanding the accounting treatment.

What this series sets out to do is **take apart each layer of contradiction and let you judge for yourself**.

## Three contradictions

### Contradiction one: net loss vs. adjusted net profit — is it making money or losing money?

This is the part most likely to confuse people.

For full-year 2025, Nuanwa Technology posted a net loss of **RMB 269 million**, up **74.2%** year over year — which looks alarming. But over the same period, adjusted net profit was **RMB 60.6 million** — which looks decent.

The gap between the two figures is **RMB 330 million**. What accounts for it?

Mostly the **fair-value loss on convertible redeemable preferred shares and convertible instruments**. In plain terms: in its earlier funding rounds, Nuanwa issued preferred shares that are classified as financial liabilities under accounting rules. As the company's valuation changes, the book value of this "liability" fluctuates, generating paper gains or losses.

**Key judgment**: this is not an operating loss but a "paper loss" produced by accounting treatment. Once the company goes public, the preferred shares convert into ordinary shares and this portion of the loss disappears automatically.

**Counter-argument**: even stripping out the preferred-share effect, the adjusted net margin is only **5.9%** (RMB 60.6 million / RMB 1.024 billion) — extremely thin. For a company with a 47% gross margin, a 6% net margin suggests the expense structure is still too heavy. And "adjusted net profit" is a non-standard metric the company defines itself; management has an incentive to make it look better than it is.

### Contradiction two: more and more customers, but unable to keep the old ones

Nuanwa Technology's customer count grew from 112 in 2023 to **245** in 2025 — more than doubling in three years. The number of insurers it covers grew from 62 to **115**, including nine of China's top ten insurance companies.

That sounds good. But two metrics buried in the prospectus expose the problem:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Customer retention rate | 74.2% | 77.7% | **69.6%** |
| Revenue retention rate | 160.2% | 134.0% | **96.5%** |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's prospectus, Sina Finance

**The customer retention rate fell from 77.7% to 69.6%** — meaning nearly **30% of existing customers churned** in 2025.

More seriously, **the revenue retention rate dropped below 100%** — 96.5% means the renewal spending of existing customers actually **shrank**. New-customer revenue can no longer fully offset the loss of revenue from departing customers.

**Key judgment**: for a healthy SaaS/tech-services business, revenue retention should stay above **120%** (existing customers not only renew but buy more). Nuanwa's 160% in 2023 was very healthy, but the ratio has slid in a straight line to 96.5% within two years — that is a **warning sign**.

**Counter-argument**: the insurtech industry is still at an early stage, and customer churn after a trial period is normal. Nuanwa's total customer count is still growing quickly and its coverage keeps expanding. As long as the customers who stick around are the large ones (nine of the top ten insurers are already covered), quality matters more than quantity.

### Contradiction three: calls itself "independent," yet can't do without ZhongAn

Nuanwa Technology was incubated by ZhongAn Insurance in 2018; founder Lu Min and ZhongAn Online each hold **31.65%** of the company. ZhongAn is simultaneously Nuanwa's largest shareholder, its largest customer, and a key business partner.

Over the past three years, ZhongAn Online's contribution to Nuanwa's revenue has been:

| Year | Revenue from ZhongAn (RMB million) | Share of total revenue |
|------|---------------------|------------|
| 2023 | 400 | **61.8%** |
| 2024 | 427 | **45.2%** |
| 2025 | ~443 | **43.3%** |

Source: Nuanwa Technology's prospectus

On the surface, ZhongAn's share has fallen from 62% to 43% — de-dependence appears to be progressing. But note: **the absolute revenue amount from ZhongAn is still growing** (from RMB 400 million to about RMB 443 million). The share is falling not because ZhongAn is paying less, but because revenue from non-ZhongAn customers is growing faster.

**Key judgment**: a company that calls itself "independent" derives **more than 40% of its revenue from its own controlling shareholder**. Is this market-based commercial cooperation, or "paper prosperity" propped up by related-party transactions? If ZhongAn decided tomorrow to build its own in-house team for these functions, what would happen to Nuanwa?

**Counter-argument**: ZhongAn and Nuanwa share a co-founding relationship; ZhongAn holds 31.65% and was deeply involved in incubating the company, so the two parties' interests are tightly aligned. ZhongAn has no incentive to "abandon" Nuanwa — building an in-house team is not necessarily cheaper than outsourcing to Nuanwa. And the fact that the absolute revenue ZhongAn contributes keeps growing suggests the partnership is deepening rather than shrinking.

## What exactly is Nuanwa Technology?

If you had to define Nuanwa Technology in one sentence:

> **Nuanwa Technology = an AI technology company covering the full underwriting-and-claims workflow for insurers, with annual revenue just over RMB 1 billion, adjusted net profit just turned positive, but deeply tied to ZhongAn Online, and with a customer retention rate that is now declining.**

## A small preview: Nuanwa Technology's "two faces"

Here's a hook to hold onto. The following numbers will be explained in detail in later articles — for now, just remember the scale:

**The bright face**:
- China's largest independent AI technology company in the insurance industry (Frost & Sullivan)
- Covers **9** of China's top ten insurers
- Has processed more than **221 million** insurance cases cumulatively
- Has facilitated cumulative first-year premiums of **RMB 13 billion**
- Policy renewal rate of **98.4%**
- Cross-selling rate of **65.0%** (industry average: 15%-25%)

**The dark face**:
- Cumulative net losses of more than **RMB 660 million** over three years
- Gross margin has declined more than **11 percentage points** over three years (58.3% -> 47.2%)
- **43%** of revenue comes from controlling shareholder ZhongAn Online
- Customer retention rate has fallen to **69.6%**
- Revenue retention rate has dropped below **100%**
- Its insurance brokerage subsidiary, Aibang Insurance, has repeatedly been the subject of consumer complaints and has appeared on the "315" consumer-rights exposure list

Which face is more real? Or are both real? That is exactly what the next three articles will answer.
