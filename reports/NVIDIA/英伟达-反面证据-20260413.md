## 1. Re-assessing the CUDA moat in the age of AI coding tools

### 1.1 The Claude Code "30-minute CUDA-to-ROCm port" event

**Fact**: In January 2026, developer johnnytshi publicly demonstrated porting a CUDA backend to AMD ROCm using Claude Code in roughly 30 minutes, without relying on an intermediate layer such as HIPIFY, achieving near-native performance. The case sparked widespread discussion on X, HardForum, and wccftech, and some commentators called it "a death-knell signal for the CUDA moat."
- Sources: [Techstrong.ai 2026-01](https://techstrong.ai/features/claude-code-ports-nvidia-cuda-to-amd-rocm-in-30-minutes/), [wccftech 2026-01](https://wccftech.com/the-claude-code-has-managed-to-port-nvidia-cuda-backend-to-rocm-in-just-30-minutes/), [GitHub gist by johnnytshi](https://gist.github.com/johnnytshi/33d3cec152faf46ff36e91cbf36fd28a)

**Counter-evidence / caveats**: commentators broadly noted that this case involved a simple kernel; AI agents still cannot handle complex, interconnected codebases or extreme hardware-level optimizations (cache hierarchy, warp scheduling). The gap mainly shows up at the data-layout level. This is evidence of a "lower barrier to entry," not a "vanished moat."

### 1.2 Progress on ROCm 7 / HIP 7 and translation tooling

- ROCm 7.0 / HIP 7.0 were released in the second half of 2025, with an explicit strategy of "aligning more closely with CUDA semantics" to simplify cross-vendor porting. Sources: [Phoronix 2025](https://www.phoronix.com/news/AMD-ROCm-7.0-HIP-Plans), [AMD's official ROCm blog](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/ecosystems-and-partners/transition-to-hip-7.0-blog/README.html)
- The academic CASS model achieved 95% accuracy at source-code-level translation and 37.5% at assembly level, surpassing traditional HIPIFY. Source: [OpenReview CASS paper](https://openreview.net/pdf/8c2f640c9dbbefef7c1bd23020ae87e08c0e8648.pdf)
- An independent April 2026 benchmark found CUDA leading ROCm by about 10-30% on compute-intensive workloads, a gap that has narrowed significantly (versus 2-3x in 2023). Source: [ThunderCompute 2026-04](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/rocm-vs-cuda-gpu-computing)

### 1.3 The Triton compiler and PyTorch's multi-backend push

- Triton 3.6.0 was released on 2026-01-20; the third Triton Developer Conference was held on 2025-10-21 at Microsoft's Silicon Valley campus. Version 3.5.0 introduced AMD HIP AOT compilation. Sources: [GitHub triton-lang](https://github.com/triton-lang/triton), [NVIDIA GTC 2025 Triton Blackwell session](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/on-demand/session/gtc25-s72876/)
- TorchInductor has become the mainstream compilation path; vLLM already uses torch.compile in production. Source: [vLLM Blog 2025-08](https://blog.vllm.ai/2025/08/20/torch-compile.html)
- TPUs do not go through the Triton/Inductor stack and instead rely on PyTorch/XLA. As of 2025: "whoever serves the Inductor-Triton pipeline best wins." Source: [State of PyTorch Hardware 2025](https://tunguz.github.io/PyTorch_Hardware_2025/)

### 1.4 Counter-argument: the moat around 100k-GPU clusters still holds

- With NCCL 2.29, AllGather/ReduceScatter become the bottleneck at 100k+ GPU training scale; Meta extended it with NCCLX, and fault detection plus recovery takes 3 minutes. Sources: [arXiv 2510.20171 Collective Communication for 100k+ GPUs](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20171v1), [Mycroft, SOSP25](https://geraldleizhang.com/publications/Mycroft_SOSP25.pdf)
- This means that training jobs at the 100,000-GPU scale remain heavily dependent on the CUDA/NVLink/InfiniBand ecosystem for communication-stack tuning; AI coding tools cannot yet crack system-level optimization in the short term.
- **Not found**: any publicly disclosed, first-hand case attributing a large-scale failure of a frontier LLM training job to "a non-CUDA-stack defect."

**Data confidence: High** (the Claude Code event, Triton version history, and the CASS paper are all primary sources; the communication-stack bottleneck is backed by academic papers)

**Updated judgment**: The code-layer CUDA moat visibly eroded on two fronts in 2026 — "AI-assisted porting" and "open-source compilers"; but the system-level moat (100k-GPU communication, HBM scheduling, NVLink fabric) has not yet been shaken. The porting barrier dropping from "an engineering team, several months" to "one person, a few hours" is a qualitative change, and the original report's assessment of the "CUDA flywheel" needs to be discounted accordingly.

Broadcom's AI revenue growing 5x in two years directly corresponds to a market shift of ASICs displacing GPUs.

## 3. Precise data on the training-vs-inference market divergence

### 3.1 The inflection point where inference overtakes training has arrived

- Deloitte's 2026 forecast: **inference will account for about two-thirds of total AI compute in 2026** (versus one-third in 2023 and one-half in 2025). Source: [Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2026/compute-power-ai.html)
- MarketsandMarkets: the AI inference market will grow from $106.2 billion in 2025 to $255 billion in 2030, a 19.2% CAGR.
- Gartner: 55% of AI-IaaS spend in 2026 will go to inference, rising to ≥65% by 2029.
- The inference-optimized chip market will exceed $50 billion in 2026.
- Sources: [CES 2026, Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4114579/ces-2026-ai-compute-sees-a-shift-from-training-to-inference), [SDxCentral 2026](https://www.sdxcentral.com/analysis/ai-inferencing-will-define-2026-and-the-markets-wide-open/)

### 3.2 The real cost-effectiveness of inference-substitute chips

- **Groq**: $0.64 per million tokens for Llama 3.1 70B, at >240 tokens/s.
- **Cerebras**: $0.60 per million tokens for Llama 3.1 70B, at 450 tokens/s/user at 16-bit precision.
- **DeepInfra**: $0.03-0.05 per million tokens for Llama 3.1 8B (a price floor).
- Sources: [GoPenAI Token Arbitrage Benchmark 2025](https://blog.gopenai.com/the-token-arbitrage-groq-vs-deepinfra-vs-cerebras-vs-fireworks-vs-hyperbolic-2025-benchmark-ccd3c2720cc8), [IntuitionLabs, Cerebras vs SambaNova vs Groq](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/cerebras-vs-sambanova-vs-groq-ai-chips)
- The inference market has shifted from "GPU monopoly" to "routing by use case": low latency → Groq/Cerebras; batch → DeepInfra; agents → Fireworks.

### 3.3 NVDA's inference product positioning

- Blackwell Ultra (B300/GB300) shipped in H2 2025; Rubin CPX is an inference-optimized "disaggregated architecture" arriving H2 2026. **NVDA has strategically acknowledged the training/inference market split**, but Maia 200/MTIA/Inferentia2/Trainium2/TPU together with Groq/Cerebras are squeezing NVDA's inference share from below.

**Data confidence: High**

**Updated judgment**: The base report's view that "inference is taking the baton from training, and total compute is growing" is correct; but the implicit assumption that "NVDA remains the king of inference" needs to be softened. **The inference market is an open battlefield, not an extension of NVDA's territory.**

### 4.4 The Cisco analogy

- Cisco's market cap exceeded $500 billion in 2000-03 (briefly surpassing Microsoft as the world's largest company); its stock fell from $80 to $9.50 (-88%), and it took 25 years and 8 months to reclaim its prior high. Sources: [CNBC 2025-12-10](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/10/ciscos-stock-closes-at-record-for-first-time-since-dot-com-peak-2000.html), [Harding Loevner, "NVIDIA and the Cautionary Tale of Cisco Systems"](https://www.hardingloevner.com/insights/nvidia-and-the-cautionary-tale-of-cisco-systems/)

### 4.5 The paying-customer side (demand-side validation)

- Anthropic's ARR: $5 billion in August 2025 → $9 billion at year-end 2025 → **$30 billion** in March 2026 (+1400% YoY), with Claude Code alone contributing $2.5 billion+. Business customers number over 300,000, and the count of customers spending over $100k/year has grown 7x in a year. Sources: [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/anthropic-just-hit-14-billion-in-arr-up-from-1-billion-just-14-months-ago/), [The AI Corner](https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/anthropic-30b-arr-passed-openai-revenue-2026)
- Google Gemini had 750 million MAU in Q4 2025 (disclosed by Pichai).
- **Real paying-customer demand is genuinely booming** — this is a bullish argument that rebuts the "pure bubble" narrative.

**Data confidence: High**

**Updated judgment**: The base report flagged the AI capex cycle as its "most underrated risk" — this judgment **has been reinforced**. Annual spending of $700 billion, plus circular deals, plus Microsoft's free cash flow at -28%, all coexisting, make **the first half of 2027 the key observation window**. But the demand side (Anthropic's $30 billion ARR) shows this is not a perfect match for Cisco 2000 — today there is real, realized paying revenue.

## 5. The algorithmic-efficiency revolution's dent in GPU demand

### 5.1 The DeepSeek event

- DeepSeek V3 training: 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours, with base-model cost of about $5.6 million; R1's post-training GPU cost was about $294,000. By comparison, GPT-4 is estimated at over $100 million and Gemini Ultra at $191 million. Sources: [Stratechery DeepSeek FAQ](https://stratechery.com/2025/deepseek-faq/), [Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-v3-and-the-actual-cost-of)
- Benchmarks: R1 scored 90.8% on MMLU versus GPT-4's 87.2%; 79.8% on AIME 2024 versus 9.3%.
- **Important caveat**: DeepSeek itself explicitly stated in its paper that this cost figure "does not include prior research, or architecture/algorithm/data ablation experiments." Bernstein publicly questioned the credibility of the $5.6 million figure.

### 5.2 NVDA's stock-price reaction

- On 2025-01-27, NVDA fell **17% in a single day, wiping out about $589 billion in market cap** — **the largest single-day market-cap loss in U.S. stock market history** (the previous record was Meta's $240 billion). Sources: [Bloomberg 2025-01-27](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-27/asml-sinks-as-china-ai-startup-triggers-panic-in-tech-stocks), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-stock-plummets-loses-record-589-billion-as-deepseek-prompts-questions-over-ai-spending-135105824.html)
- Analysts split in their reactions: Bernstein maintained a $175 price target; Raymond James saw it as heightening hyperscalers' sense of urgency rather than reversing it.
- NVDA fell -11% for the full month of January.

### 5.3 Jensen Huang's and Sam Altman's rebuttals

- Jensen Huang at CES 2025: **"practically the entire world got AI scaling wrong"** — arguing for three layers of scaling (pre-training, post-training, test-time), each requiring enormous compute. Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/snippet/2982546/jensen-huang-says-that-practically-the-entire-world-got-ai-scaling-wrong/), [CDOTrends](https://www.cdotrends.com/story/4376/test-time-scaling-new-frontier-ai)
- Altman said publicly: "Everything starts with compute" (in an NVIDIA partnership announcement). Source: [NVIDIA Newsroom, 2025-09](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/openai-and-nvidia-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-10gw-of-nvidia-systems)
- **Jevons paradox support**: in the five months following DeepSeek, Anthropic's ARR tripled and the hyperscalers' 2026 capex rose +67% — market behavior suggests efficiency gains genuinely generate more demand rather than less.

**Data confidence: High**

**Updated judgment**: The DeepSeek event proved the market is extremely sensitive to "compute-efficiency improvement" news (a single-day -17% move); but the demand-side boom over the following 12 months rebutted the Jevons-paradox concern. **The risk is not "algorithmic efficiency itself" but "the market's expectations instantly re-rating whenever an efficiency-shock event hits"** — the base report should add "narrative risk" as its own distinct risk category.

## 7. Composite judgment table

### 7.1 NVDA's training-side dominance

- **Current state**: >90% share, no substantive substitute
- **Estimated duration**: training-side dominance **stays above 80% through 2026-2028**; the probability of accelerating erosion rises materially starting 2029
- **3 key inflection triggers**:
  1. **Anthropic's Ironwood/TPU training ramp** (400,000+ units starting 2027) — if performance-per-dollar shows a 20%+ advantage over NVDA's Rubin → a second or third frontier lab follows suit
  2. **AI coding agents cracking 100k-GPU-scale NCCL/collective-communication tuning** (currently still the core of the CUDA/NVLink moat)
  3. **MoE plus low-precision training maturing to where FP4/FP6 become mainstream**, letting ASICs (e.g., Trainium3, TPU v8) reach TCO comparable to GPUs on training-specific workloads

### 7.2 NVDA's inference-side share

- **Current state**: estimated 60-70% share (precise figure is data-missing)
- **Estimated duration**: **inference-side share will keep eroding starting in 2026, potentially falling to 40-50% by 2028**
- **3 key inflection triggers**:
  1. **Maia 200 / Trainium2 / MTIA 450** reaching large-scale deployment in 2026-2027, rapidly reducing GPUs' share of hyperscalers' internal workloads
  2. **Specialized inference chips like Groq/Cerebras** locking up the "price floor" in low-latency, high-throughput niches
  3. **OpenAI's in-house chip** (the Broadcom 10GW agreement, starting 2027), if used for its own inference workloads → directly cuts NVDA's largest single end-customer demand

### 7.3 NVDA's total demand

- **Current state**: FY26 revenue $216 billion, of which data center is $193.7 billion
- **Estimated duration**: **total demand will still exceed consensus growth through 2026-2027; 2028 is the key inflection point**
- **3 key peaking triggers**:
