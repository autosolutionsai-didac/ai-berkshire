## A Structural Shift Underway

You probably bought NVIDIA because of the "explosion in AI compute demand." But "AI compute" is not a single thing — **it is splitting into two battlefields with completely different rules, and NVIDIA's position in each is diametrically opposed.**

| Dimension | Training | Inference |
|------|------|------|
| What it does | Teaches an AI model to "learn" a capability | Puts a trained model "to work" answering questions |
| Core metric | Time per training run + reliability | Tokens produced per dollar, how low the latency is |
| Customer type | ~30 frontier labs worldwide + sovereign AI | Thousands of SaaS companies and enterprises |
| Price sensitivity | **Low** (1% performance is worth hundreds of millions of dollars) | **Extremely high** (token cost is calculated directly) |
| Interconnect needs | 10,000-GPU-scale NVLink + InfiniBand | Single GPU to 8 GPUs; small clusters suffice |
| Precision needs | FP16 / FP8 / BF16 | INT8 / INT4 / FP4 (the lower, the cheaper) |

**In short: training buys "ultimate performance"; inference buys "cost-effectiveness."**

And the inference market is rapidly overtaking the training market:

| Year | Estimated total AI accelerator market | Inference share | Inference/training ratio |
|------|---------------------------------------|------------------|---------------------------|
| 2023 | ~$100 billion | 33% | 0.5x |
| 2025 | ~$210 billion | 50% | 1x |
| **2026** | ~$250-280 billion | **67%** | **2x** |
| 2030 (est.) | ~$600-1,000 billion | 65-80% | 2-4x |

> Data sources: Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026, MarketsandMarkets, Gartner

**2026 is the inflection point** — inference compute first crosses two-thirds of total AI compute. This means the center of gravity of the AI chip market is shifting from the area where NVIDIA is strongest (training) toward the area where NVIDIA is relatively weakest (inference).

## The Training Battlefield: NVIDIA Is Still the Only Option — But Not Forever

### Why the Training Side Is Hard to Dislodge

Training frontier large models (GPT-5, Claude 4, Gemini 3 class) requires **tens of thousands of GPUs running continuously for months**. In this scenario, NVIDIA does two things no one else can:

**1. Full NVLink / NVSwitch interconnect**

NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 can fully interconnect 72 GPUs at **1.8 TB/s** of bandwidth — equivalent to a supercomputer. Competing products offer only 1/2 to 1/6 of NVIDIA's interconnect bandwidth.

When training large models, GPUs must constantly exchange data (all-reduce communication). The faster the interconnect, the more efficient the training. In 100,000-GPU-scale training clusters, the NCCL + NVLink + InfiniBand trio is NVIDIA's **hardest moat**.

**2. System-level reliability**

Training a single frontier model can cost tens of millions of dollars. If training crashes on day 80 due to a hardware or software fault, you have to start over — or at least roll back to the most recent checkpoint.

NVIDIA has accumulated years of experience in fault detection and automatic recovery for large-cluster training. A Meta academic paper shows that fault detection and recovery for 100,000-GPU-scale training requires response times on the order of **3 minutes**. **No publicly disclosed frontier-model training failure to date has been attributed to a "non-CUDA-stack defect."**

### The Sole Exception: Google

**Google is the only company in the world that has proven it can train a frontier large model entirely without NVIDIA GPUs.**

Gemini 3 was trained entirely on TPUs — no NVIDIA GPUs were used. The TPU Ironwood (v7) cluster scales to 9,216 chips per pod, achieves full interconnect via a 3D torus topology, and delivers 42.5 ExaFLOPS of total cluster compute.

But outside Google — OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, xAI — remain deeply dependent on NVIDIA for training.

**Counterargument**: AWS has already deployed nearly 500,000 Trainium2 chips in Project Rainier, and Anthropic is using it to train models. While there is currently no public evidence that Trainium2 can train GPT-5-class frontier models, the gap is narrowing. Trainium3, arriving in 2027, could become the second alternative on the training side.

### Training Share Outlook

| Time | NVIDIA training share | Biggest challenger |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| 2026 | **>90%** | Google TPU |
| 2029 (est.) | **70-80%** | TPU + Trainium3 |
| 2031 (est.) | **55-65%** | Multiple ASICs |

## The Inference Battlefield: NVIDIA's Most Vulnerable Front

### Why the Inference Market Is Easier to Tear Open

The training market is "winner-take-all" — you must have the strongest chip and the largest cluster. The inference market is "a hundred flowers blooming" — different scenarios call for different solutions.

| Inference scenario | Core requirement | Best-fit solution |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Interactive conversation (ChatGPT) | Low latency (<100ms) | NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM or Groq LPU |
| Batch processing (document summarization) | High throughput, low cost | AMD MI355X, DeepInfra |
| Recommendation systems (Meta ad ranking) | Extreme scale, low power | Meta MTIA |
| Cloud API services | Flexible scheduling | AWS Trainium / Google TPU |
| Edge devices | Extremely low power | Dedicated ASICs |

**The inference market is inherently more fragmented and more price-sensitive.** NVIDIA's "one GPU rules them all" strategy faces greater challenges on the inference side.

### Three Forces Challenging Inference

#### Force 1: In-house chips at hyperscale customers

All four major cloud providers have in-house inference chips, already in the scaled-deployment stage:

| Customer | In-house chip | Inference role | Deployment scale (2026) |
|------|---------|---------|----------------|
| AWS | Trainium2 / Inferentia2 | General-purpose inference | **500,000-1,000,000 units** |
| Google | TPU Ironwood | Inference + training | **400,000-unit contract** (Anthropic) |
| Microsoft | Maia 200 | **Inference-only** (serves GPT-5.2) | Already deployed in Iowa |
| Meta | MTIA 300-500 | Recommendation-ranking inference | **Hundreds of thousands of units** |

> Data sources: official company announcements, Bloomberg, 2026-04-06

**Key detail**: Google's TPU is shifting from "internal use" to "external sale." In 2026, Anthropic signed a **$50 billion+** TPU procurement contract (purchased directly via Broadcom), and Meta is also leasing Google TPUs. This means the TPU has gone from an internal cost center to an externally sold commodity — **directly competing with NVIDIA for customers.**

#### Force 2: AMD's cost-effectiveness offensive

AMD's MI355X (second half of 2026) already has a **consistently better TCO (total cost of ownership)** than NVIDIA's equivalent product in large-batch inference scenarios:

- At batch size 64+ large-batch inference: **per-token cost is 35-45% lower than the B200**
- vLLM framework compatibility: CI pass rate rose from 37% in November 2025 to **93%** in January 2026
- The 288GB of large memory has a natural advantage in long-context inference scenarios

> Data source: TensorWave benchmarks (2026)

**AMD's weak spot**: low-latency inference (batch size 1-4) still lags NVIDIA by **20-30%**, and long-tail customers remain hard to reach (the ROCm ecosystem is still weak).

#### Force 3: Purpose-built inference chips

| Company | Product | Inference performance | Cost | Latest development |
|------|------|---------|------|---------|
| Groq | LPU | Llama 70B at **300 tokens/s** (10x H100) | $0.64/million tokens | **Acquired by NVIDIA for $20 billion in December 2025** |
| Cerebras | WSE-3 | Llama 405B at **>1,000 tokens/s** | $0.60/million tokens | **Won a $20 billion OpenAI order in April 2026** |

> Data sources: GoPenAI 2025 benchmarks, public reporting

**NVIDIA's acquisition of Groq is a defensive move** — it eliminates the biggest threat in the low-latency inference market while folding it into NVIDIA's own product line. But Cerebras landing a major OpenAI order shows that purpose-built inference chips have already "broken out" — no longer lab curiosities, but large-scale deployments backed by real money.

### An Overlooked Variable: Test-Time Compute

There is a variable in the inference market that favors NVIDIA — **test-time compute scaling**.

Take Claude's "thinking mode" and OpenAI's o1/o3 as examples: each inference call is no longer a simple "input → output" — the model performs substantial internal computation before answering. This increases the compute required per inference call by **10-100x**.

**If AI applications increasingly adopt "deep thinking" modes, demand for inference compute will be larger than the market currently expects** — this is the biggest growth driver for NVIDIA's inference business.

**Counterargument**: algorithmic efficiency is also improving rapidly. DeepSeek V3 trained a model approaching GPT-4-level performance for $5.6 million, at 1/20th the training cost of GPT-4. If a similar efficiency revolution occurs on the inference side, the assumption of "unlimited growth in compute demand" would break down.

## NVIDIA's Inference Product Line — How It's Responding

NVIDIA is not sitting still. Its inference product line is iterating rapidly:

| Product | Memory | Inference compute | Price (est.) | Launch |
|------|------|---------|-----------|---------|
| H200 | 141 GB HBM3e | — | $35-45K | Shipping |
| B200 | 192 GB HBM3e | FP4 18 PFLOPS | $35-45K | Shipping |
| GB200 NVL72 | 13.5 TB (system-level) | FP4 1.4 EFLOPS | $3-3.5 million/rack | Shipping |
| B300 / Blackwell Ultra | 288 GB | FP8 ~12 PFLOPS | $45-50K | Q4 2025 |
| Rubin (R100) | HBM4 | ~50 PFLOPS | TBD | H2 2026 |
| **Rubin Ultra NVL576** | **365 TB/rack** | **FP4 15 EFLOPS** | TBD | **H2 2027** |

**The key thing to watch**: Rubin Ultra NVL576 is a super-system with full interconnect across 576 GPUs, delivering **14x** the compute of the GB200 NVL72 (FP4 15 EFLOPS vs. 1.4 EFLOPS). If it ships on time and hits its performance targets, NVIDIA's inference moat could be extended by another three years.

**On the software side, simultaneously**: TensorRT-LLM's move to NIM containerization boosts the effective inference performance of the same GPU by **2-3x**. This is NVIDIA's strategy of "using software to make up for hardware" — even as hardware competitors close the gap, software optimization can keep TCO ahead.

## Inference Share Outlook: From 75% to 50%

Putting the above analysis together, the evolution path for NVIDIA's inference share looks roughly like this:

| Time | Inference share | Absolute inference revenue (est.) | Driving factors |
|------|---------|-------------------|---------|
| 2024 | ~90% | ~$30 billion | GPU exclusivity |
| 2026 | **65-75%** | **~$45 billion** | In-house ASICs begin scaled deployment |
| 2028 (est.) | **40-50%** | **~$60-80 billion** | Maia/MTIA/Trainium reach scale |
| 2030 (est.) | **45-55%** | **~$100-120 billion** | Market growth offsets share decline |

**Core judgment: a decline in share is a near-certainty, but absolute revenue keeps growing.** That's because the total size of the inference market is expanding rapidly — from roughly $30 billion in 2024 to potentially over $200 billion by 2030.

**The impact on valuation lies not in share but in gross margin.** The inference market is more price-sensitive, and NVIDIA will need to lean more on "volume" than "price" to grow. A decline in gross margin from 75% to 65-70% is a **high-probability event**.
