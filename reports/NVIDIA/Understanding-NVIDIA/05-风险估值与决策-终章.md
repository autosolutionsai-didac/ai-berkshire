## Putting the judgments from the first four parts together

Having read through the four preceding parts, NVIDIA's contradiction is already clear: **it is simultaneously the most profitable AI-infrastructure company in the world today, and a company whose moat is being systematically dug up by its wealthiest customers.** Both things are true at once.

| Dimension | Conclusion |
|------|------|
| Business essence | The de facto standard for AI compute, with crushing profitability (ROE 124%, gross margin 75%, FCF $96.7B) |
| CUDA moat | Deep but being eroded from the top. The code layer will weaken significantly within 2-3 years; the system layer remains solid for 5-10 years |
| Training | Safe for 2-3 years (>80% share), but Google TPU and Trainium3 are closing in |
| Inference | Being eroded by three forces at once; the drop in share from 75% to 50% is an irreversible trend |
| Competitive landscape | The top 4 customers account for 61% of revenue and are all building their own chips — the customer is the competitor |
| Pricing power | Still present but being compressed — gross margin has fallen from a peak of 78% to 71%, and is projected to stabilize at 65-70% in five years |

The question is not "will NVIDIA decline," but "at the current valuation, has the market already paid for every certainty, without discounting for the erosion that is currently underway."

## Risk One: The AI Capex Cycle — Historically Never Sustained Beyond 3 Years

This is NVIDIA's single biggest risk, and also **the one most easily overlooked**.

### 2026 capital expenditure of the four major cloud providers

| Company | 2026 CapEx (guidance) | YoY change |
|------|---------------------|---------|
| Amazon | ~$200 billion | +67% |
| Alphabet | $185 billion | +71% |
| Meta | $115-135 billion | +53-80% |
| Microsoft | ~$120 billion+ | +33%+ |
| **Total** | **$635-700 billion** | **+67-74%** |

> Source: CNBC 2026-02-06, company earnings guidance

Roughly 75% (~$450 billion) flows into AI infrastructure. **This is a direct source of NVIDIA's revenue.**

**But the historical pattern is**: hyperscale cloud providers' capex growth has never exceeded +50% YoY for more than 3 consecutive years.

| Year | Four major cloud providers' CapEx growth |
|------|---------------------------------------|
| 2024 | +40% |
| 2025 | +55% |
| 2026 (current) | +67-74% |
| 2027 (?) | **The year historically most likely to mark the inflection point** |

If 2027 CapEx growth falls from +67% to +10%, NVIDIA's revenue growth could fall from +50% to +15-20%, with PE compressing from 40x to 25-30x. **This is a classic "growth-plus-valuation" double whammy.**

**The counterargument**: There is real, paying demand behind AI. Anthropic's ARR jumped from $5 billion in August 2025 to **$30 billion** in March 2026 (a 6x increase in 7 months), and Google Gemini has 750 million monthly active users. This is entirely different from Cisco in 2000, where it was "pure infrastructure hype with no one actually paying."

**The counter to the counterargument**: The demand is real, but $700 billion/year in capex means cloud providers need to earn back $700 billion+ per year in AI-driven revenue to justify their return-on-investment expectations. Currently, the combined revenue of all AI companies falls far short of that scale. **Microsoft's FY2026 FCF is projected to decline 28% (Barclays estimate), and Amazon's FCF could turn negative.** These pressures will eventually feed through into capex decisions.

### Circular-deal risk

There is another variable that fundamental research has underweighted:

- NVIDIA has agreed to invest up to **$100 billion** in OpenAI
- OpenAI has committed to paying Oracle **$300 billion** over 5 years for compute resources (the Stargate project)
- OpenAI has committed to purchasing millions of NVIDIA GPUs
- NVIDIA has also invested in companies such as CoreWeave and xAI

**In 2026, Bloomberg produced an interactive diagram calling this the "AI Circular Deals" phenomenon** — NVIDIA invests in a customer, the customer uses the raised funds to buy NVIDIA GPUs, and that purchase is booked as NVIDIA revenue.

**NVIDIA has not separately disclosed what share of its FY2026-FY2027 incremental revenue comes from "self-financed" purchases.** This remains a data black box.

## Risk Two: The DeepSeek Event — A Rehearsal for Narrative Collapse

On January 27, 2025, the Chinese AI company DeepSeek released its V3 model — trained at a cost of about $5.6 million (1/20th of GPT-4's), with performance approaching GPT-4 level.

**NVIDIA plunged 17% that day, wiping out $589 billion in market cap — the largest single-day market-cap loss in U.S. stock market history.**

A month later, the market recovered. The reason was the Jevons Paradox at work — efficiency gains actually stimulated more demand (Anthropic's ARR tripled in 5 months, and the four major cloud providers' capex grew +67%).

**But the real lesson of the DeepSeek event isn't about "algorithmic efficiency" — it's about "narrative risk"**:

- The market is **extremely sensitive** to any event suggesting "GPU demand might be overestimated"
- For a company with a $5 trillion market cap, a single day's 17% drop triggered by one research paper equals $589 billion
- **The next trigger could be**: Anthropic's Ironwood performance data, a breakthrough in Huawei's CANN ecosystem, or hyperscale customer capex turning negative for the first time

**Implication for investors**: Even if your long-term judgment is correct (that AI demand keeps growing), **a short-term narrative collapse can wipe out 30-40% of market cap within days**. Holding NVIDIA requires strong psychological resilience.

## Risk Three: Jensen Huang — A Great CEO, But Also a Single Point of Failure

Jensen Huang's track record of strategic decisions ranks among the best in tech history:

| Time | Decision | Outcome |
|------|------|------|
| 2006 | Launched CUDA, betting on general-purpose computing over a 10-year horizon | Built the deepest moat of the AI era |
| 2014 | Pivoted the entire company to deep learning | Captured the first wave of AI |
| 2020 | Acquired Mellanox for $7 billion | Networking revenue grew more than 10x |
| 2024 | 10:1 stock split plus large-scale buybacks | Retail-investor-friendly and boosted EPS |
| 2025 | Acquired Groq for $20 billion | Eliminated the biggest threat in low-latency inference |

**But Jensen Huang is 63 and has explicitly declined to establish a succession plan.** NVIDIA operates with a flat, 60-person management structure that is highly dependent on Huang's personal decision-making.

A $5.4 trillion company with no succession plan is a single point of failure. Historical precedents where "a company grew mediocre after a great CEO retired" include GE (after Welch) and Apple (which lost its way for a time after Jobs's death in 2011).

## Valuation: What Kind of Future Does $5.4 Trillion Require

### Current valuation levels

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|------|------|------|
| Share price (May 2026) | **~$221** | — |
| Market cap | **~$5.4 trillion** | Largest in the world |
| Shares outstanding | ~24.3 billion | — |
| Q1 FY2027 EPS | **$2.39** | Annualized EPS ~$9.56 |
| PE (Q1 annualized) | **~23x** | Not expensive for 85% growth |
| PE (FY2026 full-year EPS $4.90) | **~45x** | On the expensive side |
| EV/Sales (FY2026) | **~25x** | Approaching Cisco's 2000 peak (31x) |
| FCF Yield | ~1.8% (based on FY2026) | On the low side |

> Source: NVIDIA Q1 FY2027 earnings, Yahoo Finance

**The valuation trick**: If you annualize Q1's explosive growth (+85%), PE is only 23x — it looks cheap. But if you use full-year FY2026 profit (which already reflects the slowdown), PE is 45x — it looks expensive.

**The key is the sustainability of growth**: how long can 85% growth be maintained? If FY2027 full-year growth falls to 40%, PE based on FY2027 could come back down to 16-18x — reasonable. But if growth falls to 20%, the market would compress the multiple from 45x to 25x — that would be a halving.

### Three-scenario valuation

| Scenario | Probability | FY2028 EPS (est.) | Reasonable PE | Target price | vs $221 |
|------|------|-----------------|--------|-------|---------|
| Bull (CUDA holds, wins in both training and inference) | 25% | $14+ | 30x | $420+ | +90% |
| Base (inference share falls to 50%, gross margin 66%) | 50% | $8-10 | 22x | $176-220 | -20% to 0% |
| Bear (capex peaks in 2027 plus accelerated in-house chip adoption) | 25% | $5-6 | 15x | $75-90 | -60% to -66% |

**Under the base case, the current $221 already prices in most of the growth.** The expected return over the next two years is roughly 0-5%/year — not enough to compensate for the risk.

### Reverse-DCF check

**What does the current share price of $221 imply?**

- FCF needs to grow from $96.7 billion to $300-350 billion over the next 10 years
- This requires AI compute demand to keep growing at its current pace, NVIDIA to maintain a combined share above 60%, and gross margin not to decline significantly
- **Possible, but not high-probability — it requires multiple assumptions to hold simultaneously**

## Duan Yongping's NVIDIA Trade: A Case Study

Duan Yongping's evolving stance on NVIDIA is an excellent case study in investment decision-making:

| Time | Stance | Action |
|------|------|------|
| Early 2023-2024 | "Don't understand it — tech companies are hard to understand" | Zero position |
| 2024-03 | "NVIDIA is very impressive, its products are highly differentiated" | Sold same-day-expiry calls (purely for fun) |
| 2024-04 | "Haven't yet figured out how long the monopoly can last, or how long demand can last" | Sold $860 puts (signaling interest) |
| 2025-02 | "Still haven't fully figured it out, but preparing to start selling NVDA puts" | Began participating via options |
| 2025-03 | "Bought NVDA at $92.5, deferred delivery by a year" | Sold deep puts, locking in a $92.5 purchase price |
| 2025-04 | Tariff-driven selloff, added to position | Bought Apple, NVIDIA, Google, TSMC |
| **2025 Q4** | **Went from "selling puts to stay engaged" to an actual 13F position** | **+1,111% (+6.64 million shares), 7.24 million shares, 7.7% of portfolio** |
| 2026-03 | "NVIDIA is an insurance company — 20% annualized and I'm happy" | Continues selling puts/covered calls to collect premium |

> Source: Duan Yongping's posts on Xueqiu (60 relevant records), H&H International 13F holdings data

**Key observations**:

1. **Duan Yongping went from "don't understand it" to "7.7% position" over 2 years — this was not an impulsive purchase, but a gradual process of validation**
2. **His anchors are always Apple (50%+) and Berkshire (20%) — NVIDIA's 7.7% represents "willing to take a medium position in something he doesn't fully understand"**
3. **His purchase prices were generally in the $92-130 range — significantly below the current $221**
4. **He manages risk using options (selling puts, covered calls) — the "NVIDIA insurance company" strategy**
5. **He has never taken NVIDIA above a 10% position — a sharp contrast with the high-conviction sizing of his "Apple 50%+" stake**

**A Duan Yongping-style question**:

> "Why does Duan Yongping dare to buy 7.7% but not 30%?"

The answer: **NVIDIA is a business he "can see clearly for the next 3-5 years but not for 10 years."** That happens to match a 7-10% position size — enough to share in the AI upside, but not enough to be badly hurt in a cyclical downturn.

> "If the price is right, of course I'd choose Apple — I understand it better, after all."

**— Duan Yongping's own words. When two good companies are on the table, he picks the one he understands more deeply.**

## How Thick Is the Cushion — What If the Judgment Is Wrong

### Hard-asset cushion

| Item | Value per share | Share of the $221 price |
|------|---------|----------------|
| Shareholder equity | ~$6.44 | **2.9%** |
| Net tangible assets | ~$4.91 | **2.2%** |

**NVIDIA has almost no "asset cushion" in the traditional sense.** Buying NVIDIA is a pure bet on future cash flows, not a purchase of assets.

### Downside in extreme scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Corresponding share price | Decline |
|------|------|---------|------|
| Moderate valuation correction (PE 40x→30x) | 40% | $170 | -23% |
| CapEx cycle peaks (PE compresses to 22x) | 30% | $110 | -50% |
| Large-scale substitution by in-house chips | 20% | $65 | -70% |
| Extreme crash (Cisco-style) | 10% | $35 | -84% |

**The good news**: NVIDIA will not go bankrupt. Zero leverage, $52 billion in net cash, and $96.7 billion in annual free cash flow mean the company could continue operating and buying back shares even if revenue were cut in half.

**The bad news**: **Your principal could lose 50-70%, and it could take 3-10 years to recover.**

### Key monitoring signals

**Signals to add to the position**:
1. Share price falls below $130 (the base-case DCF range)
2. The capex cycle pulls back but AI-demand-side data remains strong
3. Rubin Ultra is delivered on schedule with performance significantly exceeding expectations

**Signals to trim or exit**:
1. Hyperscale customers' quarterly capex growth turns **negative** for the first time
2. A single major customer's purchases decline 20%+ quarter-over-quarter
3. Gross margin falls below **65%** for two consecutive quarters
4. Jensen Huang health issues or retirement announcement

## Closing: An Ultimate Question About NVIDIA

> **If you plan to hold for 10 years, NVIDIA's share in 10 years might be 40-55% with a gross margin of 65-70% — does that match today's $5.4 trillion valuation?**

There is no standard answer to this question. But if you feel hesitant about it, that means you don't yet have enough conviction to take a large position.

Duan Yongping's choice was to use a 7.7% position to express the judgment that "AI is an unstoppable trend, and NVIDIA is a good company," while managing risk through options. He did not go all-in — because he admits he doesn't understand the 10-year picture.

**Admitting "I don't understand" is not weakness — it's discipline.** In investing, knowing what you don't know matters more than knowing what you do.
