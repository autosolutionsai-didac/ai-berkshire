## NVIDIA Is Not Facing a Single Opponent

A 90% market share and a 75% gross margin — those two numbers alone amount to a bounty notice. Every company in the world with money and capability has an incentive to spend money building a substitute. Not out of ideology, but out of arithmetic: for every $100 NVIDIA earns, $75 is profit, and a large chunk of that $75 could otherwise stay in the buyer's pocket.

NVIDIA faces threats from five directions, each with a different intensity:

| Threat vector | Representative | Threat level | Timeline |
|---------|------|---------|-------|
| Customers' in-house chips | Google TPU, AWS Trainium, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA | **Highest** | Already happening |
| Broadcom (helping customers build chips) | Custom ASIC design services | **High** | Accelerating |
| AMD | MI300X / MI355X / MI400 | **Medium** | Ongoing catch-up |
| AI coding + open-source compilers | Claude Code, Triton, ROCm | **Medium** | Eroding the CUDA code layer |
| Domestic Chinese substitutes | Huawei Ascend | **Medium** (regional) | China market closing off |

## Threat One: Customer-as-Competitor — Already Shifted from a "Future Threat" to a "Present Reality"

### Panorama of the Four Major In-House Chip Programs

| Customer | In-house chip | Capability | Scale (2026) | Key contracts |
|------|---------|------|-------------|---------|
| **Google** | TPU Ironwood (v7) | **Can substitute for NVIDIA in both training and inference** | 9,216 chips/pod, 42.5 EFLOPS | Anthropic **$50 billion+**, Meta leasing |
| **AWS** | Trainium2 / Trainium3 | Inference validated, training partially validated | Project Rainier: **500,000-1,000,000 units** | Anthropic, OpenAI, and Apple all using it |
| **Microsoft** | Maia 200 | **Inference-only chip** | Already deployed in Iowa | Serves GPT-5.2 |
| **Meta** | MTIA 300-500 | Recommendation-ranking inference | Hundreds of thousands of units, a new generation every 6 months | Internal use |

> Sources: Google Cloud Blog, AWS official, Microsoft Blog (2026-01), Meta official (2026-03), Bloomberg (2026-04-06)

**A few facts worth remembering**:

1. **Google is the only company that has proven it can train frontier models entirely without NVIDIA** — Gemini 3 was trained end-to-end on TPUs
2. **Anthropic has gone from being an "incremental large customer" of NVIDIA to the biggest champion of in-house chips** — 500,000-1,000,000 Trainium2 chips plus 400,000 TPU Ironwood chips, with all future gigawatt-scale compute bet on non-NVIDIA solutions
3. **TechCrunch reported in March 2026 that Trainium has won usage from Anthropic, OpenAI, and even Apple** — AWS's chips have upgraded from "internal substitution" to "external sales"
4. **Of the four, only Google has validated training substitution** — the other three mainly substitute on the inference side

### "The Short-Term Threat of In-House Chips Is Overstated" — This View Needs Revising

Many analysts have said: "Google has been building TPUs for 10 years and is still NVIDIA's biggest customer — the pace of in-house substitution is slower than people think."

That was true in 2024. But 2025-2026 brought a qualitative shift:

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| 2025-11 | AWS officially brings 500,000 Trainium2 chips online for Project Rainier | From "testing" to "production deployment" |
| 2026-01 | Microsoft Maia 200 begins serving GPT-5.2 | Inference-side substitution enters production |
| 2026-03 | Meta officially announces MTIA 300/400/450/500, a four-generation roadmap, one generation every 6 months | Twice the industry's typical pace |
| 2026-04 | Anthropic signs for 400,000 TPU Ironwood chips, a contract worth ~$10 billion | **A gigawatt-scale contract + SEC disclosure — no longer just a rumor** |

**Judgment: in-house chips have moved from "long-term threat" to "present reality." 2025 Q4-2026 Q2 is the inflection point for ASIC production at scale.**

## Threat Two: Broadcom — a More Dangerous "Power Behind the Throne" Than AMD

AMD is NVIDIA's direct competitor in the open market. But **Broadcom may be the bigger threat** — because it isn't competing with NVIDIA at all; it is **helping NVIDIA's own customers build their own chips**.

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Number of custom AI chip customers | **6**: Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic + 2 undisclosed (presumed to be ByteDance and Apple) |
| FY2025 AI revenue | **$19.9 billion** (+63%) |
| FY2027 CEO target | **>$100 billion** |
| Backlog | **$73 billion** |
| Google TPU long-term contract | Through **2031** |
| OpenAI contract | "Titan" XPU, targeting **10GW** of compute |

> Sources: CNBC (2025-12), Broadcom earnings, Next Platform

**Broadcom's two-year AI revenue target rises from $19.9 billion to over $100 billion (roughly 5x) — and every dollar of that increment corresponds to a market shift from NVIDIA GPUs to custom ASICs.**

Broadcom's role is that of an "arms dealer" — it doesn't compete with NVIDIA directly; instead it designs TPUs for Google, MTIA for Meta, and Titan for OpenAI. **Its success is built on NVIDIA's losses.**

## Threat Three: AMD — the Value-for-Money Disruptor

AMD is not NVIDIA's biggest threat (in-house chips are), but it is the most persistent one.

### Current state

| Metric | AMD MI300X | NVIDIA B200 |
|------|-----------|------------|
| HBM capacity | **192 GB** | **192 GB** |
| Memory bandwidth | 5.3 TB/s | **8 TB/s** |
| FP8 compute | 2,615 TFLOPS | **~9,000 TFLOPS (sparse)** |
| Price | **$10-15K** | $35-45K |
| Interconnect bandwidth | ~896 GB/s | **1.8 TB/s (NVLink 5)** |

> Sources: AMD official, NVIDIA official

**AMD's individual hardware specs are narrowing the gap, but NVIDIA still holds a 2-3 year system-level lead in full-stack integration.**

### AMD's Real Bottleneck

AMD's biggest problem is not hardware — it's **software**.

The ROCm-vs-CUDA gap has narrowed from 2-3x in 2023 to 10-30% in 2026 — but in training scenarios that demand extreme performance, "a 10-30% gap" is still "unacceptable."

More critically, there's the long-tail ecosystem: debugging tools (AMD has nothing at the level of Nsight), the optimization feedback loop (cuDNN is hand-tuned to 90%+ utilization for every GPU generation), and framework integration (ROCm is still a second-class citizen in PyTorch, with bug fixes lagging 6-12 months behind).

**AMD's strategy is "don't try to beat NVIDIA on the extreme end — win on the value-for-money battlefield"** — and the MI355X has already overtaken NVIDIA on TCO in high-volume inference scenarios. This works in the inference market but is nearly ineffective in the training market.

### A Historical Parallel: Intel's Lesson

Intel's share of the x86 server market fell from **98%** in 2017 to **73%** in 2025 (within x86). Add the erosion from ARM servers, and Intel's "broad-sense share" may already have fallen to 55-60%.

**Going from 98% to 73% took about 8 years.** Intel's decline was largely due to its own execution failures (falling behind on process technology, missing mobile). NVIDIA's execution is far stronger than Intel's — a new generation every year, actively defending its position.

**But the biggest lesson is this**: Intel's roughly 60% gross margin was enough to attract substitutes. NVIDIA's gross margin runs as high as **75%** — the incentive to substitute can only be stronger.

## Threat Four: AI Coding + Open-Source Compilers — the End of the CUDA Code Layer

This was discussed in detail in Part 02; here's a summary:

| Year | AI coding capability | CUDA→ROCm migration cost | Meaning |
|------|-----------|-------------------|------|
| 2023 | Basic | A team + several months | CUDA code lock-in is solid |
| 2025 | Can write tensor-core kernels | 1-2 people + several weeks | Starting to loosen |
| 2026 | Can port end-to-end | **1 person + a few hours** | **The code-lock-in layer has essentially vanished** |

**But the system-level moat (tens-of-thousands-of-GPU-scale communication, extreme optimization, full-stack integration) remains solid.** AI tools can translate simple kernels, but they cannot crack NCCL-level distributed systems optimization.

**An interesting, counterintuitive phenomenon**: 99% of the GPU code on the internet is written in CUDA. That means large AI models' training data contains far more CUDA code than ROCm code — **AI is better at writing CUDA than ROCm.** This is NVIDIA's "reverse flywheel" — the more advanced AI coding becomes, the more it actually reinforces the CUDA ecosystem.

## Threat Five: the China Market — from "Already Absorbed" to "Permanently Lost"

### Short term: losses already realized

- Q1 2025: the US government halted H20 shipments to China, and NVIDIA took a **$4.5 billion** inventory write-down plus lost $2.5 billion in shippable revenue
- The earlier Trump-era ban had already caused a **$5.5 billion** write-off
- In July 2025 the policy reversed and H20 shipments resumed
- The B30A (a cut-down Blackwell variant) still awaits approval

### Long term: a lost market

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Huawei Ascend 910C's 2026 production target | **600,000 units** (about double 2025) |
| Ascend 950DT SuperCluster | 524 EFLOPS FP8 (shipping 2026 Q4) |
| CANN (Huawei's CUDA equivalent) open-sourced | December 31, 2025 |
| Huawei's ecosystem investment | **$2.1 billion/year** for 5 years |

> Sources: TrendForce (2025-09), Huawei official HC conference

**China's AI compute market potentially represents $20-50 billion per year — NVIDIA is being permanently excluded from it.** This is not a short-term policy fluctuation but a structural consequence of US-China tech decoupling.

**Counterargument**: China's share of NVIDIA's total revenue has already fallen to about 3%, and the impact has largely been absorbed. If the B30A is approved, there is still some incremental room for growth.

**The counter to the counterargument**: that 3% revenue share obscures a fact — the incremental TAM in the China market may be the single largest piece NVIDIA has lost. Absent export controls, NVIDIA could sell an extra $20-50 billion per year — growth space that has now been permanently blocked off.

## A Precise Breakdown of the Moat — Who Has Escaped Over the Wall, and Who's Still Locked Inside

Putting all the competitive threats together, NVIDIA's customer base splits into three tiers:

| Customer group | CUDA moat status | Reason |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **Google** | Already escaped | Gemini 3 trained entirely on TPU; JAX/XLA is a complete replacement |
| **AWS / Anthropic** | Escaping | Millions of Trainium2 chips, but frontier training not yet fully validated |
| **Meta** | Escaped on inference, locked in on training | 600,000 H100s for training cannot be replaced; MTIA does inference only |
| **Microsoft** | Escaped on inference, locked in on training | Maia 200 is inference-only; training still deeply dependent on NVIDIA |
| **OpenAI** | Escaping in the medium term | Broadcom partnership on Titan XPU deploying in 2027 |
| **Startups / universities / research institutions** | **Deeply locked in** | No money or people to build in-house chips; CUDA is the only option |

**The moat is eroding from the top down — the biggest 5-6 customers are climbing over the wall; but 99% of AI developers worldwide are still locked inside the city.**

The problem is: those 5-6 customers account for **61%** of NVIDIA's revenue.

## Impact on Pricing Power

NVIDIA used to be **the toll bridge** — the only road, so it could price however it liked. Going forward, NVIDIA is **the best bridge** — there are other bridges, but its is the fastest and widest.

| Before | After |
|------|------|
| Customer wants to buy an AI chip → can only go to NVIDIA | Customer → NVIDIA **or** TPU / Trainium / Maia |
| 78% gross margin, no one negotiates | Customer has leverage: "If you don't cut the price, I'll buy TPUs" |
| Pricing power comes from monopoly | Pricing power comes from being "the best" — but "best" must be proven every year |

**Pricing power won't vanish overnight.** But the drop from 78% to 71% (FY2025 → FY2026) is already a signal. Under a neutral outlook, gross margin may stabilize in the **65-70%** range five years from now.

## Conclusion of This Part

| Threat | Short term (1-2 years) | Medium term (3-5 years) | Long term (5-10 years) |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Customers' in-house chips | Substitution begins on inference | Training side starts loosening | Major erosion |
| Broadcom | Order flow accelerates | Revenue could reach $100 billion+ (a direct mirror of NVIDIA's losses) | Becomes the second pole of AI chips |
| AMD | Leads on inference value-for-money | Still a clear gap on training | Gap continues to narrow |
| AI coding + compilers | Erosion at the code layer | System-level lock-in starts loosening | CUDA lock-in essentially disappears |
| China market | Already lost | Permanently blocked | TAM permanently forfeited |

**Overall judgment**: NVIDIA is safe on the training side for the next 2-3 years, but is already losing share faster on the inference side. On a five-year view, "near-monopoly" will become "biggest player but not a monopoly." **This company will not decline — but its excess monopoly profits are being structurally compressed.**
