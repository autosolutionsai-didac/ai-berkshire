### 2.3 AMD's Core Bottleneck

**AMD's biggest problem is not hardware, but software**:
- The ROCm-to-CUDA gap has narrowed from 2-3x in 2023 to **10-30%** in 2026 — Source: [ThunderCompute, 2026-04](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/rocm-vs-cuda-gpu-computing)
- But the gap in **long-tail library support, debugging tools (Nsight-level), and closed-loop performance optimization** will still take 3-5 years to close
- MI300X can run most standard PyTorch workloads, but **extreme optimization and ease of production deployment** still lag far behind CUDA
- Key reference: AI coding tools (such as Claude Code) can already port simple CUDA kernels to ROCm within 30 minutes, **but complex interconnect codebases and system-level optimization remain out of reach**

### 2.4 In-House Chips vs. AMD: Which Is More Dangerous?

| Dimension | In-house chips (TPU/Trainium/Maia/MTIA) | AMD |
|------|----------------------------------|-----|
| **Economic motivation** | Extremely strong — in-house use saves 30-70% in cost | Moderate — an alternative, but still a purchased solution |
| **Software ecosystem** | Self-built (JAX/XLA/NeuronSDK) or PyTorch-adapted | ROCm, weaker than CUDA |
| **Training substitution capability** | Verified by Google; partially verified by AWS | Capable, but the ecosystem is immature |
| **Inference substitution capability** | All capable; some already deployed at scale | Capable |
| **Scale** | Combined, on the order of millions of units | Far smaller than NVIDIA's shipment volume |
| **True threat to NVIDIA** | **Extremely high — directly cutting purchase volume from NVIDIA's biggest customers** | Moderate — mainly taking share at the margins |

**Judgment: the threat from in-house chips far exceeds that from AMD.**

- AMD is "a cheaper alternative in the same dimension" — NVIDIA still has a technology generation gap and a software moat to defend with
- In-house chips represent "customers turning into competitors" — **your four biggest customers are building their own weapons**, a structural threat that cannot be countered by cutting prices

## III. NVIDIA's Moat — How Deep Is It Still?

### 3.1 Layer-by-Layer Moat Analysis

#### Layer One: The CUDA Ecosystem (the Moat Is Being Eroded)

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Registered CUDA developers | 4 million+ (cumulative), roughly 1.5-2 million active | NVIDIA GTC 2024 |
| GPU-accelerated libraries/SDKs | 800+ | NVIDIA website |
| PyTorch default backend | CUDA (>95% actual usage) | PyTorch community |
| ROCm vs. CUDA gap | Narrowed to 10-30% on compute-intensive workloads (2026) | ThunderCompute |

**Erosion signals**:
- AI coding tools such as Claude Code can port simple CUDA to ROCm in 30 minutes — a qualitative change in the barrier to entry
- Triton 3.6.0 (OpenAI's open-source compiler) has introduced AMD HIP AOT compilation — cross-platform programming has become a reality
- Google has launched the TorchTPU project, collaborating with Meta to bring native PyTorch support to TPU
- The CASS model (academic research) achieves 95% accuracy in source-level translation

**But evidence that the moat still holds**:
- Tuning the communication stack (NCCL/NVLink/InfiniBand) for 100,000-GPU-scale cluster training — AI coding tools cannot crack this in the near term
- **No case has been found** of a publicly documented large-scale frontier LLM training failure attributed to "non-CUDA stack defects"
- Full toolchain integration (the closed loop of debugging + profiling + deployment optimization) — ROCm's gap here remains large

**Assessment: the code-layer moat has clearly eroded; the system-level moat (100,000-GPU communication/scheduling) has not yet been shaken**

---

#### Layer Two: NVLink/NVSwitch Interconnect (the Hardest Moat)

| Interconnect technology | Bandwidth | Gap |
|---------|------|------|
| NVLink 5.0 (B200) | **1.8 TB/s bidirectional** | Industry-leading |
| GB200 NVL72 | **72-GPU full interconnect** (non-blocking) | Unique |
| AMD Infinity Fabric | ~896 GB/s | Limited to within the same package |
| Google TPU ICI | ~4.8 Tb/s/chip | Strong within the Pod, but a closed ecosystem |
| Intel Gaudi3 | ~300 GB/s | Clearly behind |

**Key insight**:
- GB200 NVL72's 72-GPU full interconnect is an **exclusive advantage** for large-model training (all-reduce communication)
- Competitors have narrowed the gap in intra-node interconnect (AMD's unified memory has an advantage here), but **inter-node interconnect** remains NVIDIA's killer feature
- The Ultra Ethernet Consortium (with AMD/Intel/Broadcom participating) is trying to catch up, but its maturity lags by **2-3 years**

---

#### Layer Three: Pricing Power (Still Extremely Strong)

**Gross margin trend**:

| Quarter | Non-GAAP gross margin | Notes |
|------|---------------|------|
| FY2024 Q1 (April 2023) | 66.8% | H100 volume ramp begins |
| FY2024 Q4 (January 2024) | 76.7% | Peak |
| FY2025 Q1 (April 2024) | **78.9%** | All-time high |
| FY2025 Q3 (October 2024) | 75.0% | Holding at an elevated level |
| FY2025 Q4 (January 2025) | ~73.5% | Blackwell yield ramp |

Source: [NVIDIA Investor Relations](https://investor.nvidia.com)

A **73-79% gross margin is top-tier in the semiconductor industry** (Intel ~40-45%, AMD ~50-52%).

**Pricing strategy**: NVIDIA follows a "raise performance, don't cut prices" strategy — B200's cost-performance (perf/$) is ~4x better than H100's, but **the absolute price does not fall**. A full GB200 NVL72 rack costs $2-3M, locking in system-level procurement.

**Evidence of pricing pressure**:
- Large cloud providers have some bargaining power on H100 (the cyclical transition before Blackwell's launch), but this is standard product-generation practice
- SemiAnalysis estimates large customers receive actual discounts of roughly 15-25% — standard industry practice, not a sign of weakening pricing power
- **None of the Big Four customers has publicly complained that NVIDIA's pricing is too high** — but all of them are developing in-house chips, which is itself the biggest implicit response to that pricing power

**Judgment: pricing power remains intact in the short term (2-3 years), but the real purpose of customers' in-house chip development is to escape that pricing power**

## V. Historical Precedent: The Lesson of Intel

### 5.1 How Long Did It Take Intel to Go from 98% to 73%?

| Time | Event | Intel x86 server share |
|------|------|-------------------|
| 2017 | Before the EPYC launch | **~98%** |
| 2020 | Apple drops Intel | ~90% |
| 2021 Q4 | AMD's rise | **77%** (AMD 18%) |
| 2025 Q2 | — | **72.7%** (AMD 27.3%) |
| 2025 | ARM servers | An additional **~15-21%** share |

Source: [Semi Engineering](https://semiengineering.com/data-center-cpu-dominance-is-shifting-to-amd-and-arm/), [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/semiconductors/intel-is-losing-market-share-left-right-and-center-)

**It took about 8 years for Intel to fall from 98% to ~73% (within x86)**. Factoring in ARM erosion, Intel's "broad share" may have already dropped to ~55-60%.

### 5.2 Analogy with NVIDIA

| Dimension | Intel back then | NVIDIA today |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Ecosystem lock-in | x86 instruction set | CUDA |
| Gross margin | ~60% (peak), attracting substitution | ~75% (peak), **higher** — a stronger motivation to substitute |
| Customer in-house development | Apple's M-series, AWS Graviton | Google TPU, AWS Trainium, MS Maia, Meta MTIA |
| Execution | **Failed** (process node fell behind, missed mobile) | **Extremely strong** (annual iteration, NVLink leadership) |
| Time to substitution | ~8 years for a clear share decline | — |

**Key differences**:
- Intel's decline was largely due to **its own execution failures**, not its ecosystem being breached
- NVIDIA's current execution is extremely strong — one new product generation per year (accelerated from a two-year cadence), proactively defending its position
- But **the biggest risk is not "another NVIDIA," but "every major customer building its own NVIDIA"** — this is closer to the ARM model

**Another case**: AWS Graviton has already shifted more than half of AWS's data center CPUs to in-house ARM chips, claiming 40% better cost-performance than x86. This path has already been proven out.

### 6.3 Overall Judgment Across Three Time Horizons

#### In 3 Years (2029): Still Dominant, but Growth Slowing

- **Training side**: NVIDIA remains the preferred choice for training (70-80% share); the NVLink/NVSwitch interconnect advantage persists
- **Inference side**: share falls from 60-70% to 35-45%, the main battleground for share loss
- **Pricing power**: gross margin holds above 70%, but growth shifts from being driven by "volume + price" to "volume" alone
- **Risk signal**: hyperscaler CapEx growth turns negative for the first time — high growth has never before been sustained for more than 3 consecutive years
- **Confidence level**: **Relatively high**

#### In 5 Years (2031): The Largest Player, but Not a Monopoly

- **Training side**: 55-65%; TPU/Trainium can already train models of all sizes, and CUDA migration costs have fallen sharply
- **Inference side**: 25-35%; ASICs dominate the inference market
- **Pricing power**: gross margin may revert to the 65-70% range — still good, but no longer "excess monopoly profit"
- **Absolute revenue**: growth in the overall AI market may offset the decline in share — **total revenue won't necessarily fall, but growth will slow markedly**
- **Confidence level**: **Moderate**

#### In 10 Years (2036): Highly Uncertain

- If the Transformer architecture continues to dominate → NVIDIA may still hold 40-55% training share through continuous iteration
- If a paradigm shift occurs (a non-Transformer architecture) → all current chip roadmaps optimized for Transformers could be upended, forcing both NVIDIA and ASIC vendors to start over
- **Analogy**: no one predicted the Transformer 10 years ago; the computing paradigm 10 years from now is equally unpredictable
- **Confidence level**: **Low**

## VII. Investment Research Conclusion (Both Sides)

### The Bull Case

1. **Market growth outpaces share decline**: even if NVIDIA's share falls from 90% to 60%, if the total AI accelerator market grows from $100 billion to $500 billion, NVIDIA's absolute revenue still increases
2. **Extremely strong execution**: one new product generation per year, a 2-3 year NVLink generation gap, and management discipline rarely seen in the semiconductor industry
3. **The CUDA system-level moat still stands**: communication/scheduling optimization for 100,000-GPU clusters has no substitute within 3-5 years
4. **"Inference overtaking training" favors NVIDIA**: the inference market is more fragmented, but its total size is larger — NVIDIA could still earn more even with a lower share
5. **The Blackwell/Rubin product cycle**: 2026-2027 still has strong product-transition tailwinds

### The Bear Case

1. **The four biggest customers are the competitors**: customers accounting for a combined 61% of revenue are all developing in-house chips — this is a structural threat, not a cyclical fluctuation
2. **Inference is the main battlefield, and NVIDIA's inference share is already eroding at an accelerating pace**: from ~70% in 2024 to potentially below 40% by 2028
3. **Broadcom's AI revenue growing 8x in two years** is direct evidence of accelerating ASIC substitution
4. **75%+ gross margin invites substitution**: historical lesson (Intel's ~60% margin was already enough to invite it) — NVIDIA's excess profit is the biggest economic motivation behind customers' in-house chip efforts
5. **Gemini 3 was trained entirely on TPUs** — the narrative that "NVIDIA is the only option for training" has been disproven
6. **Circular-transaction risk**: NVIDIA invests $100 billion in OpenAI → OpenAI purchases NVIDIA GPUs → in essence, "paying for its own future revenue"
7. **The CUDA code-layer moat is being eroded on two fronts — by AI coding tools and open-source compilers alike** — the migration barrier has fallen from "an engineering team over several months" to "a single person in a few hours"

### Questions from Duan Yongping's Perspective

> "Do you plan to hold this for 10 years? If NVIDIA's share in 10 years might be 40-55% with a 65-70% gross margin — does that match today's valuation?"

> "The best businesses are ones customers **don't want to switch away from** (Moutai, Apple). NVIDIA's problem is that its customers **very much want to switch** — and they have the money and the ability to build their own."

> "Don't assume a company will always be great just because it's great today. Moats do get filled in — the only question is how fast."

### Buffett's Perspective

> "When your biggest customer is also your biggest potential competitor, your pricing power is borrowed, not owned."

## VIII. A Precise Breakdown of the CUDA Moat — "Soft Code, Hard System" Is an Oversimplification

The original assessment that "the CUDA code layer is being eroded while the system-level moat remains hard" needs revision. The moat is not binary — it has **six layers**:

| Layer | Specific content | Current state | Difficulty of replacement |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| ① Single-kernel porting | CUDA kernel → ROCm/HIP | AI tools can complete simple kernels in 30 minutes | **Low** |
| ② Core libraries | cuDNN, TensorRT | AMD's MIOpen covers 70-80% of operators | **Medium** |
| ③ Framework integration | PyTorch defaults to CUDA | ROCm can run most PyTorch workloads; Google is pushing TorchTPU | **Medium** |
| ④ Communication libraries | NCCL | RCCL/Gloo are weaker than NCCL; but PCCL already exceeds RCCL by 60-80% at 1,024+ GPUs | **Medium-high** |
| ⑤ Interconnect hardware | NVLink/NVSwitch | Google's ICI 3D torus has independently solved this (9,216-chip Pod); Ultra Ethernet lags by 2-3 years | **High (but alternatives already exist)** |
| ⑥ Full-stack integration | End-to-end 100,000-GPU training | Google has already achieved this (Gemini 3); fault tolerance is being automated by tools such as Clockwork.io/AutoClusters | **Highest (but not insurmountable)** |

Source: [Google TPU7x documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x), [PCCL paper, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18658v1), [Clockwork.io TorchPass](https://www.morningstar.com/news/accesswire/1145681msn/clockworkio-introduces-a-new-class-of-fault-tolerance-to-end-failure-driven-gpu-waste-in-ai-training)

### Where "the System-Level Moat Remains Hard" Is Right, and Where It's Wrong

**What's right**: Worldwide, only two entities can independently carry out training at a scale of 100,000+ chips — NVIDIA (the general-purpose solution) and Google (TPU). For the vast majority of AI companies, universities, and startups, CUDA+NVLink remains the only viable option.

**What's wrong**: Google has already proven this problem is solvable. TPU Ironwood delivers 1,200 GB/s bidirectional ICI bandwidth per chip, a 3D torus topology, 9,216 chips per Pod, and a Jupiter network supporting 100,000+ chips. **If the system-level moat were truly "still hard," Google could not have trained Gemini 3 entirely on TPUs.**

**A more precise judgment**: the moat is collapsing from the top down — the 5-6 largest customers are climbing over the wall, but 99% of the world's AI developers are still locked inside the city.

| User group | CUDA moat status | Reason |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **Google** | ❌ Already broken free | Gemini 3 trained entirely on TPU, with a complete internal JAX/XLA stack |
| **AWS/Anthropic** | ⚠️ Breaking free | 500,000-1,000,000 Trainium2 chips, but frontier training has not been fully verified |
| **Meta** | ⚠️ Free on inference, locked in on training | 600,000 H100s for training; MTIA + rented TPU for inference |
| **Microsoft** | ⚠️ Free on inference, locked in on training | Maia 200 for inference; training still relies on NVIDIA |
| **OpenAI** | ⚠️ Breaking free over the medium term | Titan XPU, developed with Broadcom, deploying in 2027 |
| **Startups/universities** | ✅ Deeply locked in | No money or people to develop chips in-house; CUDA is the only option |

## X. Google Selling TPUs Externally — A Paradigm Shift

### History: Before 2024, Never Sold Hardware, Only Leased via GCP

- 2015: TPU v1 for pure internal use
- 2018: Cloud TPU launches on GCP; customers rent by the hour and never receive the chips themselves
- 2020-2024: Continued to lease only, never sell

### 2025-2026: The Paradigm Shift Has Occurred

**Anthropic — the first external customer to "directly purchase TPUs"**:

| Stage | Model | Scale | Amount |
|------|------|------|------|
| Stage one | GCP cloud lease | Over 1GW of compute | Tens of billions of dollars in RPO |
| Stage two | **Broadcom directly sells complete Ironwood racks** | **400,000 TPUv7 chips** | **~$10 billion** |
| Stage three | Mixed GCP + direct purchase | Remaining 600,000 chips | ~$42 billion in RPO |
| **Total** | — | **Nearly 1 million TPUs** | **$50 billion+** |

Source: [Bloomberg 2026-04-06](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/broadcom-confirms-deal-to-ship-google-tpu-chips-to-anthropic), [Anthropic official](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute)

**Meta — the second external customer**:
- In 2026, leasing TPUs through Google Cloud (contract already signed)
- Negotiating in 2027 to directly purchase TPUs for deployment in Meta's own data centers

Source: [Dataconomy 2026-02](https://dataconomy.com/2026/02/27/meta-signs-multibillion-dollar-deal-to-rent-google-tpus-for-ai-training/)

### Why Is Google Now Willing to Sell?

1. **The TorchTPU project** lets PyTorch run natively on TPUs — sharply lowering the migration barrier
2. **Broadcom handles manufacturing and sales** — Google focuses only on design, while Broadcom builds and sells
3. **Economic motivation**: Anthropic alone represents a $50 billion+ contract, turning TPU from a cost center into a profit center
4. **Competitive strategy**: helping NVIDIA's customers break free from NVIDIA achieves three things at once (earning money + weakening a competitor + locking customers into Google's chip ecosystem)

### Impact on NVIDIA's Pricing Power

Previously, TPUs were locked inside Google = no market competition for NVIDIA. Now TPUs have become a purchasable commodity = **directly taking NVIDIA's customers and orders**.

| Before | After |
|------|------|
| Customers wanting AI chips → could only go to NVIDIA | Customers → NVIDIA **or** Google TPU (via Broadcom) |
| NVIDIA could set any price (78% gross margin) | Customers now have bargaining leverage: "If you don't lower your price, I'll buy TPUs" |
| Pricing power came from monopoly | Pricing power is diluted by competition |

**But pricing power won't disappear overnight** — TPU production capacity is limited (Broadcom/TSMC bottlenecks), migration costs are real, NVLink still holds a technology generation lead, and small and mid-sized customers won't buy TPUs.

> **Buffett's perspective**: NVIDIA used to be **the toll bridge** (the only road, priced however it liked); going forward it will be **the best bridge** (other bridges exist, but mine is the fastest and widest). The best bridge is still a good business, but its valuation multiple shouldn't be the same as a toll bridge's.
