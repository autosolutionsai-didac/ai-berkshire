### 1.3 A Munger-Style Reading

**Key insight: this is a market where aggregate growth is slowing but structural upgrading continues.** Traditional security hardware has nearly hit its ceiling; incremental growth now comes from "rising smart-technology penetration." With AI penetration at only 8-10%, roughly 90% of the installed base of devices has yet to be upgraded for intelligence — this is both an opportunity and a driver of industry transformation. But a caution is warranted: the shift toward intelligence may change the rules of competition in ways that favor players with strong software and algorithm capabilities, not necessarily the traditional hardware leaders.

## IV. Why Does Hikvision Dominate Alone?

### 4.1 Hikvision's Five-Layer Moat

**(1) Scale economies and cost advantage**
- Global video surveillance market share above 1/4, giving it extremely strong supply-chain bargaining power
- Gross margin of 45.88%, notably higher than Dahua's 40.62%, indicating greater pricing headroom under comparable competition
- Scale spreads R&D and fixed costs over a larger base, creating a positive feedback loop

**(2) Absolute dominance in R&D spending**
- Cumulative R&D spend over the past 5 years exceeds 50 billion yuan, more than 10% of revenue
- R&D staff exceed 20,000, nearly 50% of total headcount
- Cumulative global patent filings exceed 7,000, with invention patents accounting for 50%
- Uniview spends approximately 550 million yuan on R&D per year, while Hikvision spends over 10 billion yuan in a single year — a gap of nearly 20x

**(3) Integrated hardware-software solution capability**
- Roughly 80% of R&D staff are software engineers, which overturns the outside perception of Hikvision as merely a "hardware company"
- Its AI Cloud architecture (edge nodes + edge domains + cloud center) forms a complete technology stack
- In-house development spans the full chain, from front-end cameras to back-end storage, platform software, and algorithms

**(4) Channel and customer stickiness**
- A nationwide distribution network and service system
- Long-term partnerships with core customers such as government, public security, and transportation agencies
- Once a complete solution is deployed, customer switching costs are extremely high

**(5) Brand barrier**
- A mental association of "security = Hikvision" among customers
- A strong brand also established in international markets (outside of sanctioned regions)

### 4.2 Where Does the Gap Between Uniview and Hikvision Lie?

| Dimension | Hikvision | Uniview |
|------|---------|---------|
| Revenue scale | 92.5 billion yuan | ~5.5 billion yuan (17x smaller) |
| Net profit margin | 15.3% | 5.7% |
| R&D spend | ~10 billion yuan/year | ~550 million yuan/year (18x smaller) |
| Global share | 25.9% | 3.2% |
| Brand recognition | Top-of-mind in the industry | Known among industry customers, weak in commercial/consumer markets |
| Channel coverage | Nationwide + global | Mainly project-based, weak commercial channels |
| Large model | Guanlan large model | Wutong large model |
| Product pricing | Full coverage from mid to high end | Skews mid-to-high end, weak price competitiveness |
| Profit model | Diversified: large projects + SMB commercial + overseas | Dependent on large projects, low margins |

**A Munger-style summary**: Uniview's core problem is "diseconomies of scale." In an industry that demands sustained heavy R&D investment, a company with 5.5 billion yuan in annual revenue and 300 million yuan in net profit will struggle to compete with a rival that invests 10 billion yuan in R&D every year. Hikvision's moat is, in essence, a "scale-R&D-product-customer" positive feedback flywheel that Uniview finds very difficult to break.

## V. AI + Security: From "Being Able to See" to "Being Able to Understand"

### 5.1 Technology Evolution Roadmap

```
Phase 1 (2000-2010): Analog to digital — "able to see"
Phase 2 (2010-2017): HD networking — "able to see clearly"
Phase 3 (2017-2025): AI deep learning — "able to understand"
Phase 4 (2025-    ): Large models + agents — "able to think"
```

### 5.2 2025: The Inaugural Year of Large-Scale Deployment of Security Large Models

The industry has reached a consensus that 2025 is the inaugural year of large-scale deployment of large models in security. The core changes:

**(1) The four major security large models**

| Company | Large model name | Characteristics |
|------|----------|------|
| Hikvision | Guanlan large model | Multimodal; text-based search NVR already commercialized |
| Dahua Technology | Xinghan large model | Vision + language understanding |
| Uniview | Wutong large model | Empowers multiple product lines |
| Yitu Technology | Tianwen large model | Deep visual analysis |

**(2) Key technological breakthroughs**
- **Multimodal understanding**: from simple face/license-plate recognition to complex behavior understanding, anomaly detection, and cross-camera correlation
- **Natural-language interaction**: Hikvision's text-search NVR can locate target footage within seconds via natural-language queries
- **Falling costs**: an NVR with large-model text-search capability costs only a few hundred yuan more than a traditional NVR, democratizing the technology
- **AI-SaaS model**: shifting from selling hardware to selling services, potentially disrupting the business model

**(3) Impact on the competitive landscape**
- Large models require massive amounts of data for training → favors Hikvision, which has the largest installed base
- Large models require enormous R&D investment → further widens the gap between the leader and its followers
- But this also creates opportunity: if a second-tier player builds a differentiated large model for a specific vertical scenario, it could achieve a localized breakthrough

## VI. Policy Environment: Both the "Driver" and the "Ceiling" of Demand

### 6.1 Overview of Key Policies

| Policy / program | Timing | Core content | Impact on security |
|----------|------|---------|------------|
| Safe City | starting 2005 | Comprehensive urban security systems | Launched the era of large-scale security infrastructure build-out |
| Skynet Project | starting 2012 | Full coverage of urban video surveillance | Triggered a surge in government (G-side) demand |
| Sharp Eyes Project | starting 2016 | Networked video surveillance across county, township, and village levels | Pushed demand down to the rural grassroots level |
| Digital Government / Smart City | starting 2020 | Digital governance of cities | Shift from standalone security to comprehensive IoT |
| "New Infrastructure" | starting 2020 | 5G, AI, and big-data infrastructure | A new round of investment in intelligent upgrades |

### 6.2 Analysis of Policy Impact

**Favorable factors**:
- The 14th Five-Year Plan sets an explicit 7% annual growth target for the security market
- Continued smart-city construction is generating new demand
- The shift from "new construction" to "operations and upgrades" leaves substantial room for intelligent retrofitting of the installed base

**Risk factors**:
- The large-scale construction phase of programs such as Sharp Eyes has passed, and new incremental projects are declining
- Growing fiscal pressure on local governments could constrain security spending
- The "cyclicality" of a policy-driven market: demand can fall off a cliff once a project is completed

**Munger's reminder**: companies that rely too heavily on the government (G-side) market (such as Uniview) face greater policy-cycle risk. Hikvision's diversification (G-side + B-side + C-side) is a healthier structure.

## VII. Overseas Markets: Opportunity and Risk Coexist

### 7.1 Impact of Sanctions

| Event | Timing | Impact |
|------|------|------|
| U.S. Entity List | October 2019 | Hikvision and Dahua added to the list, barred from purchasing U.S. components |
| U.S. NDAA ban | Continuously strengthened | Federal agencies prohibited from using Hikvision and Dahua products |
| Five Eyes allies following suit | Continuously expanding | The UK, Australia, and others have followed with their own restrictions |

### 7.2 Response Strategies

- **Supply-chain localization**: Hikvision and Dahua have set up production bases in India, Vietnam, and Brazil
- **Domestic chip substitution**: shifting from reliance on Nvidia and Intel toward HiSilicon chips and domestic solutions
- **Market shift**: pivoting from Europe and the U.S. toward Belt and Road markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America
- **Brand insulation**: some companies are attempting to sidestep the effects of sanctions through sub-brands or partnership arrangements

### 7.3 Changes in the Overseas Market Landscape

**Markets lost**:
- Hikvision's U.S. market share fell from 4.8% to 2.5%
- Demand from the European public sector has shrunk

**Markets growing**:
- Emerging markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Africa
- Uniview's overseas business is now growing faster than its domestic business

**A Munger perspective**: sanctions represent a "permanent impairment," not a "temporary difficulty." Under the trend of U.S.-China decoupling, the ceiling facing Chinese security companies in developed-market countries is a near-certainty. But growth headroom in emerging markets remains substantial — the key question is who can build brand and channel presence there.

## VIII. Threat from New Entrants: Huawei Has Arrived, But It's Not as Scary as It Sounds

### 8.1 The Threat from Huawei HoloSens

**Huawei's advantages**:
- HiSilicon chips hold roughly 70% of the security-chip market (a commanding position upstream)
- A technology base built on 5G plus the HarmonyOS ecosystem
- Overall smart-city solution capabilities
- Brand pull and government relationships

**Huawei's limitations**:
- Security is just one of Huawei's many business lines, so its focus and investment intensity fall short of Hikvision's
- The security industry demands "hard, unglamorous work": heavy operational lift around installation, commissioning, and after-sales service
- Hikvision's Hu Yangzhong has assessed that "the entry of large companies like Huawei has not changed the industry landscape; each player has its own focus"
- Huawei HoloSens currently sits in the second tier, at the same level as Uniview

**The actual outcome**: Huawei has been in the security market for nearly 8 years (since late 2017), and its market share remains in the second tier — it has not posed a substantive threat to Hikvision.

### 8.2 Alibaba and Other Internet Giants

- Alibaba focuses mainly on the platform layer of City Brain / smart cities, where it cooperates with security-equipment vendors more than it competes with them
- Internet companies lack hardware manufacturing and offline service capabilities, so they do not pose a direct near-term threat
- Over the long run, if AI capability becomes the core competitive factor, the threat from internet giants will grow

### 8.3 A Munger-Style Judgment

> "The moat in the security industry isn't just technology — it's the accumulation of channels, service, and customer relationships. Capital can't buy these things, and time can't be sped up. Huawei has been in this market for 8 years and still hasn't changed the landscape, which shows the industry's barriers are higher than they appear."

## IX. Industry Endgame: What Structure Will the Security Industry Settle Into?

### 9.1 Three Possible Endgames

**Scenario 1: Hikvision keeps dominating, oligopoly hardens (50% probability)**
- Hikvision keeps steamrolling the field via its scale + R&D + channel flywheel
- Dahua stays #2 but the gap widens
- Uniview and other second-tier players survive in niche segments
- Analogy: Gree Electric's position in the air-conditioning industry

**Scenario 2: AI reshapes the landscape, differentiated competition emerges (30% probability)**
- In the large-model era, software and algorithm capability become more important than hardware
- Companies with differentiated AI capability could break through in vertical scenarios
- The industry shifts from "selling hardware" to "selling services," transforming the business model
- Analogy: the smartphone industry's shift from hardware competition to ecosystem competition

**Scenario 3: Cross-industry disruption, tech giants redefine security (20% probability)**
- Huawei, Alibaba, and others rebuild the security value chain through a platform-plus-ecosystem approach
- Traditional security companies are reduced to hardware contract manufacturers
- But this would require a time window of more than 10 years
- Analogy: cloud computing's disruption of traditional IT

### 9.2 Direction of Industry Evolution

```
Now: Security → Future: Smart IoT (AIoT)
Now: Selling hardware (one-time revenue) → Future: Selling services (recurring subscriptions)
Now: G-side dominant → Future: Rising share of B-side and C-side
Now: Domestic-focused → Future: Globalized (but constrained in developed markets)
Now: Video-centric → Future: Fused multimodal sensing
```

### 9.3 Endgame Judgment on Uniview

**A Munger-style conclusion**:

**The fundamental predicament Uniview faces is being "squeezed in the middle"**:
- Above it, Hikvision (crushing it on scale, R&D, and channels)
- In the middle, Dahua Technology (6 times Uniview's scale, with stronger brand recognition)
- Below it, Huawei HoloSens (a stronger technology base and greater brand pull)
- Alongside it, Tiandy and others (more intense price competition)

**Uniview's path forward lies in**:
1. **Deepening its focus on vertical industries**: building differentiated advantages in transportation (in synergy with Qianfang Technology), education/culture/sports, and other niche scenarios
2. **Overseas breakthroughs**: Uniview's overseas growth already exceeds its domestic growth, and it has not been placed on the U.S. Entity List (an advantage relative to Hikvision and Dahua)
3. **Scenario-specific deployment of the Wutong large model**: if it can outperform Hikvision in specific scenarios, it has a chance at a localized breakthrough in the AI era
4. **The possibility of being acquired**: as a subsidiary of Qianfang Technology, it is already not an independent entity, and further capital-market restructuring cannot be ruled out in the future

**But one should be clear-eyed**: with annual revenue of 5.5 billion yuan and net profit of 300 million yuan, in a race where the R&D-spend threshold runs to 10 billion yuan a year, the probability of independently becoming a first-tier player is low. As Munger has said: **"In an industry dominated by economies of scale, being the number-three player is a painful place to be."**

## X. Investment Perspective Summary

### 10.1 Industry Characteristics Assessment

| Assessment dimension | Rating | Notes |
|---------|------|------|
| Industry growth potential | Moderate | Aggregate growth is slowing, but intelligent upgrading drives structural growth |
| Stability of competitive landscape | Fairly high | Hikvision's dominant position is unlikely to change in the near term |
| Depth of moat | Fairly deep | Scale + channels + technology + customer relationships form multiple layers of barriers |
| Degree of policy dependence | Medium-high | Large G-side share, exposed to fiscal cycles |
| Risk of technological disruption | Moderate | AI large models may change the rules, but also tend to favor the leader |
| Overseas expansion potential | Constrained | Sanction impact is near-certain; emerging markets offer room |

### 10.2 Core Conclusions

1. **The security industry follows a "good company within a decent industry" logic**: the industry itself is reasonably decent, but only the leader (Hikvision) truly captures the industry's dividends.
2. **Uniview is not a good standalone investment target**: too small in scale, too thin in profit, and awkwardly positioned competitively. If one wants exposure to security, Hikvision is the only name worth deep research.
3. **AI large models are a variable, but most likely favor the leader**: large models require investment in data and compute, which happens to be exactly where Hikvision's strengths lie.
4. **Sanctions are a near-certain negative factor**: but Hikvision is already actively transforming and adjusting, and growth in emerging markets can partially offset the impact.
5. **Uniview's value shows up more from the angle of "being consolidated"**: as a subsidiary of Qianfang Technology, its value depends on synergies within the Qianfang group.
