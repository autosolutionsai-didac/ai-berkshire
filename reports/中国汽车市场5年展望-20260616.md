# China's Auto Market Five Years Out: Better or Worse?

> Analysis date: June 16, 2026
> This article does not constitute investment advice.

---

## Core Conclusion

**China's auto market will be "better" five years from now — but "better" belongs to a small set of winners; for most participants it will be "worse."**

This isn't a simple "good or bad" question. The more accurate answer is:

- **Total industry volume**: has peaked; the 34-36 million unit ceiling has been reached
- **New energy vehicles (NEVs)**: penetration moving from 47% toward 80%, with very high certainty
- **Overseas expansion**: from 8.32 million units toward 10 million+ units, the second growth pole
- **Shakeout**: 50+ brands consolidating down to roughly 15, with joint-venture brands hit hardest
- **Profit margins**: bottoming out (3.2%), with a possible recovery beginning in Q3 2026
- **Investment opportunities**: components (CATL) > vehicle-assembly leaders (BYD) > platforms (Autohome/Dongchedi)

**In one line: total volume is in a plateau phase, but the structure is undergoing massive change. China's auto industry is completing a qualitative shift from "big" to "strong" — at the cost of most players being pushed out of the game.**

## I. Where We Stand Now: China's Auto Market in 2026

### Core data at a glance

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (known) |
|------|--------|--------|---------------|
| Total sales | 31.44 million units | **34.40 million units** (+9.4%) | 12.21 million units, Jan-May |
| NEV sales | 12.87 million units | **16.49 million units** (+28%) | 5.80 million units, Jan-May |
| NEV penetration | 40.9% | **47.9%** | **62.9%** in May alone (passenger vehicles) |
| Exports | 5.86 million units | **8.32 million units** (+30%) | 4.06 million units, Jan-May (+63%) |
| Domestic-brand share | ~65% | **>70%** | **75%** in April |
| Industry profit margin | 4.4% | **4.1%** | just **3.2%** in Q1 |
| ICE (fuel) vehicle retail | — | 10.94 million units (-9%) | just 560,000 units in May (**-39%**) |

### Three historic inflection points have already occurred

**Inflection point 1: NEV penetration broke through 60%** — In May 2026, passenger-vehicle retail penetration hit 62.9%. Internal-combustion vehicles have flipped from "mainstream" to a "minority."

**Inflection point 2: Domestic-brand share broke through 75%** — Joint-venture brand share has fallen from 62% in 2014 to below 25%, a historic reversal.

**Inflection point 3: Exports approaching 10 million units** — In the first five months of 2026, exports reached 4.06 million units (+63%), putting the full-year figure on track to exceed 10 million. China is shifting from a "large auto-consuming nation" to a "large auto-exporting nation."

## III. Five Highly Certain Trends

### Trend 1: ICE vehicles are exiting faster (certainty: ★★★★★)

May's ICE retail figure plunged 39% year-over-year — this is not noise, it's a trend.

| Period | Estimated annual ICE sales | Share |
|------|------------------|------|
| 2025 | ~18 million units | ~52% |
| 2028 | ~9 million units | ~26% |
| 2031 | <5 million units | <15% |

Hainan will ban ICE vehicle sales starting in 2030; tier-1/2 cities will see ICE vehicles largely exit new-car sales around 2030; nationwide, traditional ICE vehicles are expected to fully exit by 2040-2050.

**Investment implication:** The ICE supply chain (traditional transmissions, engines, exhaust systems) will contract at an accelerating pace. Betting on an ICE recovery means betting against the trend.

### Trend 2: The shakeout will be most brutal in 2026-2028 (certainty: ★★★★★)

**List of brands already exited or endangered:**

| Status | Brands |
|------|------|
| Already bankrupt/exited | WM Motor, Neta, HiPhi, Jiyue, Evergrande Auto, Enovate, Aiways, Byton, Zi You Jia |
| Exited China market | Skoda (mid-2026), Acura (2023), Jeep (2025), Mitsubishi (2025) |
| High risk | Hyundai-Kia (annual sales below 200,000 units), other second-tier joint ventures |

**Elimination criteria:**
- Annual sales <100,000 units → probability of exit >80%
- Monthly sales <1,000 units → already "the walking dead"
- "Assembly-only" companies with no core technology → the first to go

**Endgame structure: roughly 15 companies — 5-6 state-owned/private-sector leaders, 3-4 surviving new entrants, 3-4 remaining joint ventures (much smaller in scale).**

### Trend 3: Autonomous driving shifts from a bonus feature to a matter of survival (certainty: ★★★★)

| Period | Autonomous-driving penetration |
|------|---------|
| 2025 | Urban NOA (Navigate on Autopilot) penetration 11.6%, 2.67 million units |
| 2026 | Projected 4 million units, first L3 vehicles hitting the road |
| 2028 | L3 fully rolled out, urban NOA becomes standard |
| 2031 | L4 becomes routine in tier-1 cities, Robotaxi fleet reaches 500,000 units |

**Key changes:**
- Huawei ADS costs just 10,000 yuan as an optional add-on; autonomous-driving "democratization" has reached the 150,000-yuan price tier
- Tesla FSD enters China (Q3 2026), but may already be "late"
- Automakers lacking autonomous-driving capability will be eliminated faster

### Trend 4: Overseas expansion is shifting from quantity to quality (certainty: ★★★★)

| Year | Export volume | Model |
|------|--------|------|
| 2024 | 5.86 million units | Primarily complete-vehicle exports |
| 2025 | 8.32 million units | Complete vehicles + CKD (knock-down kits) |
| 2026 | ~10 million units | Beginning local manufacturing |
| 2031 | ~10-12 million units | "Reverse joint ventures" + localized production |

**Key changes:**
- BYD's Hungary plant went into production in late 2025; Changan's Thailand plant is already operating
- Chinese automakers are shifting from "selling cars" to "exporting the supply chain"
- Tariff barriers (EU 17-38%, Mexico 50%) are forcing overseas plant construction
- By 2031, localized overseas production capacity could reach 3-5 million units

### Trend 5: Profit margins are bottoming out and recovering — but only for the winners (certainty: ★★★)

| Period | Industry profit margin | Driver |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 2025 | 4.1% | Price war at its fiercest |
| Q1 2026 | 3.2% | Historic low |
| Q3 2026 | Inflection point (projected) | Weak brands exiting, price war easing |
| 2028 | ~5-6% (est.) | Improved competitive structure, consolidation at the top |
| 2031 | ~6-7% (est.) | Approaching a healthy level |

**Note: the margin recovery is structural — leading companies will recover to 8-10%, while trailing companies keep losing money and exit.** Don't judge this by the "industry average."

## IV. Three Core Uncertainties

### Uncertainty 1: When will Robotaxis substantively replace private cars?

**Current state:** Pony.ai has already achieved per-vehicle monthly operating profitability in Guangzhou and Shenzhen (average daily net revenue of 338 yuan); Baidu's Apollo Go (Luobo Kuaipao) has surpassed 9 million cumulative rides. Goldman Sachs projects China's Robotaxi fleet will reach 500,000 units by 2030.

**Where opinions diverge:** Optimists believe Robotaxis will substantially replace private cars after 2028; skeptics believe the impact will remain limited before 2035.

**My assessment:** By 2031, Robotaxi's substantive impact on private-car demand will be roughly **-5% to -10%** — it will not upend the private-car market, but it will significantly reshape mobility patterns.

### Uncertainty 2: Will trade protectionism close off the path for Chinese automakers going overseas?

**Current tariff landscape:**
- EU: 17-38% (currently negotiating a "minimum price mechanism" as a replacement)
- Mexico: 50% (effective January 2026)
- United States: essentially closed off
- Canada, Germany: easing restrictions in early 2026

**My assessment:** A slowdown in overseas-expansion growth in the near term (2026-2027) is highly likely, but localized plant construction will open new channels. By 2031, China's export structure will shift from "complete-vehicle exports" to "supply-chain exports" — total volume will stay above 10 million units while the profit structure improves.

### Uncertainty 3: Will solid-state batteries change the rules of the game?

**Progress:** Toyota plans mass production in 2027; CATL plans small-scale production in 2027 and large-scale production by 2030.

**If solid-state batteries reach large-scale production in 2029-2030:**
- Range would double (1,000+ km), with a full charge in 10 minutes
- Battery-electric vehicles would thoroughly crush hybrids and range-extended vehicles
- Range-extended models could shrink sharply before 2031
- Japanese automakers (with Toyota leading on solid-state batteries) could get a chance to turn the tables

**My assessment:** The probability of solid-state batteries reaching large-scale adoption before 2031 is roughly 30%. The more likely path is semi-solid-state batteries first (already in production), with full solid-state gradually ramping up after 2030.

## V. Ranking Investment Opportunities

### Profit distribution across the supply chain: who's making money?

**A striking fact:** CATL's 2025 net profit was 72.2 billion yuan — more than BYD (32.6 billion yuan) plus Geely plus Chery combined. Components are far more profitable than vehicle assembly.

| Segment | Representative company | Profit margin | Certainty | Investment value |
|------|---------|--------|--------|---------|
| **EV batteries** | CATL | Net margin >15% | ★★★★★ | **Top pick** |
| **Vehicle-assembly leader** | BYD | Net margin ~4% | ★★★★ | **Select pick** |
| **Autonomous-driving supply chain** | Huawei / Horizon Robotics / Momenta | — | ★★★ | High growth, high risk |
| **Charging infrastructure** | TELD / Star Charge | Low | ★★★★ | Steady |
| **Auto export-supply-chain ecosystem** | Component exporters | Medium | ★★★★ | Undervalued |
| **Auto platforms** | Autohome / Dongchedi | Declining | ★★ | Watch and wait |

### Five-year investment thesis summary

1. **The surest money is in components** — no matter who wins the vehicle-assembly war, everyone needs CATL's batteries. It's like selling shovels during a gold rush
2. **In vehicle assembly, buy only the leader** — BYD's scale effects from selling 4.6 million units a year are already established; in a shakeout, the winner-takes-most effect will make the strong even stronger
3. **Autonomous driving is the biggest variable on a five-year view** — a trillion-yuan-scale incremental market, but the competitive structure isn't settled yet (Huawei and Momenta both strong), so wait for confirmation
4. **Overseas expansion is the most underrated narrative** — a growth story moving from 7 million to 10 million units, with margins higher than in the domestic market
5. **Avoid the traditional ICE supply chain** — transmissions, engines, exhaust systems — these assets are depreciating at an accelerating pace

## VI. The Ultimate Answer: Better or Worse?

### Better for whom:

| Participant | Five years from now | Reason |
|--------|-------|------|
| BYD and other domestic leaders | **Much better** | Share gains + overseas expansion + scale effects, winner-take-most |
| CATL and other battery giants | **Better** | Demand growth is near-certain + technology lead + expansion into energy storage/robotics |
| Chinese consumers | **Better** | Better products, lower prices, more choice, autonomous driving becoming mainstream |
| Autonomous-driving supply chain | **Better** | Going from a market worth nothing to one worth a trillion yuan |
| Charging infrastructure | **Better** | Going from 20 million charging points to 50 million+ |

### Worse for whom:

| Participant | Five years from now | Reason |
|--------|-------|------|
| Joint-venture brands | **Much worse** | Share falls from 25% to below 15%, several exit |
| Weak domestic brands | **Disappear** | Most brands with annual sales below 100,000 units will no longer exist |
| Traditional ICE supply chain | **Worse** | Demand for engines/transmissions shrinks by 60%+ |
| Traditional 4S dealerships | **Worse** | Displaced by the direct-sales model + lower maintenance demand from NEVs |
| Vertical auto platforms | **Worse** | Stagnant market + fading traffic dividend |
| Overall industry profit margin (short term) | **Worse before it gets better** | 2026-2027 is the most painful phase |

### One-line summary

> **China's auto market will be "better" five years from now — but it's a "phoenix rising from the ashes" kind of better.** The fire (price wars + the shakeout + bottoming margins) is burning down the old order, and what rises from the ashes will be the world's most competitive Chinese auto industry. BYD selling 4.6 million units, exports of 8.32 million units, NEV penetration at 63% — these numbers would have been unimaginable five years ago, and looking back five years from now, they may turn out to be just the starting point. But for the 50%+ of brands being eliminated — and their employees, dealers, and suppliers — these five years will be a brutal shakeout.
