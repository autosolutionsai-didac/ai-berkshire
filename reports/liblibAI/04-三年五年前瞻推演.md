# LiblibAI: 3-Year and 5-Year Forward Scenario Analysis

**Analysis date**: May 23, 2026
**Confidence labels**: 🟢 High / 🟡 Medium / 🔴 Low

---

## I. Scenario framework: five key variables

| Variable | Optimistic assumption | Neutral assumption | Pessimistic assumption |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| V1: General-purpose foundation models' image capability | Progress is slow; professional needs still require a workflow platform | Covers 80% of everyday needs; professional users still need customization | Approaches professional quality, sharply compressing the value of the middle layer |
| V2: ByteDance Jimeng competition | Moderate, focused on the consumer end | Intense, fighting for mid-tier users | Overwhelming, full suppression |
| V3: Lovart's overseas growth | Explosive, ARR growing 3-5x a year | Steady, ARR growing 50-100% a year | Slowing, facing head-on competition from Canva/Adobe |
| V4: Domestic monetization | Enterprise SaaS opens up new opportunities | Paid-conversion rate rises slowly | Low willingness to pay, suppressed by free competitors |
| V5: Industry regulation | Favorable, mainly promoting development | Tightening but predictable | Heavy regulation, business constrained |

## II. Three-year-out scenario (2029)

### Optimistic scenario (probability: 20%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Combined revenue | ARR of $300-500 million, with Lovart contributing 60%+ and domestic 30-35% |
| User scale | 50 million registered / 8 million MAU domestically; Lovart 5 million+ globally |
| Valuation | $4.0-6.0 billion (pre-IPO or post-listing) |
| Market position | Top 5 globally among AI creation platforms |
| Team size | 800-1,500 people, with the overseas team at 30% |
| IPO status | Lists on the Hong Kong or U.S. exchange in 2028-2029, market cap of $5.0-8.0 billion |

**Path to this outcome**: general-purpose foundation models continue to have shortcomings in professional-use cases, validating "model marketplace + professional workflow" as irreplaceable. Lovart establishes brand recognition in the design-agent category. Chen Mian's ByteDance background helps replicate CapCut's overseas playbook.

### Neutral scenario (probability: 50%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Combined revenue | ARR of $100-200 million, with Lovart contributing 50% and domestic 40% |
| User scale | 40 million registered / 6 million MAU domestically; Lovart 2-3 million |
| Valuation | $1.5-3.0 billion (Series C/D) |
| Market position | First tier domestically (coexisting with Meitu, Jimeng, and Kling) |
| Team size | 500-800 people |
| IPO status | Not yet listed, in preparation |

**Key challenges**: the domestic paid-conversion rate fails to rise (an old problem for Chinese SaaS), and overseas growth is constrained by brand recognition and localization. Profitable, but with thin margins.

### Pessimistic scenario (probability: 30%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Combined revenue | ARR of $30-50 million, growth stalled |
| User scale | MAU falls to 3 million; Lovart stalls at 1 million |
| Valuation | $500-800 million (down-round risk) |
| Market position | Suppressed by Jimeng/Kling, marginalized overseas by Canva/Adobe |
| IPO status | IPO prospects gone; seeking acquisition |

**Most likely failure modes**:
1. GPT-5/6 natively supports high-quality image generation, rendering the "model marketplace" pointless
2. ByteDance deeply integrates Jimeng into CapCut/Douyin, covering the full chain with free access plus traffic
3. A compliance black-swan event: lingering effects from the CCTV exposé, plus tightening regulation

## III. Five-year-out scenario (2031)

### Optimistic scenario (probability: 15%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Revenue | ARR of $800 million-$1.5 billion |
| Valuation / market cap | $8.0-15.0 billion (already listed) |
| Product form | An "AI creative operating system" covering image/video/3D/audio/interaction design |
| Competitive landscape | A three-way standoff with Canva and Adobe in AI-native creation (leading in Asia-Pacific) |

### Neutral scenario (probability: 45%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Revenue | ARR of $200-500 million |
| Valuation | $2.0-5.0 billion |
| Product form | Image + video as dual core products, having already gone through 1-2 major pivots |
| Status | Already IPO'd or pre-IPO |

**Structural changes anticipated**:
1. The AI agent revolution — a shift from "humans using tools" to "agents using the platform"
2. The rise of 3D/spatial computing — driven by Apple Vision Pro/Meta Quest
3. Restructuring of the content-copyright system — platforms with compliant content libraries gain a structural advantage

### Pessimistic scenario (probability: 40%)

| Dimension | Projected outcome |
|------|----------|
| Revenue | ARR below $100 million, or no longer independent |
| Status | Acquired by ByteDance/Alibaba/Tencent ($800 million-$1.5 billion), or has shrunk substantially |

**Why the 5-year pessimistic probability is higher than the 3-year one**: five years is enough time for technological change to upend an entire industry. A computing-paradigm shift happens roughly every 5 years (2011 mobile internet → 2016 cloud computing → 2021 low-code → 2026 AI agents). By 2031, the "prompt + model generation" paradigm may already be outdated.

## V. Investment return projection

**Assuming entry at the current $1 billion valuation:**

| Scenario | Probability | Valuation after 3 years | 3-year return | Valuation after 5 years | 5-year return |
|------|------|----------|---------|----------|---------|
| Optimistic | 20% | $5.0 billion | 5.0x | $10.0 billion | 10.0x |
| Neutral | 50% | $2.0 billion | 2.0x | $3.5 billion | 3.5x |
| Pessimistic | 30% | $600 million | 0.6x | $1.0 billion (acquired) | 1.0x |

**Probability-weighted expected return**:
- 3-year: **2.18x** (annualized IRR ~30%)
- 5-year: **3.48x** (annualized IRR ~28%)

**Benchmarks**: Canva (42x seven years after its Series A), Midjourney (~25x over 2 years), Stability AI (0.15x over 3 years). LiblibAI's expected return of 2-3.5x is "reasonable but unremarkable" among private-market AI investments.

## VI. "If you were CEO" scenario

### Priority one: go all-in on Lovart overseas

- **Rationale**: domestically the company faces free-price suppression from ByteDance and low willingness to pay, whereas Lovart has already found PMF and its overseas ARPU is 10-20x the domestic figure
- **Actions**: expand the team to 150-200 people, establish a local operations center in the U.S., break into the SMB market, deepen integration with Shopify/Etsy
- **Probability of success**: 45%

### Priority two: build LibTV as the domestic differentiation moat

- **Rationale**: the image market has already become a red ocean, whereas video-creation workflows are complex (creating stickiness) and priced 76-92% below Runway/Pika
- **Actions**: target the three vertical use cases of e-commerce short video, paid knowledge content, and corporate promotional video; build a video-template UGC ecosystem
- **Probability of success**: 55%

### Priority three: build enterprise-grade SaaS

- **Rationale**: enterprise ARPU is 50-100x consumer ARPU, with higher renewal rates. Canva's rise in valuation from $1 billion to $42 billion was driven in significant part by growth in Teams enterprise revenue
- **Actions**: build a 15-20 person enterprise sales team, develop custom solutions targeting e-commerce/advertising/gaming/film, offer private deployment
- **Probability of success**: 40%

## VII. Endgame judgment

**LiblibAI's most likely outcome is to become "a quality asset acquired by a giant" (40%), but there is a 30-35% chance it becomes "the visual-content workflow platform of the AI era."**

| Option | Probability | Analysis |
|------|------|------|
| A: The Adobe of the AI era | 5% | Adobe represents 40 years of accumulation; LiblibAI doesn't build its own foundation model, so its moat isn't deep enough |
| B: The Shutterstock of the AI era (a variant) | 25% | More accurately described as an "AI visual-content workflow platform" — more technically sophisticated than Shutterstock |
| C: Acquired by a giant | 40% | The most likely outcome. ByteDance/Alibaba/Tencent are all potential buyers, at $800 million-$1.5 billion |
| D: Gradual marginalization | 20% | Driven by base-model breakthroughs + squeeze from giants + underperformance overseas |
| E: Lovart succeeds independently | 10% | Requires ARR to far exceed expectations, and the design-agent category to be validated as a large market |

---

## Core conclusions summary

1. **The core contradiction**: the moat is built on "aggregation," but aggregation only has value if downstream users need to aggregate. If general-purpose foundation models satisfy needs "in one stop," the value of the aggregation layer gets compressed

2. **Lovart is the make-or-break variable**. The domestic ceiling is visible; overseas is the only path to exponential growth. Whether ARR breaks $100 million before 2027 determines how the story unfolds

3. **Chen Mian's execution is a genuine plus**. Going from "RMB 4,000 in the bank" to a "$1 billion valuation," combined with ByteDance's globalization-operations DNA

4. **The $1 billion valuation offers mediocre risk-adjusted returns**. Expected return of 2-3.5x, with upside capped by the "middle layer" positioning and downside exposed to technology-paradigm shifts

5. **The time window is tight**. LiblibAI must complete the transition from "high-growth startup" to "sustainably profitable" by 2027-2028, or the value of the middle layer will keep eroding
