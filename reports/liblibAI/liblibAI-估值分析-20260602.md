## V. Core risk factors

### 1. Moat depth is in question (the biggest risk)

The technical barriers of AI design tools are dissolving rapidly:
- Adobe Firefly, Canva AI, and Figma AI are all accelerating their catch-up
- ByteDance's Jimeng AI already has monthly visits approaching ten million
- Rapid iteration at the model layer means the "tool differentiation" window may be short
- Is LiblibAI's moat really its **community ecosystem** or its **product experience**? If it's the latter, it's only a matter of time before Big Tech catches up

### 2. Unclear path to profitability

- Behind Lovart's $30 million ARR, what are the GPU inference costs, customer acquisition costs, and team costs?
- AI application gross margins typically run 40-60% (heavy compute consumption), far below traditional SaaS's 70-80%
- Losses could grow larger as scale grows — unless pricing power emerges

### 3. Domestic and overseas markets are disconnected

- Lovart's overseas success does not guarantee Xingliu Agent's domestic success — the domestic design market has lower willingness to pay
- The two markets require two separate operating systems, doubling management complexity

### 4. Founder-style risk

- Chen Mian has changed companies six times in ten years; long-term operating capability remains unproven
- The transition from "serial blitz entrepreneur" to "long-term operator" has not yet been demonstrated

### 5. Primary-market liquidity risk

- For an unlisted company valued at $1 billion, exit channels are limited
- If a Series C round or IPO does not go smoothly, early investors could face a liquidity squeeze

## VI. Conclusion

### Current valuation (post-Series B): approximately $1 billion

### Reasonable valuation range

| Perspective | Valuation range |
|------|---------|
| Static (based on end-2025 ARR) | $0.8-1.5 billion |
| Dynamic (based on expected end-2026 ARR) | **$1.5-2.0 billion** (neutral) |
| Optimistic ceiling | $3.0-3.75 billion |
| Pessimistic floor | $0.6-0.9 billion |

### Core judgment

**The current $1 billion valuation looks cheap under the neutral assumption, but not extremely undervalued.**

Rationale:
1. Lovart's growth rate is genuinely impressive (0 to $30 million ARR in four months), demonstrating product-market fit
2. The dual-product-line combination (community + Agent) is a differentiated advantage in the AI creation space
3. The backing of Sequoia, CMC, and Ant Group provides some credibility premium
4. But the AI tools track iterates extremely fast — today's lead does not guarantee tomorrow's moat
5. Being unprofitable with high GPU costs means the real risk of "the bigger it gets, the more it loses" is genuine

**In one sentence**: if Lovart can prove sustained growth in 2026 (ARR breaking $100 million), then $1 billion is a bargain; if growth slows and competition intensifies, then $1 billion is buying at the top.
