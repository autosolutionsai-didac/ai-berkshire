## II. Deep analysis of each threat

### Threat 1: Native image generation from general-purpose foundation models (GPT-4o/5/5.5, Gemini)

**Threat level: Existential**

#### Current capability boundary

- **Already covered**: social-media graphics, marketing poster drafts, product concept images, basic e-commerce hero images, emoji/stickers, text rendering (near-perfect accuracy), basic photorealism (87% persuasiveness score) 🟢
- **Not yet covered**: IP character consistency control, fine-grained style customization (LoRA-level fidelity), complex workflow orchestration (ControlNet-precision pose control), batch production, 3D asset generation 🟡

#### Will GPT-5/5.5 support LoRA-level fine control?

**Not in the short term (2026-2027)**. OpenAI closed new-user registration for fine-tuning in May 2026; its strategic direction is "general model + prompt control" rather than "user-defined fine-tuning."

**Uncertain in the medium term (2028-2029)**. If native image generation can achieve 80% of LoRA-level style fidelity purely through prompting, LiblibAI's model community will see substantial value erosion.

#### Judgment: mainly chronic erosion, but with a risk of a "step-function leap"

Model updates roughly every 6-12 months will progressively compress the space in which professional tools can survive. **The most dangerous scenario**: a model generation suddenly supports "character consistency + style reference images + multi-image batch processing," which would constitute a sudden substitution. This risk peaks in 2027-2028.

---

### Threat 2: ByteDance Jimeng AI

**Threat level: Extremely high**

#### Latest developments

- Monthly visits exceed 16 million, with app downloads up 229% quarter-over-quarter 🟢
- Self-developed Seedream 5.0 Lite + Seedance 2.0, giving it independent control over its technology 🟢
- Many features are free, cross-subsidized through ByteDance's ecosystem (Douyin, CapCut) 🟢

#### ByteDance's full-stack advantages

| Capability layer | ByteDance's advantage | LiblibAI's gap |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Foundation model | Self-developed Seedream/Seedance | Does not build its own; relies on open source |
| Compute | One of the largest GPU clusters in China | Must purchase from cloud vendors |
| Traffic distribution | Douyin's 700 million+ DAU, CapCut | No proprietary traffic entry point |
| Data flywheel | Massive user data feeding back into the system | Data volume gap of 10-50x |
| Commercial closed loop | Create-distribute-monetize, all in one | Covers only the creation stage |
| Capital firepower | Nearly unlimited | $130 million Series B |

#### Will Jimeng enter the model-community space?

ByteDance most likely will not directly build a "model marketplace" — it doesn't fit its preference for centralized control. But it will bypass the model community via "official models + open API + CapCut plugin ecosystem," addressing users' style-customization needs at the application layer. This "changing the rules of the game" threat is bigger than direct competition.

#### LiblibAI vs. Jimeng: room for differentiation

| Dimension | LiblibAI's advantage | Sustainability |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Model diversity | 500,000+ community LoRAs, far exceeding Jimeng | 🟢 High |
| Professional depth | ComfyUI-level professional workflows | 🟡 Medium |
| Creator ecosystem | Tipping/paid revenue-sharing mechanism | 🟢 High |
| Neutrality | Not tied to a single base model | 🟡 Medium |

### Threat 3: Kuaishou Kling AI — an underestimated giant

- Reportedly planning a spin-off for independent fundraising, valued at **$20 billion**, raising $2 billion 🟢
- ARR has already reached **$500 million**, on par with Midjourney 🟢
- Kling 3.0's "All-in-One" philosophy has topped the art/design category download charts in 40+ countries' app stores globally 🟢
- Kuaishou's 2026 capital expenditure is projected at RMB 26 billion, primarily for Kling's compute 🟢

Currently focused on video generation, giving it some differentiation from LiblibAI's core image business, but as its unified multimodal framework extends to cover image capability, conflict will inevitably intensify.

---

### Threat 4: ComfyUI open-source local deployment

- Over 4 million users, 89,200 GitHub stars 🟢
- Completed a $30 million funding round in April 2026 at a $500 million valuation 🟢
- Launched Comfy Cloud, transitioning from purely local to a local + cloud hybrid model

**Judgment: long-term chronic erosion**. It mainly siphons off high-end professional users, with roughly a 30-40% overlap with LiblibAI's core user base. Rising GPU prices have suppressed local deployment in the short term, but in the medium term (2028-2030), as VRAM requirements fall and frameworks are optimized, the barrier to local deployment will drop significantly.

## III. Analysis of response strategies

### Strategy 1: Agent-based transformation (Lovart/Xingliu/LibTV)

#### What is LiblibAI actually doing?

- **Lovart**: 10 million+ users, 70+ countries, ARR of ~$80 million 🟢
- **Xingliu Agent**: the domestic version of Lovart, connecting to a dozen-plus large models, supporting one-stop generation of images/video/audio/3D 🟢
- **LibTV**: launched in March 2026, drew 100,000 users on its first day online, and **passed $1 million in daily revenue within a month** 🟢

#### Judgment: the right direction, but facing a "two-front war" risk

**Why it's the right call**:
1. Moving up from the tool layer to the workflow layer raises the company's position in the value chain and improves stickiness
2. It solves the pain point of "users don't know which model to choose"
3. LibTV's $1 million daily revenue proves that video-agent monetization is viable

**Risks**:
1. Adobe launched Firefly AI Assistant in April 2026, integrating 30+ top AI models
2. Running four product lines simultaneously puts enormous pressure on a Series B company
3. Agent intelligence is capped by the fact that the company does not build its own foundation model

#### Recommendations

- Consolidate domestic product lines: unify Xingliu Agent and LibTV into a single "creative production agent"
- Strengthen "model orchestration" as a differentiator: have the agent automatically select the optimal combination of models

---

### Strategy 2: Going overseas (Lovart)

**This can partially offset domestic pressure, but overseas competition is equally intense.**

Favorable factors: overseas competitive intensity is lower than domestic; Civitai has declined due to a payment crisis; pricing advantage ($90/month vs. six-figure sums for a design agency)

Unfavorable factors: Adobe Firefly's enterprise-grade integration far exceeds Lovart's; Midjourney V7/V8 leads in quality; data-security concerns; geopolitical risk

**Recommendation**: focus on "full-chain design automation for SMBs/independent creators"; localize in core markets (Southeast Asia, Japan and Korea); consider operating and financing the overseas business as an independent entity

---

### Strategy 3: Deepening the ecosystem (raising switching costs)

**Recommendations**:
- Encourage creators to accumulate "creation data assets" (training datasets, tuning history), which are harder to migrate than model files
- Strengthen creators' social graphs (follows, collaborations, communities)
- Launch "platform-exclusive incentive" and "exclusivity period" mechanisms
- Cut the creator revenue-share cut from 15% to 10%, and launch a "creator fund"

---

### Strategy 4: Vertical-industry penetration

| Industry | Priority | Rationale |
|------|--------|------|
| Gaming | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Highest value from the LoRA ecosystem, hardest for general-purpose models to replace, strong willingness to pay |
| E-commerce | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Existing customer base; Xingliu Agent can serve them directly |
| Short dramas / film | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | LibTV is precisely positioned here; $1 million daily revenue validates PMF |
| Advertising / marketing | ⭐⭐⭐ | A video + design combination play |
| Architectural design | ⭐⭐⭐ | Low competition |

**Gaming should be the top priority**: IP character consistency, multi-angle image generation, and specific art styles are exactly the scenarios where the LoRA ecosystem excels and general-purpose models are hardest-pressed to replace.

## IV. Competitive endgame projection (2029)

### Most likely landscape (probability ~50%): "three-tier stratification"

| Tier | Players | Positioning |
|------|------|------|
| General-purpose layer | GPT/Gemini/Doubao | 80% of mass-market users complete simple needs here |
| Platform layer | Jimeng + Kling + LiblibAI + ComfyUI | Professional/semi-professional creators |
| Industry layer | Adobe + various vertical SaaS | Deeply embedded in industry workflows |

LiblibAI is the third-ranked player in the platform layer, valued at $1.0-3.0 billion. Lovart develops independently as a second-tier player overseas.

### Optimistic scenario (~20%): LiblibAI becomes "the GitHub of AI creation"

The model community's network effect strengthens, the agent transformation succeeds, Lovart's ARR passes $300 million, and a large Series C round is completed.

### Pessimistic scenario (~30%): marginalized or acquired

General-purpose foundation models achieve fine-grained control breakthroughs, ByteDance enters the model-community space, and the agent transition underperforms → acquired by ByteDance/Alibaba/Tencent ($500 million-$1.5 billion)

---

## V. Core conclusion

LiblibAI's survival hinges on three variables:

| Variable | What it determines | Current trend |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Whether general-purpose foundation models break through on fine-grained control | The long-term value of the model community | Safe in the short term (a 2-year window), at risk in the medium term |
| The speed and quality of the agent transformation | Whether it can upgrade into an irreplaceable creation platform | Positive (LibTV's $1 million daily revenue), but resources are spread thin |
| The independence and scale of the overseas business | Whether it gains strategic depth | Lovart is growing strongly, but faces competition from Adobe/Midjourney |

**The three most urgent priorities**:
1. Focus the product lineup — consolidate the three domestic product lines into a single agent-centric platform
2. Accelerate gaming-industry penetration — the vertical use case where LoRA value is highest
3. Push for Lovart's independent fundraising — with $80 million ARR, the conditions are already in place
