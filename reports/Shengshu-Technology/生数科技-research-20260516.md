## Step 3: Moat evaluation — "economic moat"

### Verification of five moat types

| Moat type | Assessment | Rating | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| **Brand / pricing power** | Weak | ★★☆☆☆ | API pricing is 55% below the industry average, indicating a lack of pricing power; brand awareness has some recognition among professional creators, but falls far short of Kling's and Jimeng's consumer-side visibility |
| **Switching costs** | Weak | ★★☆☆☆ | Users can switch to competitors like Kling, Jimeng, or Runway at low cost; API customers have some integration cost, but it does not amount to strong lock-in |

| **Scale effects** | Medium | ★★★☆☆ | Fixed costs for model training are high, and marginal inference cost falls as user volume grows; but the company is at a clear scale disadvantage versus ByteDance (200 billion yuan in capex) and Kuaishou |

### Moat trend assessment

| Time horizon | Judgment | Rationale |
|---------|------|------|
| Past 2 years | Widening | Went from zero to the world's No. 2 technical ranking, surpassed 30 million users, and built an initial base of technical and brand accumulation |
| Present | Under pressure | Kling and Jimeng are catching up quickly, open-source models are eroding pricing headroom, and technical homogenization is intensifying |

> **"Will this moat still exist in 10 years? What could destroy it?"**
>
> Frankly, the current "moat" looks more like a "time window" from technical first-mover advantage than a genuine structural barrier. Factors that could destroy it: (1) open-source models catching up to closed-source quality, driving API pricing to zero; (2) giants like ByteDance and Kuaishou crushing it with traffic and compute; (3) a new-generation architecture rendering U-ViT obsolete. In 10 years, this moat most likely will **not** remain — unless Shengshu Technology can build deep, scenario-specific irreplaceability on the enterprise side.

## Step 6: Industry and civilizational trends — "civilizational evolution framework"

### Industry positioning

AI video generation sits at an **early stage of a civilization-level paradigm shift** — comparable to internet search in the 2000s or mobile internet in the 2010s. Video is the highest information-density medium, and AI is lowering the barrier to video creation from "a professional team plus a million-dollar budget" to "one person plus a few hundred dollars" — a qualitative change in productivity.

### Historical technology revolution analogies

| Analogy | Corresponding stage in AI video generation | Lesson |
|------|--------------------------|------|
| Printing → desktop publishing | From professional production to individual creation | The eventual winner was the platform (Adobe), not any single tool |
| Film photography → digital cameras → smartphone photography | Creation barriers keep falling | Tool vendors' margins get squeezed, while platforms and content owners profit |
| Search engines (2000s) | Many players battling it out → the eventual winner takes all | The current race may ultimately leave only 2-3 players standing |

### TAM analysis

| Definition | Market size | CAGR | Source |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Narrow AI video generator market | About $0.7–0.8 billion in 2025 → about $3.3–3.4 billion by 2033 | 18-20% | Grand View Research |

**Key takeaway**: The narrow market remains small ($0.7-0.8 billion), but growth is fast and the ceiling is far off. The broad market (including AI video editing, analysis, etc.) offers much more room.

### Position in the industry value chain

```
Compute layer   →   Model layer      →   Application layer   →   Distribution layer
(NVIDIA/Huawei)     (Shengshu/Runway)    (industry applications)  (ByteDance/Kuaishou/YouTube)
```

Shengshu Technology positions itself as a dual-role "model layer + application layer" player, providing both underlying model capability (MaaS/API) and a consumer-facing application (SaaS). The advantage of this positioning is revenue from both ends; the disadvantage is facing competition on both ends.

> **"Looking back 20 years from now, will this company be 'the Standard Oil of this era' or 'a flash-in-the-pan 3Com'?"**
>
> It's most likely to be **somewhere in between**. AI video generation as a race will generate enormous value, but the "model layer" profit may get squeezed from both upstream and downstream — upstream compute costs are high, and downstream application scenarios are controlled by big tech. The eventual winners are more likely to be **platforms with distribution channels** (ByteDance, Kuaishou, YouTube), rather than pure technology providers. If Shengshu Technology were acquired (say, absorbed into the Alibaba Cloud ecosystem), that could be a decent outcome; if it survives independently, the challenges it faces are extremely severe. Twenty years from now, the "Standard Oil" of AI video is more likely to be a company like ByteDance rather than a pure model-layer player.

### Reverse DCF: what does the current valuation imply?

Assuming a $2 billion valuation, with:
- A required annual return of 20% (VC standard)
- Exit at 15x PS in 5 years

Then the **ARR needed in 5 years** = $2 billion × (1.2^5) / 15 ≈ **$330 million**

If current ARR really is around $20 million, that requires roughly 16.5x growth over 5 years, or a compound annual growth rate of about 75%. For a fast-growing AI startup, that is aggressive but not impossible — provided it can keep growing while wedged between big-tech competitors.

### Three-scenario valuation (adapted for a private-market investor's perspective)

Since the company is not listed, an "ARR × PS multiple" framework is used:

| Scenario | 2028 ARR assumption | PS applied | Implied valuation | vs. current valuation |
|------|-------------|--------|---------|-----------|

**Probability-weighted valuation** (subjective judgment):
- Optimistic probability 20%: $10 billion × 20% = $2 billion
- Neutral probability 50%: $2.4 billion × 50% = $1.2 billion
- Pessimistic probability 30%: $250 million × 30% = $75 million
- **Probability-weighted valuation ≈ $3.275 billion**

Under this probability weighting, the current $2 billion valuation has about 64% upside, but the distribution is severely right-skewed (a 30% chance of an 87.5% loss), so the risk/reward is asymmetric.
