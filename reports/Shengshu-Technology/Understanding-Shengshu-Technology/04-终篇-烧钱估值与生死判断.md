## First, a fundamental question: can AI video generation actually make money?

Before discussing what Shengshu Technology is worth, we have to answer an industry-level question first: **can this business — AI video generation — actually make money?**

Sora's numbers give an unsettling answer:

| Metric | Sora's data | Source |
|------|-----------|------|
| Average daily inference cost | **$15 million** | Forbes, Cantor Fitzgerald |
| Total lifetime revenue | **$2.1 million** | Media reports |
| Cost of a single 10-second video | About **$1.30** | Analyst estimate |
| User retention | Approaching **zero** after 60 days | Third-party data |
| Outcome | Shut down on **April 26, 2026** | OpenAI announcement |

**It burned more money in a single day than it earned in its entire lifetime.** This is, to date, the most expensive failure in the AI video generation race.

But Sora's failure doesn't mean the whole race is unworkable. Its failure had specific causes:

1. **Overpriced**: Sora was bundled with the ChatGPT Plus subscription ($20/month), with no independent, flexible pricing
2. **Poor product experience**: Slow generation, low success rate, inconsistent visuals
3. **Strategic misalignment**: OpenAI's core focus is ChatGPT, and Sora was only a side project that never received enough product investment
4. **Runaway inference cost**: Insufficient cost optimization specific to video inference

**The counter-argument**: Kling has proven that AI video generation can generate revenue — its ARR has already reached $500 million. But Kling's profitability is equally unknown; $500 million in revenue doesn't mean it can turn a profit, and gross margin could well be negative. Across the industry's 800-plus companies, fewer than a fifth have achieved a viable commercial loop.

## Industry economics: inference cost is the line between life and death

The cost structure of AI video generation is entirely different from that of traditional SaaS:

```
        Traditional SaaS cost structure       AI video generation cost structure

        ┌──────────────────┐         ┌──────────────────┐
        │  Fixed costs (low) │         │ Model training     │
        │  Servers, bandwidth│         │  (extremely high)  │
        │                    │         │  GPU clusters, data │
        ├──────────────────┤         ├──────────────────┤
        │ Marginal cost      │         │ Inference cost      │
        │ (extremely low)    │         │  (high)             │
        │ Nearly $0 per new  │         │  Every second of    │
        │ user               │         │  generated video    │
        │                    │         │  burns GPU compute   │
        └──────────────────┘         └──────────────────┘

        Gross margin: 70-80%           Gross margin: potentially negative
```

The appeal of traditional SaaS lies in marginal cost approaching zero — one more user adds almost no cost. But AI video generation **burns significant GPU compute every time a video is generated**, so the marginal cost stays stubbornly high.

This means: **the more users you have, the more you lose** — unless per-unit inference cost can be driven down to a level users' payments can cover.

### Shengshu Technology's cost dilemma

| Dimension | Situation | Risk level |
|------|------|---------|
| GPU sourcing | Mostly purchased externally (no large-scale proprietary GPU cluster) | High |
| Huawei Ascend adaptation | Partially migrated to Ascend 950PR | Medium (eases part of the chip-supply-disruption risk) |
| API pricing | $0.0375/second (55% below the industry average) | High (low pricing means lower gross margin) |
| Inference cost trend | Hardware advances + algorithm optimization will keep lowering it | Favorable (but the pace of decline is unknown) |

**Shengshu Technology is very likely currently at a stage of negative gross margin.** This is common among fast-growing startups, but the key question is: **how much time and cash does it have before inference cost falls to the break-even point?**

With cumulative funding of 3 billion yuan, if the monthly burn rate is in the range of 100-300 million yuan (based on comparable AI startups), the cash runway is roughly 10-30 months. **That's why it raised 2.6 billion yuan within two months — not because it's in high demand, but because it genuinely needs the cash.**

## Reverse calculation: what expectations does the $2 billion imply?

Using a reverse DCF (discounted cash flow) approach, what conditions are needed to support a $2 billion valuation:

**Assumptions**:
- VC requires an annual return of **20%**
- Exit at **15x PS** in 5 years

**Then the ARR needed in 5 years** = $2 billion x (1.2^5) / 15 = about **$330 million**

Growing from $20 million to $330 million requires **16.5x growth** over 5 years, corresponding to a compound annual growth rate of about **75%**.

| Scenario | Assumption | ARR in 5 years | Difficulty of achieving it |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Optimistic | 100%+ annual growth | $500 million+ | Requires doubling for 5 straight years — Kling's current level |
| Neutral | 50% annual growth | About $150 million | Growth decelerates year over year, falls short of the exit-valuation requirement |
| Pessimistic | 20% annual growth | About $50 million | Squeezed by big tech, weak commercialization |

**Conclusion: the $2 billion valuation requires 75% annual growth sustained for 5 years for VCs to earn a reasonable return. That's not impossible, but achieving that growth rate while wedged between big-tech players is a huge challenge.**

2. The **neutral scenario (+20%)** means: the current valuation is basically fair, with little margin of safety
3. The trigger conditions for the **pessimistic scenario (-87.5%)**: a big-tech price war + open source catching up to closed source + persistently weak commercialization

**A 30% probability of an 87.5% loss** — this risk/reward structure indicates: the $2 billion price offers **insufficient margin of safety** for the average investor.

## IPO pricing speculation

Shengshu Technology completed its joint-stock conversion in March 2026, and a Hong Kong IPO could launch sometime between the second half of 2026 and 2027.

### Hong Kong's stance on AI companies

| Reference case | Pre-IPO valuation | Post-IPO performance | Lesson |
|---------|---------|---------|------|
| MiniMax | About $10 billion | To be observed | Hong Kong tends to value AI companies conservatively |
| SenseTime | IPO market cap about HK$140 billion | Market cap shrank significantly after listing | Hong Kong doesn't buy "pure technology stories" |
| Kuaishou | Market cap once exceeded HK$1 trillion at IPO | Subsequently halved, then halved again | Secondary-market valuation logic is entirely different from primary-market logic |

**Hong Kong investors place more weight on profitability and cash flow than on a technology story.** If Shengshu Technology IPOs at a valuation of $2 billion or more, it will need to demonstrate a clear commercialization path and audited financial data.

**Possible IPO pricing range (pure speculation)**:
- If it IPOs in the second half of 2026: it could fluctuate around the Series B valuation ($2 billion), depending on market sentiment at the time
- If it IPOs in 2027: it will depend on the ARR level at that point. If ARR reaches $300-500 million, the valuation could move up; if ARR stagnates, it could face a valuation discount

## Summary: three layers of truth about Shengshu Technology

Having gone through the whole series, Shengshu Technology's picture can be summed up in three layers:

**Layer one (most certain): the technology is strong.** The original U-ViT architecture, a top-two ranking in the global Vidu Q3 benchmark, a top-tier Tsinghua team — these are all third-party-verified facts.

**Layer two (contested): commercialization is hard.** ARR of only $20 million, facing a 25x revenue gap against Kling and an unlimited-ammunition Jimeng, with 80% of the industry loss-making — whether technical leadership can translate into commercial success has, so far, no answer.

**Layer three (least certain): the valuation is expensive.** A PS of 100x (based on known ARR) prices in a great deal of optimism, leaving insufficient margin of safety. Unless end-2025 ARR really does reach $200 million (bringing PS down to 10x), the current valuation is not investor-friendly.

**One-sentence summary**:

> **Shengshu Technology is an AI video generation startup with top-two technology globally. The core question it faces isn't "is the technology strong enough," but "in a fight against big-tech crushing power and a cash-burning race, can an independent startup survive long enough to reach profitability."**

There is no standard answer to this question. The answer depends on how fast the AI video market grows, the trajectory of falling inference costs, the depth of synergy with the Alibaba Cloud ecosystem, and whether Luo Yihang can get commercialization off the ground within 12 months.
