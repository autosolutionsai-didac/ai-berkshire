## VII. 3-Year Outlook

### Optimistic scenario

- ARR grows from $300 million to $1.5–2.0 billion, with LibTV becoming the industry-standard tool for AI video production
- Overseas expansion via Lovart opens up the design market, lifting international revenue share to 30%+
- Valuation of $5–10 billion, with a possible IPO launch
- Analogous path: early Canva → present-day Canva ($40 billion valuation)

### Neutral scenario

- ARR grows to $600 million–$1 billion, but growth decelerates
- AIGC products from major players (ByteDance, Tencent) divert some users
- Increasing homogenization of AI video tools requires LibTV to keep investing to stay ahead
- Valuation range of $5–8 billion

### Pessimistic scenario

- Rapid democratization of foundation-model capability erodes the tool layer's moat (once underlying models are open-sourced, upper-layer tools lose differentiation)
- ByteDance Jimeng/Kling squeeze paying users through traffic advantages and free-tier strategies
- AI content copyright/compliance risk erupts, triggering regulatory tightening
- Overseas expansion is hindered (data compliance, geopolitics)
- Average revenue per account hits a low ceiling, and $300 million ARR proves to be the peak rather than a starting point

### Core judgment

Evoken is currently one of the fastest-growing companies at China's AI application layer. Over the next three years, it will likely continue growing rapidly toward the $1 billion ARR level. The biggest risk is that the tool layer's moat isn't deep enough — if underlying models iterate too quickly, upper-layer applications could be reduced to mere "wrappers." The founder's product instincts (a ByteDance alumnus) and ability to monetize from consumer to enterprise are the core competitive advantages.
