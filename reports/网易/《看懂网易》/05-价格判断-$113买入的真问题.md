# Price Judgment — The Real Question Behind Buying at $113

> *Understanding NetEase* series · Episode 05 · Decision · Series finale
> Reading time: about 7 minutes

---

## Before we start

This is the final piece in the *Understanding NetEase* series. The first four episodes covered the cognitive illusion, the gaming empire, Cloud Music and Youdao, and Ding Lei plus the risk factors.

This final piece answers the most direct question: **is $113 actually worth paying?**

The conclusion framework up front: we won't hand you a precise target price. Instead, we'll tell you under what conditions this looks like a "golden pit" and under what conditions it's a "value trap" — so you can decide based on your own judgment.

## I. Valuation breakdown: cross-checking four methods

### Method one: P/E

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 GAAP net profit | ~35.3 billion yuan (estimate) |
| 2025 Non-GAAP net profit | 37.3 billion yuan |
| Current market cap | ~530 billion yuan ($72.9 billion) |
| GAAP P/E | ~15x |
| Non-GAAP P/E | ~13x |
| Forward P/E (analyst consensus) | ~12.3x |

Buying a company with a 33% net margin that is still growing at 13x Non-GAAP P/E is on the low side within the Chinese internet sector.

For reference: Tencent currently trades at roughly 18-20x P/E, Pinduoduo at roughly 10-12x, and Bilibili is still unprofitable. A 13x P/E isn't the cheapest in this peer group, but given NetEase's earnings quality (33% net margin, 49.6 billion yuan in free cash flow), this pricing clearly doesn't carry an adequate valuation premium.

### Method two: EV/EBITDA

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Enterprise value (EV) | Market cap 530 billion − net cash 163.5 billion = ~366.5 billion |
| EBITDA | ~39.2 billion (estimate) |
| **EV/EBITDA** | **~9.35x** |

At 9.35x EV/EBITDA — after stripping out cash, the market is pricing NetEase's operating business at under 10x pre-tax earnings.

Benchmarked against global gaming companies: Activision Blizzard's EV/EBITDA when acquired by Microsoft was about 18x; EA is around 12x; Take-Two around 15x. 9.35x is clearly on the low end within the global gaming industry.

**But note**: Chinese ADRs as a group carry a "China discount" — geopolitical risk, differences in governance structure, and other factors mean overseas investors demand a higher risk premium. 9.35x may not signal "undervaluation" so much as "a fair reflection of the China discount."

### Method three: Free cash flow

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 free cash flow | ~49.6 billion yuan |
| Current market cap | ~530 billion yuan |
| P/FCF | ~9.9x |
| FCF yield | ~9.4% |

Buying the whole company at roughly 10x free cash flow means that if you bought NetEase for 530 billion, you'd get back 49.6 billion in cash every year — a 10.7-year payback. Net out the 163.5 billion in cash on the balance sheet (which arrives the moment you buy), and you're actually only paying 366.5 billion for the operating business — a **7.4-year payback**.

The "quality" of that 49.6 billion in free cash flow is worth noting: NetEase's capital expenditure is just 1.1 billion yuan — the company barely needs to reinvest to sustain its current earnings power. This "asset-light, high-cash-flow" combination is uncommon among listed companies globally.

### Method four: Net cash plus sum-of-the-parts valuation

Breaking NetEase into pieces and valuing each separately:

| Segment | Valuation method | Value (billion yuan) |
|------|---------|------------|
| Net cash | 1:1 | 163.5 |
| Gaming business | 12-15x profit (30-35 billion in profit) | 360-525 |
| Cloud Music | 15-20x profit (1.6 billion in profit) | 24-32 |
| Youdao | 3-5x revenue (5.9 billion in revenue, not yet stably profitable) | 17.7-29.5 |
| Innovation & other | 5-8x profit (estimated 1-1.5 billion in profit) | 5-12 |
| **Total** | — | **570.2-762.0** |
| Current market cap | — | 530 |
| **Undervaluation** | — | **7%-30%** |

All four methods point in the same direction: **at the current price, NetEase is undervalued by 10-30%, depending on your assumptions about gaming growth.**

**The counter-argument**: every valuation model here assumes profits can be sustained or grown. If the gaming industry hits a systemic shock (tighter game-license approvals, AI disruption, shifting user preferences), or if Marvel Rivals fails to develop as hoped and overseas expansion remains stuck — a 13x P/E might not be "undervaluation" but rather "fair pricing of growth uncertainty."

## II. Comparison with Tencent: which is the better buy?

This is the question that's unavoidable when investing in NetEase — for the same money, is Tencent or NetEase the better home?

| Dimension | NetEase | Tencent |
|------|------|------|
| P/E (Non-GAAP) | ~13x | ~18-20x |
| Net margin | 33% | ~28% |
| Gross margin | 64.3% | ~53% |
| FCF | ~49.6 billion | Higher, but capex is also higher |
| Net cash as % of market cap | 31% | Lower |
| Business diversification | Gaming 82%, highly concentrated | Gaming 38% + social + advertising + fintech + cloud |
| Overseas revenue | ~10% | ~30% |
| Growth runway | Mainly overseas expansion and new titles | Multi-driver |
| Governance risk | Ding Lei holds 45.5% and decides alone | Professional management team |

**The case for NetEase**: cheaper (P/E more than 30% lower), higher margins, "cleaner" cash flow (capex is minimal), and a thicker net-cash cushion.

**The case for Tencent**: more diversified business (risk is more spread out), stronger overseas presence (3x NetEase's), more growth drivers, better governance structure, and an exclusive lock on the 53.5-billion-yuan mini-program gaming market.

**What the data shows**: NetEase looks more like a "high-dividend, low-growth" value stock — extremely efficient at generating profit but with limited room to grow. Tencent looks more like a "moderate-dividend, moderate-to-high-growth" blend of growth and value — slightly less efficient at generating profit but with a wider growth path.

**This isn't a question of "which is better,"** it's a question of "what do you want." If you're after certainty and cash returns, NetEase fits better. If you're after long-term growth and business breadth, Tencent fits better. The two aren't mutually exclusive — allocating to both in different proportions is also a reasonable strategy.

## III. Where's the margin of safety?

### Layer one: 163.5 billion yuan in net cash

163.5 billion yuan in net cash on the balance sheet, equal to 31% of market cap. In plain terms: **buy the entire company and three-tenths of your money comes straight back off the balance sheet.** The remaining seven-tenths buys you an operating business earning 37.3 billion yuan a year (Non-GAAP).

### Layer two: 49.6 billion yuan in free cash flow

49.6 billion yuan in free cash flow every year — with capex of only 1.1 billion. That means even if NetEase stopped growing and stopped developing new games starting today, it would still generate close to 50 billion in cash every year.

More critically, there's the **willingness to distribute**: buybacks exceeding $2 billion, a dividend of $2/ADS, a 60% payout ratio — management is returning cash to shareholders.

### Layer three: the resilience of the gaming business

Fantasy Westward Journey set a new record after 23 years of operation — that kind of product longevity is an asset that is extremely hard to replicate. Even if every new title fails and overseas expansion is completely blocked, NetEase's gaming revenue is unlikely to fall off a cliff over the next 3-5 years, resting on its existing evergreen product lineup alone.

**Three layers of cushion stacked together: net cash equal to three-tenths of market cap, nearly 50 billion yuan in annual free cash flow, and an extremely resilient core product lineup.** That makes the probability of a "large loss" quite low.

## IV. Under what conditions is this a "value trap"?

A high margin of safety doesn't mean a loss is impossible. If the following two things happen **at the same time**, the current price may not be the bottom:

### 1. Gaming growth stalls

If, over the next 2-3 years:
- Fantasy Westward Journey's concurrent-user count begins a substantive decline (an irreversible aging of the player base)
- Marvel Rivals fails to sustain annual revenue above $300 million (the brand licensing doesn't earn back its cost)
- No new hit title emerges
- Overseas revenue share stays stuck around 10%

Under this scenario, the 92.1-billion-yuan gaming revenue base could enter a slow decline. A company with declining revenue — even one with high margins and strong cash flow — will not be given a fair valuation by the market.

### 2. The China discount keeps widening

If US-China relations continue to deteriorate and the systemic discount on Chinese ADRs widens from the current 30-40% to 50-60% — then even with no change in fundamentals, the share price could fall further.

**The probability of both happening together is moderate-to-low, but if they do stack up, there could be another 15-25% of downside.**

## V. Under what conditions is this a "golden pit"?

If **any two** of the following three things happen, the current price will, in hindsight, clearly have been a golden pit:

### 1. Marvel Rivals becomes an evergreen title

If, after 1-2 more years of live operation, Marvel Rivals:
- Stabilizes concurrent users above 150,000-200,000
- Sustains annual revenue above $500 million
- Recovers its Steam positive-review rate to above 70%

That would mean NetEase has successfully built a globally significant long-running title — overseas revenue share could rise from 10% to 15-20%, opening up an entirely new growth runway.

### 2. Overseas revenue share rises to 20%

Going from 10% to 20% would mean overseas gaming revenue growing from about 9 billion yuan to about 20 billion yuan (assuming total gaming revenue grows to 100+ billion). A shift of this magnitude would be enough for the market to re-rate NetEase's growth outlook — upgrading it from a "Chinese gaming company" to a "global gaming company."

### 3. The China-ADR discount narrows

If US-China relations ease, or if confidence in China's internet sector growth recovers, the systemic discount on Chinese ADRs could narrow from 30-40% to 15-20%. Valuation recovery on this one factor alone would imply 30-50% of upside in the share price.

**If "Marvel Rivals becomes evergreen" and "the China-ADR discount narrows" both happen — P/E recovers from 13x to 18-20x, implying a share price of $155-175. Starting from $113, that's a gain of 37-55%.**

## VI. Is $113 actually worth paying?

We won't give a precise answer — instead, a decision framework.

### Five questions to ask yourself before buying

**First, how do you judge NetEase's gaming growth?** If you believe Fantasy Westward Journey has another 5-10 years left in it, that Marvel Rivals can become a mid-tier-or-better evergreen title, and that Youdao's AI business can scale — then buying at 13x P/E offers a considerable return. If you believe the evergreen titles are aging, that new products lack successors, and that overseas expansion won't take off — then 13x P/E is just "fair pricing."

**Second, how do you view Ding Lei as a person?** A 45.5% stake means buying NetEase is buying Ding Lei. If you trust his product judgment and capital-allocation ability, that's a plus. If you're concerned about the "one person decides everything" risk, that's a minus.

**Third, how do you price the 163.5 billion yuan in net cash?** If you count it 1:1 (cash is cash), then the gaming business, net of cash, trades at only about 10x P/E — very cheap. But if you're skeptical about whether "a Chinese company can truly return cash to shareholders," the cash needs to be discounted. The current 60% payout ratio and ongoing buybacks suggest Ding Lei is returning capital to shareholders — but will that change in the future? Uncertain.

**Fourth, how long is your holding period?** Whether Marvel Rivals can go evergreen needs 1-2 years to verify; Youdao's AI business needs 3-5 years; the timeline for the China-ADR discount to narrow is uncertain. If your holding period is under 3 years, many of these variables won't have time to play out.

**Fifth, is there a better alternative for the same money?** Tencent trades at 18-20x P/E, has a more diversified business, and a stronger overseas presence — 30% more expensive but with risk spread more widely. Choosing NetEase versus Tencent comes down to your preference between "cheap but concentrated" and "a bit pricier but diversified."

### When you must re-evaluate

A handful of red lines — hitting any one of them means it's time to reassess:

1. Gaming revenue declines year-over-year for two consecutive quarters (the core growth thesis is broken)
2. Marvel Rivals' concurrent-user count falls back below 100,000 and doesn't recover (the biggest overseas bet fails)
3. Signs emerge that the Marvel brand license is difficult to renew (brand-dependency risk materializes)
4. Ding Lei sells down significantly or changes the capital-return strategy (a management signal shift)
5. Game-license policy tightens back to 2022 levels (external environment deteriorates)

Conversely, two positive signals worth watching:
- **Overseas revenue share breaks through 15%** — a sign that the international strategy is starting to work
- **Youdao's AI subscription revenue breaks through 1 billion yuan** — a sign that the second growth engine is taking shape

## VII. Final thoughts

Back to the contrast this series started with: a company with a 33% net margin, 49.6 billion yuan in free cash flow, and 163.5 billion yuan in net cash, priced at 13x P/E.

Having read all 5 episodes, the logic behind that pricing becomes clear — **the market isn't pricing "past profits," it's pricing "future uncertainty" and "the China discount."**

The uncertainty comes from: an 82% single-business dependence on gaming, a low 10% overseas share, whether Marvel Rivals can go evergreen, and the governance risk of Ding Lei deciding everything alone.

The China discount comes from: US-China relations, regulatory policy, and the systemic undervaluation of Chinese ADRs.

A 13x P/E is the market's aggregate pricing of these factors. If you believe these risks have been overblown, then $113 is a price with a margin of safety. If you believe these risks are real and could worsen, then even 13x P/E doesn't constitute an adequate margin of safety.

**Both views hold up.** The difference lies only in how you assess the probability of each variable.

An extremely profitable company facing growth challenges, a product genius of a founder with a relatively conservative approach to capital allocation, and a market that is systemically discounted for geopolitical reasons — these three things stacked together make up the NetEase investment question.

There's no textbook answer to this question. But at least, having read these 5 episodes, you should now know what the question is actually asking.
