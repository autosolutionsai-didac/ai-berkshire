## 4. Summary by Dimension

### 4.1 Business Model & Moat (Duan Yongping's Lens)

**The essence of the business**: Moutai's business isn't selling liquor — it's selling **a social currency of trust and status**. The ex-factory production cost of a bottle of Feitian Moutai is under RMB 100, the ex-factory price is RMB 1,269, and the market transaction price is above RMB 1,600 — this price-gap structure shows that consumers are buying brand premium and social value, not the liquid itself.

**Revenue Structure (2024)**:

| Segment | Revenue (RMB billion) | Share | Gross Margin |
|------|------------|------|-------|
| Moutai liquor (core) | 145.9 | 83.8% | 94.06% |
| Series liquor (secondary brands) | 24.7 | 14.2% | 79.87% |
| Other | ~3.5 | ~2.0% | — |

**Triple Irreplicable Moat**:

1. **Physical scarcity**: The microbial environment of the 7.5-km core production zone in Moutai Town cannot be replicated; base liquor production capacity is capped at roughly 56,000 tonnes/year
2. **Time barrier**: The "12987" process (one year of production, two rounds of grain input, nine steamings, eight fermentations, seven extractions) plus four years of aging means liquor produced today can't be sold for another five years. Competitors can't buy time even with money
3. **Monopoly on social consensus**: "The best liquor = Moutai" is fixed in the minds of hundreds of millions of people; the switching cost of this collective consensus is extremely high

**Signs of weakening flywheel momentum**:

- In 2025, the Feitian wholesale price plunged from RMB 2,200 to RMB 1,485, shattering the "only rises, never falls" myth
- The i-Moutai platform lets consumers buy Feitian directly at the official price of RMB 1,499, weakening the "hard to get a single bottle" sense of scarcity
- The strictest-ever anti-drinking directive in May 2025 directly hit government and business banquet occasions

**Duan Yongping's "good business" assessment**: Differentiation ★★★★★, pricing power ★★★★☆ (ex-factory pricing is extremely strong, but the terminal wholesale price is no longer ironclad), sustainable competitive advantage ★★★★★.

**Growth outlook**: Revenue is projected to grow at an annualized 6-9% over the next five years, with the growth driver shifting from "simultaneous volume and price increases" to "price hikes + capacity release + rising direct-sales mix."

**Munger-style "inverting the problem"**: Moutai is unlikely to be disrupted by another baijiu brand, but the entire baijiu category could shrink over a 20-30 year horizon. Consider Japanese sake as an analogy: production volume fell 75% over 50 years, yet the category leader, "Dassai," actually grew. Moutai will most likely follow the same path — the industry shrinks, and the leader gets stronger.

### 5.4 The Fatal Flaw in DCF: A Stress Test

**What happens if WACC drops from 9% to 4%?**

| Scenario | WACC 9% | WACC 4% | Change |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Optimistic | RMB 2,630 | RMB 13,676 | +420% |
| Neutral | RMB 1,557 | RMB 6,662 | +328% |
| Pessimistic | RMB 1,016 | RMB 3,711 | +265% |

Valuation simply explodes. The cause lies in the terminal value formula `FCF / (WACC - g)`: when WACC=4% and g=2.5%, the denominator is only 1.5%, blowing the terminal value up to RMB 10.7 trillion — 88% of the total valuation.

**This exposes DCF's fundamental problem**: the closer the discount rate and terminal growth rate get to each other, the more the valuation tends toward infinity. If a valuation method's output differs by several multiples with just a slight tweak to the inputs, it isn't giving you an answer — it's giving you an illusion.

So what should WACC actually be? In theory it equals **opportunity cost** — what this money could earn if it weren't invested in Moutai. But every investor's opportunity cost differs, so for the same company, different people will naturally arrive at different "intrinsic values." DCF's "precision" is false precision.

## 10. A Reflection on Valuation Methodology

The biggest takeaway from this research isn't arriving at a number for what Moutai is worth — it's understanding **the boundaries within which different valuation methods apply**.

**The DCF illusion**: It looks the most "scientific," but it's actually the most fragile. As WACC moves from 9% to 4%, the valuation jumps from RMB 1,557 to RMB 6,662 — a 4x difference. The terminal value formula `FCF/(WACC-g)` makes the valuation tend toward infinity as the two parameters converge. All of this "precision" rests on guesses about WACC and terminal growth rate — precisely the two parameters that are hardest to pin down.

**WACC is, at its core, opportunity cost**: The discount rate isn't a physical constant — it's "what this money could earn if not invested in Moutai." If your alternative is a Treasury bond (1.75%), Moutai looks very valuable; if you can reliably earn 15% elsewhere, Moutai isn't worth that price. For the same company, different investors will naturally compute different intrinsic values.

**The constraint on terminal growth rate**: It cannot exceed the long-run GDP growth rate (otherwise the company's profits would eventually exceed the entire nation's GDP). Setting Moutai's rate at 2-3% reflects "it can keep pace with inflation through price hikes, but volume growth is capped by population decline."

**A better approach is the process of elimination**: Rather than chasing a single precise number, use several simple methods to bound a range. An earnings yield of 4.8% far exceeds the 1.75% Treasury yield, PE at 20.8x sits at a historic low, and the dividend yield of 3.8% is growing each year — these three facts are more persuasive than any DCF calculation.

**Buffett's wisdom**: "If something needs a precise calculation to tell you it's cheap, it isn't cheap enough." What matters most for Moutai isn't whether it's worth RMB 1,557 or RMB 1,717, but whether it's a good business and whether the current price is reasonable. The answer to the former is a confident "yes"; the answer to the latter is "most likely yes."
