### 3. Price war

| Share of dealers with price inversion | - | 84.4% | 81.9% |

> Sources: [Securities Times](https://stcn.com/article/detail/1679241.html), [Gasgoo](https://i.gasgoo.com/news/70444594.html), [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/wm/2025-01-06/doc-ineeacvr2256440.shtml)

**Key events**:
- **2023**: Tesla fired the first shot, slashing prices on the Model 3/Y and triggering industry-wide follow-on cuts. Over 30 brands joined the price war.
- **2024**: More than 70 brands and 330+ models participated in various forms. Industry profit margin fell to 4.3%, below the overall industrial average.
- **2025**: The price war moved from "grabbing share" into a "fight for survival" phase. Single-month margin in December was just 1.8%, a historic low. OEMs pushed the pressure down the supply chain by extending payment terms (in some cases to 12 months) and demanding price cuts exceeding 10%.

### 3. The fate of gasoline vehicles

**Short term (2026-2028)**:
- Sales of pure gasoline new vehicles will rapidly shrink to a 30-35% share
- Mainstream joint-venture brands are accelerating the rollout of PHEV/EREV models (e.g., Toyota, Volkswagen)
- Some pure-gasoline models are being discontinued, with production lines shifting to hybrids

**Medium term (2028-2030)**:
- The share of new vehicles that are pure gasoline in the traditional sense will fall to 15-20%
- Per the "Energy-Saving and New Energy Vehicle Technology Roadmap 2.0," average fuel consumption of new vehicles is to fall to 4.0 L/100km by 2030, forcing full-scale hybridization
- Pure gasoline vehicles will be retained only in some commercial-vehicle and special-use scenarios

**Key judgment**: Gasoline vehicles will not disappear suddenly, but will transition through "hybridization." By 2030, the vast majority of "gasoline vehicles" on the market will actually be HEV/PHEV hybrids, with pure gasoline new vehicles becoming a minority.

## III. 10-Year Outlook (2026-2035)

### 1. The ultimate structure of China's auto market

**Market size**:
- Domestic new-vehicle sales stabilizing at 32-36 million units/year (driven mainly by stock replacement)
- Overseas sales plus local production reaching 15-20 million units/year
- Chinese automakers' total global production and sales could reach 50-55 million units/year

**Competitive structure**: expected to form a "3+5+N" pyramid structure
- **3 super-automakers** (annual sales of 5 million+ units): BYD, plus 2 more emerging from among Geely, Changan, and Chery
- **5 large automakers** (annual sales of 1-3 million units): Li Auto, NIO, Xiaomi, and others, plus surviving joint-venture brands (Volkswagen China, Toyota China)
- **N niche-market players** (annual sales of 100,000-500,000 units): focused on niche tracks such as off-road, luxury, and performance

> Sources: [PwC](https://imgtec.eetrend.com/blog/2023/100570308.html), [36Kr](https://36kr.com/p/3183228334743171)

### 2. Will gasoline vehicles disappear

**Conclusion: Pure gasoline vehicles will nearly disappear, but the internal combustion engine will not fully exit**

| Milestone | Pure gasoline new-vehicle share (forecast) | Hybrid (HEV/PHEV) share | Pure EV share |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|----------|
| 2025 (actual) | ~46% | ~22% | ~32% |
| 2028 | ~25% | ~30% | ~45% |
| 2030 | ~15% | ~25% | ~60% |
| 2035 | <5% | ~15% | ~80% |

> Sources: [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/t/2025-07-10/doc-infeytkq0220191.shtml), [36Kr](https://36kr.com/p/3183228334743171)

**Key judgment**:
- By 2035, NEV penetration (including pure EV, PHEV, and EREV) will reach 85-90%
- The remaining 10-15% will be dominated by large-battery hybrids, with pure gasoline essentially exiting the passenger-vehicle market
- Pure-EV substitution in commercial vehicles (heavy trucks, etc.) is slower, with NEV penetration around 75% by 2035
- Internal combustion engines will retain value in scenarios such as range extenders and hybrids, but their role will shift from "lead actor" to "supporting actor"

**Warning signs already visible**: Neta and WM Motor have effectively exited; Jiyue abruptly shut down; some second-tier joint-venture brands (e.g., Stellantis's China brands) have contracted sharply

### 5. The global competitiveness of Chinese automakers

**Current advantages**:

| Dimension | Chinese automaker competitiveness |
|------|---------------|
| NEV technology | Global leader (battery, electric drive, PHEV technology) |
| Intelligence | First tier (end-to-end autonomous driving, smart cockpit) |
| Supply-chain cost | Significant advantage (30-40% lower than Western peers) |
| Product iteration speed | 12-18 months (vs. 36-48 months for traditional automakers) |
| Price competitiveness | 20-30% lower price for comparable-tier products |

**2030 global-structure forecast (McKinsey)**:
- Chinese companies will make up 3-4 of the global top 10 automakers (versus only BYD today)

**Major challenges**:

**Autohome's predicament**:
- Media services revenue fell from a 2019 peak of ~CNY 3.6 billion to CNY 1.15 billion in 2025 (-24.3%)

**Structural disruption to platforms in the NEV era**:

1. **Direct-sales models erode the value of leads**: Direct-sales brands such as Tesla, NIO, and Xiaomi do not need third-party lead-generation platforms, and automakers are building their own owned-traffic pools
2. **Changing content ecosystem**: Short video (Douyin/Dongchedi) is displacing image-and-text content (Autohome), radically changing how users obtain information
3. **Shorter purchase path**: The share of online configuration and online ordering for NEVs is rising, compressing the traditional "look-compare-inquire-buy" path
4. **Shrinking after-sales business**: EV maintenance needs are far below those of gasoline vehicles, putting after-sales-related business under pressure to shrink

**Forecast for platform evolution over the next 5-10 years**:

| Trend | Impact |
|------|------|
| Direct-sales brand share rising above 40% | Continued shrinkage of the lead-generation business |
| AI-assisted car selection/smart recommendations | Platform content value being replaced by AI |
| Automakers' own apps/communities | User stickiness shifting away from third-party platforms |
| Rise of Robotaxi/shared mobility | Fewer individual car-purchase decisions |
| Used cars/auto finance | Becoming a new profit-growth point for platforms |

**Core judgment**: Automotive internet platforms are facing a fundamental challenge to their business model. The traditional "traffic-leads-advertising" triangle model is being deconstructed in the era of NEV direct sales. Dongchedi, relying on ByteDance's ecosystem, still has room to grow, but Autohome needs to transform from an "auto information platform" into an "auto transaction and service platform," or it will face continued decline.

## Summary: Three Historic Turning Points in China's Auto Market

### Turning point one: NEV substitution is already irreversible
From 31.6% penetration in 2023 to 47.9% in 2025, and an expected 70%+ by 2030, NEVs have moved from a "policy-driven" phase into a "market-driven" one. This is not a linear process but the accelerating phase of an S-curve.

### Turning point two: Domestic-brand dominance is already established
The jump in domestic-brand share from 56% in 2023 to 70% in 2025 is not a temporary phenomenon but a structural shift. The window for joint-venture brands to counterattack has already closed; the question going forward is not "whether domestic brands can overtake joint ventures" but "which joint-venture brands can still survive."

### Turning point three: China is moving from an "automotive power" to an "automotive superpower"
Exports rose from 4.91 million to 7.098 million units, overseas plant construction is accelerating, and intelligent-driving technology leads the world. The forecast that 3-4 of the global top 10 automakers will be Chinese companies by 2030 is becoming reality. China's auto industry is redefining the global competitive landscape.
