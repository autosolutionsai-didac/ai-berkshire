# Tencent Music — Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Buffett's lens: is this company earning real cash, or just paper profit?

**Analysis date**: June 20, 2026
**Share price**: USD 9.08 (NYSE: TME)
**Market cap**: approx. USD 14.35 billion (RMB ~97.0 billion)
**Exchange rate**: 1 USD = 6.76 RMB

---

## I. Revenue and Profit Trends (2021-2025 + Q1 2026)

### 1.1 Revenue Trend

| Year | Revenue (RMB billion) | YoY Growth | Trend Assessment |
|------|-------------|---------|---------|
| 2021 | 31.24 | — | Dragged down by the decline in social entertainment |
| 2022 | 28.34 | -9.3% | Livestreaming regulation plus social-entertainment shrinkage |
| 2023 | 27.75 | -2.1% | Bottomed out; online music begins carrying the business |
| 2024 | 28.40 | +2.3% | Turning point confirmed, driven by subscription growth |
| 2025 | 32.90 | +15.8% | Strong rebound, broad-based growth |
| Q1 2026 | 7.90 | +7.3% | Growth slowing but still healthy |

**Sources**: TME annual reports (ir.tencentmusic.com), PRNewswire announcements, MacroTrends

**Key findings**:
- Revenue declined for three straight years from 2021 to 2023, primarily because the social-entertainment business (livestream tipping) was squeezed by regulation, falling from over 70% of revenue at its peak to roughly 20% by 2025
- 2024 was the inflection point: growth in online music services fully offset the decline in social entertainment
- 2025 saw a broad recovery — full-year online music service revenue grew 21.7%, and music subscription revenue grew 13.2%

### 1.2 Net Profit Trend (Attributable to Shareholders)

| Year | Net Profit (RMB billion) | YoY Growth | Net Margin |
|------|---------------|---------|-------|
| 2021 | 3.03 | — | 9.7% |
| 2022 | 3.68 | +21.5% | 13.0% |
| 2023 | 4.92 | +33.7% | 17.7% |
| 2024 | 6.64 | +35.0% | 23.4% |
| 2025 | 11.06 | +66.6% | 33.6% |

**Counter-check — the "watered-down" quality of 2025 net profit**:
- 2025 net profit includes a one-time gain: in Q1, the company recognized a deemed disposal gain on an associate of RMB 2.37 billion (about 21% of full-year net profit)
- **Excluding the one-time gain**: adjusted net profit was approximately RMB 8.69 billion, up roughly 30.8% year over year — still robust, but far from the headline 66.6%
- Non-IFRS net profit (excluding SBC and similar items): RMB 9.9 billion, up 22% year over year, which better reflects operating reality
- **Conclusion**: profit growth is real, but the GAAP figure is inflated by a one-time gain

### 1.3 Operating Profit Trend

| Year | Operating Profit (RMB billion) | YoY Growth | Operating Margin |
|------|-----------------|---------|----------|
| 2022 | 3.53 | — | 12.5% |
| 2023 | 4.97 | +40.8% | 17.9% |
| 2024 | 8.70 | +75.1% (estimate) | 30.6% (estimate) |
| 2025 | 13.36 | +53.4% | 40.6% |

**Rating: ★★★★☆**

> Revenue has bottomed and rebounded, and profit growth has stayed high — the transformation is clearly working. But the 2025 GAAP net-profit figure includes a one-time gain that warrants caution, and Q1 2026 growth has already slowed to 7.3%, raising a question mark over how sustainable the high growth rate is.

## II. Profitability Metrics

### 2.1 Core Ratios

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| Gross margin | 30.1% | 31.0% | 35.3% | 40.1% | 44.0% | Continuing to expand ↑ |
| Operating margin | ~7% | 12.5% | 17.9% | ~23.4% | ~28.4% (Non-IFRS ~30.2%) | Continuing to expand ↑ |
| Net margin | 9.7% | 13.0% | 17.7% | 23.4% | 33.6% (GAAP) / 30.1% (Non-IFRS) | Continuing to expand ↑ |
| ROE | ~4.5% | ~5.5% | ~7.5% | ~9.5% | ~14.3% | Improving ↑ |
| ROA | ~3% | ~3.5% | ~5% | ~6% | ~11% (estimate) | Improving ↑ |

**Sources**: TME annual reports, MacroTrends, GuruFocus (ROE 14.26%, ROIC 8.84%)

### 2.2 A Buffett-Style Reading

**Positive**:
- Gross margin rose from 30% to 44%, a 14-percentage-point improvement over five years — a very rare degree of margin expansion
- Core driver: the low-margin, high-turnover social-entertainment business has shrunk and been replaced by high-margin, subscription-based online music
- Clear operating leverage: revenue grew only 16%, but operating profit grew 53%

**Counter-check — can the margin expansion continue?**:
- Social-entertainment revenue has already shrunk to a low base, so the "removal of a drag" effect on margins going forward will weaken
- Music licensing costs could rise: as record labels see TME's profitability improve, they have more negotiating leverage
- ROE is only 14.3% — improving, but still far below what a truly excellent business earns (25%+), largely because a large cash pile on the balance sheet is dragging down returns on capital

**Rating: ★★★★☆**

> The margin-expansion trend is excellent — the shift from "buying scale with losses" to a "high-margin subscription model" is nearly complete. But the low ROE is a real flaw; Buffett would ask: why isn't the excess cash being returned to shareholders?

**Sources**: MacroTrends, GuruFocus, TME annual reports

### 3.2 Capital Expenditure

TME runs an asset-light model, and capital expenditure is extremely low:
- Average annual capex of roughly USD 100-150 million, only 2-3% of revenue
- FCF conversion is very high, close to 85-90% of operating cash flow

### 3.3 Buffett's "Real Cash" Test

| Test | Result | Verdict |
|-------|------|------|
| FCF > net profit? | Average FCF/net profit exceeded 100% from 2021-2024 | Pass ✅ |
| Low capex? | Capex only 2-3% of revenue | Pass ✅ |
| Sustained FCF growth? | 2021→2024 CAGR ~21% | Pass ✅ |
| Cash generation vs. paper profit | FCF has consistently exceeded net profit | Earning real cash ✅ |

**Counter-check**:
- The 2025 FCF/net-profit ratio may fall to ~92% (GAAP) because the one-time gain inflates the net-profit denominator
- Worth watching whether content-licensing prepayments are being capitalized rather than expensed, which could distort FCF

**Rating: ★★★★★**

> This is a cash machine. FCF has consistently exceeded net profit and capex is extremely low — a textbook fit for Buffett's definition of a "good business." Cumulative five-year free cash flow of roughly USD 5.8 billion, against a current market cap of USD 14.3 billion, implies a payback period of only ~2.5 years.

## IV. Balance Sheet Health

### 4.1 Core Metrics

| Metric | Value (end of 2025) | Assessment |
|------|----------------|------|
| Cash and equivalents + term deposits | ~RMB 36.0 billion (~USD 5.3 billion) | Extremely ample |
| Total liabilities | ~RMB 14.3 billion | Low |
| Long-term borrowings | ~RMB 3.7 billion | Extremely low |
| Total shareholders' equity | ~RMB 83.0 billion | Substantial |
| Leverage ratio (debt/equity) | ~7.6% | Almost unlevered |
| Net cash (cash - total debt) | ~RMB 21.0 billion (~USD 3.1 billion) | 22% of market cap |
| Current ratio | >3x (estimate) | Excellent |

**Sources**: TME annual reports, StockAnalysis, SimplyWallSt

### 4.2 A Buffett-Style View

**Positive**:
- The balance sheet is extremely clean, with almost no leverage
- Net cash of RMB 21.0 billion equals ~22% of market cap — effectively an 8% discount built into the purchase price
- No near-term debt maturity pressure; financial safety is very high

**Counter-check — is too much cash a "good thing" or a "bad thing"?**:
- In Buffett's own words: "A good management team won't let cash sit idle on the balance sheet."
- TME's cash/market-cap ratio of 22% suggests management's capital-allocation efficiency is on the low side
- There have been buybacks (roughly USD 500 million in 2024), but cash continues to pile up
- The May 2026 announcement of the Ximalaya acquisition raises the question of whether a large deal is wise — this RMB 21.0 billion in net cash may be the "ammunition" management is stockpiling for M&A
- **Risk**: a large cash pile can invite low-quality acquisitions ("diworsification")

**Rating: ★★★★☆**

> The balance sheet is fortress-like, but having too much cash that isn't being distributed or deployed efficiently is a mark against management. Buffett would say: better to return the excess cash to shareholders.

### 6.4 Counter-Check — Why Is It So Cheap?

The market's low valuation must have a cause. It needs to be confronted directly:

1. **China-concept discount**: geopolitical risk, VIE structure, delisting risk (though it has fallen sharply)
2. **The Tencent Holdings shadow**: TME is a Tencent subsidiary, so corporate governance may not be fully independent, and there is related-party transaction risk
3. **Continued shrinkage of social entertainment**: its share has fallen, but it is still a drag — social-entertainment revenue was down 11% year over year in Q1 2026
4. **Ximalaya acquisition risk**: a large deal could consume net cash, and integration risk is uncertain
5. **From a 52-week high of $26.70 to a current $9.08**: the share price has been cut in half, then in half again — what negative expectation is the market pricing in?
6. **Slowing growth**: Q1 2026 revenue growth was only 7.3% (vs. 15.8% for full-year 2025), and subscription growth slowed to 6.6%

**Rating: ★★★★☆**

> The margin of safety is ample. Even under the Non-IFRS base case there is still 39% upside, while the bear case implies only 20% downside. A PB below 1x and net cash equal to 37% of the share price provide a solid value floor. But the market's low valuation has its reasons — China risk premium and slowing growth are real concerns.
