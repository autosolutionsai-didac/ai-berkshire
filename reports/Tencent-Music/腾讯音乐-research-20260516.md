## III. Summary of Analysis by Dimension

### 3.1 Business Model & Moat (Duan Yongping's lens)

- **In transition from "dual-engine" to "China's Spotify"**: online music's share of revenue rose from 28% in 2019 to 81% in 2025; social entertainment is in structural decline
- **The moat is "scale effects + Tencent ecosystem," not copyright exclusivity**: after exclusive licensing rights were broken up in 2021, TME proved it could still win on scale and ecosystem alone
- **SVIP is the key growth engine**: more than 20 million subscribers, penetration at 12% with a 20-25% target, driving ARPPU from RMB 11.9 toward RMB 15
- **The biggest threat is Qishui Music**: MAU of 140 million (daily actives already exceed NetEase Cloud Music), riding Douyin's traffic and a low-price strategy (RMB 8 monthly card versus RMB 18 for QQ Music)
- **Duan Yongping's take**: a "decent but not top-tier" business — it doesn't control the core content (copyright sits with record labels), and its pricing power is constrained by competition

### 3.2 Financials & Valuation (Buffett's lens)

- **Margins are improving rapidly**: gross margin rose from 29% in 2021 to 44% in 2025, operating margin from ~14% to 41% — a structural improvement
- **RMB 32.3 billion net cash equals 33% of market cap**: the "real PE" net of cash is only 6.4x — extremely cheap for a company growing 15%
- **FCF yield of 12.1%**: far above the risk-free rate, supporting a buy case under Buffett's "earnings yield" logic
- **EV/EBITDA of 4.35x**: the lowest valuation among global music-streaming names (Spotify trades at 30x)
- **Yet the stock fell from $26.7 to $8.76 over 52 weeks (-67%)**: what risk is the market pricing in?
- **Conservative intrinsic value of $11.5-12.7**: the current $8.76 implies a 24-31% margin of safety

### 3.3 Industry & Competition (Munger's lens)

- **Room for both the paying-user ratio and ARPPU to rise is well established**: China's 22% paying-user ratio is only half of the 47% seen in Europe and the US, and ARPPU is only 30% of Spotify's
- **TME holds a dominant lead with 69% MAU share**: 127 million paying users, more than 3x NetEase Cloud Music
- **Copyright costs are falling now that exclusive licensing has been abolished**: the structural improvement in gross margin has already been validated
- **Qishui Music is the biggest variable**: MAU of 140 million and still growing at +90%, but not yet monetized
- **AI-generated music is a 5-to-10-year risk**: not fatal in the near term, but it will reshape the industry's cost structure
- **Munger's take**: "investment returns depend more on whether the valuation is cheap than on whether the industry itself is growing at an outsized rate"

### 3.4 Risk & Management (Li Lu's lens)

- **The biggest risk is competition, not regulation**: Qishui Music is reshaping the competitive landscape, and TME's 16 consecutive quarters of MAU decline is a signal that cannot be ignored
- **Management gets a B+**: it has successfully completed the revenue-mix transformation and allocated capital sensibly (a $1 billion buyback plus $370 million in dividends), but has no effective countermeasure against ByteDance's offensive
- **Tencent Holdings owns 58% of the equity and 90% of the voting power**: this is more of an enabler (the WeChat entry point) than a constraint, but minority shareholders have no real say
- **Delisting risk for Chinese ADRs is manageable**: PCAOB audit inspections have already been passed, and the Hong Kong secondary listing provides a cushion
- **Long-term structure judgment**: a "one dominant, two strong" landscape (TME first, Qishui second, NetEase Cloud Music third) — no winner-take-all outcome is likely

## IV. Investment Thesis

### The bull case
1. **PE of 10x, with net cash equal to 33% of market cap**: only 6.4x net of cash — extremely cheap for a company growing 15%
2. **Structural margin improvement**: gross margin up from 29% to 44%, with more room to run as copyright costs fall
3. **Room for both the paying-user ratio and ARPPU to rise**: from 22% to 35-40%, and from RMB 11.9 to RMB 15 — a mathematically grounded growth path
4. **The social-entertainment drag is nearly exhausted**: its share of revenue has already fallen to 19%, and the pace of its absolute decline is narrowing
5. **Capital returns are accelerating**: a $1 billion buyback, annual dividends, and the Ximalaya acquisition show cash being put to efficient use
6. **FCF yield of 12%**: a "buy and get paid" logic under the Buffett framework
7. **Spotify comparison**: both are the dominant player in music streaming, yet Spotify trades at 30x PE versus TME's 10x — too wide a gap

### The bear case
1. **16 consecutive quarters of MAU decline**: from 615 million down to 528 million, the user base is shrinking, and it is uncertain when it will bottom
2. **Qishui Music's growth is staggering**: 140 million MAU and growing at +90%, riding Douyin's 700 million daily actives, with TME having no effective countermeasure
3. **TME doesn't control the core content**: copyright sits with record labels, so TME is fundamentally a "distributor" rather than a "content owner"
4. **The 67% 52-week decline may be pricing in unknown risk**: the market may be seeing negative factors we have not yet understood
5. **Tencent controlling-shareholder risk**: 58% ownership plus 90% voting power means minority-shareholder interests could be sacrificed
6. **Long-term disruption from AI-generated music**: if AI erodes the value of copyright, TME's copyright-cost advantage would lose its meaning
7. **A systemic discount on Chinese internet stocks**: geopolitical and regulatory uncertainty is suppressing valuations across the entire sector

## VIII. Summary

TME is a company undergoing a genuine transformation: social entertainment has shrunk from 72% of revenue to 19%, while online music has risen from 28% to 81%; gross margin has climbed from 29% to 44%; paying users have grown from 76 million to 127 million. Business quality is improving markedly. At a current PE of 10x, just 6.4x net of cash, and a 12% FCF yield, multiple metrics point to an undervalued stock.

The core risks are two factors that are hard to quantify: first, whether Qishui Music's offensive — riding Douyin's 700 million daily actives — can be contained (TME's MAU has already declined for 16 consecutive quarters); second, exactly what risk the market is pricing into the 67% 52-week crash. If the answer to both questions is "manageable," then the current price is a very good buying opportunity. If the answer is "unmanageable," then even a 10x PE may not be the bottom.

Investing in TME is fundamentally a bet that "the price is already cheap enough and the odds are already good enough" — a bet on the long-term trend toward paid music and on whether TME's structural lead in the Chinese music market can be sustained.
