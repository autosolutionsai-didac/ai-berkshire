# Tencent Music (TME) — Risk and Management Assessment

**Analysis dimension**: Risk assessment and management quality | **Analytical framework**: Li Lu's investment lens
**Research date**: June 20, 2026 | **Ticker**: NYSE: TME / HKEX: 1698.HK

---

## One-Line Conclusion

TME's management has completed a difficult but correct transformation, with capital allocation ability in the upper-middle range; but the company is facing a structural contradiction between "profit growth vs. user attrition" — it is making more and more money while users leave one after another. The biggest invisible risk isn't Douyin's Qishui Music taking users away, but management using "stop disclosing MAU" to dodge that contradiction. Li Lu would ask: when a company starts hiding data, is that usually good news or bad news?

Basically passing, but the regression in disclosure transparency is alarming. Stopping disclosure after 16 consecutive quarters of MAU decline — the timing is too suspicious.

## II. Regulatory Risk

### 2.1 Antitrust: The Lasting Impact of the Exclusive-Copyright Ban

**Recap**: In July 2021, the State Administration for Market Regulation required Tencent to terminate exclusive music copyright agreements within 30 days and to stop the practice of paying large upfront advances.

**Current status**:

- Exclusive copyrights have been fully terminated; TME has shifted to non-exclusive licensing
- Its negotiating position shifted from "monopolist" to "largest buyer" — bargaining power remains but is significantly weakened
- Competitors (NetEase Cloud Music, Qishui Music) gained equal access to catalogs

**Assessment of the continuing impact**:

| Dimension | Impact | Severity |
|------|------|------|
| Loss of catalog differentiation | TME can no longer attract users with exclusive songs | High |
| Copyright cost optimization | Actually reduced the burden of large upfront advances | Positive |
| Competitive landscape shift | Allowed Qishui Music to quickly fill out its catalog | High |
| Follow-on regulatory pressure | The rectification period has passed, no new penalty risk for now | Low |

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk)**

The direct antitrust shock has passed, but its indirect effect — the structural change in the competitive landscape — is still playing out.

### 2.2 Content Regulation: Live-Streaming Tipping Restrictions and Minor Protection

**Regulatory developments**:

- The Regulation on the Protection of Minors Online continues to tighten: banning the push of harmful information to minors and limiting usage time
- TME launched the "Sprout Guardian Program": late-night usage bans, age-tiered content recommendations, and age-tiered catalogs for minors
- In 2025, over 250,000 non-compliant songs were taken down, and more than 600,000 high-risk copyright cases were handled
- The Cyberspace Administration of China issued the "Measures for Classifying Online Information That May Affect the Physical and Mental Health of Minors" in January 2026

**Impact on TME**:

The social entertainment (live-tipping) business has already shrunk substantially under regulatory pressure — from 19.8 billion yuan in 2020 to about 6.18 billion yuan in 2025. But most of this risk has already been released, since TME's revenue focus has shifted to subscriptions.

**Rating: ★★ (Lower risk)**

The worst has already happened. Social entertainment revenue has already shrunk by nearly 70%, leaving limited further downside. But content-compliance costs are a long-term fixed expense.

### 2.3 Data Security and Cross-Border Compliance

**HFCAA (Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act) risk**:

| Milestone | Status |
|--------|------|
| August 2022 China-US audit cooperation agreement | Signed |
| PCAOB completed its first audit inspection | Passed |
| Gensler's departure after 2025 | Regulatory pressure has eased somewhat |
| Trump administration's "America First Investment Policy" | Uncertain stance toward China concept stocks |
| Potential forced-delisting window | As early as mid-2026 |

**TME's response**: Already secondary-listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (1698.HK), giving it a fallback even if forced to delist. But a forced ADR conversion would still cause a short-term liquidity shock and a valuation discount.

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk)**

VIE + HFCAA is a systemic risk facing all China concept stocks. TME has hedged this (via its Hong Kong listing), but the policy uncertainty cannot be eliminated.

## III. Competitive Risk

### 3.1 Douyin/Qishui Music: A Structural Shock

**This is the single biggest threat TME faces.**

| Metric | Tencent Music (three apps combined) | Qishui Music | NetEase Cloud Music |
|------|----------------------|---------|-----------|
| MAU (September 2025) | Kugou 210M + QQ Music 190M + Kuwo 110M = 510M | 120M | 150M |
| MAU (March 2026) | About 500M (continuing to decline) | 140M | — |
| MAU YoY growth | Kugou -8.1%, QQ Music -2.8%, Kuwo -8.0% | +85-90% | +1.5% |
| DAU (March 2026) | — | About 50M | — |
| Business model | Primarily paid subscription | Free + advertising | Paid subscription + community |
| Core competitive edge | Catalog scale + user inertia | Douyin ecosystem traffic loop + algorithmic recommendation | Community atmosphere + comment culture |

**Why is Qishui Music a structural threat rather than a cyclical one?**

1. **Reshaping consumption habits**: For those born after 2000 or 2005, "paying to listen to music" is itself counterintuitive. Qishui Music's free model is defining a new generation's expectations for music consumption
2. **Traffic-loop dominance**: A user hears a background track while scrolling Douyin → taps through to hear the full song on Qishui Music. That path is ten times shorter than "open QQ Music and search"
3. **Algorithm vs. search**: TME operates on a "search" logic (users know what they want to hear), while Qishui Music operates on a "recommendation" logic (letting users discover what they want to hear). The latter suits the mobile-internet era better
4. **ByteDance's resource commitment**: ByteDance's annual revenue exceeds one trillion yuan; music is a strategic-level business for it, not an experiment that can be cut at any time

> Li Lu would ask: are users staying because they "like TME," or because of "habit"? If it's the latter, that habit will break the moment a more convenient alternative appears.

**Rating: ★★★★★ (Extremely high risk)**

This is a survival-level competitive challenge for TME. The question is not whether it will have an impact, but how large that impact will be and how much ground TME can hold.

### 3.2 NetEase Cloud Music's Differentiated Competition

NetEase Cloud Music holds about a 19% market share, positioned differently as a "music community" — comment culture, playlist ecosystem, and independent musicians. In 2024, TME's gross margin was 42.3% vs. NetEase Cloud's 33.7%, giving TME a clear lead in monetization efficiency.

NetEase Cloud's threat to TME is more about "diversion" than "disruption" — the overlap between the two user bases is limited.

**Rating: ★★ (Lower risk)**

NetEase Cloud is an established competitor, not an incremental threat. The real incremental threat comes from Qishui Music.

### 3.3 The Potential Disruption of Copyright Value by AI Music Generation

**Current state**:

- Tools such as Suno and Udio can already produce music approaching professional quality
- Universal and Warner have reached licensing settlement agreements with Suno
- In 2025, TME took down 250,000 songs and handled over 600,000 high-risk copyright cases, many involving AI-generated content
- TME launched its AI Content Governance Framework 2.0, labeling and tiering AI-generated content

**Potential disruption paths**:

| Scenario | Probability | Impact on TME |
|------|------|---------|
| AI-assisted creation becomes mainstream | High (already happening) | Neutral to slightly positive — as a distribution platform, TME benefits from increased content supply |
| AI fully replaces human creation | Low (unlikely within 5-10 years) | Disrupts the foundation of the copyright business model |
| Users create their own AI music to meet personal needs | Medium | Reduces demand for professional music, indirectly lowering subscription willingness |

**TME's response**: Tianqin Lab is building AI music production tools, positioning AI as "empowering creators" rather than "replacing creators." The direction is correct, but there is no clear commercialization path yet.

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk, could escalate over the long term)**

In the short term, AI music is mostly a governance-cost issue (fighting piracy); over the long term, it could reshape the foundation of the copyright business model.

## IV. Business Risk

### 4.1 The Ongoing Decline in Social Entertainment Revenue: Where Is the Bottom?

| Year | Social entertainment revenue (billion yuan) | YoY | % of total revenue |
|------|-------------------|------|-----------|
| 2020 | 19.80 | — | 72% |
| 2021 | 16.80 (est.) | -15% | 60% (est.) |
| 2022 | 11.40 (est.) | -32% | 40% |
| 2023 | 10.43 | -9% | 38% |
| 2024 | 6.66 | -36% | 24% |
| 2025 | 6.18 (est.) | -7% | 19% |
| 2026 Q1 | 1.38 | — | 18% |

**Bottom assessment**:

- The rate of decline is narrowing: from -36% to about -7%, the pace of decay has slowed
- At 18% of total revenue in 2026 Q1, social entertainment is nearing "residual business" status within total revenue
- WeSing's MAU has shrunk substantially, but it still retains a core user base
- **Expected to bottom out and stabilize at 5.0-5.5 billion yuan per year in 2026-2027**

**Rating: ★★ (Lower risk — the worst is largely over)**

Social entertainment has fallen from 19.8 billion to 6.2 billion yuan, already releasing most of the downside risk. Even if it went to zero, the impact on the current valuation would be limited (it's only 19% of revenue).

### 4.2 New Business Uncertainty

| New business | Investment | Progress | Uncertainty | Rating |
|--------|------|------|---------|------|
| Long-form audio (Ximalaya) | $1.26 billion acquisition | Closed in May 2026 | High integration risk, Ximalaya's own profitability is weak | ★★★★ (High risk) |
| SVIP super-membership | Bundles music + long-form audio + perks | Already over 20 million users | Can ARPPU keep rising? | ★★ (Low risk) |
| AI music tools | R&D investment via Tianqin Lab | "Qiming Xing" AI Sing/AI Compose already launched | Commercialization path unclear | ★★★ (Moderate) |
| Live shows/offline | Platform for offline concerts | TME Live and other products | Asset-light model, manageable risk | ★★ (Low risk) |
| SM Entertainment investment | 1.29 billion yuan | Became second-largest shareholder | K-pop cyclical risk | ★★★ (Moderate) |

**The deeper risk of the Ximalaya acquisition**:

Ximalaya is one of China's largest audio platforms, but its long-term profitability is questionable — its repeated failed IPO attempts speak for themselves. TME is paying 9 billion yuan for a business that has yet to prove sustained profitability, betting that "audio ecosystem synergy" will produce a 1+1>2 effect. If the synergy falls short, this acquisition could turn into value destruction.

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk)**

The new business layout is directionally sound but carries real execution risk, especially with the Ximalaya integration.

### 4.3 Rigid Copyright Costs vs. Elastic Revenue Growth

**Core contradiction**:

- Copyright cost is TME's single largest cost item and is inherently rigid — the bargaining power of record labels (Universal, Sony, Warner) will not disappear
- Revenue-side growth elasticity depends on paid-user growth and ARPPU improvement
- Paid-user growth has already slowed: net quarterly additions have fallen from 3-5 million to 1-2 million
- ARPPU has reached 11.9 yuan/month, still only a quarter of Spotify's, leaving theoretical room to grow
- But amid consumption downgrading, room for price increases is limited — the annual fee already exceeds 140 yuan, approaching users' "value anchor"

**Gross margin trend**:

| Period | Gross margin | Trend |
|------|--------|------|
| 2022 Q1 | Low point | — |
| 2024 Q1 | 41% | ↑ |
| 2024 Q4 | 43.6% | ↑ |
| Full-year 2025 | About 42-43% | → |
| 2026 outlook | Flat or slightly lower | → |

After exclusive copyrights were abolished, copyright costs actually declined (no more need for large upfront advances), which in turn helped improve gross margin. But if competition intensifies (Qishui Music begins large-scale copyright purchasing), the bargaining power of the three major labels could rise.

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk)**

Rigid copyright costs are a structural issue, but they have been improving in the short term. The key variable is whether Qishui Music will drive up copyright-acquisition bidding.

## V. Macro Risk

### 5.1 The Impact of Consumption Downgrading on Willingness to Pay

**Current situation**:

- China's economic growth is slowing, and young people face employment pressure
- TME has lowered its guidance for new paid subscriptions: from 3-5 million per quarter to 1-2 million
- Management itself has acknowledged that "2026 subscription revenue may face short-term pressure"
- Qishui Music's free model is more attractive in a consumption-downgrading environment

**Impact on TME**:

Music subscriptions are "soft" spending — when the economy weakens, users may downgrade from SVIP to standard membership, or switch from paid to ad-supported free listening. This resembles the downgrade dynamics already seen with video subscriptions (iQIYI has already gone through this).

But music subscriptions typically have higher stickiness than video: a user's playlists, favorites, and listening history create a form of "data lock-in," making switching costs higher than for video platforms.

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk)**

Consumption downgrading is a real risk but not fatal. The essential, habitual nature of music subscriptions and the data lock-in effect provide some buffer.

### 5.2 China Concept Stock Policy Environment

- The Trump administration's "America First Investment Policy" has an uncertain effect on China concept stocks
- HFCAA compliance risk exists, but the China-US audit cooperation agreement has been signed
- Former SEC Chair Gensler (who held a negative view of VIE structures) left office in January 2025
- TME is already listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, giving it a fallback

**Rating: ★★★ (Moderate risk — systemic, uncontrollable)**

## VI. Governance Structure

### 6.1 Relationship with Tencent Holdings

| Dimension | Details | Risk assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Ownership stake | Tencent holds more than 50% of TME's voting rights | The controlling shareholder has absolute control over major decisions |
| Positive impact | Traffic referral from the WeChat/QQ ecosystem, shared technical resources, brand endorsement | Ecosystem synergy is one of TME's core advantages |
| Negative impact | Limited independent decision-making room, may be required to align with group strategy | Interests may not always align with minority shareholders |
| Related-party transactions | Multiple related-party transactions with the Tencent group (technical services, traffic purchases, etc.) | Disclosed as required |
| Same-industry competition | Tencent's Channels (video accounts) also distribute music content | Potential competition, but currently mostly synergistic |

**Core judgment**: Tencent's control is "more beneficial than harmful" for TME. The WeChat/QQ traffic gateway, technical support, and brand endorsement are important advantages TME holds relative to Qishui Music. But the presence of a controlling shareholder also means TME will never be a fully independent entity — management must balance group interests against those of the listed company.

**Rating: ★★ (Lower risk — Tencent's support outweighs the interference)**

### 6.2 VIE Structural Risk

As a US-listed China concept stock, TME uses a VIE (Variable Interest Entity) structure. This means US investors hold equity in a Cayman Islands shell company, controlling the onshore operating entity through contracts.

**The substance of the risk**:

- The legal status of VIE structures under Chinese law remains a gray area
- If the Chinese government decided not to recognize the legality of VIE contracts, investors could lose everything
- But this risk has existed for over 20 years, and the Chinese government has in practice consistently acquiesced to it

**Rating: ★★ (Lower risk — a tail risk, extremely low probability but extreme consequences)**

The shareholder return policy is above average among China concept stocks. Dividends plus buybacks together amount to about 6-7% of market cap, with ample net cash. But buyback execution pace remains slow and needs ongoing monitoring.

## VII. Long-Term Certainty: What Will TME Look Like in 10 Years?

### 7.1 Base Case (50% probability)

In 10 years, TME will still be China's largest music-streaming platform, but its market position will shift from "sole leader" to a "two-strong" or even "three-strong" landscape (alongside Qishui Music and NetEase Cloud Music). Paid users will grow from 127 million to 150-180 million, and ARPPU will roughly double to around 25 yuan/month. Annual revenue will be about 60-80 billion yuan, with net margin holding at 25-30%. The Ximalaya integration succeeds, forming a combined "music + audio" platform.

**Expected annualized return**: 8-12%

### 7.2 Bullish Case (20% probability)

AI music tools become a new growth engine, the Ximalaya integration exceeds expectations, SVIP penetration keeps rising, and ARPPU catches up with Spotify's. TME becomes China's version of Spotify + Audible.

**Expected annualized return**: 15-20%

### 7.3 Bearish Case (30% probability)

Qishui Music's free model continues to erode TME's user base, and the younger generation shifts entirely toward short-video- and AI-recommendation-driven music consumption. TME's paid-user growth stalls or even declines, and room for ARPPU improvement is sealed off by consumption downgrading. The Ximalaya integration fails and becomes a burden. TME turns into a "profitable but not growing" value trap.

**Expected annualized return**: 0-5%

### 7.4 What Could Disrupt TME's Business Model?

| Disruptive factor | Probability | Time window | Degree of disruption |
|---------|------|---------|---------|
| Qishui Music + Douyin ecosystem reshaping the entry point into music consumption | High | Happening now | Medium-high |
| AI music generation reducing copyright value to zero | Low | 10+ years | Extreme |
| Super-app embedded music features (listening directly within WeChat/Douyin) | Medium | 3-5 years | High |
| Regulation requiring music platforms to lower prices or go free | Very low | Unpredictable | Extreme |
| Metaverse/spatial audio creating an entirely new experience | Low | 5-10 years | Medium |

**The biggest "invisible risk"**:

It is not Qishui Music stealing users away (everyone already sees that), but **the paradigm shift in how music itself is consumed**. When short-video background tracks become the primary way young people "listen to music," the very rationale for a standalone music app's existence gets fundamentally called into question — much like MP3 players were replaced by smartphones. TME needs to prove that the "standalone music app" category still has a reason to exist.

## Overall Rating: ★★★ (3 stars / Moderate-to-high risk)

**One-line conclusion**: TME is a company whose "earnings power is strengthening while its user base is weakening." Management is passing-to-above-average but not excellent, and the biggest risk is not any single event but **the structural trend of the music-consumption entry point migrating from "standalone apps" toward being "embedded within short-video ecosystems."** Li Lu would say: this company's management deserves basic trust, but in the worst case (Qishui Music + consumption downgrading + failed M&A hitting simultaneously), the stock could fall another 30-40% from current levels. The market has already seen the risk of user attrition (the stock has already plunged 25%), but it may not yet have fully priced in the signal that "management has started hiding data."
