## A word up front

This is the final article in the "Understanding TME" series. The first 4 articles dismantled the perception illusion, the subscription growth engine, the Qishui Music threat, and the Ximalaya acquisition and risks.

This final article answers the most direct question: **Is $8.47 actually worth buying?**

Let's state the conclusion framework up front: no precise price target will be given. Instead, this article tells you under what conditions this is a "golden pit" and under what conditions it's a "value trap" — so you can decide based on your own judgment.

## I. Valuation breakdown: cross-checking three methods

### Method one: P/E approach

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 Non-IFRS net profit | 9.6 billion yuan |
| Current market cap | ~96 billion yuan |
| P/E | ~10x |
| Net cash | 38 billion yuan |
| Market cap net of cash | ~58 billion yuan |
| **P/E net of cash** | **~6x** |

Buying a company whose profit is growing 22% at roughly 6x P/E. Applying a conservative 13-14x P/E (in line with the Chinese internet-sector average), that implies a market cap of roughly 129-139 billion yuan + 38 billion yuan in cash = 167-177 billion yuan, or roughly $12-13 per share.

### Method two: Free cash flow approach

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 free cash flow | ~11 billion yuan |
| Current market cap | ~96 billion yuan |
| FCF yield (free cash flow yield) | **~12%** |

What does a 12% free cash flow yield mean? If you bought the entire company for 96 billion, you'd get back 11 billion in free cash each year — an 8.7-year payback period. Net out the 38 billion in cash on the balance sheet (effectively "received the moment you buy"), and you're really only paying 58 billion for the business — a 5.3-year payback period.

For comparison: the one-year government bond yield is about 1.5%, bank wealth-management products yield 3-4%, and the CSI 300 dividend yield is about 3%. A 12% FCF yield is 8x the government bond yield.

### Method three: Sum-of-the-parts valuation (net cash + business value)

Split Tencent Music into two parts:

| Component | Valuation method | Value |
|------|---------|------|
| Net cash | 1:1 | 38 billion yuan |
| Online music business | 12-15x profit | 75-95 billion yuan (est.) |
| Social entertainment business | 3-5x profit (declining) | 3-6 billion yuan (est.) |
| **Total** | — | **116-139 billion yuan** |
| Current market cap | — | 96 billion yuan |
| **Degree of undervaluation** | — | **17-31%** |

All three methods point in the same direction: **at the current price, Tencent Music is undervalued by 20-30%.**

**Counterargument**: the valuation models assume profits can be sustained. If large-scale monetization by Qishui Music causes Tencent Music's paying-user growth to stall, or if the Ximalaya integration fails and eats into profit — roughly 6x P/E might not be "cheap," it might be "fair pricing of risk."

## II. Where is the margin of safety?

The margin of safety isn't a single number — it's a set of protective layers. Tencent Music has three:

### Layer one: 38 billion yuan in cash

38 billion yuan in net cash on the balance sheet, roughly 40% of market cap. That means even if the business value went to zero (an extreme assumption), your principal loss would be capped at 60%. And the probability of the business value going to zero is close to zero — music subscription is a necessity, and Tencent Music is the industry leader.

Put another way: **you're paying 96 billion for 38 billion in cash plus a machine that earns 9.9 billion a year. The cash portion carries zero risk; all the risk sits in the business portion.**

### Layer two: sustained free cash flow generation

11 billion yuan in free cash flow every year means: even if the stock price never rises, the cash the company generates each year still compounds per-share value. Cumulative buybacks have reached $2.5-2.6 billion (20% of market cap), and 2025 dividends were $368 million — management is returning cash to shareholders, not hoarding it.

By comparison, Pinduoduo (422.3 billion in cash, zero buybacks, zero dividends), Tencent Music's capital-return actions are markedly stronger.

### Layer three: the business is still improving

Gross margin of 44.2%, 127.4 million paying users (+5.3%), ARPPU of 11.9 yuan (+7.2%) — all core operating metrics are improving. **This is not a machine that's breaking down — it's a machine that's getting better, just one the market currently doesn't favor.**

## III. Under what conditions is this a "value trap"?

Cheap valuation plus high margin of safety doesn't rule out the possibility of a value trap. If the following three things happen **simultaneously**, the current price may genuinely not be the bottom:

### 1. Accelerating erosion from Qishui Music

If, within the next 2 years, Qishui Music:
- Breaks through 250 million MAU
- Starts building out paid membership at scale (keeping the monthly subscription at 8 yuan)
- Tencent Music's paying-user growth rate falls to zero or turns negative

Then Tencent Music's "rising pay rate + rising ARPPU" growth story would be broken. A company with no growth doesn't look cheap at 10x P/E.

### 2. Ximalaya integration fails

$2.9 billion has been committed. If, 18-24 months from now:
- Ximalaya's users and creators leave in large numbers
- Content synergy cannot be realized
- Integration losses drag down overall profit margins

Then that money would have been wasted — the 38 billion in cash effectively becomes 35 billion, with ongoing investment still needed for integration.

### 3. Chinese ADR delisting becomes reality

If U.S.-China relations deteriorate to the point of forced delisting of Chinese ADRs:
- U.S. equity liquidity is lost
- Hong Kong's market cannot fully absorb the shift (average daily trading volume there is far below the U.S. market's)
- Forced-selling institutional capital dumps shares

**The probability of all three things happening together isn't high, but if they truly do stack up — there could still be another 30-40% of downside.**

## IV. Under what conditions is this a "golden pit"?

If **any two** of the following three things happen, the current price, in hindsight, is clearly a golden buying opportunity:

### 1. Pay rate breaks through 30%

A pay rate rising from 24% to 30% implies paying users growing from 127.4 million to roughly 160 million (assuming MAU stabilizes around 530 million). More than 30 million new paying users x roughly 11-12 yuan per month ≈ an annual revenue increase of 4-5 billion yuan.

Combined with ARPPU improvement, online music revenue could grow from 26.7 billion to 35-40 billion yuan. Growth of this magnitude is enough to support a P/E above 15x.

### 2. Super VIP penetration reaches 25%

Super VIP members growing from 20 million to 32 million (25% penetration), each paying an extra 12-15 yuan per month → roughly 1.7-2.2 billion yuan in additional annual revenue. More importantly, rising Super VIP penetration signals the "premiumization" strategy is succeeding — the market would assign a higher valuation multiple.

### 3. Qishui Music's monetization fails

Most of Qishui Music's users came for "free or low-price" content. If Qishui starts charging for real, it might find:
- Users of the 8-yuan monthly plan have extremely low willingness to pay more — they were already paying that 8 yuan reluctantly
- Raising the price to 15 yuan would send large numbers of users flowing back to Tencent Music
- Ad revenue cannot cover licensing costs

In this scenario, Qishui Music would stay confined to its position as "Douyin's music player," posing no fundamental threat to Tencent Music's paid business.

**If the pay rate breaks through 30% and Qishui's monetization fails** — Tencent Music's P/E could re-rate from 10x to 15-18x, implying a share price of $14-17. Starting from $8.47, that's a gain of 65-100%.

## V. Is $8.47 actually worth buying?

No precise answer will be given — instead, a decision framework.

### Five questions to ask yourself before buying

**First, how do you judge Qishui Music's MAU over the next 3 years?** If you believe Qishui will break through 300 million MAU and successfully monetize — Tencent Music's paid-growth logic will be fundamentally undermined. If you believe Qishui will plateau around 200 million and struggle to monetize — the current stock price has already over-reflected this threat.

**Second, how do you judge the threat from Qishui Music?** If you believe Qishui will grab a large share of paying users — then the current price may not yet be the bottom. If you believe Qishui can only operate in the low-end market — then the current price has already over-reflected the fear.

**Third, how long are you willing to wait for the Ximalaya integration?** It will take at least 2-3 years to know whether the integration succeeds or fails. If your holding period is under 3 years, this uncertainty is too large for you.

**Fourth, can you stomach another 30-40% decline?** The 52-week range already went from $26.70 to $8.47. If it falls further to $5-6 (due to ADR-delisting panic or some other black-swan event), can you avoid selling?

**Fifth, is there a better option for the same money?** At the same point in time, Tencent trades at roughly 16x P/E, Apple at roughly 30x, and Spotify at roughly 37x. Tencent Music's 10x P/E is indeed the cheapest — but cheapness alone isn't a reason to buy, unless you also believe "this company's quality deserves a higher valuation."

### When you must reassess

A few red lines — hitting any one should trigger a reassessment:

1. Paying users decline quarter-over-quarter (the core growth logic is broken)
2. Qishui Music rolls out a mature paid model and rapidly acquires users (the competitive landscape deteriorates)
3. 12 months after the Ximalaya integration, there's still no visible synergy ($2.9 billion may be wasted)
4. Chinese ADR delisting policy meaningfully worsens (SEC forces delisting)
5. Gross margin declines for two consecutive quarters (a signal of deteriorating cost structure)

Conversely, one positive signal: **MAU stabilizes or rebounds.** This is the metric the market fears most — once it stabilizes, the "users are leaving" narrative breaks, and valuation re-rating could happen quickly.

---

## VI. Final word

Back to the contradiction this series opened with: profit up 25%, stock down 68%.

Having gone through 5 articles, the explanation for this contradiction becomes clear — **the market isn't pricing "past profit"; it's pricing "future uncertainty."**

That uncertainty comes from: whether Qishui Music can be held off, whether the Ximalaya integration will succeed, whether Chinese ADRs will be delisted, and when MAU will bottom out.

Roughly 6x P/E net of cash is the market's pricing of these uncertainties. If you believe these risks are being overblown — then $8.47 is a good price. If you believe these risks are real and could worsen — then even roughly 6x P/E doesn't constitute a margin of safety.

**Both judgments have merit.** The difference lies only in how you assess the probability of each variable.

A company that's getting better, a competitor that's getting stronger, and a market that's being re-priced — these three things together form the investment question that is Tencent Music.

There's no standard answer to this question. But at least, having read these 5 articles, you should now know what the question is actually asking.
