# Novo Nordisk (NVO) — Financial Valuation Analysis

> Core question: why is a PE of about 10x so low for a GLP-1 leader? What is the true valuation level?

## II. PE of About 10x — Why So Low?

This is the most critical question in this research. Novo Nordisk's current TTM PE is about 10-12x, versus a 10-year median of 24x. The reasons break down as follows:

### 2.1 The Direct Cause: The 2026 Guidance Shock

On February 4, 2026, Novo issued full-year 2026 guidance that stunned the market: **sales and operating profit are expected to decline 5-13% at constant exchange rates**. This is the first time in years the company has issued negative-growth guidance. The stock plunged 17-18% that day.

Core drivers:
- **US price cuts**: to counter compounded-drug competition and improve access, Novo proactively cut prices sharply
- **Impact of compounding pharmacies**: cheap generic semaglutide diverted significant demand
- **Competitive pressure from Eli Lilly**: Mounjaro and Zepbound have reached a 60% share of the US market
- **Patent expirations outside the US**: patent protection has ended in multiple markets

### 2.2 A Note on the ADR Structure

NVO is an ADR (American Depositary Receipt), maintaining a 1:1 relationship with the B-shares listed in Copenhagen (following the September 2023 stock split). The ADR is denominated in US dollars, so:
- Novo reports results in Danish kroner; when the krone weakens against the dollar, the ADR price bears additional pressure
- FY2025 revenue was up 6% in kroner terms, but the increase is smaller once converted to dollars
- **Exchange rates are not the main reason the PE is low**, but they have indeed amplified the decline

### 2.3 Were There Any One-Off Impairments?

There were no material one-off impairments in FY2025. The slowdown in net profit growth (only +1%) was mainly due to:
- Operating margin declining from its previous peak to 41.3%
- Increased R&D spending (in preparation for pipeline advancement)
- FY2024's negative free cash flow was due to large-scale capital expenditure (capacity expansion), not an operating problem

### 2.4 Core Conclusion: The Market Is Pricing In a "Growth Cliff"

The PE compression from 24x to 10x essentially reflects the market repricing Novo from a "high-growth biotech company" to a "mature pharmaceutical company whose growth has peaked." This pricing implies the following assumptions:
- Revenue will indeed decline in 2026
- The competitive landscape will deteriorate permanently
- Pricing power will continue to erode

## III. The Latest Q1 2026 Data — Has an Inflection Point Emerged?

| Metric | Q1 2026 |
|------|---------|
| Adjusted operating profit | DKK 32.9 billion |
| Ozempic revenue | ~DKK 28 billion (-8% YoY, but beat expectations) |
| Wegovy injectable revenue | DKK 18.2 billion (+12% YoY) |
| Wegovy oral revenue | DKK 2.26 billion (far exceeding the expected DKK 1.16 billion) |
| Full-year guidance raised | improved from -5%~-13% to -4%~-12% |

**The oral Wegovy's better-than-expected performance is the biggest highlight.** After only one quarter on the market, it garnered roughly 1.3 million prescriptions, with revenue nearly double analysts' expectations. This suggests:
1. The oral formulation can indeed open up new patient populations
2. The $149/month pricing strategy is working
3. Novo's competitiveness has not collapsed the way the market's pricing implies

## IV. A Genuine Valuation Assessment

**Current valuation parameters (mid-May 2026):**
- ADR share price: approximately $44-46
- Market cap: approximately $155-206 billion (sources vary slightly)
- TTM PE: approximately 10-12x
- Forward PE (based on 2026E earnings): likely in the 12-15x range

**Comparative benchmarks:**
- Eli Lilly (LLY) current PE: approximately 50-60x
- Average PE for large global pharmaceutical companies: 15-20x
- Novo's own 10-year median PE: 24x

**Buffett's perspective on the judgment:**

> "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."

Novo Nordisk's financial quality remains excellent — a 33% net margin, 21% ROIC, and a strong recovery in free cash flow. A 10x PE means either the market is right (Novo's growth engine has permanently stalled), or the market is being excessively pessimistic (the current difficulties are a temporary price war and product-cycle transition).

Judging by oral Wegovy's Q1 2026 performance, the latter probability seems greater. If Novo can stabilize revenue and return to growth in 2027 (CagriSema approval, oral product line ramp-up), a PE reversion to 15-18x would be reasonable, implying 50-80% of potential upside.

**Financial valuation score: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

Financial quality is first-rate, and the valuation sits at a historic extreme low. The risk is whether the 2026 revenue decline turns out worse than guided, and whether Eli Lilly's competitive advantage widens further.
