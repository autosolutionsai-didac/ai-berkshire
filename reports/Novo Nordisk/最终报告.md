## Summary of the investment thesis

### Why it deserves attention (the bull case)

1. **Valuation is extremely compressed**: a 10x P/E versus a 10-year median of 24x — the market is pricing in "permanent decline," but Q1 2026 data already shows signs of an inflection point
2. **Oral Wegovy beat expectations**: DKK 2.26 billion in revenue in its first quarter on the market (2x analyst expectations), 1.3 million prescriptions, opening up a huge new pool of patients
3. **Financial quality remains top-tier**: 33% net margin, 21% ROIC, free cash flow recovered to DKK 28.3 billion
4. **Ample pipeline firepower**: CagriSema (expected approval Q4 2026) plus Amycretin/Zenagamtide (Phase III initiated, potential for >20% weight loss)
5. **The market is large enough**: GLP-1 is expected to exceed $150 billion by 2030 — even with declining share, the absolute size can still grow
6. **The new CEO is acting decisively**: laying off 9,000 employees, saving $1.3 billion a year, refocusing on the core business, accelerating the pipeline

### Why caution is warranted (the bear case)

1. **Eli Lilly's product superiority is a fact**: in head-to-head trials, tirzepatide proved "superior" to semaglutide — this is not a marketing problem, it's a science problem
2. **Market share is continuously eroding**: from ~60% down to ~40% in the U.S., and Eli Lilly has also surpassed Novo internationally
3. **2026 revenue will decline materially**: guidance of -4% to -12% (CER), the first negative growth in years
4. **Pricing power is being eroded**: triple pressure from compounded-drug competition, insurance-coverage negotiations, and voluntary price cuts
5. **CagriSema uncertainty**: the single-chamber device was abandoned in favor of a dual-chamber device, and the pace of approval and commercialization could fall short of expectations
6. **CEO transition-period execution risk**: large-scale layoffs plus organizational restructuring could drag on operational efficiency in the short term

| Danish krone exchange rate | Small | Has amplified the dollar-denominated ADR's decline |
| One-off impairments | None | No material one-off impairment in FY2025 |

**Conclusion: the low P/E is not an accounting-basis issue — it is a genuine reflection of fundamental expectations. But the assumptions implied by a 10x P/E are overly pessimistic — they assume Novo's growth engine has stalled permanently, which contradicts oral Wegovy's above-expectations performance and the ample pipeline in reserve.**

## Commentary from the four masters

### Duan Yongping's perspective: looking at the essence of the business
> GLP-1 is one of the best businesses of the past decade — high repeat purchase, an inelastic need, and a huge market. The issue isn't the sector — it's whether Novo can hold its position within it. Oral Wegovy's success proves Novo still has the capacity to innovate, but Eli Lilly's dual-target molecule genuinely has a stronger product. This is a good business, but the competitive landscape has shifted from "one dominant player" to "two strong rivals." Buying the second-place player in a good business at a 10x P/E requires the patience to wait for the next generation of products to be validated.

### Buffett's perspective: looking at the margin of safety
> A 33% net margin, 21% ROIC, and recovering free cash flow — these numbers prove Novo remains a high-quality enterprise. Buying such an enterprise at a 10x P/E leaves limited downside even if you're wrong. The key question is: is the 2026 difficulty temporary (like Coca-Cola's trough in 1999) or structural (like Nokia facing the iPhone in 2010)? Judging from oral Wegovy's outperformance and the pipeline in reserve, it's more likely the former. But it must be acknowledged that the uncertainty this time is greater than a typical "buy the cyclical trough" situation — because the competitor (Eli Lilly) is genuinely strong.

### Munger's perspective: looking at the competitive landscape
> A duopoly market is not inherently a bad thing — look at Coca-Cola and Pepsi, Boeing and Airbus. The question is whether the two players in a duopoly are evenly matched. The current landscape has Eli Lilly leading on product strength, while Novo has accumulated advantages in brand and distribution but is being caught up. If Amycretin's Phase III data proves Novo has closed the efficacy gap, then now is an excellent buying opportunity. If not, Novo could become the "Pepsi" of the GLP-1 market — profitable but not exceptional.

### Li Lu's perspective: looking at management and risk
> Investing shouldn't just weigh the odds — it should also weigh the probability. The new CEO has done the right things — cutting staff to refocus, accelerating the pipeline, pricing pragmatically. But he's leading an organization that has already made strategic mistakes (underestimating Eli Lilly, setbacks in CagriSema's development). Until management proves itself, position sizing should stay conservative. CagriSema's Q4 approval plus continued ramp-up of the oral product line = a signal to add to the position. Amycretin's Phase III readout = the basis for a long-term hold decision.
