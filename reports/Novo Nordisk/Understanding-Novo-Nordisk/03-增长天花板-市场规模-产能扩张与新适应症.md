## 1. Wall Street's disagreement: $95 billion or $190 billion?

Goldman Sachs says $95 billion. Morgan Stanley says $190 billion.

These are two starkly different conclusions, reached by the same market, using broadly the same public data, from two different sets of analysts. If even Wall Street disagrees this much, you should treat any "precise forecast number" with skepticism.

**How big is the GLP-1 weight-loss drug market, really?** This is the premise that needs to be sorted out before discussing Novo Nordisk's growth runway.

| Institution | 2030 global GLP-1 market forecast | Change | Source |
|------|--------------------------|------|------|
| **Goldman Sachs** | **$95 billion** | Down **27%** from its previous $130 billion estimate | Goldman Sachs Research, 2026 |
| **Morgan Stanley** | **$190 billion** (by 2035) | Up **27%** from its previous $150 billion estimate | Morgan Stanley Research, 2026 |
| **JPMorgan** | No precise figure given; projects ~30 million US GLP-1 users by 2030 | Revised upward from its previous estimate | JPMorgan Research, 2026 |

**Two top investment banks forecasting the same category with numbers 2x apart — and even the time horizons differ (Goldman's is 2030, Morgan Stanley's is 2035).** That alone tells you how large the uncertainty is.

### Why did Goldman revise down?

Goldman's chain of logic is clear:

1. **Patient discontinuation rates are higher than expected** — the one-year discontinuation rate for GLP-1 weight-loss drugs is roughly **40-50%** (source: multiple tracking studies), far higher than for diabetes medications
2. **Cash-pay patients are price-sensitive** — even with the price cut to $149/month (oral Wegovy), a large population still can't afford it or is unwilling to pay long-term
3. **Insurance coverage is rolling out slower than expected** — while Medicare has begun covering it, implementation takes time, and roughly 40% of commercial insurance plans still don't cover weight-loss use
4. **US market peak revised down from $95 billion to $70 billion, international market revised up from $35 billion to $50 billion — a net downward revision overall**

### Why did Morgan Stanley revise up?

Morgan Stanley sees different drivers:

1. **The advent of oral formulations is a game-changer** — people who "fear needles" can finally enter the market, substantially expanding the pool of potential patients
2. **Medicare coverage is a structural positive** — among the 67 million Medicare beneficiaries in the US, a large obese population will become eligible for GLP-1 prescriptions, with a patient copay cap of just $50/month
3. **International markets are ramping up quickly** — rising obesity rates in populous countries like Brazil and China; generics will push prices down but will also expand the patient population
4. **New indications keep expanding the addressable market** — cardiovascular, fatty liver disease, and kidney protection are new indications opening up market space for GLP-1 "beyond weight loss"

**Combining both lines of reasoning:** both Goldman's caution and Morgan Stanley's optimism have merit. Under a neutral assumption, **a 2030 global GLP-1 market of $80-120 billion** is a reasonable range. Even at the low end of $80 billion, that is still 1-1.5x FY2025 global GLP-1 revenue (about $80 billion, including Eli Lilly) — growth room genuinely exists, but it's not "unlimited expansion."

**Counterpoint:** all of these forecasts rest on one assumption — that no major long-term safety issue emerges with GLP-1 drugs. A 2026 study flagged a possible association between GLP-1 drugs and certain gastrointestinal conditions worth monitoring (source: ScienceDaily, May 2026). If a class-wide safety risk were to emerge, all of these forecasts would be invalidated.

## 2. How much fuel does Novo Nordisk's growth engine have left?

Even if the overall GLP-1 market keeps growing, whether Novo Nordisk's own growth story holds up depends on three variables.

### Variable one: the ramp-up speed of the oral product line

Oral Wegovy is currently Novo Nordisk's clearest growth story.

**Verified data points:**

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Q1 2026 oral Wegovy revenue | **DKK 2.26 billion (~$355 million)** | Novo Nordisk Q1 2026 earnings |
| Patients treated in first 10 weeks on the market | **~400,000** | NBC News, March 2026 |
| Q1 prescription count | **~1.3 million** | CNBC, May 2026 |
| Analyst expectation | DKK 1.16 billion | Actual was **nearly 2x** the expectation |

The strategic logic of the oral version is: **capture incremental users from the population that "fears needles," rather than replacing existing injectable users**. If this logic holds, oral and injectable can coexist and the overall pie grows. If it doesn't hold (oral cannibalizes injectable), the revenue mix will worsen (the oral version's per-unit price is far lower than the injectable one).

Q1 data leans positive — but it's just one quarter, not yet enough of a sample to judge. Watch whether the growth rate holds up in Q2 and Q3.

### Variable two: can new indications open a second growth curve?

Semaglutide isn't just a weight-loss drug. Novo Nordisk is using the same molecule to expand into multiple indications:

| Indication | Progress | Market potential | Certainty |
|--------|------|---------|--------|
| **Cardiovascular protection** | The SELECT trial has already demonstrated a **20%** reduction in cardiac event risk; the FDA has already approved Wegovy for the cardiovascular indication | Very large — cardiovascular disease is the world's leading cause of death | High |
| **Fatty liver disease (NASH/MASH)** | In Phase III trials | Large — over **150 million** NASH patients globally | Medium |
| **Kidney protection** | Under expanded research | Medium-large — about **850 million** chronic kidney disease patients | Medium-low |
| **Adolescent obesity** | Oral Wegovy adolescent indication in clinical trials | Medium — expands population coverage | Medium |

**The cardiovascular indication matters most.** The SELECT trial was a milestone — it proved GLP-1 isn't just "cosmetic weight loss" but delivers real cardiovascular benefit. That means:

1. Physicians have a stronger clinical basis for prescribing (not just weight loss, but cardiac protection too)
2. Insurers find it harder to deny coverage (drugs backed by cardiovascular-benefit data typically get better reimbursement)
3. The patient population expands from "people who want to get thin" to "obese people who need cardiac protection"

**Counterpoint:** expanding into new indications takes time, and Eli Lilly is doing the same thing. Research into tirzepatide's cardiovascular and renal benefits is likewise progressing. New indications won't be exclusive territory for Novo Nordisk.

### Variable three: can manufacturing capacity keep up with demand?

The GLP-1 supply bottleneck is an easily overlooked but very real issue.

Novo Nordisk's capital expenditure trend over the past two years:

| Year | Capex focus | Free cash flow |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| FY2024 | Large-scale capacity expansion | **DKK -14.7 billion** (negative free cash flow) |
| FY2025 | Continued capacity expansion | **DKK 28.3 billion** (returned to positive) |
| 2026+ | Continued investment | Expected to keep improving |

FY2024's negative free cash flow was not an operational problem; it was purely a function of heavy factory-construction spending. By FY2025 it had already recovered.

But the payoff from capacity expansion takes time. One of Novo Nordisk's competitive advantages is **the world's largest GLP-1 manufacturing capacity** — a "hardware barrier" that late entrants (including generic drugmakers) will struggle to close in the near term.

**Counterpoint:** Eli Lilly's capacity expansion is equally aggressive, with over $20 billion invested in 2025-2026. And in emerging markets, Indian generic drugmakers already launched generic semaglutide in 2026 — at least five companies participating, priced 80% below the original (source: CNBC, March 2026). The manufacturing-capacity moat works in high-end markets, but it's being bypassed in emerging markets.

## 3. Volume up, price down — the core tension in the GLP-1 market

Every discussion of the growth ceiling eventually runs into the same question: **volume is growing, but price is falling. After volume rises and price falls, does revenue go up or down?**

This is the core tension facing Novo Nordisk in 2026.

### Timeline of price cuts

| Timing | Event | Impact |
|------|------|------|
| January 2026 | Oral Wegovy launches, starting dose **$149/month** | Low price to acquire customers |
| February 2026 | Announced that from 2027, injectable Wegovy/Ozempic list price will drop to **$675/month** (a 50% cut) | Substantial concession of pricing power |
| Q1 2026 | Adjusted sales at constant exchange rates **-4%** (excluding a 340B rebate reversal) | Impact of the price cuts already visible |
| Full-year 2026 guidance | Adjusted revenue and profit **-4% to -12%** (at constant exchange rates) | Pressure across the full year |

### Can volume make up for price?

This is an arithmetic problem:

- **Injectable Wegovy's list price is dropping from $1,349 to $675** — a 50% cut
- To maintain the same revenue, prescription volume needs to **double**
- The global obese population base is large enough (650 million), but doubling prescription volume takes time
- The oral version's $149/month price point is lower still — it ramps up faster but contributes less per customer

**Reasonable expectation:** 2026-2027 is the pain period of "volume up, price down," with revenue likely flat or slightly down. From 2028 onward, if oral ramp-up, Medicare coverage, and new-indication expansion all materialize together, revenue could return to growth.

**But this is a projection full of "ifs."** Any one of those "ifs" could fall short of expectations.

## 4. Overall judgment on the growth ceiling

Summarizing the analysis above:

| Growth driver | Bullish scenario | Neutral scenario | Bearish scenario |
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Oral product line ramp-up | Oral contributes $3 billion+ in 2027 | Oral contributes $1.5-2.0 billion in 2027 | Loses substantial share to Foundayo |
| New indication expansion | Cardiovascular and NASH both approved, opening a $20 billion+ incremental market | Cardiovascular already approved but NASH progress is slow | NASH trial fails |
| CagriSema approval | Approved Q4 2026, contributes revenue starting early 2027 | Approved mid-2027, slower ramp-up | Approval delayed or comes with conditions |
| Global market expansion | Emerging-market generics expand the patient pool, Novo Nordisk brand retains a premium | Emerging markets get taken over by generics, Novo Nordisk retreats to Europe/US | Global pricing spirals downward |
| **Overall growth judgment** | **Return to double-digit growth in 2027-2028** | **Return to low-single-digit growth in 2027** | **Still in negative growth in 2027** |

**If you're an investor, the most important window to watch is Q4 2026 through Q2 2027:**
1. Will CagriSema get FDA approval on schedule?
2. Is oral Wegovy's uptake curve accelerating or decelerating?
3. Can full-year 2027 guidance return to positive growth?

Once these three data points come in, the debate over "growth engine stalled" versus "temporary trough" will have an answer.

## 5. An easily overlooked structural shift: Medicare coverage

One of the most important policy changes of 2026: **US federal Medicare has begun covering GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.**

What does this mean?

| Before | After |
|------|------|
| Among the 67 million US Medicare beneficiaries, obese individuals could not get GLP-1 weight-loss drugs covered by Medicare | Eligible beneficiaries can now get a prescription, with a **$50/month** out-of-pocket cap |
| GLP-1 weight-loss drugs relied mainly on commercial insurance and cash pay | A huge new payment channel has been added |
| Older obese populations were largely excluded from the market | Older populations become a new source of incremental demand |

**Upside:** the potential patient population expands significantly. JPMorgan estimates Medicare coverage could add several million new GLP-1 users.

**Downside:** Medicare's negotiating power is far stronger than commercial insurers'. The actual selling prices drugmakers realize through the Medicare channel are typically far below list price. This is yet another manifestation of the "volume up, price down" tension.
