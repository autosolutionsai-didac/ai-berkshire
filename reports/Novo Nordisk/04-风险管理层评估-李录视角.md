# Novo Nordisk (NVO) — Risk and Management Assessment

> Core question: Can management steer Novo through this difficult stretch? What are the main risks?

## I. Management changes and assessment

### 1.1 CEO succession: from Lars to Maziar

**Former CEO Lars Fruergaard Jorgensen:**
During his tenure (2017-2025), Novo went through the GLP-1 boom, with revenue growing from about DKK 111 billion to DKK 309.1 billion. But he also drew significant criticism for failing to effectively counter Eli Lilly's competition and for setbacks in CagriSema's development. In August 2025, Lars "mutually agreed" with the board to step down — wording that typically signals a forced exit.

**New CEO Maziar Mike Doustdar:**
- **Background**: more than 20 years at Novo; previously Executive Vice President of International Operations, overseeing affiliates in 80 countries. Under his leadership, international operations revenue more than doubled to approximately DKK 112 billion (2024)
- **Core strength**: deep expertise in commercialization and market execution — exactly the capability Novo needs most right now. Not R&D innovation (that was his predecessor's strength), but holding and expanding market share in an increasingly competitive environment
- **Strategic direction**: at the January 2026 JPM conference, he laid out three clear priorities:
  1. Accelerate commercial execution
  2. Advance the R&D pipeline
  3. Balance investment against financial discipline

### 1.2 Sweeping reforms

**Layoffs of 9,000 employees:** about 11.5% of the global workforce of 78,400, expected to save DKK 8 billion (approximately $1.3 billion) per year by the end of 2026. The savings are being redirected into R&D, commercialization, and capacity expansion.

**Organizational restructuring:**
- Merged the research and early-development department with the development department, establishing a Chief Scientific Officer to oversee both under unified management
- Emil Kongshoj Larsen took over as Executive Vice President of International Operations
- Strengthened direct-to-patient (DTP) and cash-pay channels, with the CEO explicitly stating this increasingly resembles a consumer-goods business

**Board changes:** former Novo CEO Lars Sorensen (not to be confused with the outgoing Lars Fruergaard Jorgensen) has been nominated as chairman — a signal of tighter governance-level oversight.

### 1.3 Management assessment

**Positives:**
- The new CEO has a strong execution background, well suited to the current "fighting phase"
- The combination of layoffs, restructuring, and refocusing on the core business is a clear, decisive package
- Oral Wegovy's above-expectations Q1 performance proves commercial execution is working
- The DKK 1.5 billion share buyback plan for 2026 signals management's confidence in long-term value

**Concerns:**
- The CEO transition is happening during the company's most difficult period, and transition-period execution risk cannot be ignored
- An 11.5% workforce reduction could hurt organizational morale and R&D efficiency
- The new CEO lacks an R&D background — whether he can navigate the critical clinical and regulatory milestones for CagriSema and Amycretin remains an open question

## II. In-depth assessment of the R&D pipeline

### 2.1 Near-term catalysts (12-18 months)

| Pipeline | Progress | Expected timeline | Importance |
|------|------|-------------|--------|
| CagriSema (injectable) | NDA submitted to FDA | Expected approval Q4 2026, launch early 2027 | **Extremely high** — Novo's core counterattack weapon |
| Oral Wegovy | **Already launched** | Continued ramp-up | **High** — Q1 has already validated demand |
| Oral Ozempic | Planned | Expected launch in the U.S. under the "Ozempic Pill" brand in Q2 2026 | Medium-high — consolidates the diabetes business |
| Oral Wegovy adolescent indication | In clinical trials | 2026-2027 | Medium — expands the addressable population |

### 2.2 Medium- to long-term pipeline (2-5 years)

| Pipeline | Mechanism | Progress | Potential |
|------|------|------|------|
| Amycretin/Zenagamtide | Dual GLP-1 + amylin agonist | Phase III initiated (Q1 2026) | **Extremely high** — early data shows >20% weight loss, potentially matching Eli Lilly |
| IcoSema | Once-weekly basal insulin | Approved/launching | Medium — insulin upgrade |
| NASH/MASH indication | Semaglutide for fatty liver disease | Phase III | High — a new major indication |
| Cardiovascular/renal indications | Semaglutide | Under expanded study | Medium-high — platform value |

**Pipeline assessment:** Amycretin is Novo's "trump card." If Phase III data confirms >20% weight loss with a good safety profile, Novo has a chance to substantially narrow the efficacy gap with Eli Lilly by 2028-2029. CagriSema, meanwhile, is the "bridge product" for the transition period.

## III. Core risk matrix

### High-probability risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| 2026 revenue genuinely declines | Very high | Medium | The company's own guidance already confirms -4% to -12% CER |
| Eli Lilly's share continues to grow | High | High | Tirzepatide's clinical advantage plus oral-drug launch |
| Continued U.S. pricing pressure | High | High | Dual pressure from policy and competition |

### Medium-probability risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| CagriSema FDA approval delay/conditional restrictions | Medium | Extremely high | The single-chamber injector was abandoned in favor of a dual-chamber device, adding uncertainty |
| Gray-market compounding after tighter regulation | Medium | Medium | Demand has already been proven and is very hard to eliminate entirely |
| Danish krone exchange-rate volatility | Medium | Medium | ADR investors bear additional currency risk |

### Low-probability, high-impact risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| Long-term GLP-1 safety issues | Low | Extremely high | If a serious adverse-event signal emerges, the entire category would be hit |
| Amycretin Phase III failure | Low-medium | Extremely high | Novo's long-term counterattack plan would completely collapse |
| U.S. drug-pricing control legislation | Low-medium | High | Would systematically reduce profitability across the entire industry |

## IV. Core judgment from Li Lu's perspective

> "What matters most in investing is understanding the essence of the business, and whether management is doing the right things."

**Key assessment of management:**

New CEO Doustdar has done several things worth crediting:
1. **Facing the problem head-on** — proactively cutting prices, laying off staff, and restructuring, without papering over the situation
2. **Focusing on the core** — cutting non-core businesses and concentrating resources on diabetes and obesity
3. **Accelerating the pipeline** — CagriSema's NDA has been submitted, Amycretin's Phase III has been initiated

But there are also warning signals worth watching:
1. In a CNBC interview, the CEO said "things will get worse in the short term before they get better" — wording that may hint 2026 results could land near the low end of guidance
2. Whether the shift in mindset from "monopolist" to "challenger" can permeate the entire organization still needs time to be proven

**Judgment on risk/reward:**

Buying at the current 10-12x P/E, downside appears limited (barring a category-wide GLP-1 safety issue), while upside depends on:
1. Whether the oral product line can keep exceeding expectations (the current trend is positive)
2. Whether CagriSema gets approved on schedule
3. Whether positive growth resumes in 2027

**Risk and management score: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Management has set the right direction since the leadership change, but execution is still in its early stages. Competitive risk is real, and CagriSema's approval outcome is the biggest short-term uncertainty. The good news is that valuation already reflects most of the bad news.
