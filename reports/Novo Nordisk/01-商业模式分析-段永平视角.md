# Novo Nordisk (NVO) — Business Model Analysis

> Core question: is GLP-1 a good business? What is the user stickiness, pricing power, and commercial essence?

## I. The Essence of the Business

The commercial essence of GLP-1 drugs is a **subscription model for chronic-disease medication**. Both obesity and diabetes are lifelong conditions that require ongoing management; once a patient starts taking a GLP-1 drug, weight regain after discontinuation is a common phenomenon. This means:

- **Extremely high repurchase rate**: clinical data show that after discontinuing semaglutide, patients regain about two-thirds of their lost weight within a year. This is not a "one-and-done" drug, but a product requiring continuous use.
- **An extremely high market ceiling**: roughly 650 million adults worldwide suffer from obesity, and current GLP-1 penetration is under 5%. This is a hundred-billion-dollar-plus addressable market.
- **From "treating disease" to "improving quality of life"**: GLP-1 treats not only diabetes but has also extended into weight loss, cardiovascular protection, and other areas, expanding the user base from patients to people pursuing a healthy lifestyle.

## II. Analysis of User (Patient) Stickiness

**Sources of stickiness:**

1. **Physiological dependence**: weight regain after discontinuation creates a natural incentive to continue use
2. **Significant effect, high switching cost**: injectable Wegovy can achieve roughly 15-17% weight loss; once patients experience this effect, they are reluctant to switch easily
3. **First-mover brand recognition**: Ozempic's and Wegovy's brand awareness among consumers far exceeds that of competing products, making them synonymous with GLP-1 itself
4. **The strategic significance of the oral formulation**: in January 2026 the FDA approved oral Wegovy (25mg once daily), the world's first oral GLP-1 weight-loss drug. The oral formulation lowers the barrier to use (no more injections), which should substantially expand the patient pool. In Q1 2026, oral Wegovy already generated DKK 2.26 billion (approximately $355 million) in revenue, far exceeding analysts' expected DKK 1.16 billion

**Concerns over stickiness:**

- If a competing product (such as Eli Lilly's tirzepatide) offers better efficacy and fewer side effects, patients will switch
- In head-to-head clinical trials, Eli Lilly's tirzepatide has been proven "superior" to semaglutide in weight-loss effect and tolerability

## III. Assessment of Pricing Power

**Positive factors:**

- Demand for chronic-disease drugs is rigid, and once covered by insurance, patients are insensitive to price
- Manufacturing GLP-1 drugs involves complex biopharmaceutical processes, with a higher barrier to generic entry than small-molecule drugs
- Semaglutide's core patent is protected through December 2031, and Novo has built a patent thicket through 320 US patent applications

**Negative factors:**

- Rising pricing pressure from US insurers has led Novo to proactively cut prices to expand access (the starting dose of oral Wegovy costs only $149/month)
- Competition from compounding pharmacies: cheap generic-equivalent semaglutide once diverted demand on a large scale. Although the FDA tightened oversight in February 2026 and Novo has pursued litigation to defend its rights, this threat has not been fully eliminated
- Full-year 2026 guidance: sales and operating profit are expected to decline 4-12%, with pricing pressure as the core reason

## IV. Duan Yongping's Core Judgment

> "A good business model means: doing the right things, and doing things right."

**Doing the right things:** the GLP-1 track has been the biggest structural opportunity in biopharmaceuticals over the past decade. As the first mover, Novo defined this category and built up advantages in brand, channels, and manufacturing capacity. This is "the right thing."

**Doing things right:** this is where the current controversy lies. Novo faces intense competition from Eli Lilly, and its market share is eroding. In Q1 2026, Eli Lilly's share of the US GLP-1 market had reached 60%. Novo's response strategy — leading with the oral formulation, proactive price cuts, cost-cutting through layoffs — is directionally correct, but its execution remains to be seen.

**A key question from the user's (consumer's) perspective:** GLP-1 currently behaves more like a "category business" than a "brand business." A patient's choice between Wegovy and Zepbound depends more on efficacy and price than on brand loyalty. This means Novo's moat is somewhat narrower than it appears on the surface.

**Business model score: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

The track is excellent, but within the category competition, Novo is shifting from monopolist to one of a duopoly. The successful launch of oral Wegovy is an important positive signal, but Eli Lilly's oral drug (orforglipron/Foundayo) is also about to follow.
