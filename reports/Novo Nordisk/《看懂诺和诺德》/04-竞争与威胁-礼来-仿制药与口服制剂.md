# Competition and Threats — Eli Lilly, Generics, and Oral Formulations

> *Understanding Novo Nordisk* series · Part 04

## 1. The elephant in the room: Eli Lilly

In Q1 2026, Eli Lilly's Mounjaro grew +125% year over year. In the same quarter, Novo Nordisk's Ozempic declined -8% year over year.

Put those two numbers side by side and they say more than any competitive analysis could. Before 2024, Novo Nordisk was the undisputed leader of the GLP-1 market. Today, its US market share has fallen to about 40%, with Eli Lilly taking the other 60%.

This isn't a distribution problem or a marketing problem — it's a **product problem**. To understand Novo Nordisk's current situation, you first have to understand why Eli Lilly won.

### A brutal set of comparative numbers

| Metric | Novo Nordisk | Eli Lilly | Source |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Q1 2026 diabetes drug revenue | Ozempic ~DKK 28 billion (**-8%** YoY) | Mounjaro **$8.66 billion** (**+125%** YoY) | CNBC |
| Q1 2026 weight-loss drug revenue | Injectable Wegovy DKK 18.2 billion (+12%) + oral DKK 2.26 billion | Zepbound **$4.16 billion** (**+80%** YoY) | CNBC |
| US GLP-1 market share | **~40%** | **~60%** | 24/7 Wall St |
| GLP-1 market share outside the US | Overtaken | Has overtaken Novo Nordisk | GxP News |
| Full-year 2026 guidance | Adjusted revenue/profit **-4% to -12%** | Q1 revenue **+56%** YoY; full-year guidance raised | CNBC |
| PE (TTM) | **~10-12x** | **~50-60x** | Multiple sources |
| Market cap | **~$196 billion** | **~$900 billion** | Multiple sources |

One up 125%, the other down 8%. The market has awarded Eli Lilly nearly five times Novo Nordisk's market cap — and not without reason.

### Why is Eli Lilly's product stronger?

The core difference lies in the **molecular mechanism**:

| Dimension | Semaglutide (Novo Nordisk) | Tirzepatide (Eli Lilly) |
|------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Target | GLP-1 single-target | GLP-1 + GIP **dual-target** |
| Weight loss (head-to-head) | ~15-17% | ~**20-22%** (superior to semaglutide within the same trial) |
| Tolerability | Nausea and other GI side effects more common | **Better** tolerability in head-to-head trials |
| Clinical positioning | "First mover + brand recognition" | "Stronger efficacy + better experience" |

**The head-to-head trial result is damning.** In a direct comparison within the same clinical trial, tirzepatide was "superior" to semaglutide in both weight-loss efficacy and patient tolerability. This isn't a marketing issue or a distribution issue — it is a **product gap at the level of science**.

In a prescription-drug market where efficacy is king, falling behind on product strength is the hardest disadvantage to overcome.

**Counterpoint:** While the head-to-head advantage is real, the gap isn't overwhelming. A weight-loss effect of 15-17% versus 20-22% is "clearly effective" for the average patient either way. What ultimately determines market share often isn't efficacy alone but also price, convenience, insurance coverage, and physician prescribing habits. Novo Nordisk still holds an edge in brand recognition and channel accumulation.

## 2. Oral GLP-1 competition — how much first-mover advantage is left?

Another key battle of 2026 is unfolding on the **oral formulation** front.

### The head-to-head between two oral GLP-1 drugs

| Dimension | Oral Wegovy (Novo Nordisk) | Foundayo/orforglipron (Eli Lilly) |
|------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| FDA approval date | **January 2026** (3 months ahead) | **April 1, 2026** |
| Molecule type | Peptide (biologic) | **Small molecule** (chemical drug) |
| Dosing | Once daily, **must be taken fasting in the morning**, no food/drink/other medication within 30 minutes | Once daily, **any time, no fasting required** |
| Weight-loss effect | Comparable to injectable Wegovy (~15%) | 7.8%-12.4%, depending on dose |
| Pricing (out-of-pocket/discounted) | Starting dose $149/month | As low as $25/month with commercial insurance copay |
| Q1 performance | **1.3 million prescriptions, DKK 2.26 billion** | **20,000 prescriptions** within 20 days of launch |

**Oral Wegovy's advantages:** better weight-loss efficacy (on par with the injectable version), earlier launch, and an already-established prescribing and usage pattern.

**Foundayo's advantages:** far greater convenience than oral Wegovy (no fasting requirement), a small-molecule structure (lower production cost, easier to scale and to genericize), and more aggressive pricing.

**Key judgment:** the oral GLP-1 market will likely also settle into a "duopoly" structure. Oral Wegovy's first-mover advantage is measured in months, not years. The real competitive battleground will become clearer in the second half of 2026 into 2027 — it will come down to which oral product secures insurance coverage and physician prescribing habits faster.

**One structural difference worth watching:** Foundayo is a small-molecule drug, which means its bar for generic competition is far lower than for a biologic. Over the long run, Foundayo may be easier to genericize — whereas oral Wegovy, as a biologic, is harder to copy. That's a potential advantage for Novo Nordisk's long-term patent protection.

## 3. The next-generation pipeline — who has more ammunition?

The current competitive landscape isn't the endgame. Both companies are betting on next-generation products.

### Novo Nordisk's pipeline

| Product | Mechanism | Status | Expected effect |
|------|------|------|---------|
| **CagriSema** | GLP-1 + amylin agonist (dual-component combination) | **NDA submitted to the FDA** (December 2025); approval expected Q4 2026 or early 2027 | **~22.7%** weight loss in non-diabetic obese patients |
| **Amycretin/Zenagamtide** | Dual GLP-1 + amylin agonism (single molecule) | Phase III initiated (Q1 2026) | Early data shows **>20%** weight loss |
| Oral Ozempic | Oral semaglutide | Planned Q2 2026 US launch under the "Ozempic Pill" brand | Reinforces the diabetes franchise |

### Eli Lilly's pipeline

| Product | Mechanism | Status | Expected effect |
|------|------|------|---------|
| **Retatrutide** | GLP-1 + GIP + glucagon **triple-target** | Phase III underway | Early data shows **~24%** weight loss |
| Foundayo (launched) | Oral GLP-1 (small molecule) | **FDA approved** | 7.8%-12.4% weight loss |

### Key judgments on the pipeline comparison

**Near term (2026-2027): CagriSema vs. current competitors**

CagriSema is Novo Nordisk's "bridge weapon." Its 22.7% weight-loss effect can match (or even slightly exceed) Eli Lilly's current tirzepatide (~20-22%). If it's approved in Q4 2026 and launches in 2027, it could temporarily narrow the product gap.

**But there's a significant concern:** CagriSema was originally planned with a single-chamber injector device, but had to switch to a dual-chamber device due to technical issues. That adds manufacturing complexity and regulatory uncertainty. Will the FDA impose additional requirements over the device change? Will the post-launch user experience be affected? Both are open variables.

**Medium term (2028-2030): Amycretin vs. Retatrutide**

This is the matchup that will determine the long-term landscape.

- **Amycretin (Novo Nordisk):** Dual GLP-1 + amylin agonism, with early data showing >20% weight loss. If Phase III confirms this effect, Novo Nordisk has a chance to catch back up to Eli Lilly.
- **Retatrutide (Eli Lilly):** Triple-target (GLP-1 + GIP + glucagon), with early data showing ~24% weight loss. If successful, it would be the most potent weight-loss drug available.

**Amycretin's Phase III data is Novo Nordisk's single most important catalyst over the next 2-3 years.** Success would open the door to a turnaround; failure would leave it a long-term "chaser."

## 4. The threat from generics — losing ground globally

Patent protection needs to be viewed across two separate battlefields: the US and the rest of the world.

### The US market: relatively safe until 2032

| Dimension | Situation |
|------|------|
| Core compound patent expiration | **December 2031** (including patent extensions) |
| Expected generic launch | **2032 at the earliest** |
| Companies that have signaled generic intent | **At least 13** have contacted the FDA |
| Gray-market competition from compounding pharmacies | The FDA tightened oversight in 2026, but validated demand is hard to eliminate entirely |

In the US, semaglutide still has at least **5-6 years** of patent protection remaining. This is Novo Nordisk's core moat in its largest market.

But the impact of compounding pharmacies shouldn't be dismissed. Even though the FDA has tightened oversight and Novo Nordisk has pursued litigation to protect its rights, **demand for cheap GLP-1 drugs has been proven real**. That gray market is part of what's forcing Novo Nordisk to cut prices proactively — the planned drop in Wegovy's list price to $675/month in 2027 is partly aimed at competing with compounded versions.

### The global market: already losing ground in 2026

| Market | Patent status | Generic situation |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **India** | **Expires in 2026** | At least 5 drugmakers have launched generic versions, priced **80%** lower |
| **China** | **Expires in 2026** | Multiple domestic drugmakers positioning; could become the largest generic market |
| **Brazil** | Expires 2026 | Generic competition beginning |
| **Canada** | Expires 2026 | Generic competition beginning |

**India is the most instructive case.** In 2026, at least five Indian drugmakers launched generic semaglutide, priced 80% below the original (source: CNBC, March 2026). Novo Nordisk's CEO responded that "brand value still holds" — but in a price-extremely-sensitive emerging market, how long can a brand premium survive?

**What does this mean?** Novo Nordisk's global revenue structure is set to bifurcate — the US market sustains pricing thanks to patent protection, while other markets around the world face generic pressure. In 2025, Ozempic revenue already began to decline year over year, partly due to patent expirations and intensifying competition in international markets.

## 5. The core judgment on the competitive landscape

### Is this a "Nokia moment" or a "Coca-Cola moment"?

This is the analogy question investors most need to answer:

**The Nokia-moment (structural decline) hypothesis:**
- Eli Lilly's dual/triple-target molecules represent the next-generation technology direction
- Novo Nordisk's single-target semaglutide is "last-generation"
- Market-share loss is irreversible
- Pricing power is collapsing systematically

**The Coca-Cola-moment (duopoly coexistence) hypothesis:**
- The GLP-1 market is large enough ($80-120 billion by 2030) to support two winners
- Even if Novo Nordisk's share falls from 60% to 35-40%, absolute revenue can still grow
- A century of diabetes expertise plus brand, manufacturing capacity, and a robust pipeline give it a solid foundation
- CagriSema and Amycretin have a real chance to close the product gap

**The data points more toward the latter — but with conditions.**

Novo Nordisk will not be "pushed out of the market." The GLP-1 market's defining characteristics are: demand outstrips supply (penetration is only 6%), entry barriers are high (biologics manufacturing capacity), and payers prefer having options (they won't reimburse only one company's product). In a market structured this way, the #2 player can still thrive.

**But Novo Nordisk must catch up on product strength.** If neither CagriSema nor Amycretin can match Eli Lilly's next-generation products, its market share could slide further from 40% to 30% or lower — and that would no longer be "Coca-Cola" but rather "a lesser version of Pepsi."

### Summary of the competitive landscape

| Dimension | Novo Nordisk's position | Trend |
|------|--------------|------|
| Injectable GLP-1 weight loss | Gone from monopolist to #2 | ↓ Still losing share |
| Oral GLP-1 weight loss | First mover, but the window is only months long | → About to enter head-on competition |
| Diabetes | A century of accumulated strength, but Ozempic's growth has turned negative | ↓ Plateau |
| Next-generation pipeline | CagriSema awaiting approval + Amycretin in Phase III | ? Depends on the data |
| Global markets | Emerging markets being hit by generics | ↓↓ Multiple countries' patents expire in 2026 |
| Pricing power | Proactive 50% price cut, irreversible | ↓ Trading price for volume |

## 6. A counterintuitive view

**The stronger Eli Lilly gets, the safer Novo Nordisk's valuation may actually be.**

Sounds contradictory? Here's the logic:

1. Eli Lilly trades at 50-60x PE, Novo Nordisk at 10-12x — **that 5x valuation gap already implies the assumption "Eli Lilly wins, Novo Nordisk loses"**
2. If Eli Lilly really does win (60%+ share), but Novo Nordisk as the #2 player is still profitable — then a 10x PE for Novo Nordisk is "excessive punishment"
3. If Novo Nordisk narrows the gap via CagriSema/Amycretin — then a valuation recovery from 10x to 15-18x PE represents 50-80% of upside

**The risk is: if Novo Nordisk turns out not to be "the #2 player" but rather "a gradually declining player," even a 10x PE could still be too expensive.** That depends on the pipeline data, especially Amycretin.
