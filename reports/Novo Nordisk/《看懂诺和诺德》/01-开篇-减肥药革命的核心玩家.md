## A set of numbers that will make you rethink Novo Nordisk

18 months ago, this company's market cap exceeded the entire GDP of Denmark. Today, its P/E is just 10x — lower than most of the world's "mediocre" consumer-goods companies.

It's the same company. Its net margin is 33%, its ROIC is 21%, and it owns the world's largest weight-loss drug brand. Yet the market is valuing it about the same as a traditional manufacturer in a sunset industry.

This kind of extreme contradiction means one of two things: either the market is wrong, or the company has a problem you haven't fully understood yet. Figuring out which one it is is exactly what this series sets out to do.

Novo Nordisk's stock price trajectory:

- June 2024: around **$81** — the peak of the "king of weight-loss drugs" narrative, with market cap briefly exceeding Denmark's entire GDP
- December 2025: **$72** — growth slowing, the market starting to hesitate
- February 4, 2026: **a 17% single-day crash** — full-year guidance of "revenue and profit down 4%-12%" shocked the market
- May 20, 2026: around **$44** — market cap of roughly **$196 billion**, 52-week low of $35

**In 18 months, the company went from "10th-largest by global market cap" to "P/E below 12x."** The same company, priced two completely opposite ways by the market.

Opening the FY2025 annual report and Q1 2026 earnings, Novo Nordisk has a set of figures worth comparing side by side:

|------|--------|----------------|---------|
| Total revenue | **DKK 309.1 billion (approx. $45.9 billion)** | DKK 96.8 billion (reported +32%) | Top-10 scale globally in pharma |
| Net profit | **DKK 102.4 billion (approx. $15.2 billion)** | — | 33% net margin, top-tier for the industry |

Put these numbers together — **a company with a 33% net margin, 21% ROIC, and the world's largest weight-loss drug brand, trading at a P/E compressed to 10x**. By comparison, its main competitor Eli Lilly trades at a P/E of **50-60x**.

If you think "a 10x P/E means it's cheap" — you probably haven't seen the risk clearly yet.
If you think "the growth engine has stalled" — you may be overlooking the stunning first-quarter data for oral Wegovy.

What this series sets out to do is **separate "why it's cheap" from "will it stay cheap"** so you can decide for yourself whether it's worth investing in.

## What kind of company is Novo Nordisk, really

If all you've heard is "weight-loss drugs," you only know about a tenth of what Novo Nordisk actually is.

### A century-old diabetes giant

Novo Nordisk was founded in **1923**, headquartered in Bagsvaerd, near Copenhagen, Denmark. It is the world's largest insulin manufacturer, with **more than 100 years** of history in diabetes treatment. About **40%** of the world's insulin supply comes from Novo Nordisk (source: Novo Nordisk annual report).

One of the most distinctive things about this company: it is not controlled by public shareholders in the usual sense — the **Novo Nordisk Foundation** holds about **28%** of the shares and is one of the largest corporate foundations in the world, with total assets exceeding **$100 billion**. The foundation's mission is "to support scientific research and humanitarian causes," which means Novo Nordisk's corporate governance carries a built-in "long-termism" gene.

### From treating disease to "improving lives"

Novo Nordisk's revenue structure is clear-cut:

| Business segment | FY2025 revenue | Share | Nature |
|---------|-------------|------|------|
| Diabetes care | DKK 207.1 billion | **67%** | Core cash cow |
| Obesity care | DKK 82.3 billion | **27%** | Growth engine |
| Other (rare disease, etc.) | ~DKK 19.7 billion | 6% | Stable contributor |

The biggest change over the past 5 years: **obesity care has gone from supporting role to lead role**. In 2020, obesity-care revenue was under DKK 10 billion; in 2025 it was DKK 82.3 billion — **more than an 8x increase in 5 years**.

The core driver behind this growth can be summed up in one word: **semaglutide**.

## Semaglutide: one molecule that transformed a company

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist (a glucagon-like peptide-1 class drug) developed by Novo Nordisk. By mimicking a natural human hormone, it simultaneously lowers blood sugar and suppresses appetite.

The same molecule, marketed by Novo Nordisk under **four brands** targeting different markets:

| Brand | Formulation | Indication | FY2025/Q1 2026 performance |
|------|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Ozempic** | Injection (once weekly) | Type 2 diabetes | FY2025's largest single product; down 8% YoY in Q1 2026 |
| **Wegovy** | Injection (once weekly) | Weight loss | Injectable-version Q1 2026 revenue of DKK 18.2 billion (+12%) |
| **Rybelsus** | Oral (once daily) | Type 2 diabetes | Early oral version, market gradually maturing |
| **Oral Wegovy** | Oral (once daily, 25mg) | Weight loss | **FDA-approved in January 2026; Q1 revenue of DKK 2.26 billion — 2x analyst expectations** |

Oral Wegovy is the biggest highlight of 2026. In just one quarter on the market, it garnered about **1.3 million** prescriptions and has been called by Wall Street "one of the fastest drug launches ever." Its strategic significance lies in pulling the "needle-averse" population into the weight-loss drug market.

**Counterargument:** Oral Wegovy is priced at just **$149/month** (starting dose), far below the injectable version's list price of $1,349/month. Whether a low-price, high-volume model can generate sufficient profit still requires more quarters of data to confirm. Moreover, Eli Lilly's competing oral product Foundayo (orforglipron) was **approved by the FDA on April 1, 2026** and can be taken at any time of day without fasting — offering better convenience than oral Wegovy.

## Three common misconceptions about Novo Nordisk

### Misconception one: Novo Nordisk is a "weight-loss drug company"

**67%** of revenue still comes from diabetes care. While the obesity business is growing fastest, diabetes remains the cash cow.

A more accurate definition: **Novo Nordisk is a chronic-disease management company built around the GLP-1 molecule as its core platform, spanning diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular indications**. Semaglutide is not just for weight loss — it is also expanding into cardiovascular protection (the SELECT trial showed a 20% reduction in cardiac event risk), kidney protection, and fatty liver disease (NASH/MASH).

This means that even if weight-loss drug growth slows, semaglutide's "platform" value is still being unlocked.

### Misconception two: Novo Nordisk monopolizes the weight-loss drug market

**That may have been true in 2024, but it's no longer true in 2026.**

Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (brand names Mounjaro/Zepbound) has already reached about **60%** share of the U.S. GLP-1 market (source: 24/7 Wall St, May 2026). More alarming still, Eli Lilly has also surpassed Novo Nordisk in markets outside the U.S. (source: GxP News, May 2026).

Novo Nordisk is shifting from **sole dominant player** to **one of a duopoly**. This shift in identity is one of the core reasons the P/E compressed from 24x to 10x.

### Misconception three: a 10x P/E means you've found a bargain

**The 10x P/E is not an accident — it is the market's pricing of a "growth cliff."**

In February 2026, Novo Nordisk issued full-year guidance that shocked the market: adjusted revenue and operating profit are expected to **decline 4%-12%** (at constant exchange rates, revised slightly upward after the Q1 earnings report). This is the first negative-growth guidance the company has given in years.

With the P/E compressed from 24x to 10x, the market's implied assumptions are:
- The growth engine is permanently slowing
- Pricing power is being systematically eroded
- Eli Lilly's product edge is irreversible

**Whether this assumption is correct is the central question this whole series will answer.**
