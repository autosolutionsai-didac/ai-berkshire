# Valuation Judgment — Pricing a High-Growth Pharma Company at Its Low Point

> *Understanding Novo Nordisk* series · Part 05 · Finale
> Reading time: ~10 minutes

---

## 1. What exactly is a 10x PE pricing in?

Net margin of 33%, ROIC of 21%, the world's largest weight-loss drug brand, a century of accumulated diabetes expertise.

And yet the PE is only 10x.

Is the market wrong, or does the market know something you don't? The first four parts broke down the business, the moat, the growth runway, and the competitive landscape. This part answers just one question: **is a current PE of 10-12x reasonable?**

Let's start with "what is the market pricing in" — **what drove the PE down from 24x to 10x?**

| Factor | Contribution | Notes |
|------|--------|------|
| 2026 negative-growth guidance | **Largest** | Revenue/profit guided at -4% to -12%, the first decline in years |
| Intensifying competition from Eli Lilly | Large | Share fell from 60% to 40%; product falling behind |
| Proactive 50% price cut | Medium | A substantive concession of pricing power, highly symbolic |
| Patent expirations in emerging markets | Medium | Generic entrants flooding in across India, China, Brazil, etc. |
| Danish krone exchange rate | Small | ADRs are priced in USD; FX movement amplifies the decline |
| One-off impairments | None | No material one-off impairment in FY2025 |

**Conclusion: the low PE is not the result of accounting conventions or one-off factors — it is a genuine reflection of fundamental expectations.**

The core assumption implicit in the market's pricing is: **Novo Nordisk's growth engine has permanently slowed — it has gone from a "high-growth biotech company" to a "growth-plateaued mature pharma company."**

Is that assumption correct?

## 2. Three scenarios

We won't give you a "precise price target" — because a precise target creates a false sense of certainty. Instead, here are three scenarios; you decide the probabilities.

### Scenario A: Temporary trough, growth resumes in 2027

**Assumptions:**
- CagriSema receives FDA approval in Q4 2026 or early 2027 and launches commercially
- Oral Wegovy continues to beat expectations, contributing $1.5-3.0 billion in revenue in 2027
- Full-year 2027 guidance returns to low-single-digit positive growth
- Amycretin Phase III data is positive (>20% weight loss, good safety profile)
- Medicare coverage accelerates GLP-1 penetration

**Valuation implications:**
- If growth resumes in 2027, a PE recovery from 10x to **15-18x** would be reasonable (the median for large global pharma companies is 15-20x)
- Current share price ~$44 → implies **$66-$79**
- **Potential upside: 50%-80%**

**Probability of this scenario:** If forced to give a subjective judgment — moderately high. Oral Wegovy's better-than-expected Q1 2026 performance (roughly 2x analyst expectations) and the modest upward revision to full-year guidance (from -5%~-13% to -4%~-12%) both point in a positive direction. But "moderately high" is not "certain."

### Scenario B: Competitive landscape stabilizes, Novo settles as a steady #2 in a duopoly

**Assumptions:**
- CagriSema is approved but the ramp-up is unremarkable
- Oral Wegovy faces intense competition from Foundayo and growth slows
- 2027 revenue is roughly flat (±2%)
- US market share stabilizes at 35-40%
- Amycretin data has not yet read out; uncertainty persists

**Valuation implications:**
- PE may hold at **12-15x**
- Current share price ~$44 → implies **$53-$66**
- **Potential upside: 20%-50%**

**Probability of this scenario:** If forced to judge — this may be the closest thing to a realistic base case.

### Scenario C: The growth engine continues to stall

**Assumptions:**
- CagriSema approval is delayed or comes with conditions
- Oral Wegovy loses substantial share to Foundayo
- 2027 revenue continues to decline
- Amycretin Phase III data disappoints
- A GLP-1 class-wide safety issue emerges

**Valuation implications:**
- PE could compress further to **8-10x**
- Current share price ~$44 → implies **$35-$44** (i.e., at or below the current level)
- **Potential downside: 0%-20%**

**Probability of this scenario:** Low but cannot be ruled out. GLP-1 safety risk in particular deserves attention — while there is no clear warning signal today, this is a new drug class with a rapidly expanding user base, and long-term safety data is still accumulating.

## 3. Key variables and data points to watch

Don't guess at probabilities — let the data speak. Below are the most important data points over the next 12-18 months:

| Timing | Event | Impact on valuation | Importance |
|------|------|------------|--------|
| **Q2-Q3 2026** | Continued oral Wegovy uptake data | Tests whether "volume up, price down" can turn into a virtuous cycle | ★★★★ |
| **Q2 2026** | Oral Ozempic ("Ozempic Pill") US launch | Reinforces the diabetes franchise, tests the oral platform | ★★★ |
| **H2 2026** | Foundayo uptake data | Gauges the intensity of oral competition | ★★★★ |
| **Q4 2026** | **CagriSema FDA approval decision** | **The most critical near-term catalyst — approval would kick off a valuation recovery** | ★★★★★ |
| **Early 2027** | CagriSema commercial launch + full-year 2027 guidance | Can growth turn positive again? Determines whether Scenario A or B plays out | ★★★★★ |
| **2027-2028** | **Amycretin Phase III data readout** | **Determines the long-term competitive position — the most important medium-term catalyst** | ★★★★★ |

**If you can only watch one data point: the CagriSema FDA approval outcome.**
**If you can watch two: add the Amycretin Phase III data.**

These two data points will determine whether Novo Nordisk is "temporarily behind but able to catch up" or "structurally behind."

## 4. Comparisons with historical troughs

Investors like to find historical analogies. Novo Nordisk's current situation can be compared against two cases.

### Analogy one: Coca-Cola in 1999 (a bullish comparison)

In 1999, Coca-Cola faced slowing growth, a management transition, and intensifying competition from Pepsi; its PE fell from a peak of 50x to the low 20s. The market thought the growth engine had stalled.

The outcome: Coca-Cola did not decline. It adapted to the new competitive structure and kept generating profit within a duopoly. Its PE eventually recovered to the 25-30x range.

**Similarities to Novo Nordisk:** a century-old company, a shift from monopoly to duopoly, a management change, a sharply compressed PE.

### Analogy two: Pfizer in 2023 (a bearish comparison)

In 2023, after Pfizer's COVID vaccine and treatment revenue fell off a cliff, its PE was compressed to below 10x. The market saw it as "a normal reversion after the disappearance of one-off revenue."

The outcome: Pfizer's PE has stayed depressed, because it genuinely never found a new growth engine after COVID.

**Similarities to Novo Nordisk:** PE compressed to near 10x, sharp slowdown in core product growth.

**The key difference:** Pfizer's COVID revenue was "an unsustainable one-off surge." Novo Nordisk's GLP-1 revenue is "sustained revenue from a long-term chronic-disease market" — with penetration at only 6%, far from saturated. **These are two fundamentally different qualities of revenue.**

Judged by the persistence and predictability of its revenue, Novo Nordisk looks more like Coca-Cola than Pfizer. But whether that potential is realized depends on how the pipeline and competition actually play out.

## 5. Management — is the new CEO an asset or a liability?

In August 2025, former CEO Lars Fruergaard Jorgensen departed by "mutual agreement" with the board — phrasing that usually signals a forced exit. His successor is **Maziar Mike Doustdar**, who has spent more than 20 years at Novo Nordisk and previously ran international operations.

### What the new CEO has done

| Action | Details | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| **Laid off 9,000 people** | About 11.5% of the 78,400 global workforce, saving DKK 8 billion a year (about $1.3 billion) | Decisive, right direction |
| **Reorganized the company** | Merged R&D units, created a Chief Scientific Officer role, strengthened direct-to-patient channels | Focused on the core |
| **Cut prices proactively** | Oral Wegovy at $149/month; injectable version to drop to $675/month in 2027 | Pragmatic but costly |
| **Accelerated the pipeline** | CagriSema NDA submitted; Amycretin Phase III initiated | Key catalysts moving forward |
| **Share buyback** | DKK 1.5 billion buyback program | More symbolic than material in scale |

### Positive signals on management

- The new CEO's background is in **commercial execution and market operations** — precisely the capability needed most right now. The prior CEO's problem wasn't R&D; it was "fighting the war" — underestimating how fast Eli Lilly's competitive threat would materialize
- Oral Wegovy's better-than-expected Q1 performance shows commercial execution is working
- In a CNBC interview, the CEO candidly said "things will get worse before they get better" — pragmatic honesty is better than papering over problems

### Concerns about management

- The CEO transition happened during the company's most difficult period; execution risk during the handover cannot be ignored
- An 11.5% workforce reduction could hurt organizational morale and R&D efficiency
- The new CEO lacks an R&D background — whether he can steer the critical clinical milestones for CagriSema and Amycretin remains an open question

**Overall assessment:** Management is heading in the right direction, but is at the stage of "said it, hasn't fully delivered it yet." Management should be graded dynamically as execution results come in, rather than being given full marks or a death sentence in advance.

## 6. Risk checklist

Laying out all the risks:

### High-probability risks (largely priced in)

| Risk | Notes |
|------|------|
| 2026 revenue genuinely declining | Confirmed by the company's own guidance, -4% to -12% |
| Eli Lilly's share continuing to expand | Tirzepatide's clinical advantage plus the Foundayo launch |
| Continued pricing pressure | The 2027 50% price cut has already been announced and is irreversible |

### Medium-probability risks (partially priced in)

| Risk | Notes |
|------|------|
| CagriSema approval delayed or conditioned | The dual-chamber device change adds uncertainty |
| Accelerating generic competition in emerging markets | India, China patents expire in 2026 |
| Currency risk | Danish krone/US dollar fluctuations affect ADR value |

### Low-probability, high-impact risks (not fully priced in)

| Risk | Notes |
|------|------|
| **Class-wide GLP-1 safety issue** | If a serious adverse-event signal emerged, the whole class would take a hit |
| **Amycretin Phase III failure** | Novo Nordisk's long-term counterattack plan would collapse entirely |
| **US legislation on drug-price controls for Medicare** | Would systematically lower industry profitability |

**The category that most needs watching is "low probability but high impact."** The 10x PE has already priced in most "known bad news," but it has not priced in "unknown bad news."

## 7. Conclusion: no price target, but a decision framework

Back to the opening question: **is Novo Nordisk, at a 10x PE, worth buying?**

**No precise answer — because a precise answer would be a false one.** Instead, here's a decision framework:

### What kind of investor does Novo Nordisk suit?

| Investor type | Suitable? | Reason |
|-----------|---------|------|
| Steady investors seeking certainty | **Not really suitable** | Three layers of uncertainty — competitive landscape, pipeline data, pricing trend — are too high |
| Value investors who can tolerate uncertainty | **Worth watching** | 10x PE + 33% net margin + 21% ROIC = a margin of safety exists, but patience is required |
| Investors who like "distressed turnarounds" | **Best fit** | The core thesis is exactly "the market is overly pessimistic + pipeline catalysts will trigger a valuation recovery" |
| Short-term traders | **Caution** | 2026 results are still in the downtrend phase, likely more volatility in the near term |

### If you decide to follow it, a suggested action framework

| Action | Trigger condition |
|------|---------|
| **Build a starter position (1-2%)** | Worth considering at the current price (~$44) |
| **Add to 3-5%** | CagriSema gets FDA approval + 2027 guidance returns to positive growth |
| **Add to 5-8%** | Amycretin Phase III data confirms >20% weight loss + US market share stops declining |
| **Reduce/exit** | Class-wide GLP-1 safety issue / Amycretin Phase III failure / two consecutive years of revenue decline |

## 8. Summary of the whole series

Having worked through all five parts, here's the rollup of conclusions:

| Part | Core conclusion |
|------|------|
| 01 Opening | Novo Nordisk is a century-old company facing its toughest competition in a decade; the PE falling from 24x to 10x is no accident |
| 02 Business model | GLP-1 is a "chronic-disease subscription" — a good business, but the moat has shifted from "monopoly" to "duopoly competition" |
| 03 Growth ceiling | The market is large enough ($80-120 billion by 2030), but "volume up, price down" is the core tension |
| 04 Competition and threats | Eli Lilly's product genuinely is stronger, but Novo Nordisk won't be pushed out of the market — the pipeline is what matters |
| 05 Valuation judgment | The 10x PE has already priced in most of the bad news; a margin of safety exists, but upside requires catalysts to actually materialize |

**One-sentence summary: Novo Nordisk is a quality asset at a depressed valuation during a difficult period — but "quality" doesn't mean "no risk," and "cheap valuation" doesn't mean "guaranteed to go up." Its future hinges on two data points: the CagriSema approval outcome and the Amycretin Phase III data.**

Until those two data points arrive, staying attentive, keeping position sizing controlled, and not betting one-sidedly may be the most rational approach.
