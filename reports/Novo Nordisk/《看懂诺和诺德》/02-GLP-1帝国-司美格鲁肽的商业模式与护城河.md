## I. What kind of business is GLP-1, really

You stop taking Wegovy, and within a year you regain two-thirds of the weight you lost.

This single clinical fact determines what kind of business GLP-1 really is.

The business model of GLP-1 drugs is, at its core, **a subscription plan for chronic disease**. It's not "you're cured, you leave" — it's "stopping the drug means giving up the results." This explains why the ceiling for this business can be so high, and also why capital markets were once willing to price it far above traditional pharma valuations.

### Why it's a "subscription"

The commercial logic of traditional drugs is "get cured and move on" — you take a course of antibiotics, you're discharged after surgery. GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are completely different:

**Weight rebound after stopping treatment is a widespread phenomenon.** Clinical data shows that after discontinuing semaglutide, patients regain about **two-thirds** of their lost weight within a year (source: STEP 1 trial extension data). This implies:

- **Extremely high repeat-purchase rates**: it's not "use once and you're done" — continued use is required
- **Extremely high lifetime value**: a patient may stay on the drug for 5, 10 years, or longer
- **Extremely high cost of churn**: stopping treatment = weight rebound = a double blow, physical and psychological

In internet-industry terms, this is a subscription model with **extremely high retention, extremely high LTV (customer lifetime value), and naturally negative churn**.

**Counterargument:** The premise of a "subscription" is that patients can keep paying. GLP-1 drugs are expensive (injectable Wegovy is listed at $1,349/month), and about **40%** of U.S. commercial insurance plans — plus most federal Medicare, until recently — do not cover weight-loss medication. If the payment side isn't resolved, the "subscription" breaks down under financial pressure. That said, 2026 has brought a positive shift — U.S. federal Medicare has begun covering GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, capping patient out-of-pocket costs at just **$50/month** (source: NBC News, January 2026).

### How big is the market

Let's lay out the numbers:

| Dimension | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Global obese population | **approx. 650 million** adults (BMI≥30) | WHO, 2024 |
| U.S. obese population | **approx. 100 million** adults | CDC, 2024 |
| Current GLP-1 penetration (U.S.) | about **6%** of the obese/diabetic population | Morgan Stanley, 2026 |
| Projected number of U.S. GLP-1 users by 2030 | **approx. 30 million** | JPMorgan, 2026 |
| Global GLP-1 market size forecast (2030) | **approx. $95 billion** | Goldman Sachs, 2026 (revised down) |
| Global GLP-1 market size forecast (2035) | **approx. $190 billion** | Morgan Stanley, 2026 |

**Key fact: penetration is only 6%.** This means 94% of potential users haven't entered the market yet. Even under the most conservative estimate (Goldman Sachs has already cut its forecast from $130 billion to $95 billion), this is still a market approaching a hundred billion dollars by 2030.

**Counterargument:** The reasons behind Goldman Sachs's downgrade deserve attention — discontinuation rates are higher than expected, pricing pressure is greater than expected, and insurance-coverage rollout is slower than expected. $95 billion is not a number destined to materialize — it's a projection loaded with assumptions.

## II. Novo Nordisk's moat

A good business also needs strong competitive barriers. Novo Nordisk's moat in the GLP-1 market can be broken into four layers, though each layer faces a different degree of erosion.

### Layer one: brand recognition (strength: ★★★★☆)

The name "Ozempic" has become synonymous with GLP-1 — much like "Baidu it" stands for search, or "hail a Didi" stands for ride-hailing.

- Wegovy holds a **65%** share of **new prescriptions** in the U.S. weight-loss drug market (source: CNBC, May 2026)
- Within 10 weeks of launch, about **400,000** Americans started taking oral Wegovy (source: NBC News, March 2026)
- "Ozempic face" has become a social-media buzzword — the brand has penetrated pop culture

**But brand recognition is not the same as brand loyalty.** In the prescription drug market, patient choice depends more on physician recommendation, insurance coverage, and efficacy than on brand preference. If Eli Lilly's product is more effective, cheaper, or more convenient to use, patients will switch.

### Layer two: manufacturing-capacity barrier (strength: ★★★★☆)

Manufacturing biologics is entirely different from manufacturing small-molecule chemical drugs. Semaglutide is a peptide molecule that requires complex bioengineering and strict quality control.

Novo Nordisk has continuously invested tens of billions of kroner over many years to expand capacity — **FY2024's negative free cash flow was precisely because of this capacity expansion**, and it only recovered to a positive DKK 28.3 billion in FY2025.

This means that even if someone secures generic-drug approval, it would be very difficult to build sufficient capacity in the short term to meet market demand. Capacity is a "hardware moat" — money can solve it, but it takes time.

**Counterargument:** The capacity barrier is real, but it's not permanent. Eli Lilly is also expanding production at scale, investing more than **$20 billion** in 2025-2026 for global capacity construction. Indian generic-drug makers have already launched generic semaglutide in 2026, priced **80%** below the branded original (source: CNBC, March 2026).

### Layer three: patent thicket (strength: ★★★☆☆)

Novo Nordisk has built a vast patent network around semaglutide:

- **320** U.S. patent applications
- **154** already granted patents
- Core compound patent protection through **December 2031** (U.S.)
- Generic entry in the U.S. is expected no earlier than **2032**
- But patents in emerging markets like China and India **expire in 2026**

**At least 13** companies have already expressed to the FDA their intent to manufacture generic semaglutide (source: DrugPatentWatch, 2026).

**Key judgment:** Patent protection in the U.S. runs through 2031-2032, buying Novo Nordisk a window of roughly **5-6 years** in its largest market. But in other global markets, generic competition has already begun. Patent expirations in India, China, Brazil, and Canada will have a material impact on Novo Nordisk's international business.

### Layer four: oral-formulation first-mover advantage (strength: ★★★☆☆, decaying rapidly)

Oral Wegovy is the world's first approved oral GLP-1 weight-loss drug (January 2026). Q1 2026 revenue was **DKK 2.26 billion**, with about **1.3 million** prescriptions — far exceeding analyst expectations.

But this first-mover advantage has a very short half-life — Eli Lilly's competing oral product Foundayo (orforglipron) was **approved by the FDA on April 1, 2026**, and garnered **20,000** prescriptions within 20 days of launch (source: GoodRx, April 2026).

Moreover, Foundayo has a key convenience advantage: **it can be taken at any time of day, without fasting**. Oral Wegovy, by contrast, must be taken on an empty stomach in the morning, with no food, water, or other medication for 30 minutes — a real challenge for day-to-day adherence.

## III. A core question: is this a "category business" or a "brand business"?

This distinction matters enormously, because it determines how deep the moat truly is.

**Characteristics of a brand business:** consumers pay a premium for the brand itself — Kweichow Moutai, Hermès, Coca-Cola. Even with cheaper alternatives available, consumers are unwilling to switch.

**Characteristics of a category business:** consumers are buying the category (weight-loss drugs), not a specific brand. Whoever has better efficacy, a lower price, or more convenience wins the sale.

**GLP-1 currently looks more like a category business.** The evidence:

1. When Eli Lilly's tirzepatide proved "superior" to semaglutide in head-to-head trials, large numbers of patients switched from Ozempic to Mounjaro — indicating that efficacy drives choice, and brand loyalty is limited
2. Cheap compounded versions of semaglutide from compounding pharmacies once diverted significant demand — indicating high price sensitivity
3. Insurers tend to favor cheaper options for coverage — indicating that payers don't recognize brand either

**What does this mean for Novo Nordisk?** If GLP-1 is a category business, then the moat comes mainly from efficacy, price, and convenience — not from the brand itself. On these three dimensions, Novo Nordisk currently leads only on "brand recognition"; on efficacy and convenience, Eli Lilly has matched or even surpassed it.

**Counterargument:** Even in a category business, a "Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi"-style duopoly can let both players make substantial money. The GLP-1 market is large enough that Novo Nordisk doesn't need to maintain a monopoly to keep growing — it just needs to hold onto a 35-40% share.

## IV. The $149/month price point — smart strategy or self-inflicted margin damage?

The decision to price oral Wegovy's starting dose at $149/month is one of the most debated pricing calls of 2026.

**The bull case:**
- A low price can rapidly acquire customers, establish the habit of taking the medication, and then lift per-customer revenue through dose escalation (higher doses cost more)
- It opens up the payer side (insurance coverage plus affordable self-pay), substantially expanding the patient pool
- It establishes mindshare for oral GLP-1 ahead of Eli Lilly's Foundayo

**The bear case:**
- Injectable Wegovy lists at $1,349/month, versus $149/month for the oral version — if oral cannibalizes injectable, that's a substantial revenue contraction
- Eli Lilly's Foundayo has also launched at a similarly low price (as low as $25/month with commercial insurance copay), so low price is no longer a point of differentiation
- Proactively cutting price is an irreversible signal — once pricing power is given up, it's hard to take back

Novo Nordisk has also announced: **starting in January 2027, the U.S. list prices for injectable Wegovy and Ozempic will be cut to $675/month — a reduction of as much as 50%** (source: Fox Business, February 2026).

This means Novo Nordisk is actively making a choice: **trading price for volume**. Whether this strategy succeeds or fails will become clear in the 2027-2028 earnings reports.

## V. Overall assessment of the moat

| Moat | Current strength | Trend | Notes |
|--------|---------|------|------|
| Brand recognition | ★★★★☆ | → | Wegovy's brand is strong, but that isn't the same as loyalty |
| Capacity barrier | ★★★★☆ | ↓ | Eli Lilly is catching up, Indian generics are flooding in |
| Patent thicket | ★★★☆☆ | ↓ | Protected in the U.S. through 2031, ground is being lost globally |
| Oral first-mover advantage | ★★★☆☆ | ↓↓ | Foundayo has already been approved, the advantage window is only months |
| **Overall assessment** | **Moderately strong** | **Slowly eroding** | **From "monopolist" to "one of a duopoly"** |

**The most important sentence:** Novo Nordisk's moat hasn't disappeared, but its nature has fundamentally changed — from an exclusive advantage to a combined competition of scale, brand, and pipeline. The 10x P/E precisely reflects this re-rating process from "monopoly premium" to "competitive discount."
