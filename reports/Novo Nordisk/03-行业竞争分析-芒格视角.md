# Novo Nordisk (NVO) — Industry Competition Analysis

> Core question: in the GLP-1 duopoly, is Novo's moat deepening or being eroded?

## I. Panorama of the Competitive Landscape

### 1.1 The Duopoly: Novo Nordisk vs Eli Lilly

The GLP-1 market has formed a clear duopoly, but the balance of power is reversing:

| Dimension | Novo Nordisk | Eli Lilly |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Core molecule | Semaglutide (single-target GLP-1) | Tirzepatide (dual-target GLP-1/GIP) |
| Diabetes brand | Ozempic (injectable), Rybelsus (oral) | Mounjaro |
| Weight-loss brand | Wegovy (injectable + oral) | Zepbound |
| Q1 2026 US GLP-1 market share | ~40% | ~60% |
| Head-to-head clinical results | — | Tirzepatide shown to be "superior" to semaglutide in efficacy and tolerability |
| Oral GLP-1 progress | **Already approved and launched** (oral Wegovy, January 2026) | Orforglipron/Foundayo **just approved**, 20,000 prescriptions within 20 days of launch |
| Q1 2026 weight-loss drug revenue | Wegovy injectable DKK 18.2 billion + oral DKK 2.26 billion | Zepbound $4.16 billion (+80%) |
| Q1 2026 diabetes drug revenue | Ozempic ~DKK 28 billion | Mounjaro $8.66 billion (+125%) |

**Key fact:** Eli Lilly's Q1 2026 Mounjaro revenue soared 125% YoY to $8.66 billion, while Zepbound grew 80% to $4.16 billion. By contrast, Novo's Ozempic revenue declined 8% YoY. The competitive scales have clearly tipped toward Eli Lilly.

### 1.2 Second-Tier Competitors

- **Amgen**: MariTide (dual-target GLP-1/GIPR antagonist), once-monthly injection, in Phase III trials
- **Viking Therapeutics**: VK2735, both oral and injectable formulations in development
- **Pfizer**: danuglipron (oral GLP-1), previously suffered a setback but still advancing
- **Structure Therapeutics** and other small biotech companies are also positioning in the space

## II. Moat Analysis

### 2.1 Moats That Remain Solid

**1. A century of diabetes experience and brand equity**
Novo Nordisk was founded in 1923 and has more than 100 years of history in diabetes treatment. Ozempic's and Wegovy's brand recognition among consumers is unmatched — "Ozempic" has become synonymous with GLP-1 itself.

**2. The world's largest GLP-1 manufacturing capacity**
Barriers to entry in biologics manufacturing are extremely high. Novo has invested tens of billions of kroner over the years to expand capacity, which is precisely why FY2024 free cash flow was negative. This is a hardware moat that latecomers cannot quickly close.

**3. First-mover advantage in the oral formulation**
Oral Wegovy is the world's first approved oral GLP-1 weight-loss drug. Its better-than-expected Q1 2026 performance (DKK 2.26 billion, roughly 1.3 million prescriptions) proves the value of the first-mover advantage. While Eli Lilly's Foundayo has also been approved, Novo holds a lead of several months in the oral category.

**4. Patent thicket**
A patent network of 320 US patent applications and 154 granted patents, with the core compound patent protected through December 2031.

### 2.2 Moats Being Eroded

**1. Clinical efficacy is no longer leading**
This is the biggest concern. Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (a dual-target mechanism) has been proven "superior" to semaglutide in head-to-head trials. In a market where efficacy is king, falling behind on product strength is fatal.

**2. Continued loss of US market share**
Eli Lilly's share of the US GLP-1 market rose from roughly 45% in 2024 to 60% in Q1 2026, and the trend continues. More worrying still, Eli Lilly has also overtaken Novo in markets outside the US.

**3. Impact of compounded drugs**
Although the FDA has tightened oversight and Novo has pursued litigation to defend its rights, cheap compounded semaglutide has already validated demand for "affordable GLP-1." Novo was forced to launch the starting dose of oral Wegovy at $149/month, substantially weakening its pricing power.

**4. Deteriorating pricing environment**
The core reason behind the lowered 2026 guidance is US price cuts. When a drugmaker proactively lowers prices, it signals that competitive pressure has already flowed through to the income statement.

## III. Competition Among Next-Generation Products

| Product | Company | Mechanism | Progress | Expected Weight-Loss Effect |
|------|------|------|------|-------------|
| CagriSema | Novo | GLP-1 + amylin agonist | NDA submitted to FDA, approval expected Q4 2026 | ~15.7% (diabetic patients) |
| Amycretin/Zenagamtide | Novo | Dual GLP-1 + amylin agonism | Phase III initiated (Q1 2026) | Early data shows >20% weight loss |
| Retatrutide | Lilly | Triple-target GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon | Phase III ongoing | Early data shows ~24% weight loss |
| Foundayo (orforglipron) | Lilly | Oral GLP-1 | **Already approved and launched** | ~7-10% (oral) |

**Key judgment:** Whether Novo's next-generation pipeline (CagriSema + Amycretin) can narrow the efficacy gap with Eli Lilly is the core variable determining the long-term competitive landscape. CagriSema's 15.7% weight-loss effect in diabetic patients is not especially impressive, but Amycretin's early data is more promising.

## IV. Munger's Core Judgment

> "The whole reason for investing is to calculate probabilities and odds. You don't need to understand everything — only the key variables within your circle of competence."

**Structural judgment on the GLP-1 market:**

This is a market that is large enough (projected to exceed $150 billion by 2030) and growing fast enough. Even if Novo's share falls from 60% to 35-40%, its absolute revenue base can still grow. **The key question is not who wins the share war, but whether this market is large enough to support two winners.**

**Moat assessment:**

Novo's moat is shifting from being a "first-mover monopolist" to being "one of a duopoly." This does not mean the moat has disappeared — its nature has changed, from an exclusive advantage to a composite competition of scale, brand, and pipeline. In duopoly markets, both companies can earn excellent returns (see Coca-Cola and PepsiCo).

**The biggest competitive risk:** is not that Eli Lilly drives Novo out of the market (that will not happen), but the systematic decline of pricing power. If GLP-1 becomes a "must-cover" insurance category, ultimate pricing may end up being determined by payers rather than drugmakers.

**Industry competition score: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

The track is first-rate, but Novo is losing the share war. Moving from monopoly to duopoly requires a valuation reset. The good news is the market is large enough; the bad news is Eli Lilly genuinely has stronger product power.
