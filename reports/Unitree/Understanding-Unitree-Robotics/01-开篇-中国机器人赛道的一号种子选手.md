## A Post-90s Founder and His Robot Empire

A valuation of 42 billion yuan against revenue of 1.7 billion yuan. A P/S ratio of 25x, revenue up 13x in three years, and a gross margin higher than Apple's — Unitree Robotics is currently the most closely watched, and most misunderstood, company in China's robotics race.

At the 2025 Spring Festival Gala, 16 quadruped robots lined up in formation on stage, did flips, and danced street dance, and the whole country learned the name "Unitree Robotics" for the first time. But in capital markets, this company was already far from a new face — it has charted a growth curve that makes every peer in robotics envious:

| Year | Revenue | Growth | Net profit (excl. non-recurring items) | Gross margin |
|------|------|------|-----------|--------|
| 2023 | **159 million yuan** | — | Loss | — |
| 2024 | **392 million yuan** | +147% | Marginally profitable | — |
| 2025 | **1.708 billion yuan** | **+335%** | **600 million yuan (+674%)** | **60.27%** |

(Source: Unitree Robotics IPO prospectus)

Revenue rose **13x** over three years, and gross margin reached **60%** — a figure that surpasses Apple's and is almost unheard of among hardware companies.

On March 20, 2026, Unitree Robotics formally filed for an IPO on the STAR Market, planning to raise **4.2 billion yuan** at an implied valuation of roughly **42 billion yuan**. If the listing succeeds, it will become **the first humanoid-robot stock on the A-share market**.

Founder Wang Xingxing was born in 1990 in Ningbo, Zhejiang. He holds a bachelor's degree from Zhejiang Sci-Tech University and a master's in mechanical engineering from Shanghai University. After graduating, he worked at DJI for less than six months before quitting to start his own company. He was 26 when he founded Unitree Robotics in 2016, and is 36 this year — he holds **26.97%** of the equity and controls nearly **70% of voting rights**.

Unitree Robotics' current status in one sentence: **the world's No. 1 humanoid robot shipper by volume, roughly 70% global market share in quadruped robots, a 60% gross margin, and a freshly filed IPO — but a 42 billion yuan valuation against 1.7 billion yuan in revenue, a P/S of about 25x.**

This is an exciting company. But after the excitement, a few questions need to be asked with a clear head.

## Three Common Misconceptions About Unitree Robotics

### Misconception 1: Unitree Robotics "Makes Robot Dogs"

This is the most widespread perception, and it is already outdated.

In 2025, Unitree Robotics' revenue structure went through a key turning point:

| Business | Share of revenue | Change |
|------|---------|------|
| Quadruped robots (robot dogs) | ~48% | Still the base business |
| **Humanoid robots** | **~52%** | **Surpassed quadrupeds for the first time** |

(Source: Unitree Robotics IPO prospectus)

In 2025, humanoid-robot revenue surpassed quadrupeds for the first time, becoming the largest business line. Unitree is no longer a "robot dog company" — it is transitioning from quadrupeds to humanoids, and this is the real core support behind the 42 billion yuan valuation.

**But the counter-argument is**: **73.6%** of humanoid-robot revenue comes from research and education customers (data for the first three quarters of 2025, source: prospectus), and genuine industrial and consumer use cases have not yet opened up. Quadruped robots are underrated precisely because they have already completed the path from research to commercial use — humanoid robots are still stuck at the "selling to labs" stage.

### Misconception 2: Unitree = "China's Boston Dynamics"

This analogy looks reasonable but is actually quite off the mark.

| Dimension | Boston Dynamics | Unitree Robotics |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Founded | 1992, incubated out of MIT | 2016, Hangzhou |
| Technology path | Hydraulic → switched to electric drive in 2024 | **Chose electric drive from day one** |
| Product positioning | High-end, roughly $1 million per unit | Low-to-mid range, humanoid units starting at **29,900 yuan** |
| Cumulative sales | Spot robot dog: ~2,000 units | Quadruped: **30,000+ units**, humanoid: **5,500 units** |
| Business model | R&D-driven, long-term losses | Cost-driven, **already profitable in 2025** |
| Parent company | Hyundai Motor Group | Independent, about to IPO |

(Source: prospectus, TrendForce, public reports)

Boston Dynamics' approach is "tackle the hardest technology first, worry about cost later" — a single Atlas unit sells for over a million dollars. Unitree does the opposite: "push cost to the extreme first, win on volume" — the R1 humanoid robot starts at **29,900 yuan**, less than **1/200** of Atlas's price.

Boston Dynamics founder Marc Raibert has publicly said he purchased a Unitree robot for testing, and called it "very well done" (source: Guancha.cn, Feb. 2025).

A more accurate analogy is: **Unitree is to Boston Dynamics what Xiaomi is to Apple** — entering the market with extreme value for money and building a moat through scale.

**But the counter-argument is**: Xiaomi succeeded because smartphones already had a mature application ecosystem. Humanoid robots don't yet have a "killer app" — fighting a price war in a market without clear demand may just mean "cheap, but nobody's buying."

### Misconception 3: No. 1 in Shipments = Winning

In 2025, Unitree shipped **5,500** humanoid robots, ranking first in the world. But this "No. 1" needs a few caveats:

| Company | 2025 shipments | Core strength |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Unitree Robotics** | **5,500 units (No. 1)** | Value for money + full in-house hardware stack |
| AgiBot | 5,168 units (No. 2) | Stronger AI capability + supply chain integration |
| Tesla Optimus | <5,000 units | **FSD end-to-end large model**, mass production planned for 2027 |
| Figure AI | Small volume | OpenAI partnership, BMW factory pilot |
| UBTECH | ~600 units | Already listed, 3,000+ patents |

(Source: TrendForce, public reports)

Unitree and AgiBot together account for roughly **80%** of global humanoid robot shipments — but the total market is still tiny. Global humanoid robot shipments in 2025 came to fewer than **15,000 units**, less than a single day's sales for a mid-sized smartphone brand.

**The more critical question is**: being No. 1 in shipments rests on "hardware value for money" — but the ultimate competitiveness of humanoid robots likely won't lie in hardware, but in the **AI brain**. Tesla can directly transfer its FSD end-to-end large model to Optimus, and Figure AI has OpenAI's backing — Unitree's R&D spending is only **7.73%** of revenue (about **90.2 million yuan**), an order-of-magnitude gap versus Tesla's ten-thousand-person AI team.

**Being No. 1 in shipments is today's fact, not tomorrow's moat.**

## A Set of Numbers Worth Remembering

Keep the orders of magnitude in mind first — the following parts will refer back to them repeatedly:

| Metric | Figure |
|------|------|
| 2025 revenue | **1.708 billion yuan** (+335%) |
| 2025 net profit (excl. non-recurring items) | **600 million yuan** (+674%) |
| Gross margin | **60.27%** |
| R&D spending as % of revenue | **7.73%** (90.2 million yuan) |
| Humanoid robot shipments | **5,500 units** (world No. 1) |
| Cumulative quadruped robot sales | **30,000+ units** (~70% global share) |
| Share of humanoid revenue from research/education | **73.6%** |
| IPO valuation | **42 billion yuan** (P/S ~25x) |
| Series C valuation (June 2025) | **12 billion yuan** |
| Founder's equity stake | **26.97%**, controlling ~70% of voting rights |
| Team size | ~**480 people** |

Put these together, and here's what you see:

- A hardware company with astonishing growth and an extremely high gross margin
- Humanoid robots have just overtaken quadrupeds as the largest business line
- But 73% of humanoid-robot revenue still comes from research and education
- R&D spending is under 100 million yuan, not remotely in the same league as major AI companies
- A 42 billion yuan valuation against 1.7 billion yuan in revenue — the market is betting on the future
