## A Set of Facts That Should Make You Reconsider Sigenergy

A 1,102x oversubscription, the share price doubling on day one, market cap breaking through HK$160 billion — and then, within a month, a 24% drop.

The same company made IPO subscribers a fortune and left latecomers chasing the rally with losses. It has bulls calling it "the next CATL" and bears calling it an "IPO bubble." Both camps cite real facts, yet arrive at opposite conclusions. That's not normal — it usually means the market hasn't yet figured out what this company actually is.

The specifics: on April 16, 2026, Sigenergy (06656.HK) listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. It opened at HK$580 (79% above the HK$324.20 IPO price) and closed at HK$659.50, up 103% on its first day. It then pulled back from a high of HK$669 to around HK$519 by mid-May 2026 — a 22% drawdown from the peak, though still 60% above the offer price. The company was founded in May 2022; in under four years it went from zero to a market cap above HK$100 billion.

Before slapping a label on it, let's first understand what it actually is.

## What Sigenergy Actually Is

In one sentence: **Sigenergy is a company that builds integrated distributed solar-plus-storage solutions — combining solar power generation, energy storage, charging, and energy management into a single "plug-and-play" product, sold to overseas residential and commercial/industrial customers.**

Its core product is called SigenStor, a "five-in-one" machine that turns what used to require 5 devices, 5 brands, and 5 installations into **1 device, 1 installation, handled through 1 app**:

| Function Module | Traditional Approach | SigenStor |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Solar inverter | Purchased and installed separately | Built-in |
| Energy storage converter (PCS) | Purchased and installed separately | Built-in |
| Storage battery | Purchased and installed separately (5-100kWh stackable expansion) | Built-in |
| DC fast-charging module (EV charging) | Purchased and installed separately | Built-in |
| AI energy management system | Purchased separately or not available | Built-in (SigenOS) |

The value of this design: it's **20-30%** cheaper than buying components separately, and cuts installation time by more than **50%**. For installers, putting in one SigenStor unit is far simpler than installing five separate devices; for users, a single app manages the whole home's energy.

**Analogy:** if Apple's integration of the MP3 player, phone, and camera into the iPhone counts as an integration-driven innovation, then Sigenergy's integration of the inverter, storage, charging, battery, and energy management into SigenStor is a comparable attempt in the solar-storage space.

## Three Questions That Need Clarifying

Before diving into detailed analysis, let's clear up three commonly confused points.

### Question One: What Does 155x Growth Actually Mean?

155x sounds staggering, but broken down:

- 2023 revenue: RMB 58.3 million — the scale of a small installer
- 2024 revenue: RMB 1.33 billion — genuine scale shipments began
- 2025 revenue: RMB 9.001 billion — explosive growth

**The growth figure that actually matters is the 5.8x increase from 2024 to 2025**, not "155x." The 155x figure is a base-effect artifact — starting from near zero, any growth produces a staggering multiple.

**Counter-argument:** even stripping out the base effect, 5.8x annual growth is still extremely strong, showing the product genuinely hit a real market need. The question isn't whether past growth was fast, but whether it can continue.

### Question Two: Is a 50% Gross Margin Normal?

Peer gross margins: GoodWe ~30%, Ginlong (Solis) ~30%, Enphase ~45%, SolarEdge ~23%, Huawei FusionSolar ~25% (estimate). Sigenergy's **50.1%** is the highest in the industry.

**A plausible explanation:** the five-in-one integration cuts intermediate costs while pricing stays near the combined price of separate components; its premium market positioning (Europe, the US, Australia) targets price-insensitive customers; and there were few competitors in this early-stage market.

**But be wary:** a 50% gross margin is extremely rare among Chinese manufacturers. High margins inevitably attract competitors. In fact, SigenStor's average selling price has already been falling — from **RMB 3.17 per Wh** in 2023 to **RMB 2.69 per Wh** in 2024, and further to **RMB 2.22 per Wh** in the first three quarters of 2025 (source: Yema Finance). Prices are falling while margins are rising — a sign that cost control and scale effects are working, but the declining-price trend itself deserves attention.

### Question Three: How Much Is "No. 1 in the Niche" Actually Worth?

Sigenergy holds a **28.6%** share of the "stackable distributed solar-plus-storage all-in-one system" niche — No. 1 globally. That sounds impressive.

But zoom out one level:

| Scope | Sigenergy's Share | Main Competitors |
|------|:-------:|-----------|
| Stackable solar-storage all-in-one systems (a niche within a niche) | **28.6%** | Competitors fragmented |
| Distributed solar-storage market (broad definition) | **1.3%** | Huawei 18%, Tesla 6.4% |
| Global energy storage systems market | **0.2%** | Dominated by CATL, BYD |

**That 28.6% is a share of a roughly $3 billion niche. Huawei, by contrast, holds 18% of the $30 billion broad market — a scale 14 times that of Sigenergy.**

This isn't to say Sigenergy is bad. Being No. 1 in a niche growing at 60% a year genuinely reflects strong product capability and execution. But there's an order of magnitude between "No. 1 in a niche" and "No. 1 in the industry."

## An Unavoidable New Variable: Huawei's Patent Lawsuit

The research draft underlying this series was written on April 9, 2026. Since then, one major event has occurred:

**On April 3, 2026, Huawei filed a patent infringement dispute request with a local intellectual property office**, alleging that Sigenergy infringed on two patents — "Method and Device for Adjusting Inverter Bus Voltage" and "Method and Device for Determining Global Maximum Output Power." According to reports, the patents Huawei claims may be infringed could number **more than 10**, mainly involving Sigenergy's core products — its solar-storage all-in-one systems, solar-storage inverters, and commercial/industrial solar inverters (source: Jiemian News, 36Kr).

The timing — right on the eve of Sigenergy's IPO — combined with Xu Yingtong's 23-year history at Huawei, means this dispute carries implications well beyond the legal dimension. Parts 03 and 04 will explore this in detail.
