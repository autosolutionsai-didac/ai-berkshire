## I. Six Major Risks

The first three installments took apart Sigenergy's product, business model, and competitive landscape. The conclusion: this is a company with genuinely strong capabilities, standing on a track with genuinely fast real-world growth. But being capable and being worth buying are two different things.

This installment goes straight into risk and valuation. We lay every risk on the table, quantify each one, and then use a three-scenario model to check whether the current HK$519 share price is actually expensive. Ranked by severity:

### Risk 1: Single-Product Dependence Above 93% — Red Alert

**93% of revenue comes from a single product, the SigenStor.** This is Sigenergy's single biggest structural risk, bar none.

| If this happens... | Impact |
|-----------|------|
| SigenStor is displaced by a new technology route | Company revenue goes to zero |
| SigenStor suffers a large-scale quality incident | Brand collapse + recall costs |
| Supply of key components (battery cells, IGBTs) is disrupted | Production halt |
| Competitors cut prices sharply to grab share | Both gross margin and market share get hit |

**Counter-argument:** The company has already launched SigenStack, a commercial and industrial (C&I) energy storage product, in an attempt to break its single-product dependence. If SigenStack's revenue share rises to 15%+ in 2026, this risk will ease meaningfully.

**But the current fact is:** SigenStack's revenue contribution is negligible so far — the "second curve" is still a story, not a reality.

---

### Risk 2: Huawei's Counterattack — A Sword of Damocles

The previous installment analyzed in detail what Huawei's patent lawsuit means. Here are a few more key facts:

- Huawei holds an **18%** share of the distributed solar-plus-storage market — **14 times** Sigenergy's share
- Huawei's annual R&D spending is on the order of **RMB 100+ billion**; Sigenergy's is about **RMB 600 million**
- In April 2026, Huawei filed a patent-infringement complaint covering **more than 10** allegedly infringed patents
- Most of Xu Yingtong's core team are Huawei veterans — meaning Huawei is intimately familiar with their technical approach and methodology

**Worst-case scenario:** Huawei simultaneously launches a stackable five-in-one competing product and escalates the patent lawsuit to the courts. With brand power, channel power, and legal pressure hitting all at once, Sigenergy's first-mover advantage could be substantially eroded within 1–2 years.

**Best-case scenario:** Huawei's patent lawsuit ends in settlement or cross-licensing, and Huawei continues to focus on the traditional inverter market, giving Sigenergy a wider window to develop.

**Counter-argument:** Huawei itself faces U.S. sanctions and is restricted in Europe and the U.S. as well. If Huawei's main energy goes toward handling sanctions and consolidating its domestic market, its actual threat to Sigenergy may be smaller than an "all-out crushing" scenario would suggest in theory.

---

### Risk 3: Gross Margin Decline — The Highest-Certainty Risk

| Year | Gross Margin | SigenStor Average Price (RMB/Wh) |
|------|:------:|:---------------------:|
| 2023 | 31.3% | 3.17 |
| 2024 | 36.2% | 2.69 |
| 2025 | **50.1%** | 2.22 |

*Source: prospectus, Yema Finance*

Gross margin is currently rising (costs are falling faster than selling prices), but the medium-to-long-term trend is nearly certain:

1. Overseas price wars among Chinese solar-plus-storage companies have already begun
2. Once Huawei, GoodWe, and others launch competing products, the premium will narrow
3. SigenStor's average price has already fallen from 3.17 to 2.22 — a clear downward trend

**Forecast: gross margin could retreat from 50% to 35–40% within 2–3 years.** That would still be excellent by industry standards, but the impact on profit is large: at a 50% gross margin, 9 billion yuan of revenue = 4.5 billion yuan of gross profit; at a 35% gross margin, 15 billion yuan of revenue = 5.25 billion yuan of gross profit. Revenue grows 67%, but gross profit grows only 17%.

**Counter-argument:** Economies of scale and supply-chain optimization may keep pushing costs down. If Sigenergy can offset hardware gross-margin decline through software monetization (paid SigenOS features) and services revenue (operations & maintenance, cloud platform), blended gross margin could stay relatively high.

### Risk 4: Geopolitics and Tariff Barriers

| Market | Current Status | Risk Trajectory |
|------|---------|---------|
| **North America** | Chinese solar-plus-storage products face tariffs >50%, effectively locked out | Trump administration's tariff-raising trend unchanged |
| **Europe** | No targeted tariffs currently | EU is evaluating anti-subsidy measures against Chinese batteries/storage products |
| **Australia** | Currently friendly | Policy could shift with China-Australia relations |
| **Southeast Asia / Middle East** | Currently open | Growth opportunity |

**The biggest risk isn't existing markets being sealed off — it's that Europe, which accounts for 43.6% of revenue, could impose tariffs.** If the EU launches an anti-subsidy investigation into Chinese energy storage products and imposes tariffs, Sigenergy's gross margin would take a direct hit.

**Countermeasures:** Sigenergy currently manufactures mainly in China (its Nantong plant) and **has no overseas production base**. One use of the IPO proceeds is building overseas capacity — but that takes time and heavy capital, and won't solve the near-term problem.

---

### Risk 5: The Australia Product Recall Incident

In late 2025, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) issued a safety recall notice for the single-phase 8/10/12kW SigenStor energy control units (sources: ACCC Product Safety, SolarQuotes).

**The problem:** the AC plug could overheat and melt, posing a fire risk. Roughly **100 units** were affected.

**Company response:** Sigenergy issued a temporary fix via remote firmware update (reducing full-load output power) and offered free AC plug replacements for all affected units. The company attributed the problem to **improper installation** rather than a product design defect.

**Objective assessment:**
- The scope was limited (around 100 units), with no injuries or property damage
- The company responded quickly, with remote firmware updates plus free replacements
- But it exposed a quality-control gap during rapid expansion — only 2 years passed from R&D to mass shipment, raising the question of whether the quality-management system is mature
- The long-term brand impact depends on whether similar incidents recur

**Counter-argument:** Nearly every new brand runs into quality problems during rapid early-stage expansion — Tesla went through numerous recalls in its early years too. The key question is whether the company learns from this and improves its processes. An affected population of around 100 units is relatively containable.

### Risk 6: The Company Is Too Young — Operating Resilience Is Entirely Unknown

| Dimension | Fact |
|------|------|
| Founded | May 2022 (under 4 years) |
| Profitability track record | Only 1 full year of profitability, 2025 |
| Has it been through an industry downturn? | No |
| Customer stickiness (repeat-purchase rate, replacement rate) | No data |
| Organizational management capability | Never stress-tested |

**When a company has only performed well during a tailwind, you cannot tell "the company is excellent" apart from "it simply caught a good moment."** Only after living through a full industry downturn can the true depth of its moat and management's ability to handle adversity be verified.

## III. Valuation Check

### Current Valuation

| Item | At IPO (2026.4.16) | Current (mid-May 2026) |
|------|:------------------:|:-------------------:|
| Share price (HKD) | 324.20 (offer price) | **~519** |
| Market cap (HKD 100M) | ~800 | **~1,280** |
| P/E (2025 net profit RMB 2.919 billion) | ~24x | **~39x** |
| P/S (2025 revenue RMB 9.001 billion) | ~7.8x | **~12.5x** |

*Note: market cap estimated using total shares outstanding of ~247 million × current share price; P/E and P/S are converted to RMB terms.*

**Note:** From IPO to now, the company's fundamentals haven't changed (no new financial report has been released), yet market cap has risen from HK$80 billion to HK$128 billion — **valuation has expanded 60%, entirely on market sentiment.**

### Three-Scenario Valuation Model

| Scenario | 2026E Revenue | Net Margin | 2026E Net Profit | Fair P/E | Target Market Cap (HKD 100M) | Implied Share Price (HKD) | Change vs. Current Price |
|------|:---------:|:-----:|:-----------:|:------:|:---------------:|:-------------:|:------------:|
| **Bull** | RMB 18 billion | 35% | RMB 6.3 billion | 30x | 2,149 | ~870 | **+68%** |
| **Base** | RMB 15 billion | 30% | RMB 4.5 billion | 22x | 1,125 | ~455 | **-12%** |
| **Bear** | RMB 12 billion | 20% | RMB 2.4 billion | 15x | 409 | ~166 | **-68%** |

*Note: target market cap = net profit (RMB) / 0.8795 × P/E; implied share price = target market cap / 247 million shares*

**Key findings:**

1. **Under the base scenario, the current HK$519 share price already looks expensive.** If 2026 revenue is RMB 15 billion with a 30% net margin at 22x P/E, the implied share price is only HK$455 — 12% below the current price.

2. **The bull scenario requires many assumptions to hold:** revenue doubling to RMB 18 billion + net margin holding at 35% + the market awarding 30x P/E. That means no gross-margin decline, no intensified competition, no tariff shock — if any one assumption breaks down, the bull case falls apart.

3. **The bear scenario has enormous downside (-68%).** If Huawei counterattacks at full strength while gross margin drops sharply and growth slows, the current HK$128 billion market cap could get cut in half, and then in half again.

## V. Summary — Closing with a Q&A

**Q: Is Sigenergy a good company?**

Judging by product strength, execution, and founder capability, Sigenergy is genuinely outstanding. The SigenStor's five-in-one design solves a real pain point, covering 172 distributors across 85 countries in 3 years is an impressive pace, and its 50% gross margin stands head and shoulders above the industry.

**Q: Is Sigenergy a good investment?**

Currently uncertain. A good company and a good investment are two different things — a good company at the wrong price becomes a bad investment. The current **39x P/E**, for a company with only 1 year of profitability history, 93% single-product dependence, and facing a Huawei lawsuit plus intensifying competition, means the market has already priced in a great deal of good news in advance.

**Q: When might it become a good investment?**

When at least 3 of the following conditions are met:
- Valuation returns to a reasonable range (below 25x P/E, corresponding to a share price under ~HK$400)
- At least 2 quarters of post-listing results confirm growth sustainability
- Evidence that gross margin has stabilized at 40%+
- The second curve (SigenStack) reaches 15%+ of revenue
- The Huawei patent dispute is settled

**Q: If you remember only one thing?**

**Sigenergy's biggest uncertainty is not "can it grow" — the track's growth rate is 60%, and near-term growth certainty is high. The biggest uncertainty is "can it hold onto its gains after growing" — how long can a 50% gross margin last? How much share will it lose once Huawei enters in earnest? When will the fragility of single-product dependence be exposed?**

The answers to these questions will take time. And time is precisely what a company with only 4 years of history has the least of.
