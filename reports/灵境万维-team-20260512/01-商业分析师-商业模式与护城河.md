## II. The Essence of the Business Model

### Core Business Definition

The essence of Lingjing AI's business is: **using AI technology to convert text-based IP (web novels/fiction) into video content (comic-dramas) at scale, and monetizing it through content distribution**.

In one sentence: this is an "AI-driven content factory," not a technology company or platform company in the traditional sense.

### Four Business Segments

1. **Web-novel big-data platform**: Real-time monitoring of more than 10 million works across the entire web, using NLP and trend-prediction models to capture creative trends and user preferences -- essentially a "topic-selection system"
2. **AI short-fiction factory**: Co-developed an AI novel-writing model with Zhejiang University, producing an average of over 1,000 short and medium-length stories per month, 20 times the traditional pace -- essentially "raw-material production"
3. **AI animation creation engine**: Adapts text content into comic-dramas, boosting production efficiency 12x and cutting per-episode costs by 70% -- essentially the "core processing stage"
4. **Content going overseas**: Expanding into Southeast Asia, Europe, and the US based on localized AI translation and cultural adaptation -- essentially "market expansion"

### Revenue Sources

Inferred from available information:

- **To C (primary)**: Distributing comic-dramas through short-video platforms (Douyin, Hongguo, etc.), generating revenue via IAP (in-app purchases to unlock content) and IAA (advertising monetization) models. In the hit case "I Opened a Supermarket in the Apocalypse," production cost was 150,000 yuan and revenue was 12 million yuan
- **To B (secondary/planned)**: May in the future open AI tools and the platform to external creators, shifting from "self-production" to a "platform model"
- **Monthly revenue**: Reportedly has reached 20 million yuan, with orders booked out to 2027 and total order value exceeding 100 million yuan

**Confidence: Medium** -- the data point on monthly revenue of 20 million yuan comes from a Wuhan municipal government investment-promotion news release, which may contain some inflation. However, the figures for the hit case can be cross-validated across multiple channels.

### The Key Equation of the Business Model

```
Profit = Content revenue - (AI compute cost + IP copyright cost + traffic-buying cost + labor cost)
```

**Core question**: Across the industry, more than 80% of revenue typically goes to traffic buying. Even if production cost drops to 150,000 yuan, if several million yuan in ad spend is required to generate 12 million yuan in revenue, the actual profit margin may be far less attractive than the headline numbers suggest.

## III. The Product Form of AI Animation

### What Exactly Is It?

What Lingjing AI makes is **AI comic-drama** -- an emerging content form between traditional animation and live-action short drama:

- **Not** traditional animation production (does not require original-drawing artists to draw frame by frame)
- **Not** a simple AI-generated-image slideshow (it has dynamic rendering and character movement)
- **Is** the rapid conversion of web novels/fiction into vertical-screen animated short dramas with visuals, voice acting, and plot, using AI technology
- **Length**: Each episode is roughly 3-5 minutes, tailored to short-video platform consumption habits
- **Quality positioning**: Between "motion comics" and "Japanese anime," claimed to be close to the standard of mainstream Japanese anime

### Technical Approach

- A proprietary AIGC production tool based on a DIT architecture
- Dual-engine drive of "neural rendering + cognitive computing"
- Connects the full chain of "novel adaptation -- script storyboarding -- static image generation -- dynamic rendering"
- Accumulates animation production data on its proprietary pipeline, forming a data flywheel

### Production Efficiency

- Cost per minute of production: a few hundred yuan (1/20 of traditional animation)
- Production efficiency: 10-20x that of traditional methods
- Team configuration: 1 director + 5 production staff = completes one comic-drama in one month

**Confidence: Medium** -- the technical descriptions come mainly from company PR and fundraising press releases, and lack independent third-party verification. The claim of being "close to the standard of mainstream Japanese anime" should be discounted.

## IV. Moat Analysis (Verified One by One)

### 1. Brand Moat

**Conclusion: Essentially nonexistent**

- AI comic-drama is a new category; users do not pay because it is "produced by Lingjing AI" -- users follow the content, not the brand
- Comic-drama distribution depends on third-party platforms like Douyin and Hongguo; brand recognition has almost no value in the face of platform algorithmic recommendation
- The hit "I Opened a Supermarket in the Apocalypse" has view counts, but audiences neither know nor care who the producer is

**Confidence: High**

### 2. Switching Costs

**Conclusion: Extremely low**

- C-end users: zero switching cost -- users can watch comic-dramas from other producers at any time
- B-end (if the platform opens up): creators can use any AI tool, and switching costs are low
- The only possible source of stickiness may be "accumulated character assets and IP sequels," but this is weak

**Confidence: High**

### 3. Network Effects

**Conclusion: Currently nonexistent; may exist under a future platform model**

- The current "self-production dream-factory" model has no network effects
- If it successfully transitions into an "open creator platform" (similar to YouTube/Roblox), content network effects could emerge
- But the leap from self-production to a platform is enormous, and the probability of success is questionable

**Confidence: Medium**

### 4. Economies of Scale

**Conclusion: Limited but present**

- There are economies of scale in AI model training and pipeline optimization -- the more output produced, the faster the data flywheel spins and the more precise the model becomes
- But this is not "economies of scale" in the traditional sense -- increasing output volume does not significantly lower marginal cost, since every comic-drama still requires human direction, review, and traffic buying
- The real scale advantage may lie in: mass production -> a higher probability of producing a hit -> using hit revenue to cover losses on other works (similar to a film studio's "portfolio approach")

**Confidence: Medium**

### 5. Technical Barriers

**Conclusion: Effective in the short term, questionable in the medium-to-long term**

- **Short-term advantage**: The proprietary DIT architecture and pipeline system are indeed ahead of peers, and production efficiency and quality are near the top of the industry
- **Medium-term risk**: The underlying AI model (i.e., the video-generation large model) is provided by open-source or third parties; what Lingjing AI has built is an application-layer "workflow," not the underlying model itself. As the underlying model iterates rapidly, the application layer's lead could quickly be closed
- **Long-term concern**: Major platforms such as ByteDance have already entered the space (Xiaoyunque AI), with stronger technical teams, more compute, and larger distribution platforms. The likelihood of major platforms building in-house or acquiring is high

**Confidence: Medium** -- the durability of the technical barrier is the biggest uncertainty.

### Overall Moat Assessment

**Moat assessment under Duan Yongping's standard: Weak**

By Duan Yongping's standard that "differentiation comes from a position in users' minds," Lingjing AI has not yet built a genuine moat. Its advantage is more "first-mover advantage plus execution advantage" than a structural competitive barrier.

## V. User Value Analysis

### Who Does It Create Value For?

| User/customer | Value created | Traditional alternative replaced |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Short-video users (C-end) | Low-cost/free animated entertainment content | Traditional animation (long production cycle, low output), live-action short drama |
| Web-novel IP holders | Low-cost adaptation of text IP into video | Traditional animation adaptation (costing up to several million yuan per episode) |
| Short-video platforms | Enriched content supply for the platform | Reliance on traditional content suppliers |
| Creators (future platform model) | Lower barrier to animation creation | Requires a professional team and substantial capital |

### Core User Value

The core value Lingjing AI creates is **"turning animation content from a luxury good into a fast-moving consumer good"**:

- Traditional animation: high investment, long cycle, premium route -- a single anime series can require an investment of tens of millions to over 100 million yuan
- AI comic-drama: low investment, fast turnaround, mass-production route -- a single comic-drama costs 150,000 yuan

**In essence, this uses AI technology to upgrade animation creation from a "cottage-industry model" to an "industrialized model."**

### Concerns About User Value

- The fast-moving-consumer-goods logic means severe content homogenization -- apocalypse, fantasy, and sweet-romance themes are clustered together
- Users don't pay for the "producer," they pay for "good content" -- but AI mass production makes it very hard to consistently produce good content
- With a hit rate of only 0.12%-0.16%, the vast majority of works never reach users

## VI. Assessment Against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

Duan Yongping's three core criteria for judging a good business: differentiation, pricing power, and sustainable competitive advantage.

### 1. Differentiation

**Rating: Weak**

- Product level: AI comic-dramas are highly homogeneous with one another, and users can barely distinguish Lingjing AI's work from other producers'
- Technology level: there is short-term differentiation (efficiency and quality lead), but since the underlying model is not proprietary, the differentiation is not durable
- The hit "I Opened a Supermarket in the Apocalypse" has been accused of plagiarism, showing that even the differentiation of the content itself is hard to guarantee

### 2. Pricing Power

**Rating: None**

- C-end: comic-drama pricing is set by the distribution platforms (Hongguo, Douyin), and Lingjing AI has no pricing power
- B-end: the AI comic-drama production market is intensely competitive, and reports show a "price war" has already broken out in the industry, with per-minute cost falling from thousands of yuan to a few hundred yuan
- Traffic-buying costs are set by platform algorithms, and Lingjing AI is even more passively on the receiving end
- **This is the metric Duan Yongping values most, and Lingjing AI completely fails it here**

### 3. Sustainable Competitive Advantage

**Rating: Uncertain, leaning weak**

- Positive factors: first-mover advantage, team execution, and three funding rounds in one year indicate capital-market recognition
- Negative factors:
  - The industry-wide hit rate is only 0.16%, and 90% of companies are losing money
  - Major platforms (ByteDance, Tencent) have already entered across the board
  - The technical barrier dissolves as the underlying models become open-sourced
  - The content-factory model is essentially "betting on hits," not continuously creating value

### Duan Yongping's Overall Assessment

**This is not a business Duan Yongping would invest in.** The reasons are clear:

1. **No pricing power** -- both revenue and cost are controlled by platforms
2. **No moat** -- the technical edge is not durable, and content carries no brand loyalty
3. **Essentially an "input-output" probability game** -- similar to the "bet on a hit" model in the film or gaming industries, precisely the type of business Duan Yongping has repeatedly said he avoids
4. **Reliant on platform distribution** -- its fate is not in its own hands

Duan Yongping might say: "This isn't a company 'pursuing profit above all else' -- it's more like chasing a windfall opportunity."

## VII. Business Model Outlook Three Years Out (2029)

### Scenario One: Successful Transition to a Platform (probability 20%)

- Lingjing AI successfully transitions from a "content factory" to an "AI animation creation platform"
- A large number of external creators use Lingjing AI's tools and platform to create comic-dramas
- The revenue model shifts to platform commissions + SaaS subscriptions
- This is the best outcome, but platformization requires a traffic gateway, and traffic is held by ByteDance and Tencent

### Scenario Two: Becomes a Top-Tier Content Supplier (probability 35%)

- Maintains the "AI content factory" model, but establishes a relatively leading position through scaling up and going overseas
- Similar to "Dianzhong Technology" in China's short-drama industry -- not a platform, but a core supplier to platforms
- Produces thousands of AI comic-dramas per year, with a few hits covering costs
- Profitable but with a low ceiling -- possibly a business that is "decent but not great"

### Scenario Three: Acquired by a Major Platform or Marginalized (probability 35%)

- Once ByteDance and Tencent build in-house AI comic-drama production capacity, the space for independent producers is squeezed
- Lingjing AI, as an early player, is acquired by a major platform (similar to an acqui-hire)
- Or, once the technology gap is eliminated, it gradually loses its advantage

### Scenario Four: Industry Bubble Bursts (probability 10%)

- The AI comic-drama industry as a whole fails to find a sustainable profit model
- Traffic-buying costs keep rising while hit rates keep falling
- A large number of companies are shaken out, and Lingjing AI may be among them

**Confidence: Low** -- three-year forecasts are inherently low-confidence; the above is only a framework for thinking.

## VIII. Summary of Core Judgments

### What Has Lingjing AI Done Right?

1. **Timing of the track choice**: Entering AI animation in 2023 was well-timed, right as large-model capability exploded and short-video platforms were giving recommendation-algorithm dividends to the comic-drama category
2. **Full-chain closed loop**: An integrated capability spanning topic selection, writing, production, and distribution created an early efficiency advantage
3. **Fundraising pace**: Three funding rounds in one year secured cash flow, allowing the company to survive the industry's elimination rounds

### Core Concerns from Duan Yongping's Perspective

1. **This is a "good market with no moat"** -- the market is indeed growing fast (30 billion+ yuan in 2026), but the barriers to entry for participants are low, and the competitive landscape will deteriorate rapidly
2. **Pricing power is not in its own hands** -- the revenue side is controlled by platforms, and the cost side is affected by model vendors and compute prices
3. **The essence of "betting on hits"** -- a hit rate of 0.16% means only 1 hit for every 625 titles produced, which is more like gambling than business
4. **Copyright risk** -- the hit "I Opened a Supermarket in the Apocalypse" got caught up in a plagiarism dispute, and AI mass production could exacerbate copyright issues
5. **Threat from major platforms** -- ByteDance simultaneously possesses content-generation capability (Xiaoyunque AI) and distribution channels (Douyin), making it Lingjing AI's greatest existential threat

### Final Assessment

| Dimension | Rating | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Market space | A | AI comic-drama market at 30 billion+ yuan in 2026, with clear growth momentum |
| Business model quality | C | Content factory + betting on hits, no pricing power |
| Moat | D | First-mover advantage and execution do not constitute a structural barrier |
| Team | B- | Alibaba background + rapid execution, but insufficient founder information |
| Duan Yongping "good business" test | Fail | No differentiation, no pricing power, no sustainable competitive advantage |

**One-sentence verdict**: Lingjing AI is a company with decent execution riding a windfall opportunity, but the track it occupies may be one that is "good for AI, but bad for AI companies" -- the AI technology dividend will diffuse rapidly, and the ultimate beneficiaries will be the major platforms holding the platform and the traffic, not the content producers.
