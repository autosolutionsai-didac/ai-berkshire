## Start with a set of numbers

150,000 yuan in cost, 12 million yuan in revenue in five days — that's the scorecard an AI comic-drama called "I Opened a Supermarket in the Apocalypse, and S-Rank Horrors Are Lining Up to Work for Me" posted on Douyin over New Year's 2026. The producer is a startup less than three years old, with a team under a hundred people, headquartered in Hangzhou — **LingJing Wanwei**, brand name "LingJing AI."

**An 80x return on investment** is enough to make an investor in any industry stop and take a second look. But before you get swept up by that number, look at another set of data:

- Two days after launch, views broke **220 million**
- The first episode got **73,000** likes
- It topped Douyin's comic-drama chart for three straight days over New Year's
- Production cost: **150,000 yuan**
- Five-day revenue: **12 million yuan**

(Data cross-validated across multiple outlets including Liuchuang Media, Huxiu, and 36Kr)

| Industry metric | Data | Source |
|---------|------|------|
| AI comic-dramas released in all of 2025 | **60,946** | Huxiu |
| Works exceeding 100 million views | **96** | Huxiu |
| Hit rate | **0.12%-0.16%** | TMTPost, Huxiu |
| Share of industry companies operating at a loss | **90%** | TMTPost |
| Share of revenue spent on paid traffic acquisition | **80%+** | industry reporting |

**A hit rate of 0.12%** means you need to produce roughly **625** works to "expect" one hit. The other 624 will most likely lose money outright.

That's the real face of the industry LingJing Wanwei operates in: **on one side, a rags-to-riches myth of 150,000 yuan turning into 12 million; on the other, the brutal reality that 90% of companies are losing money**.

## What exactly is LingJing Wanwei

### One-sentence definition

**LingJing Wanwei is a content factory that uses AI technology to mass-convert web novels into vertical-screen animated short dramas (comic-dramas).**

It is not a traditional animation studio (it doesn't need animators drawing frame by frame), not a pure tech company (its core revenue comes from content distribution, not tool sales), and not a platform company (it doesn't control a traffic gateway). A more precise label is — **an AI-driven content producer**.

### Four business pieces

| Segment | What it does | Nature |
|------|--------|------|
| Web-novel big-data platform | Monitors over 10 million works across the internet in real time, using algorithms to catch trends | Topic-selection system |
| AI short-fiction factory | Co-develops writing models with Zhejiang University, producing 1,000+ pieces per month | Raw-material production |
| AI animation creation engine | Converts text into comic-dramas, using a proprietary DIT-architecture tool | Core processing stage |
| Content going global | AI translation + cultural adaptation, expanding into Southeast Asia, Europe, and the US | Market expansion |

(Data source: 36Kr funding coverage, Highroad Capital press release)

Putting the four pieces together, LingJing Wanwei's business equation is clear:

> **Select a hit-worthy topic → mass-produce novels with AI → mass-produce comic-dramas with AI → distribute on platforms like Douyin/Hongguo → monetize through user payment or advertising**

This is a complete "industrialized content production line."

### Key figures

Founder **Xu Jincheng** is publicly described as coming from "the Alibaba system." But exactly what he did at Alibaba, at what rank, in which business line — **all of that is missing from public information**.

This is a signal worth noting. For a company whose core narrative is "technology-driven," the founder's technical background cannot be traced. It could be deliberate low profile, or it could be that the background isn't compelling enough. **The available information isn't enough to judge either way, but the absence itself is information.**

## Funding and scale: moving fast, but not carrying much ammunition

LingJing Wanwei completed three funding rounds within 8 months:

| Round | Time | Amount | Investors |
|------|------|--------|--------|
| Seed round | February 2025 | Several million yuan | CSC Venture Capital (Zhongke Zhaoshang) |
| Angel round | July 2025 | Tens of millions of yuan | Bairui Capital, Lingyi Venture Capital |
| Angel+ round | October 2025 | Tens of millions of yuan | Led by Guoke Investment, followed by Bairui Capital |

(Data source: 36Kr, PEdaily, Highroad Capital, cross-validated across multiple sources)

Cumulative funding of roughly **50 million to 100 million yuan** (estimated, taking the midpoint of each round's "tens of millions" range). The financial advisor was Highroad Capital.

**Counterpoint**: three rounds in one year looks fast-paced, but each round's amount isn't large. Compared with the leading player in the same track, Jiangyou Culture (a top company with **50 million yuan** in monthly revenue and a team of **1,000+**), LingJing Wanwei's ammunition is an order of magnitude smaller. In a track where large platforms are investing hundreds of millions of yuan a year, this amount of capital needs to be spent with extremely high efficiency.

As of the end of 2025, the team was nearly **100** people, with dual headquarters in Hangzhou and Wuhan. According to a Wuhan municipal government investment-promotion release, monthly revenue was about **20 million yuan**, order backlog extends to **2027**, and total order value exceeds **100 million yuan**.

**Data confidence note**: the 20-million-yuan monthly revenue figure comes from a Wuhan municipal government investment-promotion release, and such figures typically carry some promotional inflation, so the actual number may be lower. But the revenue data for the breakout hit "Doomsday Supermarket" can be cross-validated across multiple independent channels.

## A core question: 150,000 yuan into 12 million — how much was actually made?

On the surface, "Doomsday Supermarket" with a 150,000-yuan cost and 12-million-yuan revenue looks implausibly profitable.

But there's an open secret in the industry: **over 80% of revenue has to be spent on paid traffic acquisition**.

In other words:

| Item | Amount (estimated) |
|------|-----------|
| Production cost | ~150,000 yuan |
| Traffic-acquisition cost (at 80%) | ~**9.6 million yuan** |
| Platform commission (~10-20%) | ~**1.2-2.4 million yuan** |
| **Actual profit** | **~0-1 million yuan** (estimated) |

(Note: the above is an estimate based on industry convention, not official LingJing Wanwei data)

**12 million yuan in revenue sounds explosive, but after subtracting traffic acquisition and platform commission, the profit may be only in the low single-digit millions or even less. And this is the hit — the other 624 non-hit works can't even recoup their traffic acquisition costs.**

**Counterpoint**: LingJing Wanwei has its own in-house traffic-acquisition team, which may be more efficient than the industry average. In addition, a hit's long-tail revenue (sequels, IP spin-offs) could bring additional returns. A production cost as low as 150,000 yuan means the cost of failure is extremely low, and "keep trying until one hits" is the core logic of this model.

## Industry panorama: a 30-billion-yuan market, but 90% is losing money

AI comic-dramas are one of the hottest tracks in China's content industry from 2024 to 2026. Key data:

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| 2025 comic-drama market size | **16.8 billion yuan** | iiMedia Research |
| 2026 comic-drama market size (forecast) | **22.0-31.4 billion yuan** | iiMedia Research, 36Kr |
| 2030 market size (forecast) | **85.8 billion yuan** | industry reports |
| 2026 comic-drama user count (forecast) | **280 million** | iiMedia Research |
| Titles released in January 2026 | **14,000** (daily average of 470) | 21st Century Business Herald |
| AI vs. traditional production cost | Reduced by **80-95%** | multiple sources |

The market is growing explosively, but —

**Nearly every major internet company has already entered the field**:

| Company | Move |
|------|------|
| ByteDance | Hongguo Comic-Drama (**24.04 million** monthly active users), Jimeng AI |
| Kuaishou | Keling (annual revenue exceeding **$100 million**), Zaomeng Zhuanjia |
| Tencent | Huolong Comic-Drama app (launched February 2026) |
| Baidu | Youmanju app (launched December 2025) |
| 360 | Nami Comic-Drama production line |
| China Literature | Comic-Drama Assistant, ten comic-dramas surpassing 100 million views |
| Bilibili/iQIYI/Youku/Mango TV | Each has set up a comic-drama channel |

(Data source: 36Kr, Securities Times, TMTPost)

**On April 1, 2026**, the National Radio and Television Administration's new AI comic-drama filing regulations officially took effect: all unfiled works are **forcibly taken down**, and new works must be **filed before launch**. Review is tiered into three levels by investment amount. This means the industry is moving from "wild growth" into a "licensed operation" phase.
