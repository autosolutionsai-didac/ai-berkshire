## The brutal reality of China's large-model race

Large models are one of the most brutally competitive races in China today: four mountains loom overhead, and four startups valued above RMB 100 billion each are circling nearby. Where can Zhipu AI break through?

As of May 2026, China's large-model race has formed two camps:

**Camp one: the big-tech armies**
- ByteDance (Doubao), Alibaba (Tongyi Qianwen/Qwen), Baidu (Ernie Bot), Tencent (Hunyuan)
- Characteristics: money, data, traffic, and use-case scenarios

**Camp two: the five startup rivals**
- DeepSeek, Zhipu AI, MiniMax, Moonshot AI (Kimi), and StepFun
- Characteristics: sharp technology, soaring valuations, but lacking scenarios and traffic

(Source: 36Kr, Sina Finance, 21st Century Business Herald)

### DeepSeek: Zhipu's number-one threat

If you could name only one rival most worth watching, the answer is **DeepSeek**.

A direct comparison between DeepSeek and Zhipu:

| Dimension | Zhipu | DeepSeek |
|------|------|----------|
| Technical level | GLM-5.1, top tier | DeepSeek-V4, top tier |
| Open-source strategy | Fully open under MIT | Fully open under MIT |
| API pricing | Mid-range | **Extremely low** (the lowest tier in the industry) |
| Commercial revenue | RMB 724 million (2025) | Projected to exceed RMB 5 billion in 2026 |
| Consumer product | Zhipu Qingyan (MAU ~8.38 million) | DeepSeek App (viral growth) |
| Financing | RMB 5.2 billion raised via IPO | Long relied on internal funds; first reports of external financing surfaced in 2026 |
| Team background | Tsinghua academic lineage | High-Flyer quantitative fund, engineering + algorithms |
| Cost control | Average | **Extreme** ("train a stronger model for less money") |

(Source: each company's public disclosures, CNBC, Securities Times)

The threat DeepSeek poses to Zhipu is **all-encompassing**:

1. **Not behind on technology** — DeepSeek-V4's overall capability is on par with GLM-5.1
2. **Crushing on cost** — DeepSeek's API pricing is far lower than Zhipu's, directly threatening Zhipu's API revenue
3. **Stronger brand** — DeepSeek's "DeepSeek moment" in early 2025 earned it global attention, giving it far more brand recognition than Zhipu
4. **Financially healthier** — DeepSeek is backed by the quantitative fund High-Flyer, has ample cash, and isn't under pressure to list or raise money

**Counterpoint**: DeepSeek's advantage is being "cheap," but cheap isn't a permanent moat. Zhipu has advantages DeepSeek lacks in domestic-chip compatibility (Huawei Ascend) and government/enterprise clients — many government agencies and state-owned enterprises won't choose an AI company with a quant-fund background, and instead prefer Zhipu with its "Tsinghua-affiliated + state-capital-backed" profile.

### MiniMax: a Hong Kong-listed rival in the same lane

MiniMax listed in Hong Kong almost simultaneously with Zhipu, at a market cap likewise in the HK$300 billion range. The differences between the two:

| Dimension | Zhipu | MiniMax |
|------|------|---------|
| Model positioning | Full-stack flagship | Cost-efficiency first |
| Revenue mix | Primarily on-premise (74%) | Balanced between consumer and API |
| Overseas footprint | API coverage in 218 countries | Overseas products (Talkie, etc.) with a genuine user base |
| Team DNA | Tsinghua academic | SenseTime alumni, stronger at commercialization |

MiniMax's consumer products (Talkie and others) have a genuine overseas user base, something Zhipu lacks. But in enterprise services, Zhipu's customer quality is higher (used by 9 of the top 10 internet companies).

### Moonshot AI/Kimi: the consumer winner

Kimi's consumer performance far outstrips Zhipu Qingyan's — it broke into the market with the differentiated feature of "long-context processing," winning over large numbers of students and knowledge workers. But Kimi lags far behind Zhipu on the enterprise side.

The overlap between the two companies' competition is actually low — **Zhipu fights on-premise/enterprise, Kimi fights consumer** — so in the near term they aren't direct rivals.

## The hunt by big tech: the asymmetric pressure Zhipu faces

Competition among startups is "matched-weight technical rivalry"; competition from big tech is **asymmetric pressure from a higher dimension**.

### ByteDance/Doubao: crushed by traffic

| Dimension | Zhipu Qingyan | Doubao |
|------|---------|------------|
| MAU | ~8.38 million | **227 million** |
| Traffic source | Standalone app | Douyin ecosystem (700 million+ daily active users) |
| Use cases | Standalone chat | Embedded in short video, search, e-commerce, office tools |
| Model capability | GLM-5.1 (top-tier) | ByteDance's in-house model (rapid iteration) |

**The gap is 27x.** This isn't a case of Zhipu "doing a bad job" — rather, on the consumer side, an AI company without a traffic entry point stands almost no chance of winning.

### Alibaba/Tongyi Qianwen: a head-on clash in open source

Tongyi Qianwen (Qwen) is Zhipu's biggest rival in open source:

| Dimension | Zhipu GLM | Alibaba's Qwen |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Open-source license | MIT | Apache 2.0 |
| Model lineup | GLM series | Qwen series (broader) |
| Global token share | Competing with Qwen | **2.6%** (and growing fast) |
| Backing resources | Tsinghua + state capital | Alibaba Cloud (world's third-largest cloud) |

Qwen's advantage lies in Alibaba Cloud's global infrastructure, which can directly provide deployment and distribution channels for Qwen. Zhipu has no cloud platform of its own and must rely on third parties for API distribution.

### Baidu/Ernie Bot: a long-standing enterprise rival

Baidu was among the earliest to lay out an AI strategy, and its Ernie models are already deeply integrated into search, autonomous driving (Apollo), cloud services, and more. Baidu's enterprise capability and government relationships compete directly with Zhipu.

But Baidu's market share has been eroding in recent years — there's growing talk of "Baidu falling behind." Zhipu may already have leapfrogged Baidu a generation in technology (GLM-5.1 vs. Ernie 4.5).

## What exactly is Zhipu's competitive niche?

Zooming out across the whole competitive landscape, Zhipu's positioning becomes clearer:

| Dimension | Zhipu's position |
|------|-----------|
| Consumer | Already out of the running (crushed by big tech) |
| Global API market | Second tier (DeepSeek leads) |
| China government/enterprise on-premise | **First tier** (boosted by Tsinghua affiliation + state backing) |
| Open-source ecosystem | First tier (alongside Qwen and DeepSeek) |
| AI agents | First-mover, but no established advantage |
| Domestic-chip support | **A unique advantage** (deep compatibility with Huawei Ascend) |

**Zhipu's niche is "the preferred AI foundation-model provider for China's government and enterprise market"** — driven not just by technology, but by non-technical factors like the Tsinghua brand, state-capital backing, and domestic-chip compatibility.

How large is this niche? The good news is that China's government/enterprise market is enormous; the bad news is that competitors are pouring in just as fast (Baidu, Alibaba, and Huawei's own Pangu model).

**Counterpoint**: if Zhipu can only win in "government/enterprise on-premise," its ceiling is that of a traditional IT service provider, not "China's Anthropic." Anthropic is worth $60 billion precisely because its API is used by developers worldwide. If Zhipu cannot secure a foothold in the open, global API market, its HK$450 billion valuation will be difficult to justify.

## Three judgments on the competitive landscape

**Judgment one: the five-startup lineup is unstable, and at least one is likely to fall behind within two years**

All five companies are valued above RMB 100 billion, but most are still posting massive losses. Once the financing environment tightens, or a major fork emerges in the technical roadmap, the weaker-ranked companies could be marginalized or acquired.

**Judgment two: big tech's endgame advantage will become increasingly clear**

Model training costs will keep falling, but **distribution channels and use-case advantages** are something startups cannot replicate. ByteDance's Douyin, Alibaba's Taobao + DingTalk, Tencent's WeChat — these traffic gateways determine who wins on the consumer side. Startups can only look for room to maneuver in the enterprise and API markets.

**Judgment three: Zhipu's biggest moat may not be technology, but "identity"**

Tsinghua affiliation + state-capital backing + domestic-chip compatibility — in China's distinctive industrial environment, the trust and policy dividends that come with this "identity" may carry more commercial value than benchmark scores.

**Counterpoint**: an identity card isn't valid forever. If DeepSeek keeps winning on pure commercial competition, government and enterprise clients will eventually vote with their feet too. Technical and product competitiveness remains the long-term moat.
