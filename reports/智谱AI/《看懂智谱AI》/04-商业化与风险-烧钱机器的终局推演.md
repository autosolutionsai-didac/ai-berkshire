# The Endgame for a Cash-Burning Machine

## The core contradiction: RMB 724 million in revenue, RMB 4.718 billion in losses

A company burns RMB 4.7 billion a year, yet its market cap exceeds HK$400 billion — is that faith, or a rational expectation backed by data? To answer that, we first need to lay the financial truth out on the table.

### The 2025 financial picture in full

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Total revenue | RMB 312 million | **RMB 724 million** | **+131.9%** |
| Gross margin | — | **41.0%** | — |
| R&D spending | — | **RMB 3.18 billion** | — |
| Net loss | — | **RMB 4.718 billion** | — |
| Revenue-to-R&D ratio | — | **1:4.4** | Revenue covers only 23% of R&D spend |

(Source: Zhipu AI 2025 annual report)

**Revenue of RMB 724 million, loss of RMB 4.718 billion.** For every RMB 1 earned, the company loses RMB 6.5.

This isn't an ordinary "growth-phase loss" — there is a **massive structural gap** between revenue and costs.

Why is the loss so large? Breaking it down:

| Cost item | Amount (RMB billion) | % of revenue |
|--------|------------|---------|
| R&D spending | **3.18** | 439% |
| Other operating expenses (estimated) | **~2.2** | ~300% |
| **Total costs** | **~5.4** | **~746%** |
| Revenue | 0.724 | 100% |

(Source: derived from Zhipu's 2025 annual report)

**R&D spending alone is 4.4 times revenue.** This tells us that Zhipu is, at its core, currently a "research institution" rather than a "commercial company" — revenue is essentially a byproduct, while the bulk of effort and capital goes into training models.

### How long can the cash last?

| Funding item | Amount |
|--------|------|
| IPO proceeds | **HK$5.23 billion (about RMB 4.8 billion)** |
| 2025 net loss | **RMB 4.718 billion** |
| Rough calculation | IPO proceeds are **enough to cover roughly one year** of burn |

(Source: prospectus, annual report)

Of course, this is the crudest possible calculation. In reality, Zhipu also had cash on its books before listing (cumulative pre-IPO fundraising of RMB 8.3 billion, with a residual balance after years of losses), plus incoming cash flow from revenue growth — so actual available funds may support **1.5 to 2 years**.

But that also means: **if Zhipu cannot substantially increase revenue or complete a new round of financing before 2027, it will face severe funding pressure.**

**Counterpoint**: Zhipu has significant state-owned and policy capital behind it — the Beijing AI Industry Fund and Hangzhou Chengtou (Hangzhou's municipal investment arm) are both shareholders. Against the backdrop of "self-sufficient, controllable domestic AI," Zhipu is unlikely to be "cut off." But being kept alive by state capital and being profitable through one's own operations are two different things — surviving on state-backed transfusions versus surviving on self-generated cash flow implies completely different valuation logic.

## Three possible monetization paths — which one will it take?

Zhipu has three possible paths to monetization:

### Path one: On-premise deployment for enterprise clients (current mainstay)

- 2025 revenue of **RMB 534 million**, accounting for **74%** of the total, growing **+102%**
- Essentially "project-based delivery" — deploying private large models for government and enterprise clients
- Advantages: high per-deal value, strong customer stickiness, meets data-security requirements
- Disadvantages: **not scalable**. Every customer needs a customized deployment, labor costs are high, and gross margin is capped

(Source: Zhipu 2025 annual report)

The ceiling on this path is clearly visible: there are many Chinese companies delivering government/enterprise IT projects (Huawei, ZTE, iFlytek, etc.), and they all eventually run into the trap of "revenue grows but profit doesn't." If Zhipu relies solely on this path, its ceiling is **annual revenue of a few billion RMB, with thin profit or a slight loss** — not enough to support a market cap of HK$450 billion.

### Path two: MaaS/API platform (fastest-growing)

- 2025 revenue of **RMB 190 million**, accounting for **26%** of the total, growing **+293%**
- MaaS ARR of approximately **RMB 1.7 billion**
- Q1 2026 API call volume grew **400%**, with pricing up **83%**

(Source: Zhipu annual report, Securities Times)

This is Zhipu's most promising direction. The API platform's business model is "selling tokens" — each API call is billed by token count. The core advantage of this model is **declining marginal cost**: once a model is trained, the cost of each additional API call is extremely low, so gross margin rises rapidly as scale increases.

Anthropic has taken exactly this path — its 2025 ARR reached **over $1 billion**, with an even higher figure expected for 2026. If Zhipu can execute well on this path, it could genuinely become "China's Anthropic."

**But there's a harsh reality**: the API market is in the middle of a price war.

| API pricing comparison (RMB per million output tokens) | Zhipu GLM-5.1 | DeepSeek-V4 | Alibaba's Qwen |
|---------------------------------------------|------------|-------------|-------------|
| Rough tier | Mid-range | **Extremely low** | Low |

DeepSeek's pricing strategy is "rock-bottom prices to seize market share." If the price war continues, Zhipu's API gross margin will be squeezed hard, and the rosy picture of "rising volume and rising price" may not hold.

### Path three: AI agents/application layer (future upside)

AutoGLM (a phone-use agent) and CogVideoX (video generation) represent Zhipu's attempts to extend into the application layer. If AI agents genuinely become the next platform-level entry point, Zhipu's first-mover advantage could translate into commercial value.

**But this path is the least certain.** The AI agent race is still at a very early stage, and no mature business model has emerged. Moreover, tech giants (Apple, Google, ByteDance) are investing far more than Zhipu in this direction.

## A 560x price-to-sales ratio: what is the market pricing in?

Zhipu's current market cap is approximately **HK$450 billion (~RMB 410 billion)**, against 2025 revenue of **RMB 724 million** — implying a price-to-sales ratio of roughly **560x**.

This figure is completely incomprehensible under a traditional valuation framework. But the valuation logic for AI companies isn't based on current revenue — it's based on **expectations for future revenue growth and eventual scale**.

### Working backward: what is the market assuming?

If we reverse-engineer the assumptions implied by the current market cap:

| Assumption | Value |
|------|------|
| Current market cap | ~RMB 410 billion |
| If a 20x P/S ratio applies 5 years out (2031) | Requires annual revenue of **RMB 20.5 billion** |
| If a 10x P/S ratio applies 5 years out | Requires annual revenue of **RMB 41 billion** |
| If a 30x P/S ratio applies 5 years out (growth-stage premium) | Requires annual revenue of **RMB 13.7 billion** |
| 2025 revenue base | RMB 724 million |
| Growing from RMB 724 million to RMB 13.7 billion (5 years) | Requires a compound annual growth rate of **~80%** |
| Growing from RMB 724 million to RMB 20.5 billion (5 years) | Requires a compound annual growth rate of **~96%** |

Even under the most optimistic assumption (a 30x P/S ratio), Zhipu would need to sustain **average annual revenue growth of 80%** over the next five years — going from RMB 724 million to RMB 13.7 billion.

The 2025 growth rate was 132%, which looks promising. But base effects mean growth rates tend to slow year by year — going from RMB 300 million to RMB 700 million is easy; going from RMB 7 billion to RMB 14 billion is far harder.

**Counterpoint**: If MaaS ARR can genuinely grow quickly from RMB 1.7 billion to the tens-of-billions range, combined with on-premise revenue, reaching RMB 10-20 billion in revenue within five years is not impossible. Anthropic went from $0 to $1 billion in ARR in under three years. But Anthropic addresses a global (predominantly English-speaking) market, while Zhipu addresses a market centered on China — the ceilings differ.

## Three scenarios

### Scenario one: China's Anthropic (probability ~25%)

**Assumptions**:
- GLM models remain in the top tier
- MaaS/API revenue sustains 100%+ growth for 3 years
- The open-source ecosystem becomes the preferred choice for Chinese developers
- AI agents deliver a new growth curve

**Picture 5 years out**:
- Annual revenue of **RMB 10-20 billion**
- MaaS gross margin rises to **60%+**
- Reaches breakeven or slight profitability
- Reasonable market cap of **HK$200-400 billion**

Under this scenario, the current market cap is "just barely acceptable" — but only if every assumption materializes.

### Scenario two: A stable second-tier AI company (probability ~50%)

**Assumptions**:
- Technology stays competitive but not leading, gradually caught up by DeepSeek and the tech giants
- On-premise revenue grows steadily, MaaS growth slows
- Consumer-facing products remain marginal
- Operations sustained by government/enterprise clients and state backing

**Picture 5 years out**:
- Annual revenue of **RMB 3-6 billion**
- Still at a loss or barely profitable
- Market cap retreats to **HK$50-150 billion**

This is the most likely scenario — Zhipu won't disappear, but it won't become Anthropic either.

### Scenario three: Marginalization or acquisition (probability ~25%)

**Assumptions**:
- Zhipu falls behind in a generational model transition (e.g., a next-generation DeepSeek or Qwen model leapfrogs it substantially)
- API price war stalls revenue growth
- Continued massive losses, difficulty raising capital
- Core talent departs (452 equity-holding employees cash out after their lock-up period expires)

**Picture 5 years out**:
- Annual revenue **below RMB 3 billion**
- Acquired or strategically merged by a tech giant (Alibaba/Tencent/Huawei)
- A trajectory similar to SenseTime's
- Market cap **below HK$50 billion**

## Six core risks

| Risk | Level | Details |
|------|------|---------|
| **Cash burn rate** | Extremely high | Annual loss of RMB 4.7 billion; IPO proceeds cover only 1-2 years; if losses aren't substantially narrowed or new funding secured before 2027, a funding crisis looms |
| **Big-tech pressure** | High | Already out of the consumer market; on-premise and API businesses face direct competition from Alibaba (Qwen + Alibaba Cloud) and Huawei (Pangu + Ascend) |
| **Valuation bubble** | High | A 560x P/S ratio requires over 80% average annual growth for 5 years to justify; if growth falls short, the valuation will adjust sharply |
| **DeepSeek price war** | High | The API market price war could severely compress Zhipu's gross margin, undermining the "rising volume, rising price" narrative |
| **Customer concentration** | Medium-high | The top five customers account for over 40% of revenue (source: prospectus); losing a major customer would cause significant revenue swings |
| **Talent attrition** | Medium | 452 equity-holding employees may cash out and leave once their lock-up period expires; the replacement pool from the homogeneous Tsinghua-affiliated team is limited |

## Zhipu AI vs. historical analogues

Placing Zhipu within a historical frame of reference yields several possible comparisons:

| Comparable company | Similarities | Differences | Outcome |
|---------|--------|--------|------|
| **Anthropic** | API-centric, technology-driven, no consumer product | Anthropic serves the global English-speaking market with far more capital | Currently valued at $60 billion+, not yet public |
| **SenseTime** | Hong Kong listing, AI narrative, academic team, massive losses | SenseTime lacked a core technological moat | Market cap fell from HK$280 billion to ~HK$30 billion after listing |
| **iFlytek** | Tsinghua background, primarily on-premise/enterprise, government and enterprise clients | iFlytek has stronger productization capability and is already profitable | Market cap ~RMB 120 billion, stable but slowing growth |

**The most cautionary comparison is SenseTime** — also a Hong Kong-listed AI story stock with an academic-team background, massive losses, and a market cap that soared after listing. SenseTime's lesson: **once the narrative fades, a market cap unsupported by profit shrinks fast**.

**But there's one key difference between Zhipu and SenseTime**: Zhipu's GLM-5.1 genuinely ranks among the global top tier in benchmarks, while SenseTime never reached the global first tier on core technology. This gap in technical strength may let Zhipu avoid SenseTime's trajectory — but that's not guaranteed.

## Summary of this piece: a few facts, no preset conclusion

**Favorable facts**:
1. GLM-5.1's technical level genuinely sits in the global top tier
2. MaaS ARR has grown 60x, with rising volume and rising price
3. The Tsinghua + state-capital background provides a short-term funding cushion and policy tailwinds
4. China's government/enterprise AI market is enormous, and Zhipu has a first-mover advantage
5. The open-source strategy is sound, and the developer ecosystem is building quickly

**Unfavorable facts**:
1. An annual loss of RMB 4.7 billion, with revenue equal to only 23% of R&D spend — profitability is a long way off
2. Already outpaced 27x on the consumer side by the tech giants, with no traffic entry point and no short-term fix
3. Under the API price war, the durability of "rising volume, rising price" is questionable
4. A 560x P/S ratio requires 5 straight years of 80%+ growth to be justified
5. The founding team is homogeneously Tsinghua-affiliated, with limited commercial-genes
6. There are similarities to SenseTime's early trajectory

**Put these facts together, and you'll find Zhipu AI is a textbook "high-risk, high-reward" name** — the technology genuinely is strong, but commercial validation is far from complete; growth genuinely is fast, but the loss gap is equally massive; the narrative genuinely is good, but the valuation depends heavily on that narrative holding.

How you view this company depends on how you answer the following questions:
- Do you believe China's large-model race can produce a company on the level of Anthropic?
- Do you think Zhipu is that company? Or is DeepSeek more likely to be?
- Can you stomach the risk of the market cap retreating from HK$450 billion to HK$100 billion or lower?
- Do you believe a "Tsinghua-affiliated + state-capital-backed" identity is an advantage or a burden in long-term competition?

**There are no standard answers to these questions.** But if, after reading all four pieces, you can answer them more clearly — this series will have achieved its purpose.
