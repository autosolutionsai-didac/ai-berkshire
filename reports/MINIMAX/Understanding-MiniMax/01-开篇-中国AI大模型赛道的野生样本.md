## A Company That Leaves You Puzzled

On January 9, 2026, a strange company arrived on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Founded 4 years earlier, with 385 employees and annual revenue of less than 600 million yuan — yet its stock price doubled on the first day of trading, and its market cap shot past 100 billion Hong Kong dollars.

This was no ordinary AI startup listing. On its first trading day, the stock ran from an issue price of **HK$165** to **HK$345**, a gain of **109%**. Two months later it stood at **HK$680**, with a market cap of **HK$210 billion**.

As of May 2026, its share price sits around **HK$742**, with a market cap of roughly **HK$250 billion** (about US$32 billion), and a 52-week trading range of **HK$220 to HK$1,330** — the high is 6 times the low.

This company is called **MiniMax (Xiyu Technology)**.

But if you open its financial statements, you'll see a set of numbers that don't quite add up:

| Metric | 2025 | Scale assessment |
|------|--------|---------|
| Total revenue | **US$79.04 million** (about 570 million yuan) | A company with a HK$250 billion market cap earning under 600 million yuan a year |
| Revenue growth | **+158.9%** | Extremely fast |
| Gross margin | **25.4%** (vs. 12.2% in 2024) | Rapid improvement |
| Adjusted net loss | **US$251 million** (about 1.8 billion yuan) | More than 3x revenue |
| Loss for the year (including fair-value changes) | **US$1.872 billion** | A frightening headline number |
| Selling expenses | **US$51.9 million** (down 40.3% year-over-year) | An anomaly: revenue up 159% while marketing spend down 40% |
| Cumulative users | **236 million**, across 200+ countries | Overseas revenue accounts for over 70% |
| Employees | **385**, average age **29** | A ~100-billion-dollar valuation held up by people born after 1995 |

**A company earning under 600 million yuan a year, valued at HK$250 billion. An annual loss of 1.8 billion yuan. 385 people.**

If you think this is a bubble — you might be right.
If you think there's an opportunity here — you might also be right.

These two judgments aren't contradictory, because they're looking at different sides of the same company.

## Who Exactly Is MINIMAX

MINIMAX (Xiyu Technology) was founded by **Yan Junjie** in early 2022 in Shanghai. Yan Junjie, born in 1989, holds an undergraduate degree from the University of Science and Technology of China and a PhD in computer science from Carnegie Mellon University (CMU). He previously served as Vice President of SenseTime and head of its general intelligence technology division.

A key timing detail: **in late 2021**, Yan Junjie left SenseTime to start his own company. This was nearly a year before the ChatGPT breakout (November 2022) — he bet on large models earlier than almost anyone else.

Over 4 years and 7 funding rounds, the company raised more than **US$1.5 billion** cumulatively, with investors including Tencent, Alibaba, miHoYo, Hillhouse Capital, and Sequoia China. It listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in January 2026, raising **HK$5.54 billion**, with 14 cornerstone investors (Alibaba, the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, E Fund, and others) subscribing for **HK$2.723 billion**, and the public offering oversubscribed by **1,837 times**.

**From founding to IPO in just 4 years — one of the fastest IPO timelines in global AI.**

But speed isn't the same as quality. To understand this company, you first need to understand what it actually sells.

## Four Product Lines, One Foundation

MINIMAX is the only Chinese startup that has reached world-class standards across all four modalities — **text, voice, video, and music**. Its product matrix looks like this:

| Product | Positioning | Key data | Revenue share (2025) |
|------|------|---------|------------------|
| **Talkie/Xingye** | AI social role-play | **147 million** cumulative users, MAU **27.6 million**, overseas MAU **11 million** (50%+ from the US) | ~67% (AI-native products) |
| **Hailuo AI** | AI video generation + AI assistant | **42.34 million** cumulative users, Hailuo 02 supports native 1080p resolution | Included within AI-native products |
| **MiniMax Open Platform** | Large-model API service | **214,000** enterprise clients and developers, across 100+ countries | ~33% (growth rate **297%**) |
| **Speech-02** | Text-to-speech (TTS) | **Ranked #1** on both global Arena leaderboards, surpassing OpenAI and ElevenLabs, voice-similarity of **99%** | Included within the open platform |

Underlying all of this is the self-developed **M2.7 large model** — a MoE architecture with **230 billion** total parameters and **10 billion** activated parameters, supporting an ultra-long context window of **200K tokens**. It scores **56.22%** on the SWE-Pro benchmark, with coding capability close to GPT-5.3-Codex, at only **1/10th to 1/20th** the price of Opus 4.6.

**In one sentence: MINIMAX = a self-developed multimodal large model (the foundation) + Talkie (overseas AI social) + Hailuo AI (video generation) + the open platform (API service).**

## Three Common Misreadings of MINIMAX

### Misreading 1: MINIMAX is just another large-model startup

China has no shortage of large-model startups — over a hundred in 2023, fewer than ten left by 2025. Most people lump MINIMAX in with the "Six Little Tigers of large models" (Moonshot AI, Zhipu, Baichuan, 01.AI, MINIMAX, StepFun) and move on.

But MINIMAX differs fundamentally from the others: **it doesn't just build models, it builds applications; it doesn't just serve the domestic market, it goes global; it doesn't just do text, it does every modality.**

Among the Six Little Tigers:
- Moonshot AI (Kimi) focuses on long-context and search augmentation, almost entirely domestic
- Zhipu (GLM) leans academic and enterprise-service, with an open-source approach
- Baichuan and 01.AI have already scaled back or pivoted

MINIMAX has the **highest share of overseas revenue** (over 70%), the **most complete product lineup** (text + voice + video + music), and the **largest user base** (236 million) among them.

**Counterargument**: a complete product lineup also means an overstretched front. With 385 people running four product lines simultaneously, resources are seriously spread thin. A startup's ammunition should be concentrated, not scattered like buckshot.

### Misreading 2: A market cap of HK$250 billion is pure bubble

HK$250 billion market cap (about 230 billion yuan) / 570 million yuan revenue = a P/S ratio of roughly **400x**. That number does look crazy.

But a few facts are worth weighing:
- By late February 2026, ARR had already surpassed **US$150 million** (about 1.1 billion yuan), implying monthly revenue of about **US$12.5 million** — nearly **double** the 2025 monthly average
- New registrations on the open platform in February 2026 were **more than 4 times** the December 2025 level
- Inference compute costs for the M2 series have fallen more than **50%** since December 2025
- If full-year 2026 revenue reaches **US$150-200 million** (about 1.1-1.4 billion yuan), the P/S ratio would fall to roughly **160-210x**

Still expensive. But the valuation logic for AI companies isn't based on current revenue — it's based on **eventual scale and the path to get there**.

**Counterargument**: a P/S above 160x is still absurdly high. And "eventual scale" is pure imagination-based pricing — the same rhetoric was used during the 2000 internet bubble. A company losing 1.8 billion yuan a year is nowhere near breakeven.

### Misreading 3: MINIMAX will be crushed by the giants

ByteDance's Doubao already has an MAU of over **226 million** (December 2025), with daily token calls surpassing **50 trillion**. Alibaba's Qwen has an MAU of **25.72 million**. Baidu's Ernie Bot has fallen behind but still holds the advantage of a search-engine entry point.

These giants hold **more than 10x** the capital, traffic, and compute resources. How can MINIMAX possibly survive?

The answer is **niche differentiation**:
- **Talkie operates in overseas social AI**, a lane where the giants have no first-mover advantage — ByteDance's own TikTok faces its own geopolitical risks
- **Hailuo AI operates in video generation**, the fastest-growing sub-sector (100%+ annual growth), where the giants are only just starting to catch up
- **Global #1 in speech synthesis**, a vertical capability that hasn't yet been steamrolled

**Counterargument**: niche differentiation can only hold for so long. ByteDance's Jimeng is rapidly catching up in video generation; Google, having absorbed Character.AI, may re-energize its push into social AI; once the giants get serious, a startup's first-mover advantage may only last 6-12 months.
