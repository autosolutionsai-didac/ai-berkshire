## A trillion-dollar track, hellish competition

The global generative AI market is roughly **$90-100 billion** (2025 estimate, per IDC/Gartner synthesis), growing at roughly **50%** annually, projected to reach **$300 billion** by 2028. China's market is approximately **50 billion yuan**, growing at roughly **70%** annually. These numbers would make a top-tier scorecard in any industry.

The problem is: a great track does not equal a good business. History has repeatedly shown that the tracks with the most entrants and the fastest growth are often exactly where capital floods in first, price wars break out first, and profits disappear first.

Looking back at history:
- The **airline industry** is a trillion-dollar track, but from the Wright brothers to today, the net value that global airlines have created for shareholders is **negative**
- During the **dot-com bubble**, hundreds of .com companies raised funding and went public; fewer than **5%** ultimately survived
- The **solar industry** is a trillion-dollar global market, but Chinese solar companies, due to brutal price wars, have seen even the industry leaders operate at a loss

The AI large-model industry is replaying the same script: **the track is worth trillions, but the competitive landscape is hellish.**

## The four tiers of China's AI large-model industry

By 2026, the landscape of China's AI large-model industry has become quite clear:

### Tier 0: The giants — an unshakeable presence

| Company | Core product | MAU (Dec 2025) | Core advantage |
|------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **ByteDance** | Doubao | **226 million** | Traffic funneled from Douyin's 700 million daily active users + ByteDance compute + closed data loop |
| **Alibaba** | Tongyi Qianwen / Quark | **40.71 million** | Alibaba Cloud + open-source ecosystem + e-commerce scenarios |
| **Tencent** | Hunyuan / Yuanbao | — | WeChat ecosystem + enterprise services |

ByteDance's Doubao reached a MAU of **226 million** in December 2025, up **201%** year over year. Its growth has come almost entirely from traffic funneled in from the Douyin ecosystem — customer-acquisition cost is close to zero.

**ByteDance's threat to MINIMAX is a dimensional-crushing one.** ByteDance doesn't need AI to make money on its own — Doubao can improve Douyin's recommendation accuracy, ad revenue, and e-commerce conversion; AI can be a cost center for ByteDance and that's fine. MINIMAX, by contrast, must survive on AI itself making money.

### Tier 0.5: The disruptor — DeepSeek

| Metric | DeepSeek |
|------|---------|
| MAU (Dec 2025) | **135 million** |
| Core technology | DeepSeek-V3/R1, MoE architecture, training cost reduced by an order of magnitude |
| Strategy | **Fully open-source** |
| Capital backing | High-Flyer Quant (self-funded, not short of cash) |

DeepSeek's impact on the entire industry isn't just "building a better model" — it **redefined the cost structure of AI**, similar to how Linux disrupted Unix. It proved that a world-class model can be built at extremely low cost, stripping away the pricing power of every closed-source company.

**Impact on MINIMAX**: MINIMAX's open-platform API pricing is under pressure from DeepSeek. Although M2.7 has stronger multimodal capability, it is not a match for DeepSeek in pure-text reasoning.

### Tier 1: Unicorns / listed companies — on the survival line

| Company | Core product | Differentiation | Current status |
|------|---------|-------|------|
| **MINIMAX** | Talkie / Hailuo AI | Multimodal + overseas | Listed in Hong Kong; market cap approximately HK$250 billion |
| **Zhipu AI** | GLM-5 / GLM-5.1 | Academic + open-source + enterprise services | Listed in Hong Kong; GLM-5.1 billed as the "strongest open-source model" |
| **Moonshot AI** | Kimi | Long context + search augmentation | Not yet listed; valuation approximately $3 billion |
| **Baichuan** | Baichuan large model | Search + healthcare | Not yet listed; contracting |

MINIMAX and Zhipu AI are the **only two of the "six little AI tigers" to have successfully gone public**. That in itself is a filter — an IPO means access to public financing channels and a longer cash runway than unlisted peers.

### Tier 2: Contracting / pivoting

01.AI (founded by Kai-Fu Lee) has already shifted from large models toward the application layer. Most other smaller companies have exited or been acquired.

**Landscape judgment: the "shakeout" in China's AI large-model industry is more than halfway done.** The hundred-plus companies of 2023 have shrunk to fewer than ten still in the front rank by 2026. But the most brutal elimination phase may not have started yet — the giants' resource commitments are still accelerating.

## MINIMAX's three competitors

### Competitor one: ByteDance's Doubao — crushed by traffic

ByteDance is MINIMAX's biggest threat, but the two aren't competing on the same dimension.

| Dimension | ByteDance's Doubao | MINIMAX |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Traffic** | Fed by Douyin's 700 million daily active users; customer-acquisition cost near zero | Organic growth plus modest paid acquisition; must pay to acquire users |
| **Compute** | 2025 capital expenditure exceeded **100 billion yuan** (estimate) | Full-year adjusted loss of **$251 million** |
| **Data** | Short video + e-commerce + local services — the richest multimodal data | User chat data + video-generation data |
| **Monetization** | AI improves the efficiency of the existing business; doesn't need to make money on its own | Must make money from AI itself |
| **Weakness** | TikTok is exposed to overseas geopolitical risk; AI social is not its core business | — |

**Key insight**: ByteDance's AI strategy is one of **"economies of scope"** — AI is not a standalone business but a tool that empowers Douyin, Lark, and CapCut. MINIMAX's AI strategy is one of **"economies of specialization"** — AI is everything.

This means: on the giants' home turf (domestic general-purpose AI assistants), MINIMAX has almost no chance of winning. Doubao's MAU is **more than 30 times** that of Hailuo AI.

But in the giants' **non-core arenas** — overseas social AI, video generation, voice synthesis — MINIMAX has room. ByteDance's Jimeng is catching up in video generation, but for Talkie's overseas social-AI positioning, ByteDance has no direct competing product yet.

### Competitor two: DeepSeek — cost disruption

DeepSeek's threat isn't at the product level (it has almost no consumer-facing product), but at the **industry cost-structure** level.

DeepSeek's open-source, ultra-low-cost model:
1. Compresses the value of the model layer to near zero
2. Strips pricing power from every closed-source model company
3. Forces industry value to migrate toward the **application layer**

**The impact on MINIMAX cuts both ways**:
- **Bad news**: MINIMAX's open-platform API revenue faces pricing pressure, and margins may be compressed
- **Good news**: if model-layer value trends to zero, the **application layer** becomes the value-capture point — and MINIMAX happens to do both model and application. Talkie, Hailuo AI, and Speech-02 are all application-layer products

**Counter-argument**: switching costs at the application layer are also low. A user generating video on Hailuo AI today might switch to Jimeng or Kling tomorrow. "The application layer has value" does not equal "MINIMAX's application layer has value."

### Competitor three: Zhipu AI — same-tier competition

Zhipu AI is MINIMAX's most direct competitor within Tier 1. The comparison between the two companies is instructive:

| Dimension | MINIMAX | Zhipu AI |
|------|---------|------|
| Founder | Yan Junjie (CMU PhD, former SenseTime) | Zhang Peng (Tsinghua lineage) |
| Path | Closed-source + applications | **Open-source** + enterprise services |
| Core product | Talkie (overseas consumer) | GLM (enterprise) |
| Overseas share | **70%+** | Lower |
| Listing | January 2026, Hong Kong | January 2026, Hong Kong |
| Technical highlight | Comprehensive multimodal | GLM-5.1 billed as the "strongest open-source model," with SWE-bench Pro surpassing Opus 4.6 |

The two companies are taking completely different paths — MINIMAX skews consumer, overseas, and multimodal; Zhipu AI skews enterprise, domestic, and open-source. This means that at the current stage, they are **not in direct competition** — more like they are each fighting for survival in different ecological niches.

But in the long run, if the AI industry consolidates down to only 3-5 companies, MINIMAX and Zhipu AI may eventually compete for the same seat as the "surviving independent startup."

## The smile curve of the AI industry value chain

Understanding MINIMAX's competitive position requires seeing clearly how value is distributed across the AI industry chain:

```
High value ←——————————————————————————→ High value
  Chips/Compute            Model layer           Application layer
 (NVIDIA)          (compressed by open source)     (rising value)
   ★★★★★              ★★               ★★★★
```

This is a classic **smile curve** — high value at both ends (upstream chips and downstream applications), with the middle model layer squeezed.

| Value-chain segment | Current value | Trend | Representative companies |
|-----------|---------|------|-----------|
| Chips/Compute | Extremely high | Gradually declining as competition intensifies and inference is optimized | NVIDIA, TSMC |
| **Model layer** | **Low** | **Further compressed by open source and price wars** | DeepSeek (open-source), OpenAI (closed-source) |
| **Application layer** | **Rising** | **Traffic entry points + user data + scenario barriers are becoming the moat** | ChatGPT, Talkie, Kimi |

MINIMAX's positioning is **"model + application integrated"** — it builds its own models and its own applications. The logic is sound: if model-layer value trends to zero, at least the application layer can capture value.

**But the risk is**: doing both ends means burning cash on both ends. A team of 385 people must both train large models (extremely compute-intensive) and operate four consumer/enterprise-facing products — an extremely demanding resource requirement.

## Endgame projection: who survives to the end

The likely endgame for China's AI large-model industry looks something like this:

### Model layer: 3-5 survivors

| Survivor | Reason |
|--------|------|
| ByteDance | Absolute advantage in traffic, compute, and capital |
| Alibaba | Cloud computing + open-source ecosystem |
| DeepSeek | Open-source + extreme cost efficiency + technical innovation |
| 1-2 unicorns | Establish irreplaceability in a specific modality or scenario |

### Application layer: differentiated by scenario

| Scenario | Likely winner |
|------|-----------|
| General AI assistant | Doubao (ByteDance), Yuanbao (Alibaba) |
| AI social / companionship | Talkie (MINIMAX) or a new product from a giant entering the space |
| AI video generation | One or two of Hailuo AI, Kling (Kuaishou), Jimeng (ByteDance) |
| AI voice synthesis | Speech-02 (MINIMAX), OpenAI TTS |
| AI search | Kimi (Moonshot AI), Perplexity |

### MINIMAX's three possible fates

| Fate | Probability (estimate) | Condition |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Independent survival** — becomes a global AI application platform | **25-35%** | Talkie becomes a top 1-2 global AI social product; Hailuo AI/Speech secure their niche segments |
| **Acquisition** — technology and team absorbed by a giant | **30-40%** | Commercialization falls short of expectations; acquired by Tencent/Alibaba before cash runs out |

**Counter-argument**: the probabilities above are purely subjective speculation — the AI industry changes too fast for any forecast beyond 5 years to be credible. Two years ago no one predicted DeepSeek's emergence; two years from now an even bigger variable could appear.

## MINIMAX's "narrow path"

Taking the competitive landscape as a whole, MINIMAX must walk a very narrow path:

1. **Build genuine network effects around Talkie** — not just an "AI chat tool" but an "AI social platform." If Talkie's UGC ecosystem can form community stickiness akin to Discord or Reddit, it has a shot at becoming the core of independent survival.

2. **Hold a top-2 position in video and voice** — it doesn't need to dominate everyone, but it must be irreplaceable in at least one niche segment.

3. **Dig deep into ecological niches the giants overlook** — overseas markets, multimodal fusion, Gen-Z social — these are areas the giants can't yet get to or don't do well.

4. **Control the cash-burn rate and survive to the end of the industry shakeout** — post-IPO cash reserves are estimated at **10-15 billion yuan**, with monthly burn of **$21 million**, giving a runway of roughly 3-4 years. Simply outlasting same-tier competitors by 2 years could make it the last one standing.

How narrow is this path? **Extremely narrow.** But from founding in late 2021 to listing in early 2026, Yan Junjie's timing at every step has been solid. The question is: can the luck and ability of the past 4 years carry over into the next 4?
