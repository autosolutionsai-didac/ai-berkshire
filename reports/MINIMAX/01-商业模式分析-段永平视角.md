## I. The Essence of the Business Model

### 1.1 Core Business Definition

MINIMAX (Xiyu Technology) is an **AI large-model-plus-application company**, building a range of consumer and enterprise products on top of its self-developed general large model.

**Business model essence**: use in-house large-model capability to power multiple AI application products, monetizing through consumer subscriptions/in-app purchases and enterprise API calls.

### 1.2 Product Matrix and Revenue Structure

| Product | Positioning | Target market | Monetization model | Revenue share (estimate) | Confidence |
|------|------|---------|---------|----------------|--------|
| **Talkie** | AI social/roleplay | Overseas (mainly North America) | Subscription + virtual items | ~40-50% | Low |
| **Hailuo AI assistant** | AI assistant | China | Free + paid membership | ~10-15% | Low |
| **Hailuo AI (video)** | AI video generation | Global | Free tier + paid | ~10-15% | Low |
| **MiniMax API** | Model-as-a-service | Enterprise customers | Pay-per-call API | ~20-30% | Low |

> Duan Yongping's comment: "A company doing four things at once, none of them to the extreme, is not a good sign. A good company should focus on one core product and make it irreplaceable."

## II. Flywheel Effect Analysis

### MINIMAX's Theoretical Flywheel

```
User growth → accumulation of usage data → model optimization (RLHF) → better product experience → more users
    ↑                                                              ↓
    ←——————————— word of mouth / community content ——————————————————←
```

### Flywheel Verification

| Flywheel link | Status | Rating |
|---------|---------|------|
| User growth → data accumulation | Talkie has some user scale, and its social data has value | ★★★ |
| Data → model optimization | User feedback can improve the quality of character dialogue, but it doesn't form as strong a barrier as search or e-commerce data | ★★ |
| Model optimization → experience uplift | There is some effect, but the underlying model's capability depends more on compute investment | ★★ |
| Experience → user retention | Retention rates for AI apps are generally low, and this is where the flywheel leaks | ★★ |

**Conclusion**: A flywheel exists but spins slowly and leaks noticeably. Compared with Douyin's (content-recommendation) flywheel or Pinduoduo's (low-price) flywheel, MINIMAX's flywheel effect is far from strong.

> Duan Yongping's comment: "A true flywheel effect is one where more users make the product better, which strengthens the competitive advantage — it should be self-reinforcing. If a flywheel needs continuous, heavy capital injection to keep turning, then it isn't really a flywheel — it's a machine that needs constant refueling."

## III. Moat Analysis

### Item-by-Item Verification

| Moat type | Present? | Analysis | Rating |
|-----------|---------|------|------|
| **Brand** | Weak | Talkie has some name recognition in the overseas AI-social track, but brand loyalty for AI products is extremely low — users care more about functionality than brand | ★★ |
| **Switching costs** | Extremely weak | Switching costs for AI apps are close to zero. A user's chat history and custom-built characters may create a slight switching cost, but far from enough | ★ |
| **Network effects** | Weak | Talkie's UGC character ecosystem and community have weak network effects — more characters draw more users, which draws more creators. But this effect is not as strong as that of social platforms | ★★ |
| **Scale effects** | Weak | AI inference costs decline somewhat with scale, but the scale effect of GPU compute is far weaker than the marginal-cost decline typical of software | ★★ |
| **Technical barriers** | Medium | The in-house multimodal large model (text + voice + video + music) has some entry barrier, particularly in video generation (Hailuo) and voice synthesis. But large-model technology iterates so fast that it could be matched within 6-12 months | ★★★ |

### Overall Moat Assessment

**Overall rating: ★★ (very weak)**

> Duan Yongping's lens: "A moat is the lifeblood of a business. A business without a moat, no matter how good it looks, is only temporary. MINIMAX's current moat is mostly a matter of 'having a head start,' not a genuine structural advantage. In an industry where technology changes as fast as AI, a head start might only last half a year."

## V. Business Matrix and Synergy

### Synergy Assessment

```
        MiniMax's large model (underlying capability)
           /     |      \       \
     Talkie   Hailuo AI   Hailuo   API
     (overseas) (domestic) (video)  (enterprise)
```

| Synergy dimension | Description | Strength |
|---------|------|------|
| Technology sharing | All products share a common underlying large model, reducing duplicated R&D | Strong |
| Data feedback | Each product's user data can be used to improve model training | Medium |
| Cross-user traffic | Cross-referral between products is limited (overseas / domestic / enterprise user bases differ) | Weak |
| Brand synergy | The "MiniMax" brand's synergy across products is limited | Weak |

**Conclusion**: Synergy at the technology level genuinely exists, but commercial-level synergy is weak. The multiple product lines are more about spreading resources thin than creating synergistic value.

## VI. Assessment against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Criteria

### 6.1 Differentiation

| Comparison dimension | MINIMAX | ByteDance Doubao | DeepSeek | Moonshot Kimi |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Core positioning | Multimodal + overseas social | All-around AI assistant | Open-source large model | Long-context + search |
| Degree of differentiation | Medium (video generation + social) | Low (wins on traffic) | High (open source + low cost) | Medium (long-context feature) |
| Moat depth | Shallow | Deep (traffic ecosystem) | Medium (open-source community) | Shallow |

**MINIMAX's differentiation is mainly reflected in**:
1. **Multimodal capability in video/voice/music** — it cannot win on pure text, but has an edge in combined multimodal capability
2. **Overseas markets** — Talkie sidesteps the traffic crush of domestic giants
3. **AI social/roleplay** — not a utility-type AI, but an entertainment/companionship-type AI

### 6.2 Pricing Power

**Rating: ★☆☆☆☆ (almost none)**

- The AI API market is in the middle of a brutal price war, with DeepSeek pushing prices to extreme lows
- Talkie's subscription pricing is constrained by users' willingness to pay (ARPU for AI social apps is far below that of streaming or gaming)
- Hailuo AI's video generation wavers between free and paid, with no stable pricing established yet
- Overall, MINIMAX **has no pricing power** and is pushed along by market competition and user expectations

> Duan Yongping's comment: "A business without pricing power isn't a good business. If raising the price drives customers away and lowering it kills your margin — that's a business getting squeezed from both ends. Moutai can raise prices as it pleases, because customers are loyal to the brand itself. Can MINIMAX raise its prices? No."

### 6.3 Sustainable Competitive Advantage

**Will the advantage still be there in 3-5 years?**

| Current advantage | 3-5 year outlook | Sustainability |
|---------|------------|---------|
| Talkie's overseas user base | Depends on whether the community ecosystem can stay active | Medium |
| Hailuo video generation technology | Highly likely to be matched (ByteDance/OpenAI investing more) | Low |
| Combined multimodal capability | Giants' multimodal capabilities are also catching up fast | Low |
| In-house model | Open-source model performance is converging, narrowing the in-house advantage | Low |

**Conclusion: sustainable competitive advantage is in doubt.** The only advantage with a chance of persisting is Talkie's overseas community ecosystem, and that requires continuous operational investment and content innovation.

| Free cash flow | ★ | Deep cash burn; free cash flow is deeply negative |

### **Business model dimension composite score: ★★☆☆☆**

> Duan Yongping's final verdict: "MINIMAX is a company with genuine technical ambition, and the team is doing something that counts as 'the right thing' — the AI direction is not wrong. But from a business standpoint, this is not a good business. No pricing power, no moat, no free cash flow, and fighting on multiple fronts in a battlefield full of giants. If you must invest in AI, better to invest in a platform company that has already proven itself, rather than a startup still burning cash to prove itself."
