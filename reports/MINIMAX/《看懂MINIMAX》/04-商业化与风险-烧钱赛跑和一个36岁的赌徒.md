## A Race Against Time

MINIMAX's 2025 financial report poses a piece of arithmetic: annual loss of **$251 million**, annual revenue of **$79.04 million** — the loss is more than three times revenue. Revenue is doubling, but the absolute size of the loss is equally staggering. The clock on the IPO proceeds is ticking down.

This is not a predicament unique to MINIMAX — it is the life-or-death race every AI startup runs: can the revenue curve catch up with the loss curve before the cash runs out? The financial report delivers a set of contradictory signals:

| Good news | Bad news |
|--------|--------|
| Revenue growth of **158.9%** | Total revenue of only **$79.04 million** (about 570 million yuan) |
| Gross margin rose from 12.2% to **25.4%** | Adjusted net loss of **$251 million** (about 1.8 billion yuan) |
| Sales expenses down **40.3%** year over year | Full-year loss (including fair value changes) of **$1.872 billion** |
| ARR surpassed **$150 million** in February 2026 | Debt-to-asset ratio of **343.3%** (including preferred shares and other financial liabilities) |
| Open platform revenue growth of **297%** | Fierce price competition in the API market, very weak pricing power |

## I. Where the Money Comes From, Where It Goes

### Revenue Structure

| Revenue source | 2025 amount | Share | Growth rate |
|---------|-----------|------|------|
| AI-native products (Talkie/Hailuo AI, etc.) | **$53.08 million** | **67%** | +143.4% |
| Open platform (API/enterprise services) | **$25.96 million** | **33%** | +197.8% |

Consumer-facing products (mainly Talkie) remain the largest revenue source, but the enterprise-facing API is growing faster. If this trend continues, the enterprise side could overtake the consumer side as the primary revenue driver within 1-2 years.

As of the end of February 2026, ARR had surpassed **$150 million**, with monthly revenue of about **$12.5 million** — nearly **double** the 2025 monthly average. If this growth rate holds for the full year, 2026 revenue could reach **$150-200 million**.

### Cost Structure

| Cost item | 2025 | Change | Analysis |
|--------|--------|------|------|
| R&D cost | About **1%** of OpenAI's | — | Extreme cost efficiency |
| Sales and distribution expenses | **$51.9 million** | Down **40.3%** year over year | Growth driver shifting from marketing spend to organic growth |
| GPU compute cost | Not separately disclosed | — | The biggest cost black hole |

**Sales expenses down 40% while revenue is up 159%** — this is an extremely positive signal. It suggests MINIMAX's products are beginning to acquire users through **word of mouth and organic growth** rather than paid advertising. Starting in the second half of 2025, the growth driver shifted from "paying to buy users" to "the product attracting users on its own."

**Counter-argument**: A decline in sales expenses could also mean the company is scaling back its spending — not necessarily because it "doesn't need to spend," but because it "can't afford to spend." This needs to be validated against data from subsequent quarters.

### Cash Runway

| Metric | Value (estimate) |
|------|-----------|
| IPO proceeds | **HK$5.54 billion** (about $700 million) |
| Cumulative funding pre-IPO | Over **$1.5 billion** |
| 2025 adjusted net loss | **$251 million** |
| 2025 average monthly cash burn | About **$21 million/month** |
| Post-IPO cash reserves | Estimated **10-15 billion yuan** |
| Cash runway | Estimated **3-4 years** (assuming losses don't widen) |

A **3-4 year cash runway** means MINIMAX has a window of time — it doesn't need to be profitable by 2026, but it must show a clear path to profitability before 2028-2029.

**Key variable**: If revenue growth continues to double while costs stay controlled, the break-even point could arrive around **2028-2029**. But if the AI industry enters a new round of arms race (for example, even larger-scale model training), the cash burn rate could accelerate again.

## II. Three Paths to Commercialization

### Path One: Talkie's Overseas Subscriptions and Virtual Goods

**Current status**: Talkie is the largest revenue source, but its ARPU (average revenue per user) is extremely low.

| Comparison | Monthly ARPU (estimate) |
|------|-------------|
| Netflix | **~$15** |
| Spotify | **~$5** |
| Mobile games (top-tier titles) | **~$3-5** |
| **Talkie** | **~$0.5-1** |

The payment rate and payment depth of AI social products are far below those of games and streaming. Users come to Talkie to chat for free, and their willingness to pay is weak.

**Room for improvement**:
1. Launch higher-tier subscription plans (better AI characters, voice calls, video interaction)
2. Virtual goods and social currency (tipping AI characters, unlocking exclusive interactions)
3. Ad monetization (inserted between conversation turns)

**A rough calculation**: Even if MAU reaches **50 million** and ARPU rises to **$2/month**, Talkie's annual revenue would still be only about **$1.2 billion** — against a current market cap of HK$250 billion, that's still a PS ratio above 20x. Talkie alone cannot support the valuation.

### Path Two: The Open Platform API

**Current status**: The fastest-growing business line (+197.8%); new registered users in February 2026 were more than 4x the December 2025 figure.

The logic of API monetization:
1. M2.7's **multimodal capability** (text + voice + video) is a differentiating selling point
2. Speech-02's speech synthesis, **the world's best**, addresses real enterprise demand
3. Inference costs continue to fall (the M2 series has already dropped more than 50%), leaving room for margin improvement

**Core risk**: The API market is under pressure from DeepSeek's open-source, low-price strategy. Price wars mean: however good the product, it won't earn high margins.

### Path Three: Paid Video Generation

**Current status**: Hailuo AI has a large free user base, but paid conversion is still at an early stage.

Video generation is the **most certain** monetization track — content creators, marketing agencies, and film/TV production teams all have real willingness to pay. But competition is equally intense: Kling, Jimeng, and Runway are all chasing the same user base.

## III. Valuation — Expensive, But How Expensive?

As of May 2026, MINIMAX's key valuation metrics:

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Share price | **~HK$742** |
| Market cap | **~HK$250 billion** (about $32 billion) |
| 2025 revenue | **$79.04 million** |
| PS multiple (based on 2025 revenue) | **~400x** |
| 2026 ARR (annualized) | **~$150 million** |
| PS multiple (based on 2026 ARR) | **~213x** |
| 52-week high | **HK$1,330** |
| 52-week low | **HK$220** |

**A PS ratio of 200-400x is expensive by any measure.**

But AI companies' valuations can't be forced into a traditional PS framework. Here is a rough **reverse-engineered endgame valuation**:

| Scenario | Annual revenue in 5 years | Target net margin | Target PE | Market cap in 5 years | vs. current |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------|----------|---------|
| **Optimistic** (20% probability) | $3 billion | 15% | 30x | **$135 billion** | Upside of **4x** |
| **Neutral** (40% probability) | $1 billion | 5% | 20x | **$10 billion** | **Downside of 70%** |
| **Pessimistic** (40% probability) | $300 million | Still loss-making | — | **$2-3 billion** | **Downside of 90%** |

Weighting by the probabilities above, the expected market cap in 5 years is about **$32 billion**, which discounted back to today (assuming a 10% annual discount rate) is about **$20 billion** — below the current $32 billion market cap. In other words, from an expected-value standpoint, **the current valuation has already priced in future growth that hasn't happened yet.**

But expected-value analysis has limited relevance for AI companies — the distribution is **heavily right-skewed**. If you believe the probability of the optimistic scenario is higher (say 30% instead of 20%), the conclusion changes entirely.

**Core judgment**: The current valuation prices in a scenario where "everything goes right." If any single product line underperforms, the downside is enormous.

**Counter-argument**: The AI industry is still in its early stages — two years ago, no one could have predicted that ChatGPT would push OpenAI's valuation to $300 billion. If MINIMAX has a "ChatGPT moment" in some modality, the current valuation could turn out to be a low point instead.

## V. Six-Layer Risk Checklist

### Risk One: Ongoing Cash Burn (Severity: ★★★★★)

Adjusted annual loss of **$251 million**, burning **$21 million a month**. Although the IPO proceeds have replenished the balance sheet, if revenue growth falls short of expectations, another financing round may be needed within 3-4 years. Refinancing means dilution, and dilution means pressure on the share price.

### Risk Two: Crushed by the Giants (Severity: ★★★★★)

ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent hold a capital and traffic advantage of **more than 10x**. If the giants get serious about the tracks MINIMAX is pursuing (AI social, video generation), MINIMAX's first-mover advantage could vanish quickly.

### Risk Three: Copyright Litigation (Severity: ★★★★)

Copyright lawsuits from Disney and other Hollywood giants are not simply about paying damages — if a court issues an **injunction**, Hailuo AI could be barred from operating in the U.S. For a company that draws 70% of its revenue from overseas, this could be a fatal blow.

### Risk Four: Geopolitics (Severity: ★★★★)

Over **70%** of MINIMAX's revenue comes from overseas, and more than 50% of Talkie's users are in the U.S. If U.S.-China relations deteriorate further, Chinese AI companies' operations in the U.S. could face TikTok-style scrutiny. GPU chip import restrictions are also an ongoing risk.

### Risk Five: The AI Bubble Bursting (Severity: ★★★★)

Historical experience: **90%** of companies in any technological revolution ultimately die. If the AI industry bubble bursts, the valuations of unprofitable AI companies will be the first to come under pressure. MINIMAX currently trades at a PS ratio of 200-400x, leaving enormous downside.

### Risk Six: Technology Being Matched (Severity: ★★★)

AI technology iterates extremely fast — competitors could catch up within 6-12 months. MINIMAX's lead in voice and video is not an insurmountable moat.

## VI. How Investors Should View MINIMAX

### A Checklist Under the Value-Investing Framework

| Check item | Pass? | Notes |
|--------|-------|------|
| Can you understand the business? | ⚠️ | The AI industry changes too fast; the landscape 3 years from now is unpredictable |
| Does it have a durable competitive advantage? | ❌ | The moat is extremely shallow — weak brand, low switching costs, technical barriers easily matched |
| Is management honest and capable? | ⚠️ | First-rate technical ability, but insufficient commercialization experience, and a very short disclosure track record |
| Does the valuation have a margin of safety? | ❌ | PS ratio of 200-400x, seriously lacking margin of safety |
| Is free cash flow ample? | ❌ | Free cash flow is deeply negative |
| Will it be stronger in 10 years? | ⚠️ | Highly uncertain — independent survival probability of 25-35% |

**By traditional value-investing standards, MINIMAX fails.**

But the value-investing framework itself carries an inherent bias against early-stage tech companies — it is better suited to evaluating mature companies that already have a moat and stable cash flow. For a company like MINIMAX, a more appropriate framework is **"asymmetric risk-reward"**: if the optimistic scenario plays out, the return could be 4-10x; if the pessimistic scenario plays out, the loss could be 70-90%.

## VII. Series Summary — What MINIMAX Is

Across these four articles, MINIMAX emerges as this kind of company:

**It is the most distinctive Chinese AI startup.** A team of 385 people with an average age of 29 has built a product portfolio with the world's best voice synthesis, a top-tier global position in video generation, and top-ranked overall multimodal capability. R&D cost is just 1% of OpenAI's. Overseas revenue exceeds 70%. It took only four years to go from founding to IPO.

**But it faces the shared predicament of every AI startup.** An annual loss of 1.8 billion yuan, no moat, no pricing power, and giants that could crush it at any moment. A PS ratio of 200-400x prices in a scenario where "everything goes right" — leaving very little room for error.

**The core tension is this**: MINIMAX's **capability** is first-rate (technology, product, team efficiency), but its **circumstances** are brutal (cash burn, competition, geopolitics, bubble risk). First-rate capability in brutal circumstances does not necessarily translate into a good investment return.

To sum it up in one sentence:

> **MINIMAX is one of the Chinese AI startups with the highest probability of "surviving." But from "surviving" to "thriving," from "good company" to "good investment" — there remains enormous uncertainty in between.**

This uncertainty is not something an article can resolve for you. It requires time, data, and your own judgment.
