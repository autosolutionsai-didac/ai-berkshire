## 385 people, four fronts, each pushed into the global top ranks

OpenAI has 3,000 people, Google DeepMind has 2,000 people, and ByteDance's AI team numbers in the thousands. MINIMAX has only **385 people** — average age 29 — yet it has simultaneously pushed AI social, video generation, voice synthesis, and general-purpose large models into the industry's front rank on four fronts at once. Voice synthesis is number one globally, video generation is in the top global tier, and Talkie's overseas MAU has surpassed ten million.

This kind of per-person output has no comparable case. But the fact that "there's no comparable case" itself deserves scrutiny: is this truly elite execution, or is survivorship bias amplifying the results we're seeing? Let's break it down one by one.

## I. Talkie — the stealth champion of overseas AI social

### Product positioning

Talkie (overseas) and Xingye (domestic) are MINIMAX's AI role-play social products. Users can converse freely with AI characters, and can also create their own characters and set their personalities. It is not a tool-type AI (unlike ChatGPT, which answers questions) — it is **entertainment- and companionship-type AI**. Users come here not for productivity but to "make friends with AI."

### Key data

| Metric | Value | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Cumulative users | **147 million** | MiniMax 2025 annual report |
| Global MAU | **27.6 million** | MiniMax 2025 annual report (as of September 2025) |
| Overseas MAU | **11 million** (50%+ from the United States) | MiniMax prospectus |
| Daily active users | **Nearly 10 million** | MiniMax earnings call |
| Average daily usage time | **70 minutes** | MiniMax earnings call |
| 2025 revenue | **$18.75 million** (up 39% year over year) | MiniMax 2025 annual report (data as of September) |

**70 minutes of average daily usage time** — this number is remarkable. For comparison, TikTok's average daily usage time is around 55 minutes and Instagram's is around 30 minutes. If the data is credible, it indicates Talkie's user stickiness is extremely strong.

### Competitive landscape

| Product | Company | Current status |
|------|------|------|
| **Character.AI** | Absorbed by Google | First mover with a larger user base, but lost independence and its pace of innovation has slowed |
| **Talkie** | MINIMAX | The fastest-growing Chinese AI social product overseas, with rich character variety and an active community |
| **Chai** | Independent company | Smaller scale |
| **Janitor AI** | Independent company | Skews toward adult content; niche |

After Character.AI was absorbed by Google, its founding team returned to Google DeepMind, and product-level innovation has clearly slowed. This has given Talkie a window of opportunity.

**Core advantages**:
1. **User ecosystem** — UGC character creation forms a weak network effect: more characters attract more users, which attracts more creators
2. **Multimodal fusion** — Talkie's characters can have voice (based on Speech-02), something Character.AI lacks
3. **Overseas localization** — Gen-Z users in markets like the US, the Philippines, and the UK are highly active

**Core risks**:
1. **Retention across AI applications is generally a concern** — can engagement be sustained once novelty wears off?
2. **Extremely low ARPU** — willingness to pay for AI social products is far below that for gaming and streaming
3. **Google could re-engage at any time** — Character.AI's technology and user base still sit within Google
4. **Copyright and safety compliance** — Talkie has previously been removed from app stores in Japan and the United States

**Counter-argument**: the 70-minute average daily usage time may be skewed upward by a small number of heavy users. The real question isn't how large the MAU is, but how many people are willing to pay. Talkie's ARPU today may be only **$1-2/month** (estimate), far below Netflix's **$15/month**.

## II. Hailuo AI — China's contender in the video-generation race

### Product positioning

Hailuo AI is MINIMAX's AI video-generation tool, marketed overseas under the brand name Hailuo AI. Users input text or images and the tool generates video.

### Technical level

The latest **Hailuo 02** supports **native 1080p resolution** and adopts a Noise-aware Compute Redistribution (NCR) core architecture, placing it in the global top tier for generation speed, image quality, and physical realism.

| Product | Company | Image quality | Speed | User scale |
|------|------|------|------|---------|
| Sora | OpenAI | Top-tier | Slow | Limited by ChatGPT Plus subscription |
| Kling | Kuaishou | High | Fast | Millions of users (estimate) |
| **Hailuo 02** | **MINIMAX** | **High** | **Fast** | **Millions of users globally** |
| Jimeng | ByteDance | Medium-high | Fast | Growing rapidly |
| Runway Gen-3 | Runway | High | Medium | Overseas professional users |

Hailuo AI's **2025 revenue was $17.46 million** (as of September), making it MINIMAX's second-largest revenue source.

### The Disney copyright lawsuit — an obstacle that can't be avoided

In September 2025, Disney, Universal Pictures, and Warner Bros. jointly sued MINIMAX in US federal court in California, alleging that Hailuo AI infringed the copyrights of more than **50** film and television IPs during training and generation.

Key elements of the lawsuit:
- Plaintiffs sought damages of up to **$150,000** per infringed work, totaling up to **$7.5 million** across the 50 works
- Plaintiffs also sought an injunction to bar Hailuo AI from continuing the alleged infringement
- Plaintiffs pointed out that Hailuo AI already has content-filtering mechanisms (able to block violent and sexual content) but **chose not to block copyrighted content**

MINIMAX responded in its prospectus that, considering broader scenarios such as potential follow-on lawsuits, worst-case damages could reach **$75 million**, which is "proportionally limited" relative to the company's financial resources. (Note: this figure is a worst-case estimate from the prospectus, higher than the plaintiffs' $7.5 million claim ceiling in this specific case.)

**Sober analysis**: the damages amount itself isn't fatal, but the **risk of an injunction** is the key issue — if a court bars Hailuo AI from operating in the US, it would directly hit the overseas business. Furthermore, this lawsuit could prompt more copyright holders to file follow-on suits.

**Counter-argument**: being targeted by copyright lawsuits is almost a universal affliction across the AI industry — OpenAI, Stability AI, and Midjourney all face similar suits. Most likely this will ultimately be resolved via settlement or licensing fees, but the settlement amount and terms remain uncertain.

## III. Speech-02 — number one globally in voice synthesis

### Core achievements

MINIMAX's Speech-02 topped two globally authoritative voice-synthesis leaderboards simultaneously:

| Leaderboard | Rank | ELO score |
|------|------|---------|
| Artificial Analysis Speech Arena | **#1** | **1161** |
| Hugging Face TTS Arena | **#1 and #2** (sweeping both the HD and Turbo versions) | #1 |

On both leaderboards, Speech-02 **surpassed OpenAI's TTS and ElevenLabs** — with ElevenLabs previously recognized as the king of voice synthesis.

### Technical characteristics

- Voice-similarity fidelity reaches **99%**
- Priced at only **half** of ElevenLabs Flash V2.5, and **a quarter** of Multilingual V2
- Serves over **50,000 enterprises**

### Commercialization potential

Voice synthesis is a vertical track with **relatively high certainty**: audiobooks, podcasts, customer service, game voiceovers, video narration — every scenario has genuine willingness to pay. The global TTS market is approximately **$10-15 billion** (2025 estimate), growing at roughly **70% per year**.

**Speech-02 may be MINIMAX's most underappreciated asset** — it doesn't grab attention the way Talkie does, but its path to commercialization is clearer and its barriers to entry higher (voice synthesis requires deep accumulation of training data and fine-tuning expertise).

**Counter-argument**: although the voice-synthesis market is growing quickly, its ceiling is limited. And both OpenAI and Google are increasing investment in voice — how long can the global #1 position be maintained?

## IV. M2.7 large model — chasing OpenAI at 1% of the cost

### Core parameters

| Metric | M2.7 | Comparison |
|------|------|------|
| Architecture | MoE (Mixture of Experts) | Same technical path as DeepSeek-V3 |
| Total parameters | **230 billion** | — |
| Activated parameters | **10 billion** | Low inference cost |
| Context length | **200K tokens** | Roughly 400,000 Chinese characters |
| SWE-Pro score | **56.22%** | Close to GPT-5.3-Codex |
| VIBE-Pro score | **55.6%** | — |
| Terminal Bench 2 score | **57.0%** | — |
| R&D cost | Roughly **1%** of OpenAI's | MiniMax prospectus |

M2.7's most distinctive capability is **self-evolution** — during development, the model itself constructs complex skills within a reinforcement-learning harness, updates its own memory, and drives its own reinforcement learning, optimizing the learning process based on results. This is what MINIMAX calls the concept of "the first model to deeply participate in iterating on itself."

### Cost-performance advantage

M2.7's inference price is only **1/10 to 1/20** of Opus 4.6. Since early 2026, the inference compute cost of the M2 series has fallen by a further **more than 50%**.

In pure text ability, M2.7 is not the strongest — DeepSeek, the GPT-5 series, and Gemini 3 Pro still lead in complex reasoning. But M2.7's positioning isn't "strongest in pure text" — it's **best overall multimodal capability**: an integrated capability across text, voice, video, and music that currently no competing product fully matches.

### Open-platform data

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Enterprise customers and developers | **214,000**, spanning 100+ countries |
| 2025 open-platform revenue | **$25.96 million** (up 197.8% year over year) |
| New registered users, February 2026 | More than **4x** December 2025 |

The open platform (B2B API) is the fastest-growing business line and a potential major future revenue source.

**Counter-argument**: the API market is in the midst of a brutal price war. DeepSeek has pushed prices to extreme lows, and ByteDance's Doubao API call volume is already astronomical (average of 50 trillion tokens per day). MINIMAX's pricing power in the API market is extremely weak — it is being pushed along by the market.

## The mystery of 385-person efficiency: real prowess, or survivorship bias?

385 people, average age **29**, with **74%** in R&D roles (about 285 people), and an organizational structure with only **three levels**. With less than one-tenth the manpower of the giants, the company has produced the results above.

**Bull case**: high talent density, low communication cost, fast decision-making. Yan Junjie's CMU PhD background plus his experience as a SenseTime VP can attract top talent. A small team is more nimble than a large one.

**Bear case**: a small team has weak resilience to shocks — the departure of just a few core people could shake its foundation. As the AI race progresses, it increasingly depends on compute and data — neither of which a small team can compete on.
