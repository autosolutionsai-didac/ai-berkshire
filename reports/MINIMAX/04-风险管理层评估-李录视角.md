## II. Regulatory risk

### Risk matrix

| Risk type | Impact | Probability | Overall rating | Confidence |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|--------|
| China's generative AI regulations | High | High | **Severe** | High |
| Overseas data privacy (GDPR, etc.) | Medium | Medium | **Moderate** | Medium |
| US-China tech competition / chip restrictions | High | High | **Severe** | High |
| Hong Kong listing compliance | Low | Low | **Minor** | Medium |
| AI content-safety incidents | High | Medium | **Elevated** | Medium |

**China AI regulation**: The "Interim Measures for the Administration of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services" is already in force; MiniMax has completed its filing (confidence: high), but the regulatory direction could tighten further.

**Overseas data compliance**: Talkie's overseas operations must comply with GDPR and similar data-privacy laws. AI role-play/companionship products handle large volumes of user privacy data, and the potential risk should not be underestimated. Compare TikTok's scrutiny in the United States.

**US-China chip restrictions**: The most existential risk. Restricted supply of high-end NVIDIA GPUs directly affects the pace of model iteration and inference cost.

> "I pay very close attention to regulatory risk, because it tends to be uncontrollable and non-linear. A single policy can instantly change a company's fate."

**Giants crushing competition**: ByteDance's Doubao (fed by Douyin traffic), Baidu's Ernie (search + cloud), Alibaba's Tongyi (e-commerce + cloud) — the giants have more than 10x the capital and traffic advantage.

**Open-source substitution**: DeepSeek has proven that open-source models can achieve near-closed-source performance at extremely low cost. As large-model capability becomes increasingly commoditized, it will severely erode MINIMAX's underlying-model advantage.

> "What I care about is whether a company will still exist in 10 years, and whether it will be stronger than it is today. If an industry has many competitors, low barriers, and fast technological turnover, it is very likely a value-destroying industry."

## IV. Business risk

### Risk matrix

| Risk item | Severity | Likelihood | Overall |
|-------|--------|--------|------|
| Cash burn outpacing commercialization | Extremely high | High | **Fatal** |
| Resources spread across multiple product lines | High | High | **Severe** |
| Technology being matched or surpassed | High | Medium | **Elevated** |
| Poor user retention | Medium | High | **Elevated** |
| Loss of key talent | Medium | Medium | **Moderate** |

**The race between cash burn and commercialization**: The core risk. Large-model training costs run from tens of millions to over one hundred million dollars. MINIMAX's revenue — API usage fees, Talkie in-app purchases, Xingye subscriptions — remains small in scale. The IPO extended its runway, but if losses cannot be narrowed substantially within 2-3 years, it will face either continued dilutive financing or a cash crunch.

**Multi-product-line risk**: Simultaneously operating the MiniMax API, Talkie, Hailuo AI, and Xingye stretches the front lines too thin for a startup.

**User retention**: Retention rates for AI applications are generally a concern industry-wide. Engagement tends to fall sharply once the novelty wears off.

## VII. Long-term certainty analysis

### What will MINIMAX look like in ten years?

| Scenario | Probability (estimate) | Description |
|------|-------------|------|
| **Major success** | 10-15% | Becomes one of the leading players in China's AI application layer, with revenue in the tens of billions of yuan |
| **Modest success** | 20-25% | Gains a solid foothold in a niche segment, with revenue in the low billions of yuan |
| **Acquired** | 25-30% | Acquired by a giant, with team and technology absorbed |
| **Gradual decline** | 20-25% | Technology matched by competitors, commercialization unsuccessful, gradually marginalized |
| **Collapse / delisting** | 10-15% | Cash exhausted, company eventually shut down |

### What could disrupt its business model?

1. **Complete commoditization of large models**: The underlying model becomes a "utility," and the technical moat drops to zero
2. **Rise of AI-native applications from the giants**: ByteDance and Tencent embed AI features directly into social/short-video products
3. **AGI breakthrough**: One company achieves AGI first, potentially rendering all other AI companies obsolete overnight
4. **Sudden regulatory change**: Stringent AI regulation sharply raises compliance costs

### Can a small company survive the AGI era?

**Conclusion**: The probability of small-company survival in the AGI era is extremely low. MINIMAX's long-term survival most likely depends on:
- Building an irreplaceable user ecosystem in some application scenario before AGI arrives
- Being acquired by a giant
- AGI progress being slow enough that specialized models continue to hold value

> "In technological revolutions, the ultimate winner is often not the earliest innovator, but the company with the deepest moat. The key question is: who can build a durable competitive advantage amid technological change?"

## IX. Li Lu's final verdict

> "The essence of investing is discounting future cash flows. For a company like MINIMAX, future cash flows are highly uncertain — it is even uncertain whether the company will still exist in 10 years. This does not mean MINIMAX is a bad company; quite the opposite, its team may be among the best technically in China. But as a value investor, what I need is certainty, not possibility."

**Core conclusions:**

1. **Yan Junjie is a technical entrepreneur worthy of respect**, but a good entrepreneur does not equal a good investment target
2. **The risk is multi-layered and structural** — regulation, competition, cash burn, technological iteration, and the macro environment all stack on top of one another
3. **Fails the "long-term certainty" test** — the AI industry is highly uncertain, and a startup's odds of surviving among the giants are limited
4. **If one must invest in AI**, Li Lu would likely lean toward investing in the "shovel sellers" (NVIDIA, TSMC) or established-moat giants layering AI on top of their existing businesses
5. **The only counter-argument**: if the valuation is cheap enough and Talkie's overseas growth exceeds expectations, there may be an asymmetric risk/reward ratio — but this is closer to "speculation" than "investment"

> "In areas of extremely high uncertainty, the best investment strategy is often not to invest at all. Stay patient and wait for opportunities with higher certainty to appear. The cost of missing a good opportunity is far smaller than the cost of making a mistake."
