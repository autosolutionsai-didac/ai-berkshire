# PayPal vs. Ant Group: Similarities and Differences Between Two Payment Giants

> Analysis date: June 8, 2026
> This article does not constitute investment advice.

---

## Similarities

| Dimension | Shared Characteristics |
|------|---------|
| Origin | Both started as third-party payment services (PayPal → eBay ecosystem, Alipay → Taobao ecosystem) |
| Core function | Both solved the "buyer doesn't trust seller" trust problem — holding funds in escrow |
| Scale | Both dominate payments in their respective markets (PayPal 43% of global online payments, Alipay 36% of China) |
| Super-app ambitions | Both have tried to extend from payments into finance (credit, wealth management, insurance) plus lifestyle services |
| Regulatory pressure | Both have faced severe regulatory crackdowns (Ant's IPO was halted; PayPal faced an antitrust investigation by the UK's FCA) |
| Valuation compression | Both have shrunk sharply from their peaks (Ant fell from a $2 trillion valuation to $600 billion; PayPal fell from a $300 billion market cap to $36.4 billion) |

---

## Core Differences

| Dimension | PayPal | Ant Group |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Market** | Global (200+ countries), core markets in the US/Europe | Primarily China, international expansion still nascent |
| **Underlying architecture** | Built on top of the Visa/Mastercard card networks — a **"sub-landlord"** | Connects directly to bank accounts, bypassing card networks — a **"landlord"** |
| **Take-rate structure** | Charges 3.49% but must share about half with card networks and issuing banks, keeping only ~1.5% | Lower fee (~0.6%), but direct bank connection means nothing is shared with card networks |
| **Source of user stickiness** | Brand trust + buyer protection (functional level) | Social relationships + lifestyle scenarios + mini-program ecosystem (ecosystem level) |
| **Biggest threat** | Apple Pay (OS-level replacement) | WeChat Pay (encroachment via social scenarios; its share has already overtaken Alipay at 60%) |
| **Financial business** | Light involvement (BNPL, credit), actively scaling back | Deep involvement (Huabei, Jiebei, Yu'ebao, insurance), forced to scale back by regulators |
| **Profitability** | Net margin ~16%, free cash flow $5.6 billion | 2025 net profit RMB 15.3 billion (down 60% year over year), R&D spending of RMB 29 billion, a record high |
| **Listing status** | Listed on Nasdaq, market cap $36.4 billion | Not listed; IPO still stalled |
| **AI strategy** | Risk management + advertising + Fastlane checkout (application layer) | Ant AI assistant + Bailing large language model (infrastructure layer) |

## Revenue Funnel Comparison

### PayPal

```
Total payment volume $1.79 trillion
    │ × take rate 1.73%
Revenue $33.2 billion
    │ - card network/issuer/fraud costs $17.7 billion
Transaction margin dollars $15.5 billion
    │ - operating expenses $9.4 billion
Operating income $6.1 billion
    │ - taxes
Net income $5.2 billion → free cash flow $5.6 billion
```

Conversion rate: free cash flow / total payment volume = **0.31%**

### Ant Group (estimated, limited data)

```
Total payment volume ~RMB 118 trillion (2023 estimate, extrapolated from Alipay data)
    │ × take rate ~0.06% (far below PayPal's)
Revenue ~RMB 190 billion (2023 estimate)
    │ - operating costs
Net income RMB 15.3 billion (2025, sharply reduced by the regulatory overhaul and AI investment)
```

Conversion rate: net income / total payment volume = **~0.001%**

### The Key Difference

**PayPal's take rate (1.73%) is nearly 30 times Ant's** — not because PayPal is inherently better at making money, but because the two companies' underlying architectures are completely different:

- PayPal is built on top of Visa/Mastercard, and the card networks' fees themselves are already high (US credit card interchange fees run about 2%); PayPal adds another layer on top of that
- Ant connects directly to bank accounts, with no card-network intermediary, so the entire payment chain costs far less
- China's payments market has been trained by WeChat Pay and Alipay to expect "essentially free" usage, leaving extremely limited room for take rates

## The Most Fundamental Difference

**PayPal is a "value-added layer" on top of the card-network ecosystem; Ant is a "replacement layer" for the banking system.**

Every PayPal transaction has to travel over the Visa/Mastercard rails and pay a "toll." What it earns is the "trust plus convenience" premium it provides on top of the card networks. The problem is: when Apple Pay uses native OS capability to provide the same trust and better convenience, that premium gets squeezed.

Ant (Alipay) connects directly to users' bank accounts, without relying on card networks — China has no Visa/Mastercard-style intermediary layer. Its business model is more like "building its own railway" rather than "being a service provider on someone else's railway." But this also means it directly touches the core interests of banks and regulators, which is why it has been reined in far more harshly.

**PayPal's ceiling is set by how much the card networks let it earn; Ant's ceiling is set by how much the regulators let it do.**
