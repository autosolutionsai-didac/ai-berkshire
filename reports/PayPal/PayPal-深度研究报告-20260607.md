# PayPal (PYPL) Deep Research Report

> Analysis date: June 7, 2026
> Share price: ~$41 | Market cap: ~$36.4 billion | P/E (TTM): ~8.7x
> This article does not constitute investment advice.

---

## Core Judgment

PayPal is a payments giant with **$33.2 billion in annual revenue, $5.6 billion in free cash flow, and $15 billion of net cash** on its balance sheet — yet the market values it at only **$36.4 billion**, almost equal to one year of revenue. At 8.7x, its P/E is **one-fifth** of its 10-year median of 42x.

This is either extreme undervaluation, or the market pricing in a future of "slow decline."

**Conclusion: PayPal's current valuation sits in the gray zone between "value trap" and "historic-level undervaluation."** The core variable is whether Branded Checkout can stop declining — if it can, this is a once-in-a-decade buying opportunity; if it cannot, the low P/E is not a margin of safety but a warning of decline.

## I. Company Overview

### Who Is PayPal

PayPal was founded in 1998, acquired by eBay for $1.5 billion in 2002, and spun off as an independent public company in 2015. It is the world's largest online payments platform, with roughly **439 million active accounts** and more than **35 million merchants**, and it processed **$1.79 trillion** in total payment volume in 2025.

The core business breaks down into four segments:

| Business Line | Description | Margin |
|--------|------|--------|
| **Branded Checkout** | Consumers click the "PayPal button" to pay on a merchant's website | **Highest** (take rate ~3.5%) |
| **Unbranded Processing (Braintree/PSP)** | Back-end payment processing; merchants use PayPal's technology but consumers never see the PayPal brand | **Very low** (take rate ~2.9%, but thin margins) |
| **Venmo** | The largest P2P payment app in the US, with over 100 million monthly actives | Medium, rapidly improving |
| **Other** | Credit, BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later), the PYUSD stablecoin, advertising, etc. | Mixed |

## II. Moat Analysis

### 1. Two-Sided Network Effect ★★★☆ (moderately strong, but weakening)

PayPal has the largest two-sided network in the payments industry — 439 million active accounts plus 35 million merchants. The more consumers there are, the more merchants want to accept the PayPal button; the more merchants there are, the more consumers get accustomed to using it.

**But this network effect is being eroded:**
- Active accounts have fallen from a 2022 peak of 435 million to 388 million (on a basis excluding inactive users)
- Branded Checkout monthly actives are only about 142 million, just one-third of total active accounts
- Large numbers of users have become "zombie accounts" — they have an account but don't use it

**Counterargument:** The decline in active accounts is mainly the natural attrition of low-quality users acquired during the pandemic; the stickiness of core high-frequency users remains strong. Monthly active accounts of 225 million (+1%) is arguably the more meaningful metric.

### 2. Brand Trust ★★★★ (strong, but eroding among younger users)

PayPal is the payment method global consumers trust most on unfamiliar websites — seeing the PayPal button makes people willing to pay. This "trust premium" took more than 20 years to build, and includes:
- Buyer protection policies (refunds available if something goes wrong with a payment)
- No need to expose card details to the merchant
- Global brand recognition

**Key challenge:** Apple Pay lets a user complete a payment with a double-click of the side button plus Face ID — no redirect, no login, no password to remember. For younger users, this native experience is far superior to PayPal's. In 2026, Apple Pay is projected to have 90.5 million US users versus PayPal's 92.1 million — the gap has gone from a huge chasm to nearly even.

### 3. Data Assets and Risk Management ★★★★ (an underappreciated hidden moat)

- Every transaction is evaluated in real time using more than **500 data points**
- The AI system blocks roughly **$500 million** in fraudulent transactions per quarter
- More than 20 years of accumulated transaction data has produced machine-learning models that can spot patterns other risk systems miss
- This creates real value for merchants — lower decline rates, higher approval rates

### 4. Venmo ★★★ (large potential, but monetization still early)

- Monthly actives exceed 100 million, with extremely high brand recognition among young Americans
- 2025 revenue was approximately $1.7 billion (+20%), the fifth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth
- Debit card usage grew 60%, and "Pay with Venmo" transaction volume grew 34%
- Transitioning from a "money-losing P2P tool" to a "monetizable commerce platform"

**However**, $1.7 billion in revenue is still only 5% of PayPal's total revenue — not yet enough to change the overall picture.

### 5. Pricing Power ★★ (weak, and declining)

PayPal's take rate has been trending down consistently:
- Branded Checkout take rate is about 3.5% (high, but growth has stalled)
- Braintree take rate is about 2.9% (low, and still being pushed down further to win large clients)
- Overall take rate fell 4 basis points year over year to 1.73%

**The core contradiction: the fastest-growing business (Braintree) has the lowest margin, while the highest-margin business (Branded Checkout) has the slowest growth.** This is a structural "growth dilutes profit" dilemma.

### Moat Summary

| Dimension | Rating | Trend |
|------|------|------|
| Two-sided network effect | ★★★☆ | Narrowing (declining activity) |
| Brand trust | ★★★★ | Holding steady but slipping (losing younger users) |
| Data/risk management | ★★★★ | Solid |
| Venmo | ★★★ | Widening (monetization accelerating) |
| Pricing power | ★★ | Declining (take rate trending down) |
| **Overall** | **★★★** | **Slowly narrowing overall** |

## III. Competitive Landscape

### Online Payments Market Overview

| Competitor | Market Position | Threat to PayPal |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| **Apple Pay** | Native payment within the iOS ecosystem, 55% share of US mobile wallets | **Biggest threat** — OS-level integration that bypasses the PayPal button |
| **Stripe** | Developer-friendly API-based payment platform, valued at $72B | High — continues to lead in technical capability and the SMB market |
| **Adyen** | Payments platform for large multinational enterprises | Medium-high — competes directly with Braintree for enterprise clients |
| **Shopify Payments** | Vertically integrated payments for e-commerce merchants | Medium — displaces PayPal within the Shopify ecosystem |
| **Block (Square)** | Offline POS + Cash App | Medium — competes in offline small-merchant and P2P segments |
| **Visa/Mastercard** | Card network rails | Complex — both an infrastructure provider and a competitor for the payment entry point |
| **Alipay/WeChat Pay** | Dominant in the Chinese market | Low — limited overlap in core markets |

### Key Competitive Data

| Metric | PayPal | Apple Pay | Stripe |
|------|--------|---------|--------|
| Online payments market share | **43-45%** | ~14% | ~18-20% |
| Annual transaction volume | $1.79T | ~$8.7T (including offline) | Undisclosed |
| Valuation/market cap | $36.4B | — | $72B |
| Growth rate | +7% (TPV) | High double digits | High double digits |

**PayPal remains the #1 player in online payments, but its growth rate lags far behind its rivals. This is a "winning today, losing the trend" position.**

## IV. Strategic Turnaround: Can the New CEO Save PayPal?

### CEO Turnover Turmoil

| Period | CEO | Background |
|------|-----|------|
| 2015-2023 | Dan Schulman | PayPal veteran, grew users to over 400 million, but strategy lost focus late in his tenure |
| Sept. 2023-Feb. 2026 | Alex Chriss | Parachuted in from Intuit, pushed a margin-first strategy, raised ROIC from 9% to 11.5% |
| Feb. 2026-present | Enrique Lores | Former CEO of HP Inc., **zero experience in payments** |

**Three CEOs in two and a half years is itself a major risk signal.** The board's assessment of Chriss was that "the pace of change and execution fell short of expectations," but margins did improve under his tenure. The new CEO, Lores, comes from the printer industry, and the market is highly skeptical of this move.

### Key Products of the Alex Chriss Era

| Product | Progress | Potential |
|------|------|------|
| **Fastlane one-click checkout** | Conversion rate above 80%, 50% higher than standard checkout; checkout speed up 32% | High — doesn't require the user to have a PayPal account, can capture incremental share |
| **PPCP (merchant one-stop solution)** | Integrated payments + commerce tools for small and mid-size merchants | Medium |
| **PayPal Ads** | Launched in early 2026, an advertising platform built on 25 years of transaction data | High — high margin; real purchase data is a unique advantage |
| **PYUSD stablecoin** | Market cap jumped from $600 million to $4.1 billion, now live in 70 markets | Medium — scaling fast but profit contribution uncertain |

### The Core Contradiction Facing the Turnaround

Chriss's direction was correct — shifting from chasing user counts to chasing earnings quality. But **Branded Checkout growth fell from 6% to 1% during his tenure** — the most critical business dropped the ball at the most critical time. The board replaced him as a result, but the replacement understands payments even less, and the problem could get worse.

**The $20 billion buyback authorization equals 55% of the market cap.** If PayPal keeps buying back at the current pace ($6 billion/year), it could retire roughly 50% of shares outstanding within three years. This is an extremely aggressive capital-return program — it either signals that management believes the stock is severely undervalued, or that they can't find better growth investments.

## VII. Valuation Analysis

### Multi-Angle Valuation

| Valuation Method | Assumption | Valuation | Implied Share Price |
|---------|------|------|---------|
| **Forward P/E** | 2026 EPS of $5.35 x 12x P/E | $64.4B | ~$66 |
| **Forward P/E** | Same, x 15x P/E | $80.5B | ~$82 |
| **FCF discount** | $5.6B FCF at a 10% yield | $56.0B | ~$57 |
| **FCF discount** | $5.6B FCF at an 8% yield | $70.0B | ~$72 |
| **Sum-of-the-parts** | Branded PayPal $30B + Braintree $10B + Venmo $8B + other $5B | ~$53B | ~$54 |
| **Buyback-driven** | $6B in annual buybacks, shares outstanding down 50% after 3 years, EPS doubles to $10+ x 10x P/E | — | ~$50-60 (in 3 years) |

### Three Scenarios

| Scenario | Assumption | Fair Valuation | Implied Share Price | Upside/Downside |
|------|------|---------|---------|----------|
| **Bull** | Branded Checkout recovers to 5% growth + Venmo accelerates + advertising contributes | $70-80B | $72-82 | **+75-100%** |
| **Base** | Branded Checkout grows in the low single digits + margins stabilize + buybacks drive EPS growth | $50-60B | $51-61 | **+24-49%** |
| **Bear** | Branded Checkout keeps losing share + margins decline + payments commoditize | $30-35B | $31-36 | **-12 to -24%** |

### Key Judgment

**The direction of Branded Checkout decides everything.** If Branded Checkout can recover to 3-5% growth on the back of new products like Fastlane, a 12-15x P/E is reasonable for PayPal, implying $60-80 per share. If Branded Checkout continues to slide toward zero or negative growth, the current 8.7x P/E may not be the bottom.

## IX. Investment Conclusion

### The Core Contradiction

PayPal is a company with **strong earnings power but uncertain growth prospects**. It generates $5.6 billion in free cash flow every year, holds $15 billion of net cash, and has a $20 billion buyback authorization — these are hard strengths. But Branded Checkout growth has fallen to 1-2%, it has had three CEOs in two and a half years, and Apple Pay is structurally eroding its business — these are hard wounds.

### Conditions Under Which It's Worth Buying

1. You believe Branded Checkout can stabilize (Fastlane is the key catalyst)
2. You're willing to wait 2-3 years for buybacks to drive EPS growth
3. You treat Venmo and the advertising business as a free option
4. You can accept the execution risk of a new CEO

### Conditions Under Which It's Not Worth Buying

1. You believe payments are commoditizing and PayPal's pricing power will keep declining
2. You believe Apple Pay will replace most online PayPal checkout within 5-10 years
3. You have no confidence in the governance of a company that has swapped CEOs three times in two and a half years
4. You believe 8.7x P/E is not undervaluation but the market pricing in long-term decline

### In One Sentence

> **PayPal is like a dam that's springing leaks — the dam body itself ($33.2 billion in revenue, $5.6 billion in cash flow) remains solid, but the cracks (Branded Checkout deceleration, management turmoil, Apple's competition) are widening. 8.7x P/E is the market's repair price: if the cracks can be sealed, this price is extremely cheap; if they can't, the dam will eventually be worn away.** Michael Burry built his position at $49, betting on the former; the market's fall to $41 shows most people are betting on the latter.
