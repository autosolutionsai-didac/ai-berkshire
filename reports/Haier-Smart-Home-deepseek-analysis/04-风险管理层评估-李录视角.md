## II. Management Assessment (Li Huagang vs. Haier Management)

### 2.1 Circle-of-Competence Analysis ★★★★☆ (4 stars)

**Supporting evidence:**
- **Diversified business experience:** Li Huagang has worked within the Haier system for over 25 years, holding multiple key positions, and is familiar with the entire value chain across white goods, black goods (consumer electronics), and small appliances
- **International outlook:** has led Haier's overseas business expansion, with hands-on experience in the European, American, and Southeast Asian markets
- **Digital transformation:** has driven Haier's smart-home strategy, understanding the application of IoT and AI technology in traditional appliances

**Counter-evidence:**
- **Execution risk in the smart-home strategy:** the Sanyiniao business has absorbed enormous investment, but its profit model remains unclear
- **Traditional appliance-manufacturing DNA:** management's background is heavily weighted toward appliance manufacturing, which may create cognitive limitations in the shift toward software and service-oriented models

### 2.2 Integrity Assessment ★★★★★ (5 stars)

**Supporting evidence:**
- **30 years without a major financial scandal:** since Haier Group's listing in 1994, audit reports have consistently carried a standard unqualified opinion
- **Stable dividend policy:** the average payout ratio over the past 5 years has exceeded 50%, reaching 55% in 2025
- **Transparent related-party transactions:** transactions with other Haier Group subsidiaries are disclosed in detail in the annual report

**Risk to watch:**
- **Complex ownership structure:** Haier Group controls Haier Smart Home through a multi-layer holding structure, which may pose an ultimate-controlling-party risk

### 2.3 Strategic Vision Score ★★★☆☆ (3 stars)

| Strategic direction | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Comparative assessment |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Core strategy | Smart-home ecosystem (Sanyiniao) | Digital smart manufacturing | Haier leans toward the consumer end, Midea toward the manufacturing end |
| Technology investment | Focused on IoT and scenario experience | Focused on industrial internet and robotics | Haier is more aggressive, Midea more pragmatic |
| Internationalization path | Own-brand expansion abroad | Acquisition-led integration + localized operations | Haier emphasizes brand more, Midea emphasizes efficiency more |

**Li Lu's commentary:** "True strategy is not chasing the trend of the moment, but finding the underlying needs that never change. Haier's bet on the smart home is forward-looking, but it needs to answer: do consumers really need a fully connected, internet-of-everything refrigerator?"

## V. Business Risk Assessment

### 5.1 New-Business Loss Analysis: Sanyiniao ★★☆☆☆ (high risk)

**What the data reveals:**
- **Scale of investment:** cumulative investment of over RMB 10 billion from 2023 to 2025
- **Profit status:** still in a loss-making phase, with breakeven not expected until 2027
- **Strategic risk:** consumer acceptance of the smart home is below expectations, and the repurchase rate for scenario-based bundles is only 15%

**Li Lu's warning:** "Investing in new businesses is like flipping a coin — heads is growth, tails is losses. The key is controlling the size of the coin."

**Li Lu's perspective:** "Ten years from now, Haier's certainty will not lie in whether it becomes a tech company, but in whether it can defend its throne in refrigerators and washing machines."

### 8.2 Business-Model Disruption Risk

1. **Technological disruption:** the probability of 3D-printed appliances (manufactured directly at home) is low (<5%)
2. **Change in consumption habits:** the probability of a subscription-based appliance-sharing economy is moderate (20%)
3. **Supply-chain restructuring:** the probability of localization plus flexible manufacturing disrupting the current model is high (40%)

**The single largest source of risk:** **subscription-based appliance leasing.** If Xiaomi were to launch a "RMB 199/month whole-home appliance" subscription service, it would directly disrupt Haier's sales model.

### 10.2 Core Conclusions

**1. The single largest risk point: the Sanyiniao smart-home business** (weight 25%)
- Enormous investment (cumulative over RMB 10 billion), profit model unvalidated
- Faces ecosystem competition from Xiaomi and Huawei, an area outside Haier's traditional strengths
- If it fails, it will significantly drag down the company's overall profitability

**2. The single largest advantage: the Casarte high-end brand moat** (weight 20%)
- Gross margin above 35%, significantly higher than the industry average
- Holds over 40% share in the RMB 10,000+ refrigerator and washing machine market
- Firmly entrenched in consumer mindshare, difficult to disrupt in the short term

**3. Key indicators to monitor (Li Lu perspective):**
- **Sanyiniao per-store profitability model:** when will individual stores turn profitable?
- **Overseas business gross margin:** can it rise from 22% to 25%?
- **Copper/steel price hedging capability:** is the raw-material cost-control mechanism improving?
- **Change in Midea's market share:** is Midea eroding Haier's position in its core categories (refrigerators, washing machines)?

### 10.3 Investment Recommendation Framework

**Suitable investor types:**
- ✅ **Value investors:** given the payout ratio (52%) and low valuation (P/E 9.9x), a moderate allocation is warranted
- ⚠️ **Growth investors:** the smart-home story carries risk; a wait-and-see approach is advisable
- ❌ **Thematic investors:** not suitable for those chasing tech-trend concepts

**Position sizing recommendation:** as a defensive allocation, no more than 5% of the portfolio. If the Sanyiniao business's profit model is validated, this could be raised to 10%.

**Final warning:** Haier stands at a crossroads, transitioning from a "traditional appliance giant" to a "smart-home services provider." The probability of this transition succeeding is roughly 30%, versus a 70% probability of failure. Investors need to judge for themselves whether they are investing in a steady appliance company or betting on an uncertain tech-transformation story.

**Li Lu's words:** "The most dangerous thing in investing is not what you don't know, but not knowing what you don't know. Does Haier's management truly understand the underlying logic of the Internet of Things? That is our biggest unknown."
