# Midea's Dominance, Gree's Specialization, Xiaomi's Disruption

## Midea's revenue is 51% higher, net profit 125% higher — and the gap is still widening

The three white-goods majors appear to be racing on the same track, but in reality they are running three completely different paths. Midea is an all-category efficiency machine, Gree is a profit cow built on a single air-conditioning product, and Haier is the company with the deepest global brand portfolio. Three paths, three valuations, three risk profiles.

This piece answers a bigger question: **where does Haier stand on the overall chessboard of the white-goods industry?**

---

## I. The harsh reality of the white-goods industry: the era of volume growth is over

Let's start with an uncomfortable fact: **China's white-goods market has exited its era of incremental growth.**

A few key figures:
- Refrigerator ownership among urban Chinese households: over **100 units per 100 households** — meaning at least one per household, already saturated
- Washing machine ownership: over **100 units per 100 households** — likewise saturated
- Air-conditioner ownership: approximately **150 units per 100 households** — near saturation (1-2 units per household)
- Size of China's white-goods market: approximately **800 billion yuan**, growing **3-5%** annually
- Size of the global white-goods market: approximately **$500 billion**, growing **3-4%** annually

Sources: Euromonitor International, industry research reports

Once ownership of major categories exceeds 100 units per 100 households, incremental demand is close to zero, and growth can only come from two things:

1. **Replacement demand** (trading in old units for new): currently accounts for **~66%** of demand
2. **Product upgrading** (buying more expensive products): the premiumization trend

This means the white-goods industry has shifted from a "growth industry" to a "value industry" — the investment logic has switched from "growth" to "**earnings certainty + dividends**."

**Counterpoint**: overseas emerging markets still have room for incremental growth. Middle East & Africa grew +56%, South Asia +23%, Southeast Asia +20% (source: Haier's 2025 annual report). The rise of the global middle class is a long-term trend — you can't look at China alone.

## II. The three white-goods giants: who's strong, who's weak

### Full 2025 comparison

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Gree Electric |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Revenue** | 302.3 billion yuan (+5.7%) | **458.5 billion yuan (+12.1%)** | 191.2 billion yuan (-0.6%) |
| **Net profit** | 19.55 billion yuan (+4.4%) | **43.95 billion yuan (+14.0%)** | 28.67 billion yuan (-10.9%) |
| **Net margin** | 6.67% | 9.75% | **~15%** |
| **ROE** | 16.98% | 19.70% | **~21%** |
| **Overseas revenue share** | **51.1%** | 43% | 18% |
| **Expense ratio** | ~19% | ~15.5% | **~8-10%** |
| **P/E ratio** | about 10x | about 13.5x | **about 7-8x** |

Source: the three companies' 2025 annual reports

### Core profile of each company

**Midea Group: the king of efficiency**

Midea is the "hexagonal warrior" of the white-goods industry — leading across the board in scale, efficiency, growth rate, and category coverage.

- Revenue is **51.7%** higher than Haier's, net profit is **124.7%** higher
- Net margin of **9.75%** is **1.46x** Haier's
- Expense ratio of **15.5%** is 3.5 percentage points lower than Haier's
- Growth rate of **12.1%** far exceeds Haier's 5.7%
- Also has a B2B second curve (building technology / robotics / new energy) contributing **25.6%** of revenue

Midea's edge isn't in any single dimension but in a **systemic efficiency advantage**. Its T+3 supply chain model, digital management, and channel efficiency are each a little more efficient than Haier's at every step — and that accumulates into a 3-percentage-point gap in net margin.

**Counterpoint**: Midea's brand premium power lags Haier's. In the premium market above 10,000 yuan, COLMO's share is far behind Casarte's. Midea is more of a "volume player" — it does everything reasonably well, but lacks a particularly deep moat.

**Gree Electric: the specialist**

Gree is an extreme case — pushing a single category (air conditioners, **~70%** of revenue) to the extreme.

- Net margin of **~15%** is the highest of the three, 2.25x Haier's
- Expense ratio of **~8-10%** is the lowest of the three — because it only makes air conditioners, management is simple
- But revenue fell **-0.6%** and net profit fell **-10.9%** — growth has already peaked
- P/E ratio of only **7-8x**, cheaper even than Haier — the market is pricing in "single-category risk + growth ceiling"

Gree's profit efficiency is extremely high, but its growth difficulty is equally obvious. With the white-goods industry already in a stock-replacement phase, relying on air conditioners alone gives it a clearly visible ceiling.

**Counterpoint**: Gree's air-conditioner moat is the deepest of the three. Its distributor network is tightly bound, and Gree plus Midea together hold **~70%** of the air-conditioner market, a stable duopoly. Gree's problem isn't its moat — it's its lack of growth space.

### Haier Smart Home's "awkward position"

Looking at the three side by side, Haier sits in a **delicate position**:

- **Less comprehensive than Midea**: behind on scale, efficiency, growth rate, and category coverage
- **Not as cheap as Gree**: P/E of 10x vs. Gree's 7-8x, while Gree's net margin is more than double Haier's
- **But has unique strengths**: the deepest globalization, the strongest premium brand, near-monopoly positions in refrigerators and washing machines

If we summarize the investment logic of the three companies in one line each:

| Company | Buying it means buying |
|------|-------------|
| Midea | The best management efficiency and the most comprehensive competitiveness |
| Gree | An air-conditioner oligopoly's high margins and high dividends |
| Haier | Global footprint and premiumization potential — but you must accept an efficiency discount |

## III. By segment: where Haier is strong, where it's weak

### Haier's "iron throne": refrigerators and washing machines

| Category | Haier's share | Runner-up | Moat strength |
|------|---------|--------|-----------|
| Refrigerators (domestic) | **~47.7%** | Midea 15-18% | Extremely strong — a 30-percentage-point gap |
| Washing machines (domestic) | **~47.4%** | Midea 25-28% | Strong — but Midea is catching up |

Source: Euromonitor International (estimate)

In the refrigerator market, Haier's share is **3x** the runner-up's — this kind of overwhelming lead is extremely rare in any consumer goods industry. Three reasons:

1. **Brand mindshare**: the first brand Chinese consumers think of when buying a refrigerator is Haier
2. **Technology accumulation**: proprietary technologies such as full-space fresh-keeping and magnetic-levitation compressors
3. **Channel depth**: 5,000+ offline service centers, an installation and repair network covering the whole country

**Counterpoint**: growth in the refrigerator market has slowed to near zero (+1.11%), and the value of holding the #1 share is being diluted by "the market not growing." Defending the top spot is easy; extracting growth from it is hard.

### Haier's "soft spot": air conditioners

| Category | Gree's share | Midea's share | Haier's share |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Air conditioners (domestic) | **35-40%** | **30-35%** | **15-18%** |

Source: industry research reports (estimate)

Air conditioners are the largest single-product market in the white-goods industry (domestic market size **~500 billion yuan**), but Haier is a distant third here, trailing the top two by a wide margin. Gree plus Midea together hold **~70%**, an extremely stable duopoly.

Haier's struggle in the air-conditioner market is structural:
- Gree relies on a deeply bound distributor network, giving it an extremely high channel barrier
- Midea uses scale and efficiency to wage price wars that Haier struggles to match
- Haier's air conditioners haven't built differentiated recognition — when consumers think of air conditioners, they think of Gree first, then Midea

**What does this mean?** In the largest growth market within white goods, Haier has no pricing power. This is one of the core reasons the market discounts Haier.

### Haier's "gap": small appliances

| Category | Midea's share | Haier's share |
|------|---------|---------|
| Small appliances (domestic) | **25-30%** | **5-8%** |

Source: industry research reports (estimate)

Midea's small-appliance business (rice cookers, microwaves, electric fans, vacuum cleaners, etc.) contributes stable cash flow with relatively high gross margins. Haier, meanwhile, is **essentially absent** from small appliances.

This isn't a fatal weakness, but it means Haier lacks a "stabilizer" — when major appliances face headwinds, there's no small-appliance cash flow to cushion the blow.

## IV. The biggest wild card: Xiaomi

Most reports analyzing the white-goods industry only discuss the three giants — Midea, Gree, Haier. But if you look only at the traditional players, you'll miss a change that's already underway: **Xiaomi is entering the white-goods market as an "ecosystem player."**

A few key figures:
- Xiaomi air conditioner's 2025 online market share: **10.8%** (third in the industry)
- Share of Xiaomi air-conditioner users who use the smart connectivity features: **70%**
- Xiaomi's Wuhan factory: a self-built plant shedding its OEM-label past and moving into self-manufacturing

Source: industry research reports

Xiaomi's playbook is entirely different from traditional appliance makers':

1. **Doesn't monetize hardware**: air conditioners can be sold cheaply, with profit coming from ecosystem services
2. **Ecosystem lock-in**: users who buy a Xiaomi air conditioner are more inclined to buy a Xiaomi washing machine or refrigerator — because the Mi Home app offers one-stop control
3. **Data flywheel**: a 70% smart-connectivity usage rate means Xiaomi is accumulating large volumes of user behavior data to optimize products and recommendations

**The threat to Haier**: once Xiaomi's "ecosystem stickiness" reaches scale — that is, once users become unwilling to switch brands because of smart interconnectivity — the traditional appliance makers' "single-product competition" model will face a fundamental challenge.

**Counterpoint (bullish)**: appliances are a heavy-asset, heavy-manufacturing, heavy-channel industry. Xiaomi currently only has meaningful share in the online air-conditioner channel, with almost no offline presence. The technology barriers for refrigerators and washing machines are far higher than for air conditioners, and Xiaomi is unlikely to shake Haier's position in refrigerators and washing machines in the short term.

**Counterpoint (bearish)**: don't underestimate Xiaomi's long-term threat. Xiaomi's user base skews younger, and in 5-10 years, once this cohort becomes the primary appliance-buying demographic, brand preferences could shift fundamentally. Haier's Three-Wing Bird ("Sanyiniao") smart ecosystem currently lags Xiaomi Mi Home's compatibility and user experience.

## V. Growth space: where's the ceiling

### Domestic market: stock replacement is king

- China's white-goods market is approximately **800 billion yuan**, growing **3-5%** annually
- Growth is mainly driven by the "trade old for new" policy (a new round of policy implemented in 2025)
- The premiumization trend is clear — Casarte benefits the most
- But the long-cycle downturn in real estate is a persistent headwind

**Haier's opportunity**: stock replacement + premium upgrading + channel penetration into lower-tier markets
**Haier's risk**: real-estate downturn + policy pulling forward future demand + Midea continuing to erode refrigerator/washing-machine share

### Overseas markets: emerging markets are the biggest incremental opportunity

| Region | Growth rate | Haier's position |
|------|------|---------|
| Middle East & Africa | **+56%** | Fast growth |
| South Asia | **+23%** | #1 in Pakistan for 10 consecutive years |
| Southeast Asia | **+20%** | Leading sales in Vietnam and Thailand |
| North America | **+0.4%** | #1 by share but growth stalled |
| Europe | Low single digits | #1 among Chinese white-goods companies |

Source: Haier Smart Home's 2025 annual report

Emerging-market growth is Haier's biggest growth engine over the next 3-5 years. The rise of the global middle class is driving demand for appliances, and Haier established an early presence in these markets, giving it a first-mover advantage.

**But beware**: profit margins in emerging markets are typically lower (they require large upfront investment), and the competitive landscape is unstable. High growth ≠ high profit.

### Second curve: smart homes and enterprise business

Haier's "second curve" includes: the Three-Wing Bird scenario brand, smart buildings (revenue exceeded **10 billion yuan** in 2024), large health, robotics, and more.

**But compared with Midea**: Midea's B2B second curve (building technology + robotics + new energy) already accounts for **25.6%** of revenue and is growing faster than its traditional appliance business. Haier's second curve is under **10%** of revenue — a clear gap.

## VI. Overall assessment of the competitive landscape

### Haier's competitive-position matrix

| Dimension | Rating | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Refrigerator market | **Absolute leader** | ~48% share, 3x the runner-up |
| Washing machine market | **Leading but threatened** | ~47% share, Midea is catching up |
| Premium market | **Leading** | Casarte has a mindshare monopoly |
| Air-conditioner market | **Clearly weak** | Share <20%, Gree-Midea duopoly |
| Small-appliance market | **Essentially absent** | 5-8% share |
| Globalization | **Leading** | The only Chinese appliance company with overseas revenue above half |
| Efficiency/margin | **Clearly behind** | Net margin only two-thirds of Midea's |
| Smart ecosystem | **First mover but unproven** | Three-Wing Bird has heavy investment, small output |

### One key judgment: is the gap with Midea widening or narrowing?

This is **the most important competitive question** when investing in Haier.

Look at the data:

| Metric | 2021 gap | 2025 gap | Trend |
|------|-----------|-----------|------|
| Revenue gap | Midea +51% | Midea +51.7% | **Roughly flat** |
| Net profit gap | Midea +118% | Midea +124.7% | **Widening** |
| Net margin gap | 2.69 pct | 3.08 pct | **Widening** |
| ROE gap | 6.80 percentage points | 2.72 percentage points | **Narrowing** |

Source: the two companies' 2021-2025 annual reports

**Conclusion**: the revenue gap is roughly stable, but the **net profit gap and net margin gap are widening** — Midea is pulling further ahead on profit efficiency. The only piece of good news is that the ROE gap is narrowing, but this is mostly because Midea's ROE fell from 24% to 20% (a larger denominator), not because Haier's ROE is improving.

**Counterpoint (bullish)**: a widening gap doesn't mean Haier is deteriorating — Haier's growth is real (revenue and net profit have both grown for five consecutive years), it's just that Midea is growing faster. Haier's strengths (globalization, premiumization) and Midea's strengths (efficiency, category breadth) are different dimensions and shouldn't be simplistically compared.

**Counterpoint (bearish)**: capital markets ultimately price profit growth and profit efficiency. If Haier's net margin remains persistently below Midea's and the gap keeps widening, the market will keep assigning Haier a lower valuation multiple. The discount of a 10x P/E versus 13.5x isn't a market mistake — it's the market pricing in "the widening efficiency gap."
