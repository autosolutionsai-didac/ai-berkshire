# Globalization, Brand, and Channels — How Haier Makes Money

> "Understanding Haier Smart Home" series · Part 02 · Business model
> Reading time: approximately 15 minutes

---

## Overseas revenue is half the total, but overseas profit is far less than half

Half of Haier's revenue comes from overseas, but overseas net margin is less than half of the domestic figure. Globalization has earned Haier the title of "world's #1 by scale," but it has come at a cost: trading profit for scale.

This piece breaks it down: **where does the money come from, how good is the quality of the earnings, and how deep is the moat?**

---

## I. Where revenue comes from: walking on two legs

Haier Smart Home's **302.3 billion yuan** of 2025 revenue can be broken down along two main dimensions.

### By product: refrigerators and washing machines are the base, air conditioners are the weak spot

| Product line | 2024 revenue (billion yuan) | Growth | Market position |
|--------|----------------|------|---------|
| **Refrigerators/freezers** | 83.6 | +2.0% | #1 globally, ~47.7% domestic share |
| **Washing machines** | 63.3 | +3.0% | #1 globally, ~47.4% domestic share |
| **Air conditioners** | 49.6 | +7.6% | #3 domestically, 15-18% share |
| **Water heaters** | — | — | Leading domestic share |
| **Kitchen appliances/other** | — | — | Smaller share, highly competitive |

Source: Haier Smart Home's 2024 annual report, Euromonitor International (market shares are estimates)

**Key finding**: refrigerators and washing machines together contribute about **50%** of Haier's domestic revenue, and in both categories Haier's share is close to half — this is Haier's deepest moat. But growth in these core categories has already slowed to near-stagnation (refrigerators +2.0%, washing machines +3.0%), reflecting that the domestic white-goods market has entered a stock-replacement phase.

**Counterpoint**: the refrigerator/washing-machine share was won by "defending," not by "attacking" for growth. In the largest single-product market — air conditioners (domestic market size **~500 billion yuan**) — Haier's share is under 20%, squeezed by both Gree and Midea. This means Haier is the weaker player in the track with the largest incremental opportunity.

### By region: overseas surpasses domestic for the first time

| Region | 2025 revenue share | Growth characteristics |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **China** | 48.9% | Growth of **3-5%**, affected by the real-estate cycle |
| **North America (GE Appliances)** | ~26% | Grew only **0.4%** in 2025, under significant tariff pressure |
| **Europe (Candy + Haier)** | ~15% | Steady growth; Candy finally turned profitable after 5 years |
| **South Asia/Southeast Asia/Middle East & Africa** | ~10% | **High growth**: South Asia +23%, Middle East & Africa +56% |

Source: Haier Smart Home's 2025 annual report (some figures are analyst estimates)

In 2025, Haier's overseas revenue surpassed its domestic revenue for the first time, reaching a **51.1%** share. Among A-share appliance companies, this is unique to Haier.

**But here's a critical issue**: the overseas business is large in scale, but low in margin.

According to analyst estimates, Haier's overseas gross margin is approximately **12%** (versus approximately **42%** domestically). In other words — Haier's globalization is, at its core, "**trading profit for scale**."

| Region | Gross margin (estimate) | Net margin (estimate) |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Domestic | ~42% | ~8% |
| Overseas | ~12% | ~3-4% |

Source: brokerage research estimates, not official figures

**Counterpoint (bullish)**: overseas margins are low because the business is still in an integration phase. GE Appliances, 10 years after acquisition, has gone from losses to profitability and holds the #1 share in North American major appliances (**24.5%**). Candy turned profitable after 5 years. Emerging markets (South Asia, Middle East & Africa) are growing fast, and margins should improve as scale increases.

**Counterpoint (bearish)**: low overseas margins aren't temporary — they're structural. Developed markets are intensely competitive with low brand premiums; emerging markets require heavy upfront investment. Midea's overseas margins are also far below its domestic margins — this is an industry-wide affliction of Chinese appliance makers going global, not unique to Haier.

## II. How deep is the moat: examined one by one

### Moat 1: absolute leadership in refrigerators and washing machines

**Strength: extremely strong**

Haier's market share is **~47.7%** in refrigerators and **~47.4%** in washing machines — close to half the market in each. Globally, it has been #1 in refrigerators for **17 consecutive years** and #1 in washing machines for **16 consecutive years**.

This leadership wasn't won by being "cheap" — it was built through decades of accumulated **brand recognition + channel network + proprietary technology**. Once consumers' first thought when buying a refrigerator is Haier, that mindshare is hard for competitors to dislodge in the short term.

**Counterpoint**: Midea's share in refrigerators and washing machines is slowly rising, driven by a value-for-money strategy and more efficient penetration into lower-tier channels. The gap is narrowing, albeit slowly.

### Moat 2: Casarte's premium brand pricing power

**Strength: strong**

Casarte's share of the premium market above 10,000 yuan:
- Refrigerators: **44%** share
- Washing machines: **75%** share
- Air conditioners (15,000 yuan+): **53%** share
- Water heaters: **30%** share

Source: Haier Smart Home's 2025 annual report

This premium brand's pricing power is real — Casarte's average selling price is more than **3x** the industry average, with an estimated gross margin above **35%**. More importantly, once a premium brand is established, it takes competitors an extremely long time to catch up.

**Counterpoint**: Midea's COLMO has grown faster than Casarte in recent years, narrowing the brand gap. More importantly, Casarte accounts for only **~13%** of Haier's total revenue — this moat, while deep, protects only a small part of the company.

### Moat 3: globalized, localized operating network

**Strength: moderately strong**

Haier operates **35 industrial parks, 163 manufacturing centers, and 13 lighthouse factories (the most of any company globally)** worldwide. GE Appliances manufactures **80%** of its products domestically in the US, which gives Haier more resilience against tariff shocks than pure export-oriented companies.

Haier's globalization model is "**acquire local brands + localized operations**," rather than simply "manufacture in China, export abroad." This means Haier has GE's brand recognition and channel network in North America, Fisher & Paykel's premium positioning in Australia/New Zealand, and AQUA's local operating experience in Japan.

**Counterpoint**: localized operations mean higher costs and more complex management. The seven major brands each operate independently, with limited synergy. The systems of GE Appliances and Haier China have still not been fully integrated to this day. This "decentralized globalization" is one of the core reasons Haier's expense ratio remains stubbornly high.

### Moat 4: the Three-Wing Bird smart ecosystem

**Strength: weak, still unproven**

In theory, the Three-Wing Bird ("Sanyiniao") scenario ecosystem could create switching costs — once a user buys a full smart-home solution, the cost of switching brands rises. But the current reality is:

- Smart-home penetration is only **~15%**
- The scenario-package repurchase rate is only **~15%** (estimate)
- Revenue contribution is under **5%**
- Cumulative investment exceeds **10 billion yuan**

**Counterpoint (bullish)**: the first-mover advantage is real. Haier was the first company in the industry to propose the "scenario brand" concept, and Three-Wing Bird experience stores have been rolled out nationwide. If smart homes eventually take off, Haier is well positioned.

**Counterpoint (bearish)**: Huawei HarmonyOS and Xiaomi Mi Home have stronger ecosystem compatibility and lower user migration costs. Consumers are more willing to pay for single-product functionality than for a "scenario." Three-Wing Bird may be pointed in the right direction, but it isn't yet Haier's moat — because a moat needs to already be built, not "under construction."

### Overall moat assessment

| Moat type | Strength | Revenue coverage |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Refrigerator/washing-machine leadership | **Extremely strong** | ~50% of revenue |
| Casarte premium brand | **Strong** | ~13% of revenue |
| Localized globalization | **Moderately strong** | ~51% of revenue |
| Three-Wing Bird smart ecosystem | **Weak** | Under 5% |
| Air-conditioner category | **Weak** | ~17% of revenue |

**Conclusion**: Haier's moat is "**unevenly distributed**" — extremely deep in refrigerators/washing machines, strong in the premium segment, but nearly nonexistent in the largest single-product market (air conditioners), and not yet formed in the smart ecosystem.

## III. Is globalization earning face or substance?

This is **the question most easily overlooked** when investing in Haier.

Haier's globalization scorecard is genuinely impressive:

- #1 in North American major-appliance retail volume (GE Appliances, **24.5%** share)
- #1 in Asian retail volume (**25.9%** share)
- #1 in Australia/New Zealand retail volume (**15.9%** share)
- #1 among Chinese white-goods companies in Western Europe (**8%** share)
- #1 globally in major-appliance retail volume for 17 consecutive years

Source: Euromonitor International

But what's the substance behind the face?

**Key figure**: Haier's overall net margin in 2025 was **6.67%**. Domestic net margin was approximately **8%**, overseas net margin approximately **3-4%** (analyst estimate). With overseas revenue now above half, **overall net margin is automatically pulled down** — revenue growth (5.7%) outpacing profit growth (4.4%) traces back to this.

**Counterpoint (bullish)**:
1. GE Appliances going from a loss-making acquisition to the #1 share in North America is a textbook M&A integration case
2. Emerging markets (Middle East & Africa +56%, South Asia +23%, Southeast Asia +20%) are growing rapidly — early investment for long-term returns
3. The true value of globalization isn't short-term margin but **risk diversification** — when China's real estate market declines, overseas revenue provides a cushion

**Counterpoint (bearish)**:
1. GE Appliances' Americas revenue grew only **0.4%** in 2025 — growth has already stalled
2. North America generates **79.9 billion yuan** of revenue, and escalating tariffs could erode profit at any time
3. The overseas business contributes half of revenue but far less than half of profit — meaning Haier's "world's #1" is more of a scale narrative than a profitability narrative

## IV. Where exactly does the efficiency gap come from

The profit-margin gap between Haier and Midea (**6.67% vs. 9.75%**) isn't accidental — it reflects a systemic efficiency difference.

### Gap 1: expense ratio

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Gap |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Period expense ratio | **~19%** | **~15.5%** | Haier higher by **3.5 percentage points** |
| R&D-to-sales expense ratio | **0.3** | Higher | Haier is more channel-driven than technology-driven |

Source: 2025 annual reports

**For every 100 yuan of revenue generated, Haier spends nearly 4 yuan more in expenses than Midea.** This 3.5-percentage-point gap is almost exactly equal to the gap in the two companies' net margins (3.08 percentage points).

Reasons for the higher expense ratio:
1. The seven major brands each operate independent systems, adding management complexity
2. The offline experience-store model (Three-Wing Bird) costs more than online channels
3. Localized overseas operations add substantial local labor and marketing costs
4. R&D spending of **10.7 billion yuan** (2025), but R&D effort is spread across multiple lines — hardware, IoT, scenarios, and more

### Gap 2: operating efficiency

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group |
|------|---------|---------|
| Inventory turnover days | **72.97 days** | **68.56 days** |
| Accounts-receivable turnover days | **31.87 days** | **30.07 days** |
| Operating cycle | **104.85 days** | **98.63 days** |

Source: 2025 annual reports

Midea leads Haier across the board on operating efficiency by about **6%**. Midea's T+3 supply-chain model (produce-to-order) is more efficient, while Haier's multi-brand, multi-region operations add inventory and turnover pressure.

### Gap 3: profit output per unit

A direct comparison:

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group |
|------|---------|---------|
| Headcount (estimate) | ~100,000+ | ~160,000+ |
| Profit per employee | ~200,000 yuan | ~270,000 yuan |

Each Midea employee generates about **35%** more profit than each Haier employee.

**Counterpoint (bullish)**: the efficiency gap is narrowing. Haier's debt ratio of **57.41%** is lower than Midea's **61.17%**, making its finances more conservative and stable. Haier's Consolidated TC warehousing model (averaging 100,000 deliveries a day, covering 1,944 districts and counties within 24 hours) is improving channel efficiency.

**Counterpoint (bearish)**: the efficiency gap is structural, not cyclical. As long as Haier sticks with its "multi-brand, localized" globalization model, its expense ratio will struggle to fall to Midea's level. This isn't a management problem — it's the cost of a business-model choice.

## V. Overall assessment of the business model

Summarizing the analysis above, Haier Smart Home's business model can be characterized as follows:

**Strengths**:
1. Absolute global leadership in refrigerators and washing machines, with an extremely deep moat
2. Casarte, a premium brand, has established real pricing power and consumer mindshare
3. The most complete globalization footprint, with unmatched experience in multi-brand localized operations
4. Excellent cash-flow quality — operating cash flow / net profit has averaged **146%** over five consecutive years

**Weaknesses**:
1. Net margin of **6.67%** is far below Midea's **9.75%** and Gree's **~15%**
2. Expense ratio of **19%** is 3.5 percentage points higher than Midea's, and hard to compress quickly
3. Overseas revenue exceeds half of the total but carries low margins — "globalization" is more of a scale story than a profit story
4. The air-conditioner business is weak in the largest single-product market
5. The Three-Wing Bird ecosystem requires enormous investment but its business model remains unproven

**One-line characterization**: Haier Smart Home is a **global white-goods giant with an unevenly distributed moat** — nearly unbeatable in the areas where it excels (refrigerators/washing machines, premium), but far behind competitors on the dimension that matters most for efficiency (cost control, profit conversion).
