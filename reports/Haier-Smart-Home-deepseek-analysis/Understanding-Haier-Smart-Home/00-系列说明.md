> 4 articles that take Haier Smart Home apart layer by layer, revealing the true face of the global white-goods leader.
>
> The series takes about 60 minutes to read; reading in order is recommended.

---

## Series positioning

Haier Smart Home is not a company that "nobody pays attention to" — it is one of the **most subtly contested** names in the A-share appliance sector.

- The bulls say: No. 1 in global large-appliance retail volume for **17 consecutive years**, near-monopoly at the high end via Casarte, a P/E under **10x**, a dividend yield above **5%** — unreasonably cheap
- The bears say: net margin of **6.67%** is only two-thirds of Midea's, gross margin has fallen **4.85 percentage points** in two years, both revenue and net profit declined in Q1 2026, and despite all the "ecosystem story" talk, the expense ratio is far above peers

**Both camps are looking at the same company, yet reach very different conclusions.** For most people, the disagreement isn't really about "is Haier good or not" — it's about "whether Haier's cheapness is genuine cheapness or cheapness for a reason."

What this series sets out to do is **lay out the facts and the data first, and let you draw your own conclusion** — rather than drawing one for you.

## Series writing principles

This series follows these hard rules:

1. **Lay out the data first, then the logic, then the conclusion** — no bullish or bearish stance is preset
2. **Every core judgment carries a counter-argument** — so readers can weigh it themselves
3. **Unreliable data is deleted rather than kept** — non-official data such as market share figures are all labeled "estimate" or "unofficial figure"
4. **No "probability-weighted expected return" calculations** — assigning weights to scenarios is purely subjective and gives readers a false sense of precision
5. **Does not constitute investment advice** — a disclaimer appears at the end of every article
