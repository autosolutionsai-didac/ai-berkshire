## 4. Summary by Dimension

### 4.1 Business Model (Duan Yongping's lens) — ★★★☆☆

1. **At its core, this is a product company**, not a platform company. Revenue is 100% dependent on hardware sales; the Sanyiniao ecosystem contributes less than 5%.
2. **Casarte is the most valuable asset**: brand value grew from RMB 2.2 billion (2016) to RMB 38.0 billion (2025), with a total brand value of RMB 92.8 billion; it holds an 88% offline share in the RMB 10,000+ washing machine segment (near-monopoly). As Duan Yongping put it: "A good brand is a brand seared into the consumer's mind" — Casarte has achieved that.
3. **But Casarte accounts for only about 13% of revenue**; the flagship Haier brand's pricing power is limited, and in air conditioning Haier is more of a follower.
4. **Overseas revenue has exceeded domestic revenue for the first time** (51.1% vs. 48.9%). Haier's own-brand-plus-localized-manufacturing model leads Midea and Gree, but overseas margins remain low.
5. **Duan Yongping's take**: "This isn't a business I'd want to hold as a core position. But if the price is cheap enough, and the underlying quality is decent, holding a small position is fine."

### 4.2 Financial Valuation (Buffett's lens) — ★★★★☆

1. **Valuation is extremely low**: P/E of 9.84 sits at just the 2.58th percentile over 5 years, versus a historical median of 14.29 — implying 45% upside from mean reversion. FCF yield of 8% versus China's 10-year treasury yield of 1.7%, a spread of 625bp.
2. **Cash flow is strong**: operating cash flow / net profit has averaged 146% over the past five years — a genuine cash-generating machine.
3. **The declining gross margin is the signal Buffett would watch most closely**: it has fallen from 31.51% to 26.66%, indicating weakening pricing power. R&D spending was cut by 6.26% to protect profit — this looks like a short-term maneuver.
4. **The balance sheet isn't quite "fortress-grade"**: a 57% debt ratio and a 0.63 quick ratio don't match Buffett's preference for sub-40% leverage.
5. **Dividend yield of 5.6%, with a commitment to raise it further**: ≥58% in 2026, rising to ≥60% in 2027-28. Management's average buyback price of RMB 25.25 is above the current price of RMB 20.52 (23% higher).

### 4.3 Industry Competition (Munger's lens) — ★★★☆☆

1. **White goods has shifted from a "growth industry" to a "value industry"**: refrigerator and washing-machine penetration in urban China already exceeds 100 units per 100 households, leaving very little room for incremental growth. The investment logic has shifted from "growth" to "earnings certainty plus dividends."
2. **A three-way landscape**: Midea has the widest and deepest moat (full product range, efficiency, and a B2B second curve); Haier's moat is "wide but not deep enough" (a global brand and dual leadership in refrigerators/washers, but a high expense ratio); Gree's is "narrow but very deep" (dominant in air conditioning alone, but carries significant single-category risk).
3. **Haier's core problem: an expense ratio of 19% vs. Midea's 15.5%**. For every RMB 100 of revenue generated, Haier spends nearly RMB 4 more than Midea. This is a structural gap.
4. **Xiaomi is the biggest industry wildcard**: its online air-conditioner market share has already risen to 10.8% (third in the industry), and it has built its own factory in Wuhan to shed the OEM-brand label. 70% of Xiaomi air-conditioner users actively use the smart connectivity features — once this kind of ecosystem stickiness takes hold, it could rewrite the rules of the game.
5. **Munger's take**: "If you could only pick one white-goods company, pick Midea. Haier sits in an awkward position — not as comprehensive as Midea, not as cheap as Gree."

## 5. Investment Thesis (Bull vs. Bear)

### 🟢 Bull Case

1. **Valuation is at a historic extreme low**: P/E of 9.84 (just the 2.58th percentile over 5 years), FCF yield of 8% — this already discounts most pessimistic scenarios. Even in the pessimistic scenario (0% growth, 10x P/E), the position still doesn't lose money.
2. **The global moat is real and deep**: 17 consecutive years as the world's #1 brand, 35 industrial parks, 13 lighthouse factories (the most of any company globally), and a matrix of 7 major brands. This kind of hard-asset moat is hard for internet-native companies to replicate quickly.
3. **Cash flow is extremely solid**: operating cash flow / net profit has exceeded 130% for five straight years; dividend yield of 5.6% with a three-year commitment to raise it further (to 60%); dividends plus buybacks exceed 60% of net profit.
4. **Casarte represents genuine "mindshare monopoly"**: 88% share in the RMB 10,000+ washing machine segment, 43% share in the RMB 10,000+ refrigerator segment. The premiumization trend is irreversible, and Casarte is Haier's single most valuable strategic asset.
5. **Q1 2026 may already be the cyclical bottom**: Q1 net profit rose 113% sequentially from Q4, and the impact of U.S. tariffs is expected to ease in H2 2026. After CLSA's downgrade, market expectations are already low enough that positive surprises become easier.
6. **Strong growth in emerging markets**: Middle East & Africa +55.8%, South Asia +23.2%, Southeast Asia +13.4%. The rise of the global middle class provides a long-term growth tailwind.
7. **Management shows "aligned interests" signals**: the average buyback price of RMB 25.25 is above the current price of RMB 20.52 (23% higher), the payout ratio keeps rising, and D-shares have been bought back and cancelled (55 million shares cumulatively).

### 🔴 Bear Case

1. **Gross margin has fallen 4.85 percentage points in two years**: this is the signal Buffett would watch most closely — a true "franchise" business can pass costs on to consumers, and Haier can't.
2. **The Q1 2026 "double decline" may be a structural inflection point**: revenue -6.87%, net profit -15.22% — the first YoY quarterly revenue decline in 6 years. If it continues into Q2, it would confirm structural deterioration.
3. **Midea dominates across the board, and the gap is widening**: Midea's net profit is 2.25x Haier's, its net margin is 3.2pct higher, its expense ratio is 3.55pct lower, and it has a B2B second curve worth RMB 122.8 billion. There is no sign of Haier catching up on efficiency.
4. **A net margin of 6.67% means poor efficiency in converting revenue into shareholder returns**: the efficiency of converting revenue growth into shareholder profit is very low. The long-standing structural problem of "scaling revenue overseas without scaling profit" remains unresolved.
5. **Raw-material costs are being hit from four directions**: copper +40%, refrigerant R32 +80%, plastics +40-50%, aluminum +20%. Haier's ability to pass on price increases is weaker than Gree's (Gree's average selling price rose 2.15% against the broader trend).
6. **The broader shift in home appliances from incremental growth to a mature, stock market is irreversible**: refrigerator and washing-machine penetration in urban China already exceeds 100 units per 100 households. The long-term growth rate for white goods will likely approach — or fall below — GDP growth.
7. **Management has told too many stories**: "Ecosystem Haier + AI-native organization + big health + robotics + new energy + embodied intelligence" — yet the R&D-to-sales-expense ratio is only 0.3, and the business remains fundamentally "channel-and-marketing-driven."

## 8. Final Summary

Haier Smart Home is the **most successfully globalized company with the most complete brand matrix** in the white-goods industry — ranked #1 globally for 17 consecutive years, with a near-monopoly in refrigerators and washing machines in China, and Casarte stands as one of the most successful premiumization stories in Chinese manufacturing. Operating cash flow is strong (RMB 26.0 billion, 1.33x net profit), and dividend returns are real, backed by a three-year commitment to raise the payout further.

But the company currently faces **multiple overlapping headwinds**: gross margin down 4.85pct in two years, a simultaneous decline in Q1 2026 revenue and profit (the first in 6 years), an expense ratio of 19% far above Midea's 15.5%, a spike in raw-material costs (copper, refrigerant, etc.), U.S. tariff uncertainty, and a long-term downturn in China's property market. The core question is: can Haier convert its world-leading scale into matching profit efficiency? The data of the past five years doesn't fully support that it can.

**The RMB 20.52 price provides a sufficiently thick cushion**: P/E sits at just the 2.58th percentile over 5 years, even the pessimistic scenario doesn't lose money, and the FCF yield of 8% is highly attractive against China's 1.7% treasury-yield environment. This isn't an investment that requires "betting" — the mathematical downside protection is real. But upside requires fundamentals to improve in tandem.

**Fundamentally, investing in Haier is a decision to "buy an upper-middle-quality company at a cigar-butt price" — not a Duan-Yongping-style great business, not a Buffett-style hold-for-life, but a Li-Lu-style margin-of-safety investment made from a position of "knowing what you don't know."**
