# The Growth Engine and the China Market

> Understanding lululemon series · Part 03

## Two Engines, One Stalling, One Firing Up

In 2025, lululemon sold **fewer** clothes in North America than the year before. That same year, its growth rate in China was **+29%**.

This isn't a story of "growth slowing down" — it's a story of "engine replacement." Whether the old engine can be taken over by the new one before it stalls out completely is the central question right now. Here are the numbers:

| Region | FY2025 revenue share | FY2025 growth rate | Trend |
|------|----------------|------------|------|
| Americas | **72%** | **-1%** | 7 consecutive quarters of weakness, same-store sales -3% |
| Mainland China | **~16%** | **+29%** (Q4 +24%) | Rapid growth, accelerating store expansion |
| Rest of International | **~12%** | **+17%** | Steady growth |

Sources: lululemon FY2025 annual report; China Daily

**The Americas — 72% of revenue, the core market — is shrinking. China — only 16%, the second market — is growing at 29%.**

The question is: **can the new engine take over before the old one stalls out completely?**

---

## North America: Why Did the Old Engine Stall?

### The Full Picture

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 guidance |
|------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Americas revenue growth | +12% | +3% | **-1%** | -1% to -3% (estimate) |
| Same-store sales growth | +10% | +2% | **-3%** | Negative growth |
| Number of Americas stores | ~370 | ~390 | ~405 | ~420 |

Sources: lululemon quarterly filings; StockAnalysis

Going from +12% to -1% took only two years. There are at least four layers behind North America stalling out:

### 1. Market Saturation

lululemon has about **405 stores** in North America, covering nearly every major city and upscale shopping district. Growth driven simply by opening new stores has reached its limit.

### 2. Competitive Erosion

Alo Yoga and Vuori are eating into lululemon's marginal customers. Not the core loyal customers (their retention rate remains at 89%-92%), but the customers "considering athleisure for the first time" — who now have more options.

The overlap of users across the three platforms has grown significantly year over year, meaning consumers are **comparison-shopping across brands** rather than staying loyal to one.

### 3. Macro Consumer Environment

U.S. consumer confidence indices have continued to weaken through 2025-2026. Premium consumer goods hold up better than mass-market brands under pressure, but a $128 pair of yoga pants can still get postponed when the economy feels uncertain.

### 4. Product Innovation Stalling

The Breezethrough pullback, the Get Low see-through controversy — consecutive failures in core categories have not only cost revenue but shaken the "product excellence" brand narrative. In the consumer goods industry, **a product failure equals lost brand trust**, and repairing that takes time.

**Counterargument**: North America's negative growth could be cyclical rather than structural. Nike also went through negative same-store sales growth in North America in 2015-2017, and later recovered growth on the back of a new product cycle (Air Max, Vaporfly). If lululemon's new CEO can launch a successful product line, North America could well return to positive growth.

## China: The New Engine's Potential and Risk

### Growth Data

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| FY2025 China growth rate | **Full year +29%, Q4 +24%** | lululemon FY2025 annual report |
| FY2026 Q1 China growth guidance | **+25% to +30%** | Company guidance |
| FY2026 full-year China growth guidance | **~+20%** | Company guidance |
| Number of China stores | **170+** | lululemon company data |
| FY2026 new stores | 25-30 international stores, **most in China** | Chain Store Age |
| China market share | **Low single digits** | Retail TouchPoints |
| China brand awareness | **~15%** | Company disclosure |

Sources: lululemon annual report; China Daily; Chain Store Age; Retail TouchPoints

### How Much Room Is There, Really?

Compare lululemon's position in China with Nike's:

| Dimension | lululemon China | Nike China |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Number of stores | **170** | **6,000+** |
| Brand awareness | **~15%** | **90%+** |
| Market share | **Low single digits** | **~15%** |
| Revenue as share of global total | **~16%** | **~15%** |

lululemon's brand awareness in China is only one-sixth of Nike's, and its store count is only one thirty-fifth of Nike's. **If awareness simply rises from 15% to 30%, China revenue has room to double.**

More importantly, lululemon in China benefits from two structural trends:

**1. The upgrade in fitness spending among China's middle class**

China's sports participation rate has continued to rise over the past 5 years. Participation in yoga, running, and outdoor activities has grown rapidly, driving demand for premium athletic apparel. This is a structural trend, not a cyclical one.

**2. Preference for premium foreign brands**

In the athleisure category, Chinese consumers still show a clear preference for premium foreign brands. lululemon's positioning in China is even more premium than in North America — there, it's not just "athletic apparel," it's a **symbol of middle-class lifestyle**.

### Three Major Risks in the China Market

But betting all of the growth on China carries risk.

**Risk one: geopolitics**

The uncertainty in U.S.-China relations is a tail risk that cannot be hedged. If an extreme scenario were to occur (similar to the 2021 Xinjiang cotton incident's impact on H&M), lululemon's growth in China could go to zero instantly. Currently China accounts for 16% of revenue — if that rises to 25%-30%, the impact of this tail risk would scale up proportionally.

**Risk two: rise of domestic brands**

| Domestic competitor | Positioning | Price vs. lululemon |
|-----------|------|-------------------|
| Particle Fever | Premium athletic fashion | 30%-40% lower |
| MAIA ACTIVE | Mid-to-premium yoga/athletic | 40%-50% lower |
| Keep (fitness app brand extension) | Mid-tier | 50%-60% lower |

The quality gap with domestic brands is narrowing, while the price advantage remains significant. Among younger, price-sensitive consumers, the appeal of domestic brands is rising.

**Counterargument**: over the past 10 years, every foreign premium brand entering China has faced concerns about "local substitution," but the reality is that genuinely premium brands (Apple, Starbucks, lululemon) have rarely had their growth interrupted by domestic substitutes. Domestic brands are taking share in the low-to-mid tier, not the premium tier.

**Risk three: the inevitability of slowing growth**

A growth rate of +29% cannot continue forever. As the base gets larger and store penetration increases, growth will naturally slow. The company's own guidance has already been cut from +29% to +20%.

The key question is: **slow down to what level?** If it slows from +29% to +15%, China is still an excellent growth engine. If it slows to +5%, then lululemon really has no growth engine left.

## International Expansion: Are the Six New Markets Worth Getting Excited About?

In 2026, lululemon announced entry into six new markets: **Greece, Austria, Poland, Hungary, Romania, and India**.

Source: lululemon official press release (December 2025)

### An Objective Assessment

| Market | Potential | Risk |
|------|------|------|
| India | Population of 1.4 billion, fast-growing middle class, low athleisure penetration | Price sensitivity, infrastructure gaps, intense competition |
| Eastern Europe (Poland, Hungary, Romania, Austria) | Consumption upgrade trend, brand white space | Small market size, limited spending power |
| Greece | Tourism economy + brand showcase | Extremely small market |

**India is the only market that could plausibly bring a substantial increment.** But lululemon's $128 yoga pants face a much bigger pricing challenge in India, where per-capita GDP is $2,500, than they do in China.

Eastern Europe and Greece look more like **brand coverage** than a growth strategy — symbolically meaningful, but a limited contribution to revenue.

**Counterargument**: don't underestimate lululemon's brand pull in new markets. Nearly every time it enters a new market, it manages to replicate the "line around the block on opening day" spectacle. The brand's global awareness and aspirational appeal remain very strong — an intangible asset that the financial statements can't fully capture.

## The Math of Growth: Can China Offset North America?

A simple estimate (not a precise forecast, only meant to illustrate the order of magnitude):

| Scenario | Americas (72% of revenue) | China (16% of revenue) | Rest of International (12%) | Combined growth |
|------|----------------|----------------|----------------|---------|
| **Bull** | 0% (decline stabilizes) | +25% | +15% | **+6%** |
| **Neutral** | -2% | +20% | +12% | **+2.5%** |
| **Bear** | -5% | +10% | +5% | **-2%** |

Source: estimate based on FY2025 revenue structure, not company guidance

**Even in the neutral scenario, overall growth is only +2.5%.** This reveals a harsh reality: **China's +20% growth, multiplied by its 16% revenue share, contributes only about 3.2 percentage points to overall growth. Meanwhile North America's -2%, multiplied by its 72% revenue share, drags down 1.4 percentage points.**

For China to truly "take over" as the growth engine, at least two conditions need to be met simultaneously:

1. **China's revenue share needs to rise from 16% to above 25%** — this would take 3-5 years
2. **North America needs to at least stabilize** — this requires effective execution from the new CEO

Until then, lululemon's overall growth rate will be compressed to the **low single digits**. That's not a disaster, but it's also not what a "growth company" should look like.

## The Long-Term Certainty of the Athleisure Category

Zoom out to the big picture. Regardless of lululemon's own near-term struggles, the category it operates in has a high degree of certainty.

| Trend | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Global athleisure market CAGR | **~8%** (2025-2033) | Grand View Research |
| Market size (2025) | **~$450 billion** | Same as above |
| Market size (2033 estimate) | **~$850 billion** | Same as above |
| Athleisure penetration rate | From ~25% in 2019 to ~35% in 2025, projected ~45% by 2030 | Grand View Research |

Three irreversible structural drivers:

| Driver | Key data | Significance for lululemon |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Rising health consciousness** | Once per-capita GDP passes $10,000, fitness spending as a share of consumption systematically rises, and the number of countries crossing this threshold keeps increasing globally | Expands the potential customer base for premium athletic apparel |
| **Casualization of dress codes** | Athleisure penetration rose from about 25% in 2019 to about 35% in 2025, projected to reach 45% by 2030 | Athletic apparel is shifting from "gym-only" to everyday wear, structurally expanding demand |
| **Women's economic independence** | lululemon's core customer base is high-income women aged 25-45; female labor force participation and income continue to rise | Directly drives expansion of the premium women's athletic apparel market |

**Counterargument**: a good category doesn't guarantee a good company. The athleisure market is growing, but competition is also intensifying. The market could double while lululemon's share is cut in half — the end result could be "the category wins, the company loses."

## Summary of This Installment

1. **lululemon's growth picture is highly split**: North America (72% of revenue) is in negative growth, while China (16% of revenue) grew +29% for the full year (Q4 +24%)
2. **China is the most important growth engine**: 170 stores, 15% brand awareness, low-single-digit market share — there is plenty of room to grow
3. **But China cannot offset North America's drag in the near term**: its revenue share is too small; reaching above 25% will take 3-5 years
4. **Six new international markets**: India offers imagination, Eastern Europe is brand coverage, near-term incremental revenue is limited
5. **The category's certainty is high** (global athleisure CAGR of 8%), but a good category doesn't guarantee the company will win
6. **The two most critical variables**: whether North America can stop declining, and how high China's growth rate can be sustained
