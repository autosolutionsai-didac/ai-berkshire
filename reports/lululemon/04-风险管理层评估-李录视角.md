## CEO Calvin McDonald's Track Record Review

### Tenure Overview (January 2019 - January 2026)

Calvin McDonald took over as CEO in January 2019. Over his 7-year tenure:

| Metric | At Start (FY2018) | At Departure (FY2025) | Change |
|------|----------------|-----------------|------|
| Annual revenue | ~$3.2 billion | $11.07 billion | +246% |
| Store count | ~440 | ~720 | +64% |
| China store count | <10 | ~140 | +1,300% |
| Gross margin | 55% | 58.4% | +340bp |
| Share of DTC revenue | ~25% | ~45-50% | Nearly doubled |

> Sources: [Retail TouchPoints: How Lululemon's CEO Plans to Double the Business](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/market-news/how-lululemons-ceo-plans-to-double-the-business-despite-mounting-competition); [AInvest Leadership Transition Analysis](https://www.ainvest.com/news/lululemon-leadership-transition-turnaround-potential-2026-2601/)

### Evaluation of Key Decisions

**1. "Power of Three x2" Strategy (announced 2022)**

Goal: double revenue to $12.5 billion by 2026, driven by three pillars — product innovation, omnichannel experience, and international expansion.

Assessment: as of FY2025, revenue stood at $11.07 billion, still short of the $12.5 billion target. This goal was set amid the optimistic environment of 2022 and looks somewhat aggressive in hindsight. But the direction was correct — international expansion did indeed become the core growth engine.

**2. The China Market Bet**

Expanding from fewer than 10 stores to 140, with China revenue growing from under $400 million to approximately $1.7 billion. China revenue grew 29% year over year in FY2025, far outpacing every other region. This was one of McDonald's most successful strategic decisions during his tenure.

> Source: [China Daily: Lululemon grows on China market strength](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202603/26/WS69c730c6a310d6866eb405e1.html)

**3. Digital Transformation**

Raised e-commerce's share of revenue from ~25% to ~45-50%, building out a full omnichannel infrastructure. Digital channel revenue exceeded $1 billion in Q2 2025.

**4. The Mirror Acquisition (2020, $500 million)**

This is the most controversial decision of McDonald's tenure. In 2020, the company acquired the at-home fitness mirror brand Mirror for $500 million, later rebranded as Lululemon Studio. The business ultimately shrank dramatically, and the company wrote off nearly the entire value. This exposed a lapse in McDonald's judgment on "cross-category expansion."

### Assessment of the Departure

McDonald's departure carried a "forced" quality — it was not a planned retirement, but a decision made against the backdrop of slowing company growth and a sharp stock price decline. Li Lu often says to "watch how management performs in adversity" — McDonald performed well in favorable conditions, but lacked an effective response when facing slowing growth and intensifying competition.

### Assessment of Heidi O'Neill's Background

- More than 20 years at Nike, most recently as President of Consumer, Product and Brand
- Led Nike's DTC transformation and digital strategy
- Proven execution ability within a large-company system

**Positives**: O'Neill brings extensive brand management and DTC experience, exactly the capability Lululemon needs most right now. Her large-company governance experience at Nike could help Lululemon transition from a "startup culture" to "mature corporate governance."

**Risks**: Nike and Lululemon are two entirely different corporate cultures. Nike is a wholesale-centric mass-market brand, while Lululemon is a DTC-community-centric premium niche brand. Cultural fit is the biggest uncertainty. In addition, O'Neill left Nike while it was facing its own struggles — a signal that warrants careful interpretation.

### The China Market Through Li Lu's Lens

Li Lu, one of the most successful value investors in China-market investing, offers a framework particularly suited to assessing Lululemon's opportunity in China:

**1. Extremely low market penetration = enormous headroom**

A low single-digit market share and brand awareness in the low teens mean Lululemon is still at the early stage of its growth curve in China. For reference, Nike's brand awareness in China exceeds 90%, with a market share of about 15%. If Lululemon's awareness in China rises from 15% to 30%, revenue could at least double.

**2. Fitness consumption upgrade among China's middle class**

China's sports participation rate has continued to rise over the past 5 years. The number of participants in yoga, running, and outdoor sports has grown rapidly, driving demand for premium athletic apparel. This is a structural, not cyclical, trend.

**3. Risk: domestic substitution and geopolitics**

Domestic brands such as Particle Fever and MAIA ACTIVE are rising, priced 30%-50% below Lululemon, with the quality gap narrowing. In addition, the uncertainty in US-China relations is a tail risk that cannot be hedged.

## Civilizational Trend: The Long-Term Penetration of Athletic Lifestyle

Li Lu's investment philosophy emphasizes "the great trends of civilizational progress." Viewed through this lens, the athleisure market shows:

### Irreversible Long-Term Trends

**1. The awakening of health consciousness is a natural product of civilizational progress**

Once per-capita GDP exceeds $10,000, spending on health and fitness systematically rises as a share of the total. More and more countries are crossing this threshold, giving the athleisure market growth a deep demographic and economic foundation.

**2. The blurring of "office wear" and "yoga studio wear"**

The spread of remote work and casual dress culture has turned athletic apparel from "gym-only" into "everyday wear." This is not a cyclical fashion trend, but a structural shift in consumption habits. Athleisure penetration has risen from approximately 25% in 2019 to approximately 35% in 2025, and is projected to reach 45% by 2030.

> Source: [Grand View Research Athleisure Market Report](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/athleisure-market)

**3. Rising economic participation among women**

Rising female labor force participation and economic independence directly drive growth in the premium women's athletic apparel market. Lululemon's core customer base (women aged 25-45, high income, health-conscious) is the biggest beneficiary of this trend.

### Lululemon's Position Within the Civilizational Trend

Lululemon sits at the core of the "long-term penetration of athletic lifestyle" megatrend. This trend is unlikely to reverse for the foreseeable future. The question isn't whether the market will grow (it will), but whether Lululemon can maintain or expand its share within a growing market.

## Summary Judgment Through the Li Lu Lens

Li Lu's investment framework emphasizes three things: understanding the business, understanding management, and understanding the price.

**The business**: Lululemon operates in a long-term growth lane driven by "civilizational progress," and the fundamentals of the business (high ROIC, high gross margin, DTC model) remain healthy.

**Management**: this is currently the biggest variable. McDonald left behind a company with doubled scale, but also left behind slowing growth and a brand under challenge. O'Neill's capability and cultural fit are the most critical variables to watch over the next 12 months.

**Price**: buying a company with a 30% ROIC at 9x P/E offers ample margin of safety on the math. But Li Lu would remind us: being cheap by itself is not a reason to buy — thorough understanding is. The current low valuation reflects genuine operating challenges, and one shouldn't assume it's an opportunity simply because "it's fallen a lot."

Ultimately, if one believes the long-term penetration trend of athletic lifestyle is irreversible (most likely true), and is willing to bear the uncertainty of the management transition period, then the current price offers sufficient compensation.
