## Final investment recommendation

### Conclusion: Buy gradually (build the position in tranches)

| Scenario | Probability | Target price | Potential return |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Bull: new CEO successfully turns things around, strong international growth | 25% | $320-$340 | +170% |
| Base: slow growth, stable margins | 50% | $195-$210 | +65-75% |
| Bear: competition worsens, brand loses ground | 25% | $100-$110 | -8% to -16% |
| **Expected value** | - | **~$195** | **+64%** |

### Recommended actions

1. **At the current price ($119), it's reasonable to start building a watch position**: 1-2% of the portfolio, with ample margin of safety
2. **Add at $100-$110**: if the stock falls into this range, the risk/reward becomes even more attractive
3. **Wait for O'Neill's first full quarter after taking office (September 2026) — FY2026 Q3, expected to report in December — before deciding whether to build to a full position**
4. **Wall Street consensus target price is $190, rated "Hold"**

### Key catalysts (positive)
- Heidi O'Neill's first strategic plan announcement after taking office
- China market sustaining 20%+ growth
- North America same-store sales returning to positive growth
- FY2026E gross margin stabilizing and recovering

### Key metrics to monitor (negative)
- North America same-store sales turning negative
- Gross margin declining for consecutive periods
- China growth falling below 10%
- Being forced into large-scale discount promotions

## Commentary from the four masters

### Business-nature perspective

> "Lululemon doesn't sell yoga pants — it sells the identity label of 'I care about my health.' A DTC share above 90% means the brand narrative is fully self-controlled, which is the key feature of a good business. A 58% gross margin and the flywheel effect of 28 million members show that the underlying logic of this business hasn't been broken. But if the slowdown in North American growth and the forced discounting continue to worsen, they could shake the foundation of the business."

### Valuation perspective

> "Buying a consumer company with 30% ROIC at 9x PE — that's rare even in my fifty years of investing. The key is to ask yourself: will Lululemon be worth more in ten years than it is today? I think the odds favor yes — the long-term trend toward athleisure is intact, and the brand's foundation remains. Buying at this price, you don't need any heroic assumptions to earn a satisfactory return. The risk is that you may have to sit through one or two years of turbulence."

### Multiple-mental-models perspective

> "Invert: under what circumstances would this investment lose a lot of money? The answer is if the brand completely loses its appeal with the younger generation — falling from grace the way Under Armour did. I don't think that probability is zero, but Lululemon's community model and product strength run much deeper than Under Armour's did. What worries me more is the management transition — a CEO change is one of the single biggest risk events for a brand company. I'd suggest waiting until the new CEO proves herself before building a large position."

### Civilizational-trend perspective

> "The penetration rate of the healthy-lifestyle trend rising from 25% to 45% is a civilizational-scale certainty that matters more than any single company's short-term volatility. Lululemon sits at the core of this broader trend — especially in China, where a low single-digit market share implies enormous room to grow. When you can buy a leading brand at 9x PE amid a civilizational upcycle, while others are panicking, that is usually a good long-term decision."
