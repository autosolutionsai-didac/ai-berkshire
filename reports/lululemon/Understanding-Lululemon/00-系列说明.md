> 4 articles that take lululemon apart layer by layer to see exactly what kind of business it really is.
>
> The series takes about 50 minutes to read; reading in order is recommended.

---

## Series positioning

lululemon is not a company "nobody is watching" — it is one of the **biggest decliners and most hotly debated** names in North American consumer goods over the past two years.

- The bulls say: buying a global brand with 30% ROIC and 58% gross margin at 9x PE is a once-in-a-decade opportunity
- The bears say: consecutive negative growth in North America, a vacant CEO seat, founder pressure on the board, and irreversible brand aging

**Both camps are looking at the same company but reaching completely opposite conclusions**. The stock has fallen from **$511** to **$125**, a drop of **75%**, and the market has already voted with its feet.

What this series aims to do is **first make clear what lululemon "is," and then let you judge for yourself whether it's "worth buying"** — rather than handing you a conclusion.

## Writing principles

1. **Data first** — lay out the data, then the logic, then the conclusion; do not preset a bullish or bearish stance
2. **Both sides presented** — every core judgment is accompanied by a counter-argument
3. **Transparent sourcing** — all data is labeled with its source; estimates are labeled "estimate"
4. **No false precision** — no probability-weighted expected returns are calculated, so as not to give readers a false sense of certainty
5. **Disclaimer** — every article notes at the end that it does not constitute investment advice
