## Step 2: Nature of the Business

### One-Sentence Definition

**At its core, Adobe's business is a "subscription tax on creative and document workflows" — a productivity-tool toll that professionals must pay every month.**

### Business Model Canvas

| Dimension | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Revenue model | Subscription-based (>95% of revenue), billed per seat/usage |
| Customers | Creative professionals (designers/photographers/video producers) + enterprises (marketing teams/IT departments) |
| Lock-in mechanism | Proprietary PSD/AI/INDD file formats + workflow inertia + annual-subscription cancellation penalties |
| Gross margin | 89% (the marginal cost of software distribution approaches zero) |
| Operating leverage | With 11% revenue growth, FCF grew 25% — strong operating leverage |

### Duan Yongping-Style Inquiry

> What makes this business good? If you could describe it in one sentence, what would it be?

**"What makes Adobe good is this: customers don't pay because they like Adobe, they pay because they have no choice — a decade of PSD files, team collaboration workflows, and industry-standard requirements are all locked into Adobe. That 'have no choice' is more durable than 'want to.'"**

But on the other hand: if AI makes creation simple enough that professional tools are no longer necessary, that "have no choice" will start to loosen.

## Step 3: Moat Assessment

| Moat type | Strength | Evidence | Trend |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| Switching costs | ★★★★☆ | Locked into PSD/AI/INDD formats; 10+ years of file assets that can't be migrated | Stable, but AI may bypass it |
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★☆ | Price increases of 16-50% in 2024-2025, NRR still at 130% | Still intact, but aggressive price hikes are pushing customers to explore alternatives |
| Scale effects | ★★★☆☆ | R&D spending of $4.29 billion/year, which smaller companies cannot match | Stable |
| Network effects | ★★☆☆☆ | The file format is the de facto standard, but this isn't a traditional network effect | Weakened by Figma's collaboration features |
| Technology barriers | ★★★☆☆ | Firefly's "commercially safe" differentiation, but AI technology iterates too fast | Uncertain |

### Morningstar Moat Downgrade (March 2026)

Morningstar downgraded Adobe from a **"wide moat" to a "narrow moat"**:
- Fair value estimate cut from $560 to $380 (a 32% reduction)
- Rationale: greater uncertainty about software companies' long-term returns in the AI era
- Moat sources narrowed from "switching costs + network effects" to "switching costs" alone

### Buffett-Style Inquiry

> Will this moat still be there in 10 years? What could destroy it?

**Scenario where the moat survives**: AI becomes an enhancement embedded within Adobe's products (the way Firefly already is), professional creative work still requires Adobe-grade fine control, and the PSD format remains the industry standard.

**Scenario where the moat is destroyed**: AI lets "non-designers" generate professional-grade content directly (without Photoshop), creation shifts from "tool-driven" to "prompt-driven," and the importance of file formats declines as AI generates content in real time.

**My assessment**: the moat should hold for the next 5 years (enterprise-customer inertia is very strong), but over a 10-year horizon there is structural risk. The narrow-moat rating is reasonable.

### Munger-Style Inquiry

> Where am I most likely to be wrong? Why would smart people avoid buying, or short, this company?

**The bears' core argument**: Adobe's current valuation (14x P/E) looks cheap, but if AI truly disrupts the creative-tools market within 3-5 years, Adobe's "normalized" earnings power may not be today's $17 EPS but rather $10-12. At $10 EPS x 12x P/E = $120 — implying another 50% of downside from the current price.

**My biggest risk of being wrong**: underestimating the speed of AI disruption. Historically, technological disruption always "starts slower than expected, then ends faster than expected."

## Step 6: Industry and Civilizational Trends

### AI's Impact on Creative Tools — Li Lu's "Civilizational Evolution" Framework

For creative tools, AI represents a civilization-scale paradigm shift — comparable to the shift from manual typesetting to desktop publishing (DTP), a transition Adobe itself won last time around.

| Dimension | Analysis |
|------|------|
| TAM shift | AI may expand the creative market's TAM (more people can create), but per-unit value declines |
| Position in the value chain | Adobe sits at the "application layer," while AI value may migrate down to the "model layer" |
| Historical analogy | DTP era: Adobe (PostScript) beat manual typesetting → AI era: will models (Midjourney) beat Adobe? |

### Li Lu-Style Inquiry

> Looking back from 20 years from now, is this company "the Standard Oil of this era" or "a flash-in-the-pan 3Com"?

**My assessment**: Adobe is more likely to be a "Microsoft-like" survivor — finding its place within the new paradigm, but no longer the sole winner. It won't disappear, but its growth rate may settle from 11% down to 5-7%. Neither 3Com nor Standard Oil, but a good company whose halo has faded even as it endures.
