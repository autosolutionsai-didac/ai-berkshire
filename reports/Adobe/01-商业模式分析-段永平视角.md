# Adobe Business Model Analysis

> Analytical dimensions: nature of the business, customer value, competitive moat, long-term certainty
> Data as of: May 2026 | Based on the FY2025 annual report + Q1 FY2026 earnings

---

## I. What Kind of Business Is This?

Adobe's core business is a **subscription platform for creative-productivity tools**. Products such as Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, and After Effects form the "standard toolbox" for creative professionals worldwide. The essential characteristics of this business:

1. **Extremely high switching costs**: Designers spend years mastering the operating logic of Photoshop/Illustrator and accumulate large libraries of PSD/AI files. Switching tools means relearning the software and losing compatibility with historical assets — a genuine "lock-in effect."
2. **Industry-standard status**: .psd, .ai, and .pdf are universal industry formats. Files that a client sends to a vendor default to Adobe formats, and the entire creative industry chain operates around the Adobe ecosystem.
3. **Subscription cash flow**: Full-year FY2025 revenue was $23.77 billion, with exit ARR (annualized recurring revenue) reaching $25.2 billion, up 11.5% year over year. Subscription revenue accounts for over 93% of the total — a textbook example of a recurring-revenue model.

## II. Why Can't Users Live Without It?

**"User stickiness" is the single most important feature of this business.**

- **Deep embedding in professional workflows**: A video post-production team's Premiere + After Effects workflow, or a brand design team's Illustrator + InDesign workflow — the switching cost isn't just the software itself, it's the entire production line.
- **File-format lock-in**: Billions of PSD/AI/INDD files worldwide form a massive stock of legacy assets that only Adobe can open and edit perfectly.
- **Collaboration network effects**: Creative Cloud's cloud collaboration features create mutual dependency among team members. When your colleagues, clients, and vendors are all using Adobe, it's very hard to switch unilaterally.

In 2025, Adobe carried out a bold price-increase strategy, restructuring Creative Cloud All Apps into Standard ($54.99/month) and Pro ($69.99/month) tiers, with some plans seeing increases of as much as 16.7%. FY2025 revenue still grew 11%, indicating that user acceptance of the price increases was high. **The ability to keep raising prices without losing customers is the best proof of pricing power.**

## III. Is AI an Enhancement or a Disruption?

This is the question the market cares about most right now. My judgment is: **for Adobe, AI is an "enhancement," not a "disruption" — at least for the foreseeable future.**

**The strategic value of Firefly:**
- As of the end of 2025, Firefly had generated more than 24 billion assets cumulatively, and 75% of Fortune 500 companies had adopted it.
- AI-related ARR grew more than 3x year over year (Q1 FY2026 data).
- Generative-credit consumption grew more than 45% quarter over quarter, and Firefly subscription-and-credit-pack ARR grew 75% quarter over quarter.
- Direct revenue contribution of roughly $400 million (cumulative 2024-2025).

**The differentiated advantage of "commercial safety":**
Adobe Firefly is trained entirely on licensed content (such as Adobe Stock) and public-domain content, without using customer data. This allows Adobe to offer enterprise customers **IP indemnification** — something competitors like Midjourney and Stable Diffusion cannot provide. For brands and large enterprises, this matters enormously.

**Innovation in the consumption model:**
Adobe has introduced a "Generative Credits" consumption model, offering 4,000 credits for $19.99. This is a "razor-and-blades" model layered on top of the subscription base, and it is poised to become a new revenue-growth engine.

## IV. How Long Can This Business Last?

**Core judgment: as long as humans still need visual content creation, Adobe's business will endure.**

- Demand for visual content is growing exponentially, driven by the explosion of short video, social media, and e-commerce.
- AI lowers the barrier to creation, but that means more people need creative tools — the market is expanding, not shrinking.
- From DTP (desktop publishing) to the Web to mobile to AI, Adobe has already successfully navigated four technology-paradigm shifts.

**Risk factors**:
- Canva (180 million monthly active users) is rapidly penetrating the non-professional market.
- Figma (80-90% share of the UI/UX space) dominates the product-design market.
- Open-source AI tools are eroding the low end of the market.

But these are largely marginal encroachments rather than core substitution. The professional creative workflow remains Adobe's fundamental base, and there is no visible prospect of disruption in the near term.

## V. Duan Yongping's Perspective — Summary

> "Buying a stock means buying the company, and buying the company means buying the discounted value of its future cash flows."

Adobe meets several of the criteria of a good business:
- **High switching costs + network effects = strong stickiness**
- **Subscription model = predictable cash flow**
- **Sustained pricing power = real pricing authority**
- **AI as enhancement rather than disruption = a deepening moat**

The main concerns are uncertainty around the CEO transition and whether the long-term evolution of AI will fundamentally change creative workflows. But in terms of the essential nature of the business, this is an **excellent business**.
