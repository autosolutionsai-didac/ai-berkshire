## A Company Nobody Can Quite Figure Out

A company with 700 million yuan in revenue and a 4.7 billion yuan loss saw its market cap rocket past 400 billion yuan just four months after listing. It's not a gaming company, not a consumer-goods company — it's an AI research outfit that trains large language models, one that walked straight out of a lab in Tsinghua University's computer science department.

On January 8, 2026, Zhipu AI listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange at an IPO price of **HK$116.2 per share**, becoming "the world's first listed large-model stock." What happened next outran the output of any AI model:

- First trading day: market cap of **HK$57.89 billion**, a strong open
- 43 days later (February 20): the share price was up more than **500%**, and market cap broke **HK$323.2 billion**, surpassing JD.com and Kuaishou
- May 6: closed at **HK$928.5**, market cap around **HK$413.9 billion**
- May 14: intraday high of **HK$1,229**, market cap briefly topping **HK$500 billion**

In four months, the share price rose **nearly tenfold**.

But flip open the financial statements —

| Metric | 2025 figure |
|------|-----------|
| Total revenue | **RMB 724 million** |
| Net loss | **RMB 4.718 billion** |
| R&D spending | **RMB 3.18 billion** |
| Gross margin | 41.0% |
| Current market cap (May 2026) | **~HK$450 billion (roughly RMB 410 billion)** |
| Implied price-to-sales ratio | **roughly 560x** |

Revenue of 700 million, a loss of 4.7 billion, and a market cap of over 400 billion.

If you think this is a bubble — you might be right, but you may also be missing the peculiar valuation logic of the AI sector.
If you think this makes sense — you might be right, but you may also be paying for a narrative.

What this series sets out to do is **take Zhipu AI apart layer by layer** — technology, product, competition, commercialization, risk — so you can judge for yourself whether this is "China's Anthropic" or "the next SenseTime."

### How Deep Do the Tsinghua Roots Run?

Zhipu isn't just "a company with Tsinghua ties" — it is nearly **an extension of Tsinghua's computer science department**.

- Founder Tang Jie: head of Tsinghua's Knowledge Engineering Group (KEG), created AMiner (an academic search engine) in 2006 — the technical starting point for Zhipu
- CEO Zhang Peng: Tsinghua bachelor's, master's and PhD, a Tsinghua Innovation Leadership engineering PhD
- Chairman Liu Debing and President Wang Shaolan: both come out of the Tsinghua KEG team
- The AI research institute has roughly 400 people, and **nearly half are Tsinghua graduates** (source: PingWest reporting)

**Counterargument**: A highly homogeneous team ("Tsinghua inbreeding") could mean weaker commercial instincts and a decision-making style skewed toward academia. This issue will be explored further in later installments.

Zhipu's roster of investors is extraordinarily star-studded — it reads almost like a "family portrait" of China's tech sector:

This implies two things: first, it's unlikely to "run out of fuel" in the near term; second, it may carry some non-commercial objectives (such as "domestic substitution" or "self-sufficiency and controllability").

## Three Common Misreadings of Zhipu

### Misreading One: "Zhipu Is Just Making a ChatGPT Clone"

Not accurate. Zhipu's revenue structure clearly shows it **doesn't make money from consumers**:

| Business | 2025 revenue | Share | Growth rate |
|------|-----------|------|------|
| On-premise private deployment (B2B) | **RMB 534 million** | 73.7% | +102% |
| Cloud API / MaaS | **RMB 190 million** | 26.3% | **+293%** |

(Source: Zhipu 2025 annual report)

Zhipu Qingyan (its consumer AI assistant) has **25 million** registered users, but MAU is only about **6-8.38 million**. By comparison, ByteDance's Doubao already has MAU above **227 million** — a **27x** gap.

Zhipu's real positioning is as a **B2B + API-platform company**, closer to Anthropic than to OpenAI. The consumer product is a showcase window, not the revenue engine.

### Misreading Two: "Tsinghua Pedigree = Technology Must Be Strong"

The Tsinghua background does provide deep technical accumulation — GLM-5.1 posted the world's best result on SWE-bench Pro, fully on par with Claude Opus 4.6. That's not hollow.

**But strong technology doesn't equal a strong product.**

- Zhipu Qingyan's MAU growth has nearly stalled — from **7.02 million** in January 2025 to **8.38 million** in July, less than 20% growth over half a year
- Over the same period, Doubao surged from tens of millions to over 200 million, and DeepSeek went from zero to a viral phenomenon
- The reason: Zhipu **has no native traffic gateway**. No Douyin, no WeChat, no search engine

Technical strength is a necessary condition, but the outcome for a consumer product hinges on distribution power. Zhipu is at a natural disadvantage on this dimension.

### Misreading Three: "Valuation This High = Must Be a Bubble"

The current price-to-sales ratio of roughly **560x** looks absurd. But the valuation logic of the AI sector differs from traditional industries — the market isn't buying current revenue, it's buying **expectations of exponential future growth in API call volume**.

Data supporting that expectation:
- The MaaS platform's ARR has reached **roughly RMB 1.7 billion** (about $250 million), up **60x** over the past 12 months
- In Q1 2026, API call volume grew **400%**, while pricing rose **83%** (both volume and price up together)
- Platform registered users surpassed **4 million**, spanning **218 countries** worldwide

(Source: Zhipu 2025 annual report, Securities Times)

**Counterargument**: ARR is not the same as recognized revenue. Of the RMB 1.7 billion in ARR, only RMB 190 million (the cloud portion) is actually recognized revenue. The API-call surge partly reflects rock-bottom pricing (a price war); if competition intensifies or subsidies fade, growth could slow sharply. Anthropic's 2025 ARR already reached **over $1 billion** — Zhipu's $250 million is still small on a global scale.

## Understanding Zhipu AI Requires Answering Four Questions

Boil Zhipu AI down to one sentence:

> **Zhipu AI = 20 years of Tsinghua technical accumulation + a first-tier GLM flagship model + fast-growing B2B/API revenue + a consumer business overwhelmed by Big Tech + massive cash-burning losses + a 560x price-to-sales ratio**

Each of these elements makes sense on its own, but put together they're confusing. To see this company clearly requires answering at least four questions:

| Question | Core tension | Which part of this series covers it |
|------|---------|---------------|
| How strong is the technology, really? | GLM-5.1 is indeed on par with Opus, but how long can this model-generation advantage last? | Part 02 |
| Can the product ecosystem hold up? | API growth of 400%, but consumer usage is 27x behind Doubao | Part 02 |
| Who can it actually beat in competition? | Baidu and ByteDance above, DeepSeek and MiniMax below | Part 03 |
| Is there enough cash to burn? | Annual loss of 4.7 billion, IPO raised 5.2 billion — how long does that last? | Part 04 |

Looked at individually, none of these questions is enough to judge whether Zhipu deserves attention. **Only taken together do they form a complete picture.**
