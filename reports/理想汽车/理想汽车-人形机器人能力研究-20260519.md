## I. Dynamic dataflow architecture: Li Auto's chip ambitions

### What is a dynamic dataflow architecture?

Dataflow architecture is a processor architecture in which **data availability drives execution**, standing in fundamental opposition to the traditional Von Neumann control-flow architecture.

| Dimension | Von Neumann architecture (control flow) | Dataflow architecture |
|------|-------------------------|-----------|
| Execution driver | Program counter fetches instructions sequentially | Execution is triggered once data is ready, with no program counter |
| Parallelism | Compiler/hardware must explicitly discover parallelism | Parallelism is naturally exposed, with data dependencies encoded directly |
| Memory model | Instructions and data stored in the same memory | Data flows directly between compute units |
| Compute density | ~98% of silicon area spent on scheduling overhead | Most of the area is dedicated to actual compute units |

"Dynamic" dataflow specifically refers to dataflow machines that use content-addressable memory (CAM), allowing multiple instances of the same instruction to coexist simultaneously in the execution pipeline.

**Core advantages**: naturally high parallelism, extremely high compute density, excellent energy efficiency, low latency, and a natural fit for AI dataflow patterns.

**Core disadvantages**: significant programmability challenges, limited generality, a thin software ecosystem, and difficult debugging.

### Li Auto's M100 chip

Notably, Li Auto unveiled the M100 chip in 2026, using an "Orchestrated Dataflow" architecture aimed at edge AI inference. This signals that Li Auto isn't just building cars — it's also positioning itself in AI chips. Li Xiang has stated the goal of becoming "one of no more than three companies globally that simultaneously build foundation models, chips, an operating system, and embodied intelligence."

## II. A checklist of sufficient conditions for humanoid robots

### Core framework: a four-tier system of conditions

#### Tier 1: irreplaceable core conditions (missing any one is fatal)

**1. Embodied intelligence / large AI model capability**

This is the fundamental dividing line between humanoid robots and traditional industrial robots. The large model determines the robot's generality — how many kinds of tasks it can perform, how many kinds of environments it can adapt to. More critically, AI capability directly drives the data flywheel: a stronger model → more scenarios → more data → an even stronger model. This is a "winner-take-all" positive feedback loop.

**2. Real-world deployment capability (having actual paying customers)**

Without scenarios there is no data; without data there is no flywheel. Figure's ability to deploy 10 units at a BMW factory and produce 30,000 vehicles over 10 months is a "real-world validation" more persuasive than any technology demo.

**3. Ample, sustained funding**

Humanoid robot R&D has a long cycle, high technical risk, and slow commercial payback. Tesla has $41 billion in cash, and Apptronik has raised $935 million — the entry ticket is a colossal sum of money.

#### Tier 2: conditions that determine competitive advantage

**4. Data-flywheel closed-loop capability**

Deploy → collect data → train the model → improve capability → deploy more. The flywheel Tesla has built with Cortex 2.0 + FSD data + factory deployment is something pure robotics startups struggle to replicate.

**5. Hardware system-integration capability**

Not every component needs to be developed in-house, but actuators, sensors, dexterous hands, batteries, and more need to be integrated into a high-performance system. The industry reality of sub-80% yield rates for complete units shows that system integration is a severely underrated hard capability.

**6. Motion control + perception systems**

End-to-end latency below 30 milliseconds and a dynamic-task completion rate above 90% are hard benchmarks.

#### Tier 3: conditions that determine the speed of scale-up

**7. Supply-chain control and mass-production capability** — going from the first 200 units to 1,000 units is the hardest stage for establishing standardized systems.

**8. Energy/endurance solutions** — today's 2-4 hour battery life is far from enough; 8-12 hours is the commercialization threshold.

**9. Cost-control capability** — bringing cost down from $100,000-500,000 to $20,000-50,000.

#### Tier 4: accelerator conditions

**10. Safety certification and standards compliance**

**11. Ability to leverage industrial ecosystems** — the automotive supply chain, the large-AI-model ecosystem, and policy incentives.

**12. Depth of cross-disciplinary teams** — mechanical + electrical + AI + manufacturing + business.

### One-line summary

> The sufficient condition for building a humanoid robot is, at its core, the product of "AI capability × real-world deployment × financial staying power," multiplied by the acceleration factor of the "data flywheel."

## III. Comparing capabilities across four companies

### Company overviews

#### Li Auto — early-stage entrant

- **Organization**: Formed a humanoid robotics team (led by Lang Xianpeng) and a software/embodiment team (led by Gou Xiaofei) in January 2026, staffed in part by transfers from the former autonomous-driving AI-evaluation and data-labeling teams
- **Product**: Planning a wheeled robot (for factory use, expected mid-2026 launch) plus a bipedal humanoid robot (timeline unclear)
- **Technology**: Core advantage comes from transferring autonomous-driving technology (large AI models, perception algorithms, simulation systems), but robot-specific hardware is starting from scratch
- **Chip**: The M100 chip uses an orchestrated dataflow architecture aimed at edge AI inference
- **Funding**: RMB 101.2 billion in cash, RMB 11.3 billion in 2025 R&D spending (50% on AI), an estimated RMB 12 billion in 2026
- **Strategy**: Li Xiang positions the company as "one of no more than three companies globally that simultaneously build foundation models, chips, an operating system, and embodied intelligence"

#### XPeng Motors — product launched, sprinting toward mass production

- **Product**: IRON humanoid robot, 178cm tall, 82 degrees of freedom, 22 degrees of freedom in the hand
- **Hardware**: 42 sets of 3D-printed lattice actuators, an all-solid-state battery (30% lighter / 30% higher energy density), a dexterous hand replicating the human hand 1:1
- **AI**: 3 in-house Turing chips (2,250 TOPS combined compute), a 72-billion-parameter physical-world large model, a 10,000-GPU compute cluster (10 EFLOPS)
- **Mass production**: Scale production by end of 2026; first partner is Baosteel (industrial inspection)
- **Funding**: RMB 47.66 billion in cash, RMB 7 billion earmarked for physical AI in 2026

#### Xiaomi — accumulating technology, iterating quietly

- **Product**: CyberOne launched in 2022; a third generation registered a copyright in October 2025 but has not been formally launched
- **Hardware**: A self-developed 300Nm motor; a major dexterous-hand upgrade (recruiting engineers from Tesla's Optimus team, shrinking volume by 60%)
- **AI**: MiMo-Embodied, open-sourced — the industry's first unified cross-embodiment foundation model spanning autonomous driving and embodied intelligence, achieving SOTA on 29 benchmarks
- **Mass production**: Officially denied mass-production rumors in March 2025; already running industrial-scenario testing at the Yizhuang plant
- **Funding**: RMB 200 billion in R&D over five years, RMB 60 billion-plus for AI over three years

#### Unitree Robotics — scaled commercialization, sprinting toward an IPO

- **Product**: H1 (high-performance), G1 (priced at RMB 85,000, contributing 60% of revenue), R1 (starting at RMB 39,900)
- **Hardware**: A self-developed M107 motor (torque density of 120Nm/kg, cost just 1/5-1/10 of overseas equivalents), gait adjustment in 0.3 seconds (faster than Boston Dynamics' 0.5 seconds)
- **Commercialization**: 2025 revenue of RMB 1.708 billion (+335%), non-GAAP net profit of RMB 600 million (+674%), humanoid robot shipments of 5,500+ units (world's largest, 32.4% share)
- **IPO**: Filed for a STAR Market IPO in March 2026, seeking to raise RMB 4.2 billion at a post-money valuation of RMB 12.7 billion

## IV. Key findings

1. **Unitree Robotics is currently the only humanoid-robot company achieving profitability at scale** — 2025 revenue of RMB 1.7 billion, net profit of RMB 600 million, and shipments of 5,500 units, making its commercial validation the most solid.

2. **XPeng has the strongest full-stack technical capability among automakers** — in-house chips + in-house large models + in-house actuators + a self-built data factory. Mass production by end of 2026 is the key validation milestone.

3. **Xiaomi's "slowness" is worth watching** — CyberOne has gone four years without reaching the mass-production stage, but the MiMo-Embodied model's technical level is extremely high; once deployed, its manufacturing and channel advantages cannot be ignored.

4. **Li Auto is the latest entrant, and the gap is the largest** — the team was only formed in early 2026, and it meets just 1 of 9 sufficient conditions. RMB 101.2 billion in cash is its biggest chip, but robot hardware (motors, dexterous hands, bipedal control) cannot be fast-tracked simply by spending money. The M100 chip demonstrates AI-chip ambition, but the leap from a chip to a complete robotic system is enormous.

5. **Dexterous hands are a shared weakness across the board** — XPeng and Xiaomi are relatively ahead, while Unitree and Li Auto are both still at the starting line. The dexterous hand is the key bottleneck determining the leap from "can walk" to "can do work."

6. **Li Auto's core tension**: the gap between Li Xiang's strategic ambition (foundation models + chips + operating system + embodied intelligence) and execution reality (team just formed, hardware at zero, no product launched) is enormous. Money can shorten the timeline, but it cannot eliminate physical laws — motor design requires iteration, motion control requires real-world test data, and mass production requires working through failures.
