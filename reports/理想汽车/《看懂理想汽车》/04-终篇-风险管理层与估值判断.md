## The core questions this piece answers

The first three parts pulled apart Li Auto's product, strategy, and competitive landscape down to the studs. But for anyone trying to make an investment decision, the analysis itself is not the endpoint — what ultimately needs answering are three questions about money: is this company's leadership trustworthy, is this company spending money wisely, and is the current share price cheap or expensive.

This piece answers directly:

1. Is management trustworthy?
2. Can the pure-EV transition succeed?
3. Is the valuation actually cheap?
4. Final judgment: temporary trough or structural decline?

## I. Management: strong product instinct, but unsteady judgment

### What kind of founder is Li Xiang?

Among China's new-force EV founders, Li Xiang has **the strongest product instinct** — this is almost beyond dispute.

Evidence:
- The single-model Li ONE sold over 130,000 units a year, proving his razor-sharp read on family-user needs
- Four L-series models on sale simultaneously, all profitable — the strongest product-matrix management among the new forces
- The i6 rolled its 100,000th unit off the line 6.5 months after launch, proving he can still build a hit after correcting course

But strong product instinct is not the same as steady strategic judgment. Li Xiang has made several major errors over the past two years:

### Error one: MEGA — the most expensive misjudgment

In early 2024, Li Auto launched the pure-electric MPV MEGA, priced at **RMB 559,800**, with Li Xiang personally championing it and setting extremely high expectations.

The result: MEGA's monthly sales after launch stayed below **1,000 units** for a long stretch, far under the internal target of **8,000 units per month** (sources: 36Kr, Yicai). A car priced at RMB 550,000 selling only about a thousand units a month means tens of billions of yuan in R&D spending was largely wasted.

MEGA's failure was not because the product itself was poor, but because of a **fundamental misjudgment of market demand** — demand for a RMB 550,000 pure-electric MPV in the Chinese market is extremely niche. This exposed a blind spot in Li Xiang's ability to translate "product instinct" into accurate "market judgment."

### Error two: repeated organizational upheaval

In late 2023 the company brought in a management team with Huawei backgrounds; by the second half of 2024 all of them had been let go (source: TMTPost). The organizational structure went through three major swings within a single year — "expansion → contraction → readjustment."

Headcount was trimmed from roughly **35,000** in 2023 to **30,700** in 2025 (source: Li Auto annual report). The layoffs themselves are not the problem; the problem is that **frequent strategic zigzagging left the team without a clear sense of direction**.

### Error three: two consecutive years of large target misses

| Year | Beginning-of-year target | Actual delivered | Attainment rate |
|------|---------|---------|--------|
| 2024 | 800,000 units | 501,000 units | **62%** |
| 2025 | 640,000 units (estimated target) | 406,000 units | **63.5%** |

**(Sources: Li Auto earnings calls, industry media)**

Two straight years of an attainment rate just above 60% means either **excessive optimism** (a poor read on the market) or **deliberate overpromising** (a lack of candor). Either way, it is not a good signal for investors.

**Counterargument**: Li Xiang corrects course quickly. After MEGA's failure, the product cadence was rapidly adjusted, and i6's success is proof of that correction. In a fast-changing industry, mistakes are unavoidable — what matters is whether losses can be cut and adjustments made quickly. From this angle, Li Xiang's learning ability is among the strongest of any new-force founder.

### Core executive attrition: the least reversible risk

Between August 2025 and early 2026, at least **8 core executives** left Li Auto (sources: TMTPost, 21st Century Business Herald).

| Departed executive | Position | Impact |
|---------|------|------|
| Wang Kai | CTO | Core of technology strategy |
| Lang Xianpeng | Head of Autonomous Driving | Soul of the autonomous-driving team |
| Several members of the "three pillars" of ADAS | Core ADAS technology | Autonomous-driving team nearly rebuilt from scratch |
| Other executives | Product/operations, etc. | Organizational capability degraded |

**(Sources: TMTPost, 21st Century Business Herald)**

The collective departure of the autonomous-driving team is the most serious problem. At a moment when intelligent driving is becoming the core competitive edge, rebuilding that team implies a **2-3 year capability gap** — and those 2-3 years are precisely the window in which Huawei ADS and XPeng XNGP will widen their lead.

### Governance structure: a double-edged sword of one-man decision-making

Li Xiang holds a **21.9%** equity stake but controls **68.9%** of voting power (dual-class share structure), meaning the company's fate is entirely tied to one founder's personal judgment (source: Li Auto SEC Form 20-F).

The second-largest shareholder, Wang Xing (founder of Meituan), has continued to sell down his stake, which has fallen to roughly **18%** (source: SEC filings). A major shareholder reducing their position is not typically a positive signal.

**The upside**: decision-making efficiency is extremely high — Li Xiang alone can pivot strategy quickly.
**The downside**: there is no effective check and balance; if the founder misjudges, the company pays a heavy price — MEGA is the example.

## II. The pure-EV transition: what i6 proved, and what it exposed

### What has already been proven

1. **Li Auto is capable of building pure-EVs.** The i6 rolled its 100,000th unit off the line 6.5 months after launch — the fastest such milestone among RMB 200,000-300,000 pure-electric SUVs
2. **Users accept Li Auto making pure-EVs.** i6's strong sales show the brand constraint of "Li Auto = range-extender" can be broken
3. **Manufacturing capability for pure-EV products is not an issue.** Capacity ramp-up went smoothly with no serious quality problems

### What has not yet been proven

1. **Whether pure-EVs can be profitable.** i6's gross margin is estimated at around **15-17%** (source: industry-media composite estimates), far below the 22% peak of the range-extended L-series. Meanwhile overall gross-margin guidance for Q1 2026 is just **5%**
2. **Whether premium pure-EVs can work.** The i9 (expected to be priced above RMB 300,000) has not yet launched — this is the key test of Li Auto's pure-EV brand power
3. **Whether range-extended and pure-EV lines can run in parallel.** Fighting on two fronts means R&D, channel, and marketing resources effectively double, but revenue scale has not doubled

### The central contradiction of the pure-EV transition

Li Auto faces a dilemma:

- **Not transitioning to pure-EV**: range-extender market share erodes, and growth headroom keeps shrinking
- **Transitioning to pure-EV**: entering the most fiercely contested red ocean (Tesla / BYD / Xiaomi / XPeng are all there), and gross margin gets compressed

i6 completed the first step — proving it can sell — via "volume up, price down." But "can sell" and "can sell profitably" are two different things. If the pure-electric i-series maintains long-term gross margins below 15%, Li Auto's financial model will converge with the current profiles of NIO and XPeng — high revenue, low profit, ongoing cash burn.

**Counterargument**: Pure-EV gross margins should rise with scale. i6 is currently in the early stage of capacity ramp-up, and scale effects have not yet fully materialized. As monthly deliveries stabilize above 20,000 units, combined with supply-chain cost optimization, gross margin could recover to 17-18%. Moreover, the higher pricing of i9/i8 should lift the overall pure-EV ASP.

## III. A realistic assessment of the three "second curves"

### Curve one: overseas expansion

In 2026 Li Auto has entered the Central Asian and Middle Eastern markets, with the international version of the L9 planned for Q3 (source: CnEVPost).

**Reality check**: the pace is slow. Compared with XPeng (over 50,000 overseas deliveries in 2025) and BYD (over 400,000 overseas sales in 2025), Li Auto's overseas footprint lags by at least 2-3 years. And while the Middle East and Latin America markets Li Auto has chosen have relatively low tariff barriers, their market size is limited. In the near term (3 years), the contribution of overseas business to total volume is expected to be no more than **5-10%**.

### Curve two: in-house chips (M100)

The M100 chip uses an orchestrated dataflow architecture aimed at edge AI inference, with **1,280 TOPS** of compute at a 5nm automotive-grade process (source: Li Auto product launch event).

**Reality check**: the direction is correct, but the monetization cycle is long. M100's potential value lies in (1) reducing dependence on external chips from Nvidia and others; (2) providing a differentiated hardware foundation for autonomous driving and in-car AI; (3) the possibility of future external licensing or sales. But the path from launch to large-scale production deployment in vehicles typically takes **1-2 years**. Its financial contribution for 2026-2027 is close to zero.

### Curve three: embodied AI / humanoid robotics

In January 2026, a humanoid-robotics team was formed, with plans to launch a two-wheeled robot and a bipedal humanoid.

**Reality check**: this is a "deep out-of-the-money option." In our comparison of humanoid-robot capability across four companies (Li Auto / XPeng / Xiaomi / Unitree):

| Company | Sufficient conditions met | Stage |
|------|-------------|------|
| Unitree Robotics | 5/9 | Already shipping at scale, revenue of RMB 1.7 billion |
| XPeng Motors | 5/9 | Full-stack self-developed, mass production by year-end |
| Xiaomi | 2/9 | Deep reserves but slow to commercialize |
| **Li Auto** | **1/9 (capital only)** | Team just formed |

**(Source: ai-berkshire project internal research draft)**

Li Auto trails XPeng by at least **2 years** and Unitree by **3 years** in humanoid robotics. RMB 101.2 billion in cash can buy a time window, but it cannot buy exceptions to the laws of physics — motor design requires iteration, motion control requires real-world testing data, and mass production requires working through the inevitable pitfalls.

**Counterargument**: the robotics business shouldn't be judged against current progress, but rather as a "5-10 year option." If large AI models achieve breakthrough progress in 2028-2030, embodied AI could see explosive growth. Li Auto's automotive supply chain (manufacturing capability, sensors, AI algorithms) can partially be reused for robotics. Even if the probability is low, the payoff if it hits could be several times over.

## IV. Valuation judgment: cheap, but for a reason

### Key valuation metrics

| Metric | Data | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Market cap | **~$18.9 billion** (source: Stock Analysis, May 2026) | — |
| Net cash | **~$14.0 billion (RMB 101.2 billion)** | **64%** of market cap |
| Ex-cash enterprise value (EV) | **~$4.9 billion (~RMB 35 billion)** | Extremely low |
| P/S (price-to-sales) | **~1.0x** | The lowest tier among the new forces |
| EV/Revenue | **~0.31x** | The market assigns almost no value to the business itself |
| 2025 net profit | RMB 1.14 billion | P/E has little reference value (profit trending toward zero) |
| Net cash / ADS | **~$13** | Of the $18.5 share price, $13 is cash |

**(Sources: Stock Analysis, Yahoo Finance, Li Auto 2025 annual report)**

The simplest way to understand Li Auto's valuation:

> **You pay $18.5 for one share of Li Auto — $13 of that is cash sitting on the balance sheet, and $5.5 is the price you're paying for an automaker with RMB 112.3 billion in annual revenue.**

### Is this valuation expensive?

It depends on how you view the pricing of that **$5.5** (roughly RMB 35 billion) worth of business.

**If you believe Li Auto's profitability will recover** — paying RMB 35 billion for a company with roughly RMB 100 billion in annual revenue that once earned RMB 11.8 billion a year is clearly extremely cheap. Assuming Li Auto returns to RMB 5 billion in annual net profit by 2028, the current EV implies a P/E of just **7x**.

**If you believe Li Auto's profitability will not recover** — paying RMB 35 billion for a company with negative operating profit, negative free cash flow, and annual cash burn of RMB 10-15 billion may not be cheap at all. Because "cash" is not free — if the company keeps losing money, the cash will eventually be exhausted.

### Three-scenario valuation

| Scenario | Probability | 2029 net profit | Implied valuation range (USD/ADS) |
|------|------|-------------|---------------------|
| Optimistic: new product cycle succeeds + share stabilizes | 20% | RMB 11 billion | $20-25 |
| Base case: slow recovery, thin profit | 50% | RMB 1 billion | $14-18 |
| Pessimistic: share loss continues, falls into losses | 30% | -RMB 5.7 billion | $8-12 |

**(Source: ai-berkshire project profit forecast model)**

The current share price of ~$18.5 sits roughly at **the upper edge of the base case**. This means the market has already priced in expectations of "slow recovery but no meaningful profit."

**Upside** requires the optimistic scenario to materialize — a successful new product cycle, gross margin back above 19%, deliveries back above 600,000 units.
**Downside risk** lies in the pessimistic scenario — continued share loss, operating profit staying negative for a long stretch, accelerating cash burn.

### Margin-of-safety analysis

| Source of margin of safety | Strength | Notes |
|------------|------|------|
| Net cash/ADS ~$13 | **Strong** | A hard floor, unless the company burns through cash quickly |
| $1 billion buyback program | **Medium** | Management putting real money behind its confidence |
| Debt-to-asset ratio of 53% | **Strong** | Financially very safe, no bankruptcy risk |
| Brand value + user base | **Medium** | NPS ranked first in the industry, but monetization ability is uncertain |

**Counterargument**: net cash equal to 64% of market cap provides extremely strong downside protection. Even if the auto business went to zero entirely (an extreme assumption), shareholders would still recover 64% of their investment. This is a unique safety cushion among Chinese NEV makers.

**Our view**: the safety cushion does exist, but the $13 net-cash floor is not an "iron floor" — if Li Auto loses RMB 8-10 billion a year (the pessimistic scenario), net cash would be down to only RMB 50-60 billion in five years. The cash floor moves lower over time.

## V. The ultimate judgment: temporary trough or structural decline?

Back to the core question posed at the start of the series.

### Evidence supporting "temporary trough"

1. **RMB 100 billion in cash** provides 2-3 chances to make strategic adjustments
2. **i6 monthly sales above 20,000 units** proves the team can still build a hit product
3. **NPS of 79.1, first in the industry**, shows brand mindshare hasn't collapsed
4. **A full model refresh in the second half of 2026** could restart the product cycle
5. **The M100 chip** demonstrates technical depth
6. **A $1 billion buyback** signals management confidence

### Evidence supporting "structural decline"

1. **Operating profit has already turned negative** — the core business isn't profitable
2. **Free cash flow swung from +RMB 44.2 billion to -RMB 12.8 billion** — from cash cow to cash burner within two years
3. **Range-extender differentiation has vanished** — the core moat has been filled in
4. **Huawei / Xiaomi / BYD are not going away** — the deterioration in the competitive landscape is irreversible
5. **8 core executives have departed** — organizational capability degradation will take 2-3 years to repair
6. **The founder's judgment has been unsteady** — MEGA's failure plus two consecutive years of missed targets

### Our judgment

**Both narratives have merit, but the data leans more toward "structural factors dominating, with cyclical factors layered on top."**

Specifically:

- Product aging is **cyclical** (a refresh can address it)
- The disappearance of range-extender differentiation is **structural** (irreversible)
- The deteriorating competitive landscape is **structural** (Huawei / Xiaomi / BYD are not going away)
- Executive attrition is **semi-structural** (repairable in 2-3 years, but the window may be missed)
- The profit decline has a **cyclical** component (Q1 inventory clearing) as well as a **structural** component (ongoing ASP decline)

If only cyclical factors were at play, a model refresh would fix things; but the structural factors mean **even if the refresh succeeds, Li Auto is unlikely to return to its 2023 profitability level**.

**The most likely outcome is: Li Auto survives, but shifts from "the most profitable of the new forces" to "a second-tier automaker that can stay alive but can't make much money."**

| Article | Core finding |

A summary of the full picture in one paragraph:

> **Li Auto is a company with a RMB 100 billion cash cushion and strong user goodwill, but it is going through a triple challenge: the fading of range-extender advantages, an unverified pure-EV transition, and the rebuilding of its core team. The market's pricing of just RMB 35 billion for the ex-cash enterprise value reflects an expectation that "the business's ability to make money is trending toward zero." Whether this pricing is excessively pessimistic depends on one core variable: whether the second-half 2026 product refresh can restart profitability. If it can, Li Auto still has a chance of returning to RMB 5-10 billion in annual profit; if it cannot, it will slide from "the best-earning of the new forces" to "a second-tier automaker that can survive but not turn a profit."**

**The answer isn't in any analyst's model — it's in the delivery data and earnings reports of the next two quarters.**
