## A contrast too stark to ignore

At the end of 2023, the most-quoted line in China's auto circles was: "Who's actually making money in the NEV race? Only Li Auto." At the time, with a combination of extended-range powertrains and family SUVs, Li Auto had become an outlier in an industry where NEV makers were largely losing money: delivery champion among the new forces, net profit of RMB 11.8 billion, free cash flow of RMB 44.2 billion, and ROE of 28% — crushing most automakers globally.

Two years later, the same company's operating profit had turned negative, free cash flow had swung from +RMB 44.2 billion to -RMB 12.8 billion, and per-vehicle net profit had shrunk 91%. It's not that the industry disappeared — it's that this company's money-making logic broke down. Placed side by side, the two years of data create a contrast uncomfortable enough to notice:

| Metric | 2023 | 2025 | Change |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Deliveries (10k units) | 37.6 | 40.6 | -18.8% (vs. 2024) |
| Net profit (RMB 100mn) | **118** | **11.4** | **-90%** |
| Operating profit (RMB 100mn) | 74.1 | **-5.2** | Core business turned loss-making |
| Free cash flow (RMB 100mn) | 442 | **-128** | From positive to negative |
| ROE | 28% | **1.5%** | Nearly zero |
| Net profit per vehicle (RMB) | 31,400 | **2,800** | Down 91% |

**(Data source: Li Auto 2023-2025 annual reports)**

On May 18, 2026, Li Auto's Hong Kong-listed shares plunged approximately **14%** in a single day, closing at **HK$65.75**. The cumulative decline over two trading days exceeded **18%**. The direct trigger was the launch of the all-new L9, which Citi rated as "not a game changer," and which was priced RMB 20,000 lower than expected right at launch.

But the real reason for the crash wasn't one car — it was the market repricing something more fundamental: **Li Auto has gone from "the new force best at making money" to an ordinary automaker with negative operating profit and stalled growth.**

As of May 2026, here is Li Auto's fundamental picture:

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| US-listed share price | **~$18.5** (Source: Yahoo Finance, May 16, 2026) |
| HK-listed share price | ~HK$65-77 range (sharp swings in mid-May) |
| Market cap | **~$18.9 billion** (Source: Stock Analysis) |

| Net cash / market cap | **~64%** |

## What kind of company is Li Auto, really?

In one sentence:

> **Li Auto = an NEV maker that found its niche through "extended-range + large-space SUVs + family users." That niche peaked in 2023 and has since been systematically eroded by Huawei, Xiaomi, and BYD.**

### The success formula that once worked

Li Auto's rise wasn't an accident. It got three things exactly right.

**First, it chose the extended-range route.** During the 2019-2023 window when charging infrastructure was still underdeveloped, extended-range powertrains (able to run on either gas or charge) perfectly solved users' range anxiety. It was the right choice for that particular window of time.

**Second, it defined the "family SUV" category.** "Fridge, TV, big sofa" wasn't just a joke — Li Auto was the first brand to redefine the NEV from a "tech-geek product" into a "family mobility tool." It precisely targeted family buyers in the RMB 300,000-400,000 bracket — a group with strong purchasing power, clear needs, and high repeat-purchase rates.

**Third, extreme cost discipline.** While the industry was largely losing money, Li Auto achieved profitability through a lean SKU lineup (only one model in its early days) and efficient operations. Its 2023 gross margin was **22.2%**, far above peers.

Together these three things formed a flywheel: **good product → strong reputation → high sales volume → economies of scale → high gross margin → more resources funneled into R&D and channel expansion**.

### Why did the flywheel slow down?

Starting in 2024, every link in the flywheel began to break down.

**Extended-range is no longer scarce.** Aito (Huawei) and Xiaomi have both launched extended-range models. In 2025, 7 multinational automakers announced plans to enter the extended-range segment. Extended-range went from Li Auto's exclusive label to an industry standard.

**"Fridge, TV, big sofa" has been copied.** The Aito M7/M9, Leapmotor C16, and Xpeng G9 have all begun to emphasize family-oriented space and smart cockpits. The value proposition proved highly replicable.

**800V fast charging is eroding the extended-range advantage.** In the second half of 2025, 800V fast charging began to spread widely, delivering 300 km of range in 15 minutes of charging. This significantly weakened extended-range's "refuel to replenish" advantage.

**Loss of pricing power.** Average selling price per vehicle fell from **RMB 330,000** in 2023 to **~RMB 250,000** in 2025, a decline of **24%** (Source: Li Auto annual report). The flagship L9 was priced RMB 20,000 lower right at launch — unthinkable three years ago.

**Counter-argument**: Some argue the flywheel hasn't actually broken — Li Auto's NPS (Net Promoter Score) still stands at a high **79.1**, ranking first in the industry (Source: Li Auto annual report). Brand mindshare remains intact; this may just be the pain of a product-generation transition. The test window for this argument is the second half of 2026: if sales recover and gross margin stabilizes after the L-series refresh, the flywheel truly was only slowing temporarily rather than breaking.

## Three common misreadings of Li Auto

### Misreading 1: Li Auto's problem is just that "the product has gotten old"

This is the most popular bullish argument — the L-series hasn't been refreshed in two years; once the full refresh lands in the second half of 2026, things will be fine.

**The data doesn't fully support this view.**

Product aging is indeed a fact. But the deeper issue is **structural change in the industry**:

- In 2025, NEV penetration broke through **50%**, and competition shifted from "taking share from gasoline cars" to "NEV-on-NEV internal competition"
- Nearly **70** six-seat SUVs are on sale in the RMB 250,000-500,000 SUV segment (Source: CAAM)
- Li Auto's ranking among the new forces fell from **first** in 2023-2024 to **fourth** in 2025 (Source: 21st Century Business Herald)

A product refresh can solve the "product got old" problem, but it can't solve the "the track got crowded" problem. The former is cyclical; the latter is structural.

**Counter-argument**: Structural change does exist, but Li Auto's brand recognition in the family SUV space remains first-tier. The 2026 i6 rolled 100,000 units off the line 6.5 months after launch (Source: Zol.com.cn), proving the team can still produce a hit. If the L-series refresh succeeds equally well, sales could recover to above 500,000 units.

### Misreading 2: RMB 101.2 billion in cash = absolute safety

The hundred-billion-plus cash pile is indeed Li Auto's biggest trump card. Among the new forces, this figure leads by a wide margin — NIO's and Xpeng's cash reserves are only one-third to one-half of Li Auto's.

But cash is only ammunition, not victory.

| Comparison | Li Auto | Comparable-tier peers |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Net cash | RMB 101.2 billion | Ample |
| 2025 free cash flow | **-RMB 12.8 billion** | Most peers also burning cash |
| Annual R&D spend | **RMB 11.3 billion** | Non-discretionary (autonomous driving/pure-EV/robotics) |
| Cash burn rate (estimate) | RMB 10-15 billion per year (pessimistic) | — |
| Theoretical runway | **7-10 years** | — |

**(Source: Li Auto 2025 annual report)**

The real meaning of RMB 101.2 billion in cash is: **Li Auto has the right to make mistakes, and 2-3 chances to reset its strategy.** But having money doesn't mean spending it well — the MEGA pure-electric MPV was a major waste of resources (sales far below expectations after its 2024 launch).

**Counter-argument**: Cash indeed doesn't equal safety, but it does equal "time." In an elimination-round industry, surviving longer is itself the biggest competitive advantage. WM Motor, Evergrande Auto, and HiPhi all died because their cash ran out. Li Auto, at the very least, won't face that risk.

### Misreading 3: Li Auto is turning into a "robotics company"

In January 2026, Li Auto formed a humanoid robotics team. Li Xiang said the goal was to become "one of no more than three companies globally simultaneously building foundation models, chips, operating systems, and embodied intelligence."

**The factual basis for this narrative is extremely thin.**

In our comparison of humanoid-robotics capability across four companies, Li Auto met only **1 of 9** sufficient conditions (capital), while Unitree and Xpeng each met **5**. Li Auto's motors, dexterous hands, motion control, and scenario deployment are all at zero.

The M100 chip (an orchestrated dataflow architecture aimed at edge AI inference) does demonstrate technical ambition, but a chip is only one component of a robotics system. A good engine doesn't make a good car — you still need the chassis, suspension, transmission, and vehicle tuning.

**Counter-argument**: The robotics business shouldn't be viewed as "diverting resources from the core business," but rather as a "call option" — if it fails, the annual loss is limited (a portion of the RMB 6 billion R&D budget), but if it hits, it could deliver tens of billions in incremental profit. That said, this option has a high strike price, and the odds of landing it are far lower than Tesla's.

## Four questions you need to answer to understand Li Auto

If forced into a single framework for understanding today's Li Auto, the core contradiction is:

> **A company that holds hundreds of billions in cash but whose earning power is disappearing — it doesn't lack the capital to survive, but it lacks proof that it can once again make money efficiently.**

That's either extreme undervaluation, or the market is saying "this business's earning power is heading toward zero."
