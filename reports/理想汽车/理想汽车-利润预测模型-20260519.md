## Forecast framework

**Annual profit = Total revenue × Blended gross margin - R&D expense - SG&A expense + Non-operating gains - Income tax**

Decomposition of core driving factors:

```
Total revenue = Deliveries × Revenue per vehicle
Deliveries = China NEV market size × Li Auto market share + Overseas deliveries
Revenue per vehicle = f(product mix, pricing strategy)
Blended gross margin = f(vehicle gross margin, scale effects, competitive intensity)
Expenses = f(R&D investment intensity, headcount scale, efficiency optimization)
```

## III. 10-Year Forecast (2036)

### An honest statement about the 10-year forecast

**Uncertainty around the 10-year forecast is extremely high.** The figures below are provided only as a reference framework and should not serve as the basis for an investment decision. Reasons:

1. **The industry landscape is unpredictable.** In 2016 Tesla was still losing money, and NIO and Li Auto had not yet been founded; in 10 years, the auto industry may look completely different.
2. **Too many technology variables.** Whether L4/L5 autonomous driving becomes widespread, whether solid-state batteries disrupt the supply chain, whether AI/robotics becomes a core business line — any one of these variables could fundamentally change Li Auto's value.
3. **Management variables.** Li Xiang will be 46 by then — will he still be at the helm? The composition of the core team is entirely unpredictable.

For this reason, the 10-year forecast is presented as **scenario narratives** rather than precise figures.

### Scenario A: Successful transformation into an AI technology company (10% probability)

**Narrative**: between 2028 and 2030, Li Auto succeeds in turning autonomous-driving technology, the M100 chip, and its robotics business into a second growth curve. The vehicle business becomes the carrier and data source for AI technology and remains stable; the AI/chip/robotics business begins contributing meaningful profit. This is analogous to Tesla's path of transformation from a pure electric-vehicle maker into an "AI + energy + robotics" company.

| Metric | 2036E |
|------|-------|
| Vehicle revenue | RMB 250.0B (1 million units × RMB 250,000) |
| AI/chip/robotics revenue | RMB 50.0B |
| Total revenue | RMB 300.0B |
| Blended gross margin | 25% (lifted by AI business) |
| Operating profit | RMB 30.0B |
| **Net profit** | **~RMB 25.0B** |
| Operating margin | 10% |

**What this scenario requires**: the M100 chip achieves a significant share of the in-vehicle AI inference market; humanoid robots reach shipments in the tens of thousands of units; a "data - model - chip" flywheel is established. Given the company's current starting point, the probability is low, but not zero.

### Scenario B: Stable survival as a second-tier automaker (35% probability)

**Narrative**: after a painful adjustment period between 2028 and 2030, Li Auto stabilizes at an annual delivery scale of 700,000-800,000 units. It does not become an AI company, and AI/robotics investment is ultimately cut back or spun off. But it holds onto a foothold in the RMB 250-400K family SUV segment, comparable to the position GAC or Geely occupy in the traditional auto industry.

**Logic**: this is essentially an automaker that "makes money, but not much of it." Annual net profit of RMB 4.5B represents roughly 40% of the 2023 peak. Margins are capped by competition at 2-3%, close to the level of traditional automakers (Toyota's net margin ~7%, Volkswagen's ~4%, GM's ~3%). Given the intensity of competition in the China market, 2-3% may already be a relatively good outcome.

### Scenario C: Defensive retrenchment / acquisition (40% probability)

**Narrative**: more than 5 consecutive years of low gross margin and share loss eventually force Li Auto into a major retrenchment. Around 2030, Li Xiang may make a major strategic pivot — divesting parts of the business, seeking a merger, or narrowing down to a single product line. Comparable to the decline paths of WM Motor or Evergrande Auto in their time, though the outcome would likely be less severe given Li Auto's cash reserves.

Li Auto's most valuable assets would be: brand recognition, the M100 chip team, and cash reserves once in the RMB 100B+ range (possibly down to RMB 30-50B by then). Potential acquirers could include Huawei, Xiaomi, or even an international automaker.

While RMB 101.2B in cash looks substantial, if annual losses run RMB 8-10B plus RMB 5B of capital expenditure, it can all be burned through within 10 years. ## IV. Key Sensitivity Analysis

### Gross margin is the make-or-break line

Given Li Auto's current cost structure (R&D of ~RMB 12.0B + SG&A of ~RMB 9.0B = fixed costs of ~RMB 21.0B), operating profit at various levels of gross margin and revenue:

| Revenue \ Blended gross margin | 14% | 16% | 18% | 20% | 22% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RMB 90.0B** | -RMB 8.4B | -RMB 6.6B | -RMB 4.8B | -RMB 3.0B | -RMB 1.2B |
| **RMB 120.0B** | -RMB 4.2B | -RMB 1.8B | **+RMB 0.6B** | +RMB 3.0B | +RMB 5.4B |
| **RMB 150.0B** | RMB 0 | +RMB 3.0B | +RMB 6.0B | **+RMB 9.0B** | +RMB 12.0B |
| **RMB 180.0B** | +RMB 4.2B | +RMB 7.8B | +RMB 11.4B | +RMB 15.0B | +RMB 18.6B |
| **RMB 200.0B** | +RMB 7.0B | +RMB 11.0B | +RMB 15.0B | +RMB 19.0B | +RMB 23.0B |

**Key findings**:
- Under the current cost structure, **revenue of RMB 120.0B plus a gross margin of 18% is the breakeven point**
- 2025 actuals: revenue of RMB 112.3B, gross margin of 18.7% → operating profit of -RMB 500M. The model matches
- Returning to the 2023 profit level (operating profit of RMB 7.4B) requires **revenue of RMB 150.0B plus a gross margin of 19%**, or **revenue of RMB 120.0B plus a gross margin of 22%**
- Below revenue of RMB 90.0B, even a 22% gross margin fails to generate a profit (insufficient scale to dilute fixed costs)

## V. Core Conclusions

### The one-sentence judgment

**Over the next 3 to 10 years, Li Auto is most likely to be a company that "can survive but cannot make significant money." Probability-weighted expected net profit falls between RMB 500M and RMB 1.5B — a stark mismatch against its RMB 100B+ cash reserves in terms of return on assets.**

### Judgments by time horizon

| Time horizon | Probability-weighted net profit | Core judgment |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **3 years out (2029)** | ~RMB 500M | Struggling around breakeven. Whether the new product cycle can deliver a simultaneous rise in volume and profit is the only thing worth watching |
| **5 years out (2031)** | ~RMB 1.3B | Thin-profit state. The vehicle business holds steady, but AI/robotics most likely fails to deliver |
| **10 years out (2036)** | -RMB 500M to +RMB 1.0B | Extremely uncertain. A 45% probability of being a profitable second-tier automaker or AI company, versus a 55% probability of retrenchment/loss/extinction |

### What would it take for profit to recover above RMB 10.0B?

Working backward: net profit of RMB 10.0B → operating profit of ~RMB 9.0B → requires gross profit of ~RMB 30.0B → which requires:

**Path A**: revenue of RMB 200.0B × gross margin of 20% = gross profit of RMB 40.0B - expenses of RMB 21.0B = operating profit of RMB 19.0B (exceeds the target)
- This implies deliveries would need to reach 800,000-850,000 units (at an ASP of RMB 240,000), with gross margin returning to 20%
- Difficulty: extremely high. 800,000 units implies a market share of 3.0%+; a 20% gross margin implies weaker competition or a recovery in brand premium

**Path B**: revenue of RMB 150.0B × gross margin of 22% = gross profit of RMB 33.0B - expenses of RMB 21.0B = operating profit of RMB 12.0B
- This implies deliveries of 600,000 units plus an ASP of RMB 250,000 plus a 22% gross margin
- Difficulty: extremely high. A 22% gross margin is the 2023 peak level, and is almost unattainable given the current competitive landscape

**Conclusion**: within a pure-vehicle-business framework, it will be very hard for Li Auto to return to a RMB 10.0B profit level, unless China's new-energy-vehicle price war reaches an inflection point (weaker competition, industry consolidation), or new businesses such as AI/chips/robotics begin contributing high-margin revenue.

### The biggest uncertainties

1. **When will the price war end?** If the industry undergoes a shakeout in 2027-2028 (2-3 automakers exit) and competitive intensity eases, Li Auto's gross margin could recover to 20%+. On the other hand, BYD's and Huawei's/Xiaomi's scale and financial reserves mean the price war could persist much longer.

2. **Li Xiang's strategic execution.** The failure of the MEGA versus the success of the i6 shows that Li Xiang is capable of both major mistakes and course correction. Future key decisions (the level of AI investment, when to cut losses, the pace of overseas expansion) will determine whether Li Auto ends up in Scenario A or Scenario C.

3. **The option value of AI/robotics.** If the M100 chip and robotics are viewed as a "call option" — if it fails, the downside is bounded (RMB 6.0B of R&D per year, RMB 60.0B over 10 years, backstopped by cash reserves), but if it succeeds, it could deliver tens of billions of RMB in incremental profit. The problem is that this option's strike price is high, and Li Auto's starting point (zero hardware legacy, a newly assembled team) means its odds of success are far lower than Tesla's.
