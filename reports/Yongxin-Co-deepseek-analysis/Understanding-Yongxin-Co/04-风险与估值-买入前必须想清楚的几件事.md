## Four things you must think through before buying Yongxin

Researching a company — the hard part isn't finding its strengths, it's being honest about its risks. The first three parts laid out Yongxin's business model, financial quality, and industry landscape: a shallow moat, weak pricing power, a mature and fragmented industry — but alongside that, a top-tier dividend culture and strict financial discipline.

This final part is where we make the call — how large the risks are, whether management can be trusted, and whether the current price is reasonable.

---

## Thing one: the new management team is the biggest source of uncertainty

In March 2025, Yongxin went through a major personnel change — the chair and the general manager were replaced at the same time:

| Position | New appointee | Background | Point of concern |
|------|------|------|--------|
| Chair | **Jiang Lei** | Born 1987, holds a master's in marketing, previously worked at Lego, Disney, Mattel, and General Mills | Reportedly the daughter of the previous chair, Jiang Jizhong, according to public information; **has no experience in the packaging industry** |
| General Manager | **Pan Jian** | Senior-grade engineer, recipient of a State Council special allowance | Promoted from within, a technical background, **deep industry experience** |
| Vice Chair | Bao Zuben | A company veteran | Assisting the transition |

*Data sources: Yongxin Co., Ltd. 2025 annual report, company announcements, public information from Xueqiu (the relationship between Jiang Lei and the previous chair comes from unofficial sources, with medium confidence)*

A few points worth noting here:

**1. Uncertainty around second-generation succession.** Jiang Lei's background at consumer-goods multinationals (Lego, Disney, General Mills) is quite different from packaging manufacturing. Consumer-goods marketing and industrial manufacturing sit in entirely different circles of competence. On the upside, this could bring fresh perspective (branding, internationalization); on the downside, understanding the industry will take time.

**2. General Manager Pan Jian is the "ballast."** A technical background, promoted internally, a State Council special-allowance recipient — this is the classic profile of "someone who knows the business running operations." The Jiang Lei + Pan Jian pairing, if it works well, complements "strategy + execution"; if it doesn't, it could produce directional disagreements.

**3. The historical pattern of management transitions.** Second-generation succession cases in China's A-share market don't have a particularly high success rate. But there are exceptions too — the key is whether the new management respects the existing operating discipline, especially the dividend culture and financial conservatism.

**Current assessment: at least two full fiscal years (2026-2027) are needed to verify the new management's performance.** During this period, watch three indicators: whether the net margin stabilizes, whether the payout ratio is maintained, and whether capital allocation turns aggressive.

**Counterpoint:** Yongxin's actual controller is the Huangshan Supply and Marketing Cooperative (a collectively-owned entity), not a pure family enterprise. The collective-ownership background may impose institutional constraints on management, guarding against a "second generation squandering the business." Also, the fact that Jiang Lei spent many years at multinationals before choosing to return suggests she came prepared — this isn't a case of a purely "parachuted princess."

## Thing two: the "real" and the "illusory" in shareholder returns

Yongxin's shareholder returns are exemplary among A-share companies, but one must distinguish "real returns" from "paper returns."

### Dividends: real money

| Year | Dividend per 10 shares (RMB) | Total dividend (RMB) | Payout ratio | Dividend yield |
|------|------------------|---------------|-------|-------|
| 2022 | 5.2 | 306 million | 86.8% | ~4.5% |
| 2023 | 5.5 | 337 million | 83.6% | ~4.8% |
| 2024 | 5.5+0.7 (interim) | 380 million | **81.2%** | **5.66%** |
| 2025 | 2.7+interim (est.) | 337 million | **76.9%** | **~4.7%** |

*Data sources: Yongxin Co., Ltd. historical annual reports, Wugui Quant. The 2024 dividend yield is calculated using the April 23 closing price.*

The 2025 payout ratio dipped slightly (from 81.2% to 76.9%), mainly due to the decline in profit. But a 76.9% payout ratio is still top-tier among A-share companies.

### Ownership structure: are interests aligned?

| Shareholder | Ownership stake | Nature |
|------|---------|------|
| Huangshan Yongjia Investment | **33.09%** | Controlling shareholder (a subsidiary of the Huangshan Supply and Marketing Cooperative, collectively owned) |
| ORG Technology | **22.20%** | Strategic shareholder (a metal-packaging leader) |
| Others | ~44.71% | Institutions + retail |

*Data source: Yongxin Co., Ltd. 2025 annual report*

The controlling shareholder holds a 33% stake, and dividends are an important source of income for it — meaning the controlling shareholder's and minority shareholders' interests are aligned on dividends. **This is an institutional guarantee of "real returns."**

At the same time, zero share pledges — the controlling shareholder has not pledged its shares to borrow money — makes for strong financial safety.

**Key question: if profit keeps declining, will the dividend shrink dramatically?**

In 2025, net profit fell to RMB 438 million, with a dividend of RMB 337 million, a payout ratio of 76.9%. If net profit in 2026 continues to fall below RMB 400 million (assuming a 10% decline), even maintaining a 77% payout ratio would bring total dividends below RMB 300 million, and the corresponding dividend yield could fall below 4%.

**A 4% dividend yield would still be decent, but the appeal of "high dividend" would be diminished.** Dividend investors need to watch not just the current yield but the sustainability of the payout.

## Thing three: the risk checklist

Listing out every risk Yongxin faces and assessing its severity one by one:

| Risk | Severity | Notes |
|------|---------|------|
| **Raw material price volatility** | ★★★★★ | Synthetic resin accounts for about 58% of costs; for every 10% rise in crude oil, gross margin falls by roughly 1.2-1.5 percentage points (source: Tianfeng Securities) |
| **Industry price war** | ★★★★☆ | CR5 is only 6%; low-end competition is intense |
| **Management generational change** | ★★★☆☆ | Second-generation succession + no industry experience; needs an observation period |
| **2025 "revenue up, profit down"** | ★★★★☆ | A clear signal of weakening pricing power |
| **Cash flow deterioration** | ★★★☆☆ | Free cash flow cut in half to RMB 165 million, but linked to capacity expansion |
| **Environmental substitution** | ★★☆☆☆ | A 5-10 year window, not an immediate threat |
| **Plastic restriction policy** | ★★☆☆☆ | Core products are not on the plastic-ban catalog |
| **Customer concentration** | ★★★☆☆ | The top five customers are estimated to account for 25-35% |

**The biggest risk isn't any single factor, but the compounding of "chronic ailments"** — weak pricing power + uncontrollable raw material costs + slowing industry growth. Together, these three mean margins face sustained downward pressure.

**Counterpoint:** Yongxin has survived and remained profitable in this environment for the past 20 years. Raw material prices move in cycles, not in one direction. During the 2023-2024 raw material downcycle, Yongxin's net margin rose from 12.26% to 13.37%. One shouldn't draw conclusions from a single year's data (2025) alone.

Yongxin's PE is the highest among its peers, but so are its ROE and dividend yield. **The PE premium roughly matches its edge in ROE and dividend yield.**

### Three scenarios

Rather than precise calculations (which would give a false sense of precision), here is a directional assessment only:

**Scenario one (optimistic): new management delivers strong results + new film capacity contributes incremental volume + raw material prices stay low**
- Net profit recovers to above RMB 500 million; PE rebounds to 18-20x
- Corresponding share price of RMB 15-16, upside of about 30-40%
- This scenario requires several conditions to hold simultaneously; the probability is not high

**Scenario two (neutral): earnings flat or modestly higher, current landscape maintained**
- Net profit in the RMB 400-450 million range; PE maintained at 14-16x
- Corresponding share price of RMB 10-12; limited upside
- But collecting a 4.7% dividend each year, cumulative return over 3-5 years of about 20-25%

**Scenario three (conservative): profit keeps declining + new management underperforms**
- Net profit falls below RMB 350 million; PE compresses to 10-12x
- Corresponding share price of RMB 7-8, downside of 30-40%
- The dividend yield would fall, weakening the "bond-like" logic

**Key judgment: the current PE of 16x is not expensive, but the margin of safety is not sufficient.** In the neutral scenario, returns mainly come from the dividend, with limited capital appreciation; in the conservative scenario, the downside exceeds the optimistic scenario's upside.

## What type of investor is Yongxin suited for

Pulling together the analysis from the first four parts, Yongxin Co., Ltd. is best suited to this type of investor:

**Suitable for:**
- Those who value cash dividends and can accept low growth
- Those who have a "bond-like" allocation need (dividend yield of 4.7% versus a government bond yield of 1.7%)
- Those who keep the position size very small (recommended **no more than 2%**)
- Those with the patience to wait for a better entry price

**Not suitable for:**
- Those pursuing capital appreciation and high growth
- Those uncomfortable with a "shallow moat"
- Those who need a clear growth catalyst
- Those who cannot accept the possibility of continued profit decline

## A closing note

Yongxin Co., Ltd. is not a company that will get anyone excited. It won't double, it won't ignite a hot topic, and it won't show up as a hot discussion at investor gatherings.

But in a world of persistently falling interest rates, a company that has paid dividends for 20 consecutive years, with a 4.7% dividend yield and strict financial discipline, has its own value — as long as you don't hold unrealistic expectations of it.

Understanding Yongxin is, at its core, understanding an investment trade-off: **are you willing to accept an ordinary business in exchange for a stable cash dividend?**

Everyone's answer is different. What this series has done is simply lay out the facts needed to make that judgment.
