# The Hidden Champion of the Flexible Packaging Industry

## A company you encounter every day but have never noticed

Among the more than 5,000 listed companies on the A-share market, you could count on your fingers the ones that have achieved "20 consecutive years of dividends, a dividend yield close to 5%, and ROE consistently above 15%." Yongxin Co. (002014.SZ) is one of them — yet most investors have never even heard its name.

Founded in 1992 and headquartered in Huangshan, Anhui, the company's core business is **printed composite flexible packaging materials** and **plastic flexible-packaging film** — that is, making packaging bags for food, household-chemical, and pharmaceutical companies. The potato-chip bag you ate from today, the milk carton you drank from, the laundry-detergent pouch you used — there's a good chance it came from Yongxin. It sounds anything but glamorous. But its financial statements hide a set of numbers that don't match its obscurity at all.

| Metric | Value | Scale assessment |

Put these numbers together: **a company with 20 consecutive years of dividends, a cumulative payout ratio near 78%, a dividend yield of 4.7%, and ROE consistently above 17%, yet with a market cap of only RMB 7.2 billion and a PE of just 16x.**

If you're a dividend investor, these numbers make your eyes light up.
If you're a growth investor, these numbers leave you completely uninterested.

Who's right? The answer is that **both are seeing something true, but neither is seeing the whole picture**.

## What exactly is Yongxin Co.

In one sentence:

> **Yongxin Co. = a midstream packaging manufacturer with excellent financial discipline + one of the A-share market's most generous dividend payers + a business with a shallow moat but the ability to earn steady profits.**

Unpacking that, three things stand out.

### The first thing: product mix

Yongxin's revenue comes from four product lines (2025 annual-report data, source: StockStar):

| Product | Revenue (RMB billion) | Share | YoY growth | Gross margin |
|------|-----------|------|---------|-------|
| Printed composite packaging | **2.55** | **68.5%** | +2.95% | **25.42%** |
| Plastic flexible-packaging film | **0.83** | **22.3%** | **+14.7%** | 14.83% |
| Ink | 0.173 | 4.6% | +11.29% | — |
| Aluminized packaging | 0.062 | 1.7% | +5.64% | — |

The core business is **printed composite packaging** (nearly 70% of revenue), while the fastest-growing segment is **plastic flexible-packaging film** (+14.7% YoY). Although the film business has a lower gross margin (14.83%), its growth is significant, and it is becoming the company's second growth curve — a project with 30,000 tonnes/year of biaxially oriented multifunctional film capacity is expected to come online in 2026.

**Counterargument:** the film business's gross margin, under 15%, is far below the 25% of printed packaging. If incremental growth mainly comes from the lower-margin business, overall profitability could continue to be diluted. This is already happening: 2025 blended gross margin fell from 23.78% to 23.00%.

### The second thing: customer structure

Yongxin's downstream customers are brand owners in the **food, household-chemical, and pharmaceutical** industries — which implies two characteristics:

1. **Stable demand**: people need to eat, do laundry, and take medicine. Packaging demand tracks consumption frequency, so cyclicality is weak
2. **Powerful customers**: major brand owners (think Unilever, P&G, Yili) have far greater bargaining power than their packaging suppliers

The top five customers are estimated to account for **25%-35%** of revenue (the company has not disclosed a complete customer list; this is a brokerage estimate). Customer concentration isn't extremely high, but relationships with major customers are stable — switching packaging suppliers requires re-certification, testing, and trial production, a cycle of roughly 3-6 months.

### The third thing: geographic structure

In 2025, Yongxin's domestic sales were **RMB 3.05 billion** (+3.09%) and export sales were **RMB 560 million** (**+24.04%**). The export share rose from 12.81% to **15.05%**.

Exports are part of the incremental growth story. But it's worth noting that the rapid growth in exports is partly attributable to exchange-rate factors, and its sustainability remains to be seen.

## What happened in 2025: revenue up, profit down

2025 is a year worth focusing on — it's the first time Yongxin's profit has declined.

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Revenue | RMB 3.525 billion | RMB 3.723 billion | +5.61% |
| Net profit | RMB 468 million | **RMB 438 million** | **-6.33%** |
| Net profit excluding non-recurring items | RMB 440 million | **RMB 409 million** | **-7.17%** |
| Gross margin | 23.78% | **23.00%** | -0.78pct |
| Net margin | 13.37% | **11.87%** | -1.50pct |
| Operating cash flow | RMB 459 million | **RMB 343 million** | **-25.3%** |
| ROE | 19.18% | **17.34%** | -1.84pct |

*Source: Yongxin Co. 2024, 2025 annual reports*

**Revenue is growing, profit is declining, and cash flow is deteriorating.** These three signals appearing together usually mean one thing: the company lacks pricing power and cannot pass rising costs on to customers.

That said, Q1 2026 showed a slight improvement: revenue of RMB 940 million (+10.81%), net profit of RMB 91 million (+2.06%) — a stabilizing signal, though net margin fell further to 9.78%, and the trend still needs to be watched.

**Counterargument:** the 2025 profit decline may be a short-term fluctuation (a raw-material price upcycle) rather than a long-term trend. Over the past 5 years, net profit CAGR has been about 8.5%, and a single year's pullback should not be over-interpreted.

## Understanding Yongxin requires answering three questions

Putting the above together, Yongxin Co. can be summarized through three questions:

**Question one: where is this business's ceiling?**
The flexible packaging industry is large in scale (hundreds of billions of yuan), but Yongxin's market share is only about **2%** (estimated on a printed composite packaging basis). A low market share suggests growth room exists in theory; but the extreme fragmentation of the industry (CR5 only 6%) also means consolidation is proceeding very slowly.

**Question two: is the 5% dividend yield sustainable?**
20 consecutive years of dividends, a cumulative payout ratio of 78% — this record is extremely rare on the A-share market. But in 2025, while the payout ratio was still 76.89%, the dividend amount fell from RMB 380 million to RMB 337 million (due to the profit decline). If profit keeps falling, the absolute dividend amount will shrink too.

**Question three: is the new management reliable?**
In March 2025, Yongxin completed a management generational change — new Chairwoman **Jiang Lei** (born 1987, previously at Lego, Disney, and General Mills, reportedly the daughter of the former chairman per public information) and new General Manager **Pan Jian** (senior engineer, recipient of a State Council special allowance, promoted from within). The chairman and general manager positions were replaced at the same time, and the new chairwoman has no packaging-industry experience — this is currently one of the biggest uncertainties.

These three questions will be addressed one by one in the next three installments.

## A brief preview: is Yongxin cheap right now?

First, a few anchor numbers (detailed analysis in Part 04):

| Metric | Value |

| Current share price (mid-May 2026) | ~RMB 11.7 |
| PE (TTM) | ~16x |

| Current PE percentile | ~30% (relatively low) |
| PB | 2.72x |
| Dividend yield | ~4.7% |
| 10-year government bond yield | ~1.7% |

A dividend yield of 4.7% versus the 10-year government bond yield of 1.7% is a premium of about **300 basis points**. This yield spread is quite attractive among high-dividend names on the A-share market.

But the other side of the coin is: **if profit keeps declining, the 4.7% dividend yield is only "static" — the dynamic figure could shrink.** Is this a "high-dividend trap" or an "undervalued cash cow"? We'll dig into it in detail in Part 04.
