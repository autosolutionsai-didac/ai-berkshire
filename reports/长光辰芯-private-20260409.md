## Part One: Business Model and Product Analysis (Duan Yongping's Lens)

### 1.1 Company Overview

Gpixel was founded in 2012, spun out of the Changchun Institute of Optics, Fine Mechanics and Physics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences ("Changchun Institute of Optics"), and is China's leading design house for high-performance CMOS image sensors (CIS). The company operates a fabless (no in-house wafer fab) model, focusing on chip design and testing.

**Core positioning**: Gpixel does not make smartphone CIS (that's the red ocean of Sony, Samsung, and OmniVision) — instead it focuses on four high-end niche markets: industrial imaging, scientific imaging, professional imaging, and medical imaging.

### 1.2 Breakdown of the Nine CIS Product Families

| Product family | Application | Key specs | Competitive position |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **GMAX series** | High-end industrial area-scan (machine vision) | Global shutter, 271-megapixel, 1.5μm pixel pitch | Benchmarked against Sony's IMX series |
| **GSENSE series** | Scientific imaging (astronomy, microscopy) | 10μm large pixels, 0.75e⁻ read noise | World-leading |
| **GL series** | Industrial line-scan (printing inspection, etc.) | 16K resolution, 120kHz line rate | China No. 1 |
| **GS series** | General-purpose industrial area-scan | Global shutter, value-for-money positioning | Main force for import substitution |
| **8K image sensor** | Professional cinema (partnership with DJI) | 49-megapixel, 8K@120fps | Breakthrough product |
| **GXS medical series** | Single-use endoscopy | 1.5μm BSI, 1/19-inch optical format | Emerging market |
| **High-end scientific series** | Aerospace/defense remote sensing | 150-megapixel full-frame | Only domestic supplier |
| **Line-scan series** | Food/textile inspection | High-speed line scanning | Fast-growing |
| **Custom products** | Special customer requirements | Custom design services | High value-add |

### 2.2 Drivers of the Gross Margin's Move from 59% to 67%

Gross margin has traced a "V-shape," and the drivers deserve unpacking:

**2022: 76.2% (abnormally high)**:
- The year included delivery of a large order of especially high-priced, high-end line-scan sensors
- Product mix skewed high-end, with a high average selling price

**2023-2024: decline to 59%**:
- Expansion into the mainstream industrial market, with value-oriented products introduced
- Tiered pricing concessions to large customers
- Rising share of lower-margin products in the mix

**2025: recovery to 66.9% (+7.9 percentage points)**:
- Ramp of high-end new products (271-megapixel products such as the GMAX15271BSI) lifting average selling price
- Incremental contribution from high value-add products such as the 8K image sensor
- High gross margin on new-field products such as medical imaging
- Scale effects lowering unit foundry cost
- Product-mix optimization (rising share of high-end products)

> **Buffett would say**: "I like the story of a recovering gross margin, but the question is whether it's sustainable. If the company starts cutting prices again to chase market share, the gross margin will fall right back down."

## Part Four: Risk and Governance Analysis (Li Lu's Lens)

### 4.1 In-Depth Assessment of the Founding Team

**Wang Xinyang**, male, born 1980 (age 46), Founder, Chairman and CEO

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Education | Bachelor's from Zhejiang University → Master's from University of Southampton → PhD from TU Delft (CMOS direction) |
| Industry experience | Cypress Semiconductor (6 months) → CMOSIS (4 years, a Belgian CIS company) |
| Entrepreneurial history | Returned to China in 2012 to found Gpixel; a technical founder |
| Point of controversy | Retained his position at Changchun Institute of Optics for a long time, only fully separating in 2022 |
| Shareholding | Husband and wife combined 49.53% (absolute control) |

**Team characteristics**:
- **Strengths**: solid technical grounding; a technical lineage from TU Delft and Belgium's CMOSIS representing an orthodox pedigree in global CIS design
- **Weaknesses**: an academically oriented team with limited commercialization experience. Multiple directors, supervisors, and executives previously held concurrent posts at Changchun Institute of Optics — a history of "one foot in each boat"
- **Zhang Yanxia** (co-founder, Wang Xinyang's wife): a Zhejiang University background, involved in core management — a husband-and-wife-run enterprise

### 4.2 Digging Into the Reasons for the STAR Market Withdrawal

**Official explanation**: "considering the lengthy A-share review process, overall market sentiment, and a global strategy adjustment"

**Actual reasons** (based on comprehensive analysis):

1. **Valuation reasonableness questioned**: valuation went from RMB 2.6 billion in 2021 to RMB 10 billion in 2022 — a nearly fourfold jump in a year — drawing pointed inquiry from the Shanghai Stock Exchange
2. **Insufficient transparency on related-party transactions**: complex related-party dealings with the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Changchun Institute of Optics system, with significant discrepancies in the related-party transaction amounts disclosed in the A-share versus Hong Kong prospectuses
3. **Weak earnings growth**: revenue was nearly flat from 2022 to 2023 (RMB 604 million → RMB 605 million), making it hard to justify a RMB 10 billion valuation
4. **Equity-incentive controversy**: large equity incentive grants and dividends during the reporting period — paying dividends even while posting losses — drew scrutiny
5. **Tightening review environment**: STAR Market review has tightened, and the pass rate for semiconductor companies has fallen

> **Li Lu would say**: "Withdrawing from the STAR Market isn't necessarily a bad thing. The key is understanding whether the company genuinely chose Hong Kong for strategic reasons, or was forced there because it couldn't clear A-share review. Judging by the sharpness of the inquiry letters, the latter looks more likely."

### 4.3 Related-Party Transaction Issues

| Related party | Transaction type | Risk level |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Changchun Institute of Optics | Technical services, staff secondment | ★★★ |
| Aopu Photonics (25.56% stake) | Potential business ties | ★★ |
| Luster LightTech (10.22% stake) | Both shareholder and customer | ★★★★ |
| "Customer A" | Ongoing large-scale related-party transactions | ★★★★ |

**Core issue**: there is a significant discrepancy between the related-party transaction amounts disclosed in the A-share prospectus and those disclosed in the Hong Kong prospectus — a serious red flag.

### 4.4 Fabless Supply-Chain Risk

| Stage | Supplier | Substitutability | Risk |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| **Wafer foundry** | **Tower Semiconductor (Israel)** | **Extremely hard to replace** | **★★★★★** |
| Chip packaging | Mostly overseas + a small amount from Changguang Yuanxin | Moderate | ★★★ |
| Design EDA tools | Cadence/Synopsys | Almost impossible to replace | ★★★★ |
| IP cores | Partly outsourced | Moderate | ★★ |

**Dependence on Tower Semiconductor is the biggest risk**:
- Tower is an Israeli company (now acquired by Intel) that specializes in mature-node specialty processes
- Gpixel's CIS chip process (45nm+) happens to be exactly Tower's strength
- No domestic foundry currently can fully replace Tower's CIS specialty process
- When Tower's capacity is tight, Gpixel could face delivery delays

### 4.5 Sanctions Risk Assessment

| Risk scenario | Probability | Impact |
|---------|------|------|
| Tower barred from foundry work for Chinese companies | 15-20% | **Devastating** — core products cannot be manufactured |
| EDA tool cutoff | 10% | Severe — R&D grinds to a halt |
| Gpixel placed on the Entity List | 5-10% | Severe — overseas revenue drops to zero |
| U.S. expands semiconductor controls on China | 60% | Moderate — indirect effect on the supply chain |

**Key judgment**: Gpixel's CIS products serve industrial, scientific, and medical applications and do not (on the surface) involve military-sensitive uses, so the probability of direct sanctions is relatively low. But its origins in the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Changchun Institute of Optics, and the possibility that some products are used in fields such as aerospace remote sensing, raise the risk of being placed on the Entity List.

Indirect sanctions risk in the foundry stage deserves more attention: U.S. export controls on Chinese semiconductors keep escalating, and if TSMC/Tower and others are required to impose broader restrictions on Chinese companies, Gpixel could become collateral damage.

### 4.6 Market Ceiling Constraint

This is Gpixel's most fundamental structural problem:

- The global industrial-plus-scientific CIS market totals roughly RMB 20 billion
- Even if Gpixel reaches 20% global share: RMB 4 billion in revenue
- Even maintaining a 67% gross margin and 35% net margin: RMB 1.4 billion in net profit
- Applying a 30x PE: RMB 42 billion market cap

**Theoretical market-cap ceiling: roughly RMB 42 billion (roughly HK$46 billion)**, about 2.6x the current HK$17.4 billion.

But reaching 20% share would mean wresting substantial market share away from Sony and onsemi — an extremely difficult task.

### 4.7 Worst-Case Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Probability | Consequence |
|------|------|------|
| **Tower cutoff** | 15% | Core products halted for 6-12 months, revenue cut in half |
| **Industry downturn + customer attrition** | 20% | Revenue falls back to RMB 600 million, net profit drops to RMB 100 million |
| **Import substitution falls short** | 25% | Growth stalls at the RMB 1 billion revenue level |
| **Sanctions plus cutoff combined** | 5% | Existential crisis for the company |
| **Worst-case composite scenario** | — | Revenue falls to RMB 400 million, losses incurred, market cap falls to HK$3-5 billion |

> **Li Lu would say**: "When investing in Chinese tech companies, geopolitical risk must be treated as a core variable. Gpixel depends on Israeli foundry capacity, uses American EDA tools, and exports products to more than 30 countries — a failure at any single link could be fatal. The import-substitution story is exciting to tell, but the foundry and the tools themselves haven't been substituted yet — that is the greatest irony of all."

## Part Five: Overall Assessment and Investment Recommendation

### 5.1 SWOT Analysis

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|------|------|
| **Internal** | **S - Strengths**: technical specs approaching Sony's; China's No. 1 in industrial/scientific CIS; high-gross-margin fabless model; strong customer stickiness | **W - Weaknesses**: small revenue scale (RMB 857 million); low market ceiling; supply chain dependent on overseas suppliers; commercialization capability of the academically oriented team still unproven |
| **External** | **O - Opportunities**: medical CIS boom (29% CAGR); robotic vision tailwind; 8K cinema demand; policy support for import substitution | **T - Threats**: geopolitical sanctions risk; possible Tower cutoff; Sony competing down-market; elevated valuation |

### 5.2 Core Investment Logic

**Bull case**:
1. China's No. 1, and world's No. 3, in industrial/scientific CIS — a scarce asset
2. Gross margin of 67%+ and net margin of 34% — strong earning power
3. Three incremental use cases — medical, cinema, and robotics — expanding the TAM
4. A core beneficiary of the broader import-substitution trend
5. 2025 revenue growth of 27% — an inflection point in growth has appeared

**Bear case**:
1. IPO PE of 54.5x is expensive pricing, leaving investors an insufficient margin of safety
2. The market ceiling of roughly RMB 20 billion means even a best-case outcome caps market cap around RMB 40 billion
3. The supply chain is heavily dependent on overseas suppliers (Tower + EDA) — the "substitute" for imports is itself at risk of being choked
4. Doubts about the STAR Market withdrawal have not been fully resolved
5. Governance flaws such as discrepancies in related-party transaction disclosure and a sharp short-term valuation spike

### 5.3 Valuation Range and Investment Recommendation

| Scenario | 2026E net profit | PE applied | Implied market cap (RMB) | vs. IPO market cap |
|------|-----------|--------|------------------|-----------|
| Optimistic | RMB 450 million | 40x | RMB 18 billion | +13% |
| Neutral | RMB 380 million | 35x | RMB 13.3 billion | -17% |
| Pessimistic | RMB 300 million | 25x | RMB 7.5 billion | -53% |

**Investment recommendation**:

> **Wait and see.** The IPO pricing already fully reflects growth expectations and a scarcity premium. It is advisable to wait for the following conditions before considering a position:
>
> 1. **The share price pulls back 20-30% post-listing** (PE falling into the 35-40x range)
> 2. **The 2026 interim results confirm sustained growth** (revenue growth >25%)
> 3. **Substantive progress on supply-chain self-sufficiency** (domestic foundry substitution solutions landing)
>
> If all three conditions are met simultaneously, a position of no more than 5% of the portfolio could be considered.

### 5.4 Key Metrics to Track

| Metric | Frequency | Threshold |

| Revenue growth | Semi-annual | >20% supports the valuation; <10% pressures the valuation |
