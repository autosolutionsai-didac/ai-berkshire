## A set of numbers that will make you rethink NetEase Games

First, look at where NetEase Games sits within China's gaming industry:

| Metric | Data | Note |
|------|------|------|
| 2025 gaming revenue | 92.1 billion yuan (+11%) | Second in China, behind only Tencent |
| Share of NetEase's total revenue | 82% | The absolute core, the company's lifeline |
| Longest-running title | Fantasy Westward Journey (launched 2003, 23 years) | Record peak concurrent users of 3.58 million |
| Biggest overseas push | Marvel Rivals | First-month global revenue of $136 million |
| Overseas revenue share | ~10% | Tencent's is 30% |
| R&D spend | 17.7 billion yuan (15.7% of revenue) | A heavily R&D-driven DNA |

92.1 billion yuan in gaming revenue, growing at 11% — that's a top-tier result by global gaming industry standards. But this elephant has one obvious trait: **it dances well at home, but is still learning to walk on the international stage.**

The core question this episode answers: **can NetEase Games' 92.1 billion yuan keep growing? Where would that growth come from?**

## I. Evergreen titles: NetEase Games' "perpetual motion machine"

What's most astonishing about NetEase Games isn't its ability to produce hits — it's **the ability to keep a game alive for 20+ years and still hit new records.**

### Fantasy Westward Journey: a 23-year-old cash cow that refuses to age

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Launch date | 2003 |
| Years in operation | 23 |
| Peak concurrent users | **3.58 million (all-time record)** |
| Business model | Hybrid subscription + item-based monetization |
| Platforms | PC + mobile |

A game launched in 2003 set an all-time concurrent-user record in 2025 — a feat that's nearly impossible to find a second example of anywhere in the global gaming industry.

Why has Fantasy Westward Journey lasted this long? Three reasons:

**First, extremely deep social lock-in.** Fantasy Westward Journey's core gameplay revolves around social systems — guilds, in-game marriage, master-apprentice relationships. A player who has played for over a decade has friends in their guild, an in-game "spouse," and tens of thousands of yuan in virtual assets on their account — switching costs are enormous.

**Second, a self-sustaining economic system.** Fantasy Westward Journey runs an extremely complex virtual economy — item trading between players, gem crafting, equipment forging all form a self-perpetuating economic loop. The more mature this system becomes, the harder it is for players to leave.

**Third, continuous content updates.** 23 years of uninterrupted version updates, new expansions, and new gameplay features have kept veteran players engaged while continually attracting new ones.

**The counter-argument**: even the strongest evergreen game has a lifecycle. Fantasy Westward Journey's core user base is aging — the 30-40-year-old players who make up its main demographic have strong spending power but ever less time. Younger users' interest in this style of "classic" MMO is declining. A peak of 3.58 million concurrent users may already be close to a ceiling rather than a starting point.

### Naraka: Bladepoint — a litmus test for global potential

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Launch date | 2021 |
| Fourth-anniversary result | Topped Steam's global bestseller chart |
| Genre | Action combat (fighting + battle royale) |
| Distinguishing feature | Asian-style action aesthetics |

Naraka: Bladepoint proves that NetEase isn't uncompetitive in the global market — its combat system has won recognition from players worldwide. Topping Steam's global bestseller chart on its fourth anniversary shows this title's vitality hasn't faded.

But Naraka: Bladepoint also exposes a structural weakness for NetEase: **a single point of success is hard to replicate into a systematic global capability.** Naraka's overseas success stems more from the quality of the product itself than from any global distribution and operations network NetEase has built.

### Eggy Party: a key step toward a younger audience

Eggy Party represents NetEase's major push into the "all-ages" segment — targeting younger users and female players, with a light, cute style and rich user-generated content. This title fills a gap NetEase previously had in the casual-gaming space.

But Eggy Party also faces intense competition — it is essentially fighting for the same slice of user time as Tencent's mini-program games and various other casual titles.

## II. Marvel Rivals: the biggest variable

If there's only one variable in the entire NetEase Games story worth tracking closely, it's Marvel Rivals.

### The full data picture

| Stage | Data | Note |
|------|------|------|
| First month after launch | $136 million in global revenue | One of the standout new titles of 2025 |
| Cumulative players | 40 million | A staggering user base accumulated in a short time |
| Peak concurrent users | 640,000 | Early in launch |
| Trough concurrent users | **96,000** | An 85% drop from peak |
| Recovered concurrent users | **200,000+** | Rebounded after version updates |
| Steam positive-review rate | 63% | "Mixed" — not great |

### How should this data be read?

**$136 million in the first month shows the Marvel brand's massive drawing power.** Marvel is one of the world's most valuable entertainment brands, and NetEase securing development rights for this brand is itself a testament to its capabilities.

**Concurrent users crashing from 640,000 to 96,000 signals a problem with the initial experience.** An 85% drop in concurrent users is a serious warning sign for a competitive game. When large numbers of players try a game and leave, it usually means the core gameplay loop — pacing, matchmaking experience, character balance — did not meet expectations.

**The recovery from 96,000 back to 200,000+ shows the team correcting course quickly.** Version updates and operational adjustments pulled some players back. That's a positive signal, proof that the problem wasn't "unfixable."

**The 63% Steam positive-review rate shows there's still substantial dissatisfaction.** In the competitive-gaming space, a positive-review rate below 70% means the community's perception still needs continued repair. Positive-review rate is a lagging indicator — even if the most recent updates improved the experience, past negative reviews don't disappear.

### Can Marvel Rivals become an evergreen title?

This is the key question that will determine NetEase's growth story over the next 3-5 years.

**Evidence supporting evergreen status:**

1. **The Marvel brand's ongoing appeal.** Marvel films, comics, and animation continuously introduce new characters and storylines — each new character is natural material for in-game content updates. As long as the Marvel brand doesn't decline, the game has a continuous source of content.

2. **The particular advantage of the hero-team-shooter genre.** Marvel Rivals is a 6v6 team shooter — and the genre's flagship titles (Overwatch, Valorant) have all proven that a model of "continuous hero/character updates" can sustain player engagement for many years.

3. **Concurrent users have bottomed and are recovering.** The trend from 96,000 back up to 200,000+ is upward. If it can stabilize above 150,000-200,000 concurrent users, it will have the foundation for long-term operation.

**Evidence against evergreen status:**

1. **A 63% positive-review rate is a real handicap.** Competitive games depend heavily on community reputation — once labeled as "mostly negative reviews," new players' willingness to join drops significantly. Repairing a reputation is harder than repairing a product.

2. **The memory of an 85% crash in concurrent users is hard to erase.** Even though it has recovered to 200,000+ today, the market and players still remember it falling from 640,000 to 96,000. This "high open, low follow-through" narrative will suppress expectations for a long time.

3. **Brand licensing is a double-edged sword.** NetEase doesn't own the Marvel brand — it owns development rights. If Disney/Marvel declines to renew the contract when it expires, or raises the licensing fee, the game's economic model could change entirely. Brand-licensing risk is covered in detail in Episode 04.

**A reasonable expectation**: Marvel Rivals will most likely settle in as a "moderately successful" long-running title — with annual revenue in the $300-500 million range, rather than the $1.6-billion annualized run rate implied by its first month. If operations keep improving and new characters keep attracting players, it could edge into "minor evergreen" status. But reaching the level of a truly global evergreen title like League of Legends or Valorant — the current data doesn't support that expectation.

## III. The overseas struggle: how to break through the 10% ceiling?

### The current state

| Metric | NetEase | Tencent | Gap |
|------|------|------|------|
| Overseas gaming revenue | ~9 billion (estimate, 10% of gaming revenue) | ~77.4 billion (32% of gaming revenue) | ~8x |
| Globalization strategy | Primarily self-developed titles taken overseas | Acquisitions + self-development + publishing | Entirely different |
| Overseas studios | **7 closed** | Several mature studios in operation | — |

An 8x gap in overseas revenue is not something a game or two can close — it's a structural gap produced by two entirely different paths to globalization.

**Tencent's path: buying time with money.** Acquiring Supercell (Clash of Clans), taking a controlling stake in Riot (League of Legends), investing in Epic (Fortnite) — using capital operations to directly acquire top-tier global products and teams.

**NetEase's path: self-developed titles going global.** Relying on its own developed products to compete in global markets — lower cost, but also a lower success rate. The closure of 7 overseas studios is the concrete cost of this path.

### The lesson from closing 7 studios

The closure of 7 overseas studios is not a small number — it shows that NetEase's attempt to build localized R&D teams overseas ran into systemic difficulty.

The root of the difficulty: **a large part of game-development competitiveness depends on cultural understanding.** NetEase has extremely deep cultural insight in the Chinese market (23 years of running Fantasy Westward Journey on Chinese culture is proof of that), but this capability is very hard to simply replicate in Western or Japanese/Korean markets.

After closing the studios, NetEase's overseas strategy shifted more toward "develop in China, publish globally" — Marvel Rivals is an example of this model. This reduces cost risk, but also limits the possibility of "local innovation" in overseas markets.

**The counter-argument**: closing 7 studios can also be understood as "cutting losses in time" — Ding Lei recognized that the path of local overseas R&D wasn't working, shut it down decisively, and concentrated resources on "develop in China, publish globally." From a capital-allocation standpoint, this isn't necessarily a bad thing.

## IV. Mini-program games: absent from a 53.5-billion-yuan market

### How big a problem is this?

China's mini-program gaming market was worth 53.5 billion yuan in 2025 — almost equal to the combined revenue of NetEase Cloud Music and Youdao.

And NetEase's presence in this market is close to zero.

### Why the absence?

The main platform for mini-program games is WeChat — and WeChat belongs to Tencent.

| Dimension | Explanation |
|----------|------|
| Platform ownership | The WeChat mini-program ecosystem is fully controlled by Tencent |
| Distribution advantage | Tencent's own games naturally get more recommendations and traffic |
| Technical barriers | Mini-program games use a different technical framework from native games and need dedicated teams |
| User habits | Mini-program game users skew toward lighter, more fragmented play — a different DNA from the heavy games NetEase excels at |

**This isn't NetEase "not wanting to" enter this market — it's that the platform isn't in its own hands.** It's like a great restaurant that draws the short straw on location because a competitor owns the mall.

### How significant is the impact?

At Tencent's profit margins, a 53.5-billion-yuan market could generate more than 10 billion yuan in profit. NetEase's absence from this slice means missing out on tens of billions in potential profit every year — and this market is still growing rapidly.

**More important is the trend**: mini-program gaming is growing far faster than traditional mobile and PC games. If the mini-program gaming market grows to over 100 billion yuan in the next five years, NetEase's absence will become an increasingly large structural disadvantage.

**The counter-argument**: mini-program games may have lower profit margins than traditional mobile games — platform cuts, lower willingness to pay, shorter lifecycles. NetEase focuses on high-ARPU, heavy games, and its margins may actually be higher as a result. Being absent from mini-program games isn't necessarily bad — what matters is whether total profit can keep growing.

## V. Game licenses: an external environment that's improving

In 2025, the number of game licenses issued in China reached **1,771 — a seven-year high.**

| Year | Licenses issued | Environment |
|------|----------|------|
| 2021 | 755 | The license winter |
| 2022 | 512 | The harshest year |
| 2023 | 1,076 | Recovery begins |
| 2024 | ~1,300 (estimate) | Continued recovery |
| 2025 | **1,771** | **Seven-year high** |

The recovery in the volume of licenses issued is a clear positive for NetEase — more licenses mean more new games can launch. But the license tailwind applies to all game companies equally — whether NetEase can capture outsized gains from it depends on its own product pipeline.

## VI. Summary: the substance of the gaming empire

Summarizing this episode's analysis into a few points:

1. **Evergreen titles are NetEase Games' core moat** — Fantasy Westward Journey setting a record after 23 years, Naraka: Bladepoint topping Steam on its fourth anniversary — this ability to keep games "alive for a long time" is rare globally
2. **Marvel Rivals is the biggest variable** — a dazzling first month, then an 85% crash in concurrent users followed by a recovery to 200,000+; most likely a "moderate success" rather than a "super evergreen" title
3. **Overseas is the biggest weak point** — a 10% share, 7 studios closed, an 8x gap versus Tencent — the road to going global remains long
4. **Absence from the 53.5-billion-yuan mini-program gaming market is a structural disadvantage** — the platform isn't in NetEase's hands, and that won't change in the short term
5. **The licensing environment is improving** — 1,771 licenses marks a seven-year high, but this benefits the whole industry, not NetEase exclusively

**If you had to summarize NetEase's gaming empire in one sentence: extremely strong domestically, extremely weak overseas, growth depends on new titles and overseas expansion — and new titles and overseas expansion happen to be the most uncertain parts.**
