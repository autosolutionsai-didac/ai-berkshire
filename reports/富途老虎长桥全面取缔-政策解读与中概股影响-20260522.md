# Futu's 3.37 Million Stock Accounts Can Now Only Sell, Not Buy

*May 22, 2026*

---

**If you have an account with Futu, Tiger Brokers, or Longbridge, you need to understand today's news: starting now, your account can only sell, not buy, and the app will be completely shut down in 2 years.**

Below, in as few words as possible, is a clear explanation of the policy and its impact.

---

## I. What Exactly Has Been Banned

On May 22, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) officially announced: **Futu Securities, Tiger Brokers, and Longbridge Securities have engaged in illegal cross-border business operations, and the regulator plans to confiscate all illicit gains and impose severe penalties.**

On the same day, with State Council approval, the CSRC jointly issued, together with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security, the People's Bank of China, and other agencies — **eight departments** in total — the "Implementation Plan for the Comprehensive Rectification of Illegal Cross-Border Securities, Futures, and Fund Business Activities."

Here's the core content in plain language:

| Phase | Timing | What You Can Do |
|------|------|-----------|
| Concentrated rectification period | 2 years from now | **Sell and withdraw funds only** — buying and depositing funds are prohibited |
| After the rectification period ends | After 2 years | Domestic apps, websites, and servers **all shut down** |

The rectification targets are not limited to these three brokerages — they also include the domestic affiliated companies that helped recruit clients for them, illegal intermediaries, and even the self-media accounts that helped advertise them. **This isn't targeting a single point — it's taking down an entire chain.**

One important exception: the plan **does not restrict overseas institutions from providing services to investors located overseas**. In other words, if you are physically located abroad, you are not affected.

---

## II. Is Your Money Safe

The original text of the plan explicitly states that "investors' property safety will not be affected by the rectification." Behind this statement lie three layers of logic:

**First, the asset custody structure.** The Hong Kong stocks you buy through Futu are actually held in custody at the Hong Kong Stock Exchange's Central Clearing and Settlement System (CCASS); the U.S. stocks you buy are held in custody at the U.S. Depository Trust Company (DTCC). **Your shares don't live on Futu's servers — the app shutting down doesn't affect the existence of your shares.**

**Second, the investor protection mechanism.** Futu is a member of the U.S. SIPC, which provides up to $500,000 in protection per client for securities and cash. Client funds are held separately from company funds, so even if Futu runs into trouble, your assets would not be included in bankruptcy liquidation.

**Third, the plan is designed with an exit pathway.** The 2-year transition period, and the requirement that overseas institutions properly communicate with investors and arrange for account disposal, both show that policymakers anticipated the impact and built in a buffer.

**So what is being "shut down" is the trading channel — not your account or your assets.**

> Note: Futu itself is currently in good operating condition (2025 net profit up 101.9% year-over-year, client assets at HK$1.23 trillion, according to Futu's 2025 annual report), and there is no short-term risk of the company collapsing.

---

## III. Why This Time Is the Real Thing

Some might say, "Haven't they said this before?" This time really is different:

- **2021**: The CSRC issued a solo statement expressing "concern," with mild wording → Result: only new account openings were halted
- **Late 2022**: The CSRC pushed forward rectification, "curbing new inflows while resolving the existing stock" → Result: existing users continued as usual
- **May 2026 (today)**: **State Council approval + joint issuance by eight ministries + confiscation of illicit gains + a 2-year timetable for complete elimination**

The level has escalated from a "verbal warning" to a full "battle plan." The eight departments cover network blocking (Cyberspace Administration of China), fund interception (the People's Bank of China, State Administration of Foreign Exchange), and law enforcement crackdowns (Ministry of Public Security) — the entire chain has been arranged for.

**When regulators start designing an exit pathway for you, it means this door really is closing.**

---

## IV. Impact on Chinese Concept Stocks

Let's start with the data: Futu has 3.37 million clients with assets, and client assets total HK$1.23 trillion (according to the 2025 annual report). Adding Tiger Brokers and Longbridge, the combined user base affected by the three firms is in the millions, with assets exceeding HK$1 trillion. These users' holdings are concentrated most heavily in Chinese concept stocks.

When these accounts are restricted to "sell only," it creates **one-directional, sustained selling pressure**. But the impact is not evenly distributed:

### Large-Cap, Dual-Listed Chinese Concept Stocks (Alibaba, Tencent, JD.com, etc.)

These are listed simultaneously in the U.S. and Hong Kong. Investors forced to sell their U.S. shares can buy back the corresponding Hong Kong shares through the Stock Connect (Southbound) program. **While the U.S. side is under pressure, the Hong Kong side may actually receive inflows** — there is a mechanism to absorb the selling.

### Chinese Concept Stocks Listed Only in the U.S. (Pinduoduo, etc.) — the Hardest Hit

**Pinduoduo is listed only in the U.S. — it has no Hong Kong listing, so Stock Connect cannot absorb the selling.** The shares being forced onto the market have no legitimate channel of comparable scale to absorb them.

After the news broke, Pinduoduo's decline exceeded 6%. By comparison, dual-listed Chinese concept stocks fell relatively moderately over the same period, confirming the logic that "companies with a Hong Kong listing to absorb selling hold up better."

> The individual stocks mentioned above are used only as case studies and do not constitute any buy or sell recommendation.

### Small-Cap Chinese Concept Stocks — the Most Vulnerable

With small float, poor liquidity, and low institutional ownership, the same dollar amount of selling produces a multiplied decline. This category requires extra vigilance for stampede risk.

### How to Understand This Impact

**Company fundamentals haven't changed by a single cent because of this policy.** Pinduoduo's revenue and profit are still the same revenue and profit. Forced selling creates **price dislocation**, not **value destruction**.

But it must also be honestly acknowledged: if this capital, due to channel restrictions, no longer flows back into the U.S. stock market, there will indeed be a structural reduction in demand for Chinese concept stocks, which could to some degree affect the valuation center. The extent of the impact depends on what share this retail capital represents of total trading volume in Chinese concept stocks, and precise data on this is currently lacking.

## V. Known Options Available So Far

Here is a rundown of the options objectively available to existing users at present. Each path has its own pros and cons, and which one to choose depends on each individual's holdings, risk tolerance, and personal judgment.

**Account transfer (stock transfer):** Transfer shares directly from Futu to another overseas brokerage (such as Interactive Brokers), without needing to sell, and without triggering transaction taxes or fees. For the specific process, check "Stock Transfer Out" in the Futu app or contact customer service. Note, however, that Interactive Brokers and other overseas brokerages not named in the announcement may also face compliance risk over the medium-to-long term — the rectification plan targets all illegal cross-border business activity, not just these three firms.

**Rebuilding positions through Stock Connect (Southbound):** This applies to stocks like Alibaba, Tencent, and JD.com that are dual-listed in Hong Kong and the U.S. Open Stock Connect access through a mainland brokerage (there is a RMB 500,000 asset threshold), sell the U.S. shares, and buy the same company's Hong Kong shares through Stock Connect. But for companies listed only in the U.S., such as Pinduoduo, this path is not available.

**QDII funds:** Examples include the CSI Overseas China Internet ETF and the Nasdaq 100 ETF, which allow indirect investment in overseas markets. However, they don't allow precise control over individual stock positions, and QDII quotas are frequently tight.

**Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect:** Currently limited to residents of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.

**If you do nothing:** Within the 2-year period, the shares remain in the account — you can continue holding them but cannot add to the position. After 2 years, the app shuts down, but the shares remain in custody at the exchange's central clearing and settlement system and will not disappear. At that point, Futu is expected to issue a concrete account disposal plan (the plan requires overseas institutions to make the relevant arrangements).

---

## VI. How to Think About This

The essence of this matter: **A gray-area window through which ordinary Chinese investors could directly participate in global capital markets is being closed.**

Over the past several years, Futu and Tiger Brokers did one thing — they made it as easy for Chinese retail investors to buy U.S. and Hong Kong stocks as it is to buy A-shares. The experience was good and the barriers to entry were low, but from a legal standpoint, it had always existed in a gray area. This rectification is a cleanup of that gray area.

The window has closed, but the door remains open: **Stock Connect, QDII, and Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect** — these legitimate channels remain available. Frankly speaking, they fall short of Futu in the range of available securities, operational flexibility, and fee levels — that's a fact. But they are compliant, and they are sustainable.

A joint announcement from eight ministries, State Council approval, and a clear timetable — a policy of this magnitude has no historical precedent of being rescinded. Everyone needs to make their own judgment based on their own circumstances.
