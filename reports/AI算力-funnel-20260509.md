## Layer 2: Coarse Screen on 5 Hard Value-Investing Criteria

Screening criteria:
1. **Reasonable PE** (can be relaxed to PEG < 1.5 for high-growth names)
2. **ROE > 15%** or an improving trend
3. **Positive operating cash flow, equal to > 70% of net income**
4. **Debt-to-asset ratio < 60%**
5. **Moat quick-rating of at least ★★★**

Scoring: 1 point per criterion met, out of 5 total. A score of ≥ 4 is generally retained.

> **Sample-selection note**: Layer 2 focuses on companies among the 58 listed names from Layer 1 that have "complete 2025 annual reports, top-tier market caps, and hold key nodes in the value chain." Newly listed companies such as Moore Threads, MetaX, and Biren — which don't yet have a full year of financial reports — are placed on a watchlist.

## Layer 3: Detailed Analysis (9 Companies, 300-500 Words Each)

### 3.1 NVIDIA (NVDA.O)

**Business model**: AI-accelerated GPUs + the CUDA software ecosystem + NVLink high-speed interconnect + DGX/HGX reference system designs. FY2026 revenue was $215.9B (YoY +65%), with data-center revenue of $194B (YoY +68%). FY2026 Q4 alone came in at $68.1B (YoY +73%). The cadence of each GPU generation has compressed from two years to one (H100 → B100 → GB200 → Rubin).

**Financial quality**: FY2026 ROE of 76.3%, FCF of $96.7B, gross margin sustained above 70%. Debt-to-asset ratio of ~30%. FCF/net income exceeds 100% (including stock-compensation add-backs) — cash-flow quality is about as good as it gets.

**Moat**: The CUDA ecosystem (developer lock-in spanning 15+ years, covering millions of developers), the proprietary NVLink interconnect protocol, Mellanox networking, the DGX/HGX reference system design, and priority allocation from TSMC's most advanced process nodes. The software moat (CUDA) will most likely still be intact in 10 years, but a portion of the hardware moat will be eroded by ASICs (Google TPU/Broadcom XPU) and AMD's MI series.

**Key risks**: (1) The inference market is being substantially diverted to custom ASICs (already happening: Google/Meta/AWS are all developing in-house chips); (2) Repeated policy swings in the China market (H200 restrictions loosened, then tightened again); (3) A capex-bubble bust — the four major cloud providers may be nearing a capex inflection point.

**Valuation snapshot**: FY27 PE of 38 (consensus). PEG ~0.6. Not expensive relative to the rest of the Mag 7 on a PE basis. But at $4.82 trillion, the absolute market cap is the largest in history — getting back to a P/S below 10 would require revenue to at least double again. **Valuation is reasonable to slightly elevated, contingent on whether AI capex can double again.**

### 3.2 TSMC (TSM.N / 2330.TW)

**Business model**: Advanced-process wafer foundry. Nodes of 3nm/5nm/7nm account for 70%+ of revenue; AI accelerator makers (NVIDIA/AMD/Broadcom/Apple) depend on it almost entirely. 2025 revenue was $122.9B (YoY +31.6%), with 2026 consensus still calling for +30%.

**Financial quality**: 2025 gross margin of 59.9% (up 3.8pp from 2024), ROE of 35.4% (+5.1pp), FCF of $31.7B (+15%), D/E of 0.18, debt ratio of 12.5%. Every metric is textbook-quality.

**Moat**: Exclusive dominance in advanced processes (besides Samsung, no other player dares attempt 3nm at scale), 5+ years of accumulated EUV process learning curve, a highly concentrated and irreplaceable customer base (switching costs for NVIDIA/Apple are extremely high), and a scale barrier built from $40B+/year in capex. This moat will most likely be even deeper in 10 years — barring a geopolitical disruption.

**Key risks**: (1) Taiwan Strait geopolitics — the "sword of Damocles" hanging over the valuation; (2) A downturn in the AI capex cycle; (3) New US/Japan/German fabs failing to hit yield targets, dragging down returns on capital.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE ~25, PEG < 1. For a business with this level of certainty and ROE, a PE of 25 is what Buffett would call "buying a wonderful company at a fair price." **Valuation is reasonable to slightly cheap, with the discount mainly reflecting Taiwan Strait risk.**

### 3.3 Broadcom (AVGO.O)

**Business model**: Custom ASICs (XPU, with main customers Google TPU, Meta MTIA, and ByteDance) + networking chips (Tomahawk switch silicon, including CPO) + software (VMware). FY2025 FCF was $26.9B (+38.6%). FY2026 Q1 FCF was $8.01B. AI semiconductor revenue has grown rapidly for three consecutive quarters.

**Financial quality**: ROE of 33.37%, PE of 72 (high), but PEG of ~1. Leverage rose to ~50% because of the VMware acquisition (still rapidly deleveraging post-VMware). FCF is extremely strong.

**Moat**: (1) Deep integration with hyperscale customers (co-development cycles for ASICs run 18-24 months, making switching costs extremely high); (2) 70%+ market share in networking switch chips; (3) VMware's software business provides cycle-resilient cash flow. Ten years out, the moat around the ASIC business is questionable (cloud providers may deal more directly with TSMC), but the networking-chip and software businesses will most likely remain intact.

**Key risks**: (1) Google/Meta bringing ASIC design fully in-house (Google is already attempting this); (2) VMware integration falling short of expectations; (3) A PE of 72 implies extremely high growth expectations — once AI ASIC growth slows, downside risk to the stock is significant.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE of 72, FCF yield of ~2%. **Expensive.** Only worth buying if one believes AI ASICs represent a certain 5-year mega-trend.

### 3.4 SK Hynix (000660.KS)

**Business model**: World's #1 in HBM (62% share), deeply and exclusively tied to NVIDIA for HBM3E. A diversified DRAM/NAND memory business. FY2026 Q1 revenue was KRW 52.58 trillion (YoY +198%), operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion (YoY +405%), net margin ~77%.

**Financial quality**: ROE of 63%, D/E of 0.12 (net cash position), cash of KRW 34.9 trillion. HBM capacity is fully booked through 2026.

**Moat**: (1) HBM3E process leads Samsung by roughly half a generation; (2) A first-mover relationship with NVIDIA; (3) 5+ years of accumulated capex and process know-how. Ten years out, memory remains a cyclical business — this is the biggest variable. The HBM up-cycle could peak within 2-3 years.

**Key risks**: (1) Cyclicality (memory is a textbook cyclical stock — the company posted losses as recently as 2022); (2) The probability of Samsung overtaking it with HBM4; (3) An inflection point in AI training demand.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE ~10 (est.), P/B ~3, valuation at a cycle peak. **Cheap — if you believe AI memory demand extends beyond a single super-cycle; dangerous — if you value it as a cyclical stock.**

### 3.5 ASML (ASML.O)

**Business model**: The world's sole supplier of EUV lithography systems, and the global #1 in DUV. A mandatory chokepoint for every process node below 7nm.

**Financial quality**: ROE ~50% (est.), robust FCF, low debt-to-asset ratio. Order backlog exceeds two years.

**Moat**: Decades of accumulated expertise across physics, optics, and precision machinery — the only company in the world with mass-production EUV capability. It will most likely still be intact in 10 years — this is a civilization-scale technological barrier. **One of the deepest moats anywhere.**

**Key risks**: (1) Expanding US export restrictions on China (Low-NA EUV already banned, High-NA still pending); (2) Cyclical swings in semiconductor capex; (3) Customer adoption of High-NA EUV.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE ~30. **Reasonably valued.**

### 3.6 Arista Networks (ANET.N)

**Business model**: High-speed data-center Ethernet switches; has already surpassed Cisco in the data-center switch market. 2025 revenue was $9B (YoY +29%); the 2026 AI networking revenue target was raised from $2.75B to $3.25B.

**Financial quality**: ROE of 30%+ (est.), strong FCF, zero debt.

**Moat**: (1) A highly modular EOS software platform; (2) Deep partnerships with hyperscale customers; (3) Leading pace of high-speed Ethernet iteration. The 10-year moat is moderate — commoditization pressure in networking switches is a persistent threat, and NVIDIA's InfiniBand/Spectrum-X pose ongoing competition.

**Key risks**: (1) Encroachment from NVIDIA's networking solutions; (2) Price competition from Broadcom Tomahawk and white-box switches; (3) A downturn in the AI data-center buildout cycle.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE ~50 (est.). On the expensive side, but growth can absorb it.

### 3.7 Innolight (300308.SZ)

**Business model**: The global leader in optical modules, ramping 1.6T silicon photonics. 2026 Q1 revenue was RMB 19.5B (YoY +192%), net income RMB 5.735B (YoY +262%). 2025 annual report: net income of RMB 9.8-11.8B (YoY +89% to +128%).

**Financial quality**: ROE of 27.03%, debt-to-asset ratio of 29.7%. **Operating cash flow of RMB 3.165B against net income of ~RMB 11B, roughly 30% — this is the key weak point**, with working-capital expansion eating into cash flow. A surge in prepayments reflects upstream supply tightness; future cash-flow improvement bears watching.

**Moat**: (1) Deep ties to NVIDIA, Google, and Meta, with an edge in delivering 1.6T silicon-photonics products; (2) Scale economies; (3) Leading process yields. The 10-year moat is moderate — commoditization of optical modules and the risk of CPO disruption both exist.

**Key risks**: (1) **CPO (co-packaged optics) substitution risk** — once leading customers shift to CPO, the value of DSP-based pluggable optical modules shrinks sharply; (2) Insufficient in-house optical-chip supply, leaving the company constrained by the US/Coherent/Lumentum; (3) High customer concentration (top-4 North American customers account for >70%).

**Valuation snapshot**: PE of 77 based on 2025 net income, but 2026 growth should compress that to around PE ~35, with PEG < 1. **Valuation is reasonable to expensive — high upside, but downside risk is significant too.**

### 3.8 WUS Printed Circuit (002463.SZ)

**Business model**: High-speed PCBs (for data-center switches + AI servers). 2025 revenue was RMB 18.9B (YoY +42%), net income attributable to parent of RMB 3.82B (YoY +48%).

**Financial quality**: ROE of 28.57% (weighted), operating cash flow of RMB 3.872B ≈ 100% of net income (excellent). Debt-to-asset ratio of 46.5%. Clears every bar.

**Moat**: (1) High-end process capability for 800G/1.6T switch PCBs; (2) Deep partnerships with Broadcom and Arista; (3) Relatively early capex commitment (RMB 10B+). But industry oversupply risk exists in PCBs (industry-wide new capacity additions exceed RMB 40B). The 10-year moat is moderate-to-weak.

**Key risks**: (1) Potential oversupply in high-end PCB capacity in 2027-2028; (2) High customer concentration; (3) Slow onboarding of alternative customers.

**Valuation snapshot**: PE of ~30 with ROE of 28%. **Valuation is reasonable to slightly cheap — among the cleanest financial-quality names in Layer 2.**

### 3.9 Victory Giant Technology (300476.SZ)

**Business model**: World's #1 in AI-compute PCBs (13.8% global market share). 2025 net income was RMB 4.312B (YoY +273.52%), the world's only company mass-producing 8-layer-pair/28-layer HDI boards. 2026 Q1 net income was RMB 1.288B, with operating cash flow of RMB 2.117B (up +399% YoY).

**Financial quality**: Operating cash flow of RMB 4.603B against net income of RMB 4.312B, roughly 107%. **Financial quality is strong.** Debt-to-asset ratio is reasonable.

**Moat**: (1) The world's only mass-produced 8-layer-pair/28-layer HDI process; (2) Deep ties with leading GPU makers such as NVIDIA/Anduril; (3) Scale economies + leading yields. The 10-year moat is moderate — PCB manufacturing is, at the end of the day, a manufacturing business, and process advantages get caught up to.

**Key risks**: (1) Extremely high customer concentration in NVIDIA; (2) Industry oversupply risk; (3) Competitors catching up in process capability (Zhen Ding Technology expanding capacity by RMB 23.3B, Shennan Circuits, etc.).

**Valuation snapshot**: PE of ~50 (est.), PEG < 1. **Valuation is reasonable to expensive, but it has the strongest growth profile of the group.**

### Final Pick 1: TSMC

#### Duan Yongping's Perspective: The Nature of the Business

> "I can't understand chip technology, but I do understand this business." — Duan Yongping has repeatedly voiced admiration for the nature of TSMC's business

At its core, TSMC is "the central bank of the AI era" — every top-tier chip-design company must hand its orders over to TSMC to "print money." This is an exceptionally rare kind of business:

1. **Customer stickiness ≈ 100%**: The design cycle for NVIDIA's H100/B100 runs 2+ years and is deeply coupled to TSMC's process — switching to Samsung would mean starting over from scratch. The same holds for Apple, AMD, and Broadcom.
2. **Strong pricing power**: Wafer pricing has risen from $17,000 at 5nm to $20,000+ at 3nm, and customers pay up. Gross margin has climbed from the low 50s to 60%.
3. **Compounding effects**: The process learning curve for each node takes 3-5 years — the earlier you enter, the cheaper and more profitable it becomes. The $40B/year capex scale barrier is one only TSMC and Samsung dare to play at.

**Is this a good business? Answer: it's a top-tier good business.** ROE of 35%, gross margin of 60%, debt ratio of 12% — this combination of numbers almost never exists in manufacturing. Duan Yongping's mantra is "do the right things, and do things right" — for 50 years TSMC has done exactly one thing: contract manufacturing, taken to its extreme.

That said, the counter-argument: (1) The "distribution of profit" in this business is heavily dependent on its customers. If NVIDIA runs into trouble (say, a sharp drop in AI demand), TSMC's net income would be hit quickly; (2) Capex is extremely heavy, and FCF/net income has long run below 50% (except at cycle peaks).

#### Buffett's Perspective: The Moat (Will It Still Be There in 10 Years?)

Buffett has publicly held TSM (Berkshire built a position in 2022 Q3, then quickly trimmed it, though Jensen Huang has publicly called TSMC "the most important company on Earth"). His rapid trim reflected Taiwan Strait geopolitical risk, not doubts about the business itself.

**Five-category moat rating**:

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★★ | 3nm pricing rose 30% over 5 years, and customers accept it |
| Switching costs | ★★★★★ | Switching to Samsung would cost customers 1-2 years and hundreds of millions of dollars |
| Network effects | ★★★ | Process ecosystem, IP libraries |
| Scale effects | ★★★★★ | $40B/year in capex, hard for a single customer to replicate |
| Technology/licensing | ★★★★★ | 5+ years of EUV process learning curve, only 2 players globally |

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** Most likely yes, and even deeper. Reasoning: (1) AI compute demand will not reverse — demand for advanced nodes will only keep expanding; (2) Even if Samsung overtakes it in HBM, its logic-process technology remains 1-2 generations behind; (3) Intel's 18A ramp remains unproven, and its IDM model is inherently less flexible than a pure foundry.

The only thing that could break this script is a Taiwan Strait conflict — this is the true worry behind Buffett's "3% probability but 100% loss" risk framework.

#### Munger's Perspective: Risks and Failure Modes

> "Find the pond, and figure out which pond kills all the fish." — Munger's inversion technique

**How could TSMC fail? Three main possibilities:**

1. **Taiwan Strait conflict**: Probability is low (< 10% within 5-10 years, estimate), but if it happens, the loss is total. Diversified capacity in Arizona, Japan, and Germany could preserve 30-40% of capacity (estimate), but the stock would still likely crash 50%+ in the near-to-medium term.
2. **Customer in-sourcing**: Apple has already invested in multi-sourcing from Intel/Samsung; AWS and Google have high dependence on TSMC for their in-house chips, but if they shifted volume to Samsung on price, TSMC's profits would be squeezed. Probability is relatively low.
3. **Technology inflection point**: Below 1.4nm, physical limits may set in, pushing the semiconductor industry into a "Moore's Law is dead" phase, reducing TSMC's return on capital invested. This is a 10-15-year-out concern.

**What would it be worth in the worst case?** Assuming (1) prolonged Taiwan Strait tension without conflict, (2) global advanced-node market share falling from 60% to 50%, and (3) a PE re-rating down to 15 — the market cap would be roughly $700 billion (about -40%).

**Why don't smart people buy it?** Mainly aversion to Taiwan Strait risk. Buffett's exit was rooted in exactly this concern.

#### Li Lu's Perspective: Positioning Within a Civilizational Trend

Li Lu emphasizes "thinking like an owner, investing for the long term, and identifying civilization-scale turning points."

**Is AI a civilization-scale turning point?** Very possibly. Analogies for AI include: (1) the steam engine, Britain in 1780; (2) electricity, the 1890s; (3) the internet, 1995. AI most likely sits somewhere between "the internet" and "electricity" — a general-purpose technology capable of penetrating every industry.

**Where does TSMC sit within this civilizational turning point?** An analogy:

- In the electricity revolution, who were the winners? Not the generator manufacturers (GE eventually struggled), not the power plants (regional monopolies), but rather **the standard-equipment makers and grid operators supplying "generation + transmission + distribution."**
- In the AI revolution, TSMC is "the core production line for chips, the 'electricity' of this era." It doesn't directly embrace model-level innovation (unlike the cloud providers), but every model depends on it behind the scenes.

**10-20 years out:** TSMC will most likely still dominate AI chip foundry. But watch for: (1) nationalization trends compressing margins (new fabs in the US/Japan/Germany carrying lower yields); (2) alternative technologies such as quantum or optical computing (low probability of commercial viability before 2035, estimate).

| Li Lu (civilizational trend) | ★★★★★ |

**Action recommendations**:

- Entry strategy: Build a position in the PE 22-25 range (fair value); the current PE of ~25 is already relatively low (versus its own history and Mag 7 peers), so scale in across 3 tranches

### Final Pick 2: NVIDIA

#### Duan Yongping's Perspective: The Nature of the Business

Duan Yongping has repeatedly said publicly that he doesn't understand NVIDIA ("don't touch what you don't understand"), but he acknowledges CUDA is a genuine "hard moat."

**The nature of the business: selling the "power generator" of the AI era** — but not an ordinary generator, rather **a generator bundled with an operating system (CUDA)**, one that **has already locked in 90% of the world's AI developers**.

Revenue mix (FY2026):
- Data center: $194B (90%)
- Gaming: ~$15B
- Automotive/Pro Vis: ~$7B

**Is this a good business? Answer: today it's a top-tier business; long-term, it depends on whether the CUDA lock-in can withstand the pushback from open ecosystems (PyTorch, JAX, ROCm).**

Positives:
- ROE of 76% (unmatched among data-center businesses)
- FCF of $96.7B
- Gross margin above 70%
- CUDA developer community of 5 million+

Negatives:
- Extremely high customer concentration (top-4 cloud providers account for 50%+)
- Customers are rapidly bringing chip design in-house (Google TPU, Meta MTIA, ByteDance ASICs)
- The inference market is gradually being diverted to ASICs

#### Buffett's Perspective: The Moat

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★★ | H100 gross margin above 70%, B100 pricing rising further |
| Switching costs | ★★★★★ | Migrating code off CUDA takes 6-12 months |
| Network effects | ★★★★★ | Developer → application → developer flywheel |
| Scale effects | ★★★★ | R&D spend of $20B+/year |
| Technology/licensing | ★★★★ | Dependent on TSMC's process — not itself a manufacturing barrier |

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** The software-ecosystem moat (CUDA) will most likely remain intact, though the hardware portion will be eroded by ASICs. In 10 years, NVIDIA will most likely still be the global #1 in AI training, but its training-market share may fall from 90% to 50-60%; its inference-market share may fall from 60% to 30-40%. Absolute revenue could still be higher, though, because the overall market will have grown larger.

#### Munger's Perspective: Risks and Failure Modes

**How could it fail?**

1. **A bursting AI-capex bubble**: The four major cloud providers' combined capex has already reached $400B+/year; if monetization lags expectations, capex could fall quickly (see the dot-com investment bust of 2000 as a reference). NVIDIA's revenue is extremely sensitive to this.
2. **The CUDA moat broken by open ecosystems**: As PyTorch, compilers (such as Triton), and ASIC backends continue to evolve, developers writing for NVIDIA cards increasingly write PyTorch rather than CUDA directly, rapidly lowering migration costs. This is Jensen Huang's biggest worry.
3. **Accelerating customer in-sourcing**: Google's TPU can already handle the full training of Gemini; Meta's MTIA and ByteDance's ASICs are accelerating too. It's a strong likelihood within 3 years that NVIDIA's customer mix shifts from "top-4 cloud providers at 50%" to "top-4 at 30%."
4. **China market going to zero**: Repeated flip-flopping on H200, accelerating domestic substitution (Huawei Ascend shipped 640,000 units in 2025).
5. **Inventory risk**: Yield issues with GB200 have already caused inventory write-downs in the past. AI accelerators iterate so fast that inventory turnover between generations is extremely sensitive.

**What would it be worth in the worst case?** A PE re-rating to 20, combined with data-center revenue down 30%, would put the market cap at roughly $2.4 trillion (about -50%).

**Why don't smart people buy it?** (1) Value investors like Duan Yongping don't understand the technology; (2) Some hedge funds already believe a PE of 38 has priced in too little change in customer structure; (3) Fear of it being re-rated as a "cyclical stock."

#### Li Lu's Perspective: Positioning Within a Civilizational Trend

**AI is a civilization-scale paradigm shift** — and NVIDIA is the "core compute supplier" for that shift.

Historical analogy:

- In the 1890s electricity revolution, the winners were "General Electric" + "Westinghouse" (equipment) + "Bell" (transmission)
- In the AI revolution, NVIDIA plays the role of "General Electric" — the maker of the core power machinery

**10-20 years out:** NVIDIA will most likely still be a core player, but its share will decline. What matters is whether its market cap keeps growing — which depends on (1) whether the total AI market can grow another 5-10x, and (2) the ceiling of NVIDIA's moat within that market.

Historical precedent: from 1900 to 1929, GE's market cap during the electricity era rose several dozen-fold, but afterward it fell into a century of low growth. NVIDIA today looks more like GE circa 1925 — already at the peak of its cycle.

### Final Pick 3: SK Hynix

#### Duan Yongping's Perspective: The Nature of the Business

Duan Yongping has always been wary of cyclical stocks — "cyclical stocks are best avoided entirely, and if you do touch them, only at the very bottom of the cycle."

**The nature of the business: an HBM/DRAM oligopoly** — a business defined by "cyclicality plus oligopolistic coordination." It currently sits in an AI-driven "super-cycle," with net margin flipping from a 2022 loss to 77% in 2026 Q1.

**Is this a good business? Answer: it's a good business, but not the best business — because it's cyclical.**

That said, relative to the other two picks (NVIDIA/TSMC), it has one distinctive advantage right now: **an extremely cheap valuation (PE ~10)**. Its AI exposure is also purer — HBM's share of DRAM revenue is rising fast, reaching 40%+ by 2026.

#### Buffett's Perspective: The Moat

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★ | HBM3E prices are 4-5x standard DRAM |
| Switching costs | ★★★ | Qualifying HBM with NVIDIA takes 6+ months |
| Network effects | ★★ | — |
| Scale effects | ★★★★ | The DRAM "big three" (SK/Samsung/Micron) |
| Technology/licensing | ★★★★ | HBM4 process barriers |

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** Not necessarily. DRAM has historically had 50+ manufacturers, eventually consolidating to 3. Over the next 5-10 years HBM will most likely remain a 3-way oligopoly, but share will shift: Samsung fighting back, Micron winning NVIDIA orders. SK Hynix's current 62% share could fall to 45-50% by 2028.

Historically, Buffett has rarely bought memory stocks (aside from a small Micron position in 1995), owing to their strong cyclicality.

#### Munger's Perspective: Risks and Failure Modes

**How could it fail?**

1. **A downturn in the memory cycle**: Historically DRAM cycles run 4-5 years. The current up-cycle began in 2023 and will most likely peak in 2027-2028. ROE can fall from 60% at the top to -10% at the bottom.
2. **Samsung fighting back**: Samsung's HBM4 will ramp in 2026 Q4-2027 and could win NVIDIA orders.
3. **An inflection point in AI training demand**: 80% of HBM demand comes from AI training — once the four major cloud providers' capex turns, a 30%+ drop in HBM pricing is a strong likelihood.

**What would it be worth in the worst case?** If it reverts to the bottom of the 2022 down-cycle (net loss of $1 billion), a PE can't even be computed — on a P/B of 0.5, the market cap would be roughly $200 billion (about -70%).

**Why don't smart people buy it?** Cyclicality. "At a cycle peak, a PE of 10 looks cheap, but it's really 10x a peak-cycle EPS — at the bottom of the cycle, EPS can go to zero or negative."

#### Li Lu's Perspective: Positioning Within a Civilizational Trend

**HBM is an unavoidable requirement for AI.** Every GPU today needs HBM — this is genuine ecosystem-level demand. Will that requirement still hold in 10 years?

Possible disruptions: (1) CXL memory pooling partially substituting for HBM; (2) DRAM-on-logic (3D stacking directly onto logic dies) changing HBM's form factor; (3) optical interconnects reducing reliance on HBM.

But within 10 years (before 2030), HBM is an unavoidable bottleneck. So SK Hynix's "AI positioning" is real, but its moat is not as deep as TSMC's EUV process.
