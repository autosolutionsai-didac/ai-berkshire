## II. Moat Breakdown, Layer by Layer

### Layer 1: User Base

| Dimension | Tencent | Alibaba |
|------|------|------|
| Core product | WeChat (1.432 billion MAU) | Taobao (monthly actives up 25% YoY, absolute figure undisclosed) |
| User stickiness | Monthly retention **98%** | 46 million 88VIP members, but ordinary users are churning heavily to Pinduoduo/Douyin |
| Usage frequency | WeChat Pay averages 2.7 uses/day + round-the-clock social use | Low-frequency e-commerce (monthly opens far below WeChat) |
| Switching cost | **Extremely high** (locked in by the social graph) | Moderate (product search is substitutable) |
| Threat of substitution | Every challenger over the past decade has failed | Pinduoduo/Douyin have already siphoned off 20 percentage points of share |

**Verdict: Tencent's user base is "irreplaceable"; Alibaba's user base is "erodible."** WeChat's 1.432 billion MAU plus 98% retention is the strongest single user lock-in in Chinese internet. Taobao's monthly actives are still growing, but its GMV share keeps sliding (51% → 31%), meaning users are still there but buying less.

### Layer 2: Business Model Quality

| Dimension | Tencent | Alibaba |
|------|------|------|
| Main revenue sources | Games (32%) + Advertising (19%) + FinTech & Business Services (31%) | E-commerce commissions/ads (~55%) + Cloud (~15%) + International (~13%) |
| Gross margin | **57%** (2026 Q1) | ~38% (estimate, dragged down by instant retail/flash sales) |
| Net margin | **30%** | ~7% (FY2026 adjusted) |
| Revenue diversification | Three segments are balanced, no single one over 35% | Heavily dependent on e-commerce, and its share is still falling |
| Marginal cost | Extremely low (social and gaming have near-zero marginal cost) | Relatively high (logistics and instant retail require riders and warehousing) |
| Capital intensity | Moderate (AI spend rising, but the core business stays asset-light) | **High** (cloud infrastructure + logistics + instant retail — a triple asset-heavy load) |

**Verdict: Tencent's business model is "asset-light, high-margin, diversified"; Alibaba's is "asset-heavy, low-margin, with its core business share declining." The quality gap between the two businesses has widened sharply over the past three years.**

## III. Which One Is More Severely Undervalued?

### Tencent: Moderately Undervalued

| Valuation method | Implied value | Current market cap | Undervaluation |
|---------|------|---------|---------|
| Forward P/E (12x vs. 10-year average of 25.7x) | At 20x P/E → ~8.6 trillion HKD | 4.1 trillion HKD | **~110% upside** |
| Discounted free cash flow (FCF yield 7%→5%) | ~5.9 trillion HKD | 4.1 trillion HKD | ~44% upside |
| Sum-of-the-parts (investment portfolio + operating business) | Portfolio ~1 trillion + operating business ~5 trillion | 4.1 trillion HKD | ~46% upside |
| Analyst consensus target price | 722 HKD | 455 HKD | **~59% upside** |

**Reason for the undervaluation:** AI anxiety (Hunyuan's late start) + China-risk discount + expectations of reduced buybacks in 2026

**Is the undervaluation justified?** Partly. The uncertainty around AI investment is real, but WeChat's 1.4 billion users give AI deployment a natural vehicle. The market's concern over Tencent's AI position may be overdone.

### Alibaba: Severely Undervalued on Paper, but for Structural Reasons

| Valuation method | Implied value | Current market cap | Undervaluation |
|---------|------|---------|---------|
| FY2028 Non-GAAP profit of 200 billion × 15x P/E | ~3 trillion → ~410 billion USD | 310 billion USD | ~32% upside |
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) | Taotian 350 billion + Alibaba Cloud 150 billion + AIDC 50 billion + Ant 80 billion + others | ~450 billion USD | ~45% upside |
| Net cash + investment portfolio | 574 billion yuan (~80 billion USD) | 26% of market cap | Sizeable safety cushion |
| Analyst consensus | — | — | Sharply divided |

**Reason for the undervaluation:** Irreversible decline in e-commerce share + free cash flow turned negative + uncertain returns on AI investment + 36 billion burned on instant retail + profit plunged 62%

**Is the undervaluation justified?** Mostly, yes. Alibaba's undervaluation isn't the market getting it wrong — it's the market pricing in **real, structural risk**:
1. E-commerce share has fallen from 51% to 31%, still falling, with no sign of stabilizing
2. Free cash flow has turned negative, meaning the cash-burn rate now outpaces the earning rate
3. Even management admits there's "no precise answer" on the payoff from the 380 billion AI investment
4. The company is fighting on four fronts at once — e-commerce, instant retail, AI, and internationalization

## V. Investment Verdict

### If You Can Only Buy One: Buy Tencent

**Reasons:**
1. The moat gap is decisive — WeChat is "irreplaceable," Taobao is "erodible"
2. The earnings-quality gap is widening — Tencent's net profit is 2.5x Alibaba's, and Tencent is still growing while Alibaba is shrinking
3. The certainty gap — Tencent's 12x forward P/E is priced against profit that will very likely be realized; Alibaba's 15x forward P/E is priced against profit that may not recover for another two years
4. Free cash flow — Tencent is generating over 200 billion yuan of annualized cash inflow, while Alibaba is burning cash. One is paying shareholders; the other is asking shareholders for money

### Alibaba Isn't Un-buyable, But It Requires a Higher Risk Tolerance

**Conditions under which buying Alibaba makes sense:**
- You believe the cloud/AI business can become Alibaba's core profit engine within 3 years (replacing e-commerce)
- You can tolerate a 2-3 year profit trough
- You treat the 574 billion in cash plus the Ant Group stake as downside protection
- You have confidence in sum-of-the-parts valuation (the market currently doesn't buy the SOTP case, because none of the parts are independently priced)

### One-Line Summary

> **Tencent is like a castle whose moat keeps getting deeper, its walls still thickening; Alibaba is like an empire mid-renovation, and it's unclear whether the renovation will leave it stronger or more broken. With the former, you're buying certainty; with the latter, you're buying possibility.** At current valuations, Tencent's "certainty discount" is worth more than Alibaba's "possibility discount."
