## What Is Buffett's Mirror Test

One of Buffett's harshest disciplines: talk to yourself, not to the market.

**The five-sentence template**:
1. What is the **essence** of this business? (Circle of competence)
2. What is its **moat**? Is it widening or narrowing?
3. Is **management** trustworthy?
4. What discount does the current price represent to **intrinsic value**? (Margin of safety)
5. Even if I'm wrong, is the **downside risk** manageable?

**One hidden rule — the "but" count**: every time "but/however/if/as long as" appears in a sentence, the certainty of the argument drops by one point. If the total exceeds two, you are talking yourself into an excuse — **which itself violates the discipline of the mirror test**.

# The Essence of the Mirror Test

On the surface, the mirror exercise is about "explaining why you bought in five sentences"; at its core, it's a **mirror that reveals genuine understanding versus mere talking points**:

- **Someone who truly understands**: five sentences, concise and direct, without a single "but"
- **Someone deceiving themselves**: five sentences packed with "although/but/if/as long as"
- **Someone who doesn't understand**: can't even fill five sentences, or fills them with clichés

> *"The discipline of investing isn't 'never make mistakes' — it's 'never deceive yourself.'"*
>
> *If you find yourself needing self-reassurance while looking in the mirror, that itself is the signal.*
