# AI Foundation Model Sub-Sector — Funnel-Style Value Investing Screening Research

**Research date**: 2026-05-09
**Methodology**: 4-layer funnel (full-market scan → 5 hard-metric coarse screen → detailed analysis → final 3-company Four Masters deep dive)
**Sub-sector definition**: Foundation large models and tightly coupled players — closed/open-source frontier models, model inference services, MLOps platforms, AI labs
**Currency convention**: Unless otherwise noted, USD for US dollars, HKD for Hong Kong dollars, CNY for Chinese yuan

---

## 0. Executive Summary

- **Core contradiction**: The top of the model value chain is concentrated in **private companies** (OpenAI at $852 billion USD; Anthropic at $380 billion USD as of its latest round, reportedly about to jump to roughly $900 billion USD; xAI at $230 billion USD; ByteDance's Doubao; DeepSeek — valuations rising rapidly), so ordinary investors can only participate via **proxy exposure through large public tech companies** (Microsoft/Google/Meta/Alibaba/Tencent/Baidu)
- **Result of the 5 hard-metric screen**: Of 30+ candidates, only **6 listed companies** passed all 5 metrics: Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Alibaba, Tencent (Baidu passed marginally, with a moat discount)
- **Final 3 selections**: Alphabet (core position), Meta (satellite position), Alibaba (China-exposure satellite position)
- **Key observation**: Pure model-inference-service companies (CoreWeave, Together, Fireworks) mostly have negative operating cash flow or high debt loads, and largely fail the 5-metric screen
- **Information sufficiency rating**: B+ (first-hand data — earnings reports, official company announcements — are largely complete; valuation data mostly comes from secondary reporting and needs cross-validation)

## 3. Layer Three: Detailed Analysis (300-500 words each)

### 3.1 Microsoft (MSFT)

**Business model**: A trinity of B2B software + cloud + AI. Azure (including the OpenAI API) + Microsoft 365 Copilot + GitHub Copilot form a closed loop. The AI business's ARR had already exceeded $37 billion USD by FY26 Q3 (up 123% YoY). Microsoft holds 27% equity in OpenAI (approximately $135 billion USD in book value) plus an IP license through 2032 (non-exclusive) and a revenue-sharing arrangement (capped) through 2030.

**Financial quality**: FY26 Q3 revenue of $82.9 billion USD (+18%), operating income of $38.4 billion (+20%). Azure growth at 40%. RPO (remaining performance obligations) of $627 billion USD (+99% YoY), an exceptionally long order book. Commercial cloud at $54.5 billion (+29%). Capital expenditure is large, but OCF is extremely strong and free cash flow is stable.

**Moat**:
- **Ecosystem lock-in**: Office/Windows/Azure form three major platforms; switching costs for enterprises are extremely high (★★★★★)
- **OpenAI equity stake**: Equivalent to a low-cost bet on "the front-running rabbit," with IP usage rights through 2032 (★★★★★)
- **AI data flywheel**: The feedback data generated once Copilot is embedded in enterprise workflows is irreplaceable (★★★★)

**Main risks**:
- The April 2026 restructuring of the Microsoft-OpenAI agreement: MSFT is no longer exclusive and no longer receives revenue share from OpenAI, which can now run on Amazon or any cloud. **Azure's exclusivity dividend has ended**
- AI capex as a share of OCF has already exceeded 50%+, creating pressure to prove out returns
- Regulatory: frequent EU antitrust investigations

**Valuation quick take**: PE of 24.5 (down 25% from its 12-month average of 32.6), relatively cheap versus history. **Reasonable, on the cheap side**

### 4.1 Alphabet (GOOGL) — Overall recommendation ★★★★★

#### 4.1.1 Duan Yongping's perspective: the nature of the business

> "Do the right things, and do things right. When you look at a company, look at its business model, its corporate culture, and its moat."

Alphabet's essence is the world's largest, highest-margin "information infrastructure company." Search has been its cash cow for the past 25 years, contributing 60%+ of revenue and nearly 80% of profit; the characteristics of this business are:
1. **Extremely high ROIC**: search advertising requires almost no heavy assets;
2. **Network effects**: more users → more advertisers → more accurate algorithms → better user experience;
3. **Scale confers pricing power**: ad CPM is set by auction, but Google's dominant traffic share effectively makes it a "price maker."

But this "old business" is now being disrupted by AI — AI answers mean users no longer click through to search results. Alphabet's key response is **self-disruption**. Gemini 3 + AI Overviews + AI Mode are not about preserving old-style search, but about remaining the "information gateway" in the AI era. Q1 2026 data shows: Cloud +63%, Gemini Enterprise paid users +40% QoQ, AI-model-derived revenue +800% YoY, and net income +81%. This is a company that has **proactively stepped outside its comfort zone and has already proven the transition's success with its financial results**.

Duan Yongping would ask "is this a good business?" — **Yes**, because: core search remains cash-rich (annual free cash flow on the order of $100 billion+ USD), AI is a move from one high-margin business into another potentially higher-margin business (cloud + model API + Workspace AI), the culture is healthy (founder control plus a pragmatic Pichai), and the moat is being upgraded rather than eroded.

#### 4.1.2 Buffett's perspective: the moat

> "I look for companies with durable competitive advantages, moats that are deep and wide, with lots of crocodiles in them."

| Moat type | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Data flywheel** | ★★★★★ | 8 billion+ daily search queries plus YouTube video, Maps geodata, Android user behavior, and Workspace documents; any competitor would need 10 years to replicate this |
| **Compute scale** | ★★★★★ | Self-developed TPU v6/v7 plus a global data-center footprint; 2026 capex of $180-190 billion USD, which few competitors can afford; lower Nvidia dependence than OpenAI/Anthropic/Meta/Microsoft |
| **Research talent density** | ★★★★★ | Roughly 5,000+ AI researchers following the merger of DeepMind, Google Brain, and Quantum AI; the Transformer, Diffusion, AlphaGo, and AlphaFold all originated here |
| **Brand pricing power** | ★★★★ | "Google it" has become a verb; advertisers cannot bypass it |
| **Switching costs (enterprise)** | ★★★★ | Workspace's 2.2 billion users plus Cloud's $460 billion order backlog |

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** Three things will decide it:
1. Whether AI-answer substitution for search destroys advertising → Google is already counter-attacking with AI Mode;
2. Whether TPUs can continue to keep pace with Nvidia GPUs → Gemini 3 training costs are already down 30%, proving the compute advantage is sustainable;
3. Antitrust breakup → even if broken up, the sum of Search/Cloud/YouTube as three independent companies could exceed the current market cap (compare Standard Oil, whose post-breakup pieces were together worth more).

**Conclusion**: The moat will not only still be there in 10 years, but may deepen further in the AI era.

#### 4.1.3 Munger's perspective: risks and failure modes

> "Tell me where I'm going to die, so I never go there."

Scenarios that could cause Alphabet to fail or underperform:
1. **A rapid collapse in search advertising** (25% probability): AI-answer substitution causes search click-through rates to fall off a cliff by 50%+, and new AI ad models fail to keep pace → the valuation could be cut in half in the short term
2. **Gemini loses to GPT-6/Claude 5** (25% probability): Anthropic/OpenAI open a meaningful gap in frontier capability, causing large-scale enterprise-customer migration
3. **Antitrust breakup** (15% probability): DOJ forces a split of Search + Chrome or Search + Android; the breakup itself may not reduce total value, but it would compress the valuation in the short term
4. **Capex black hole** (30% probability): $180-190 billion in capex persists for 3-5 years while ROIC declines, shrinking free cash flow
5. **Open-source parity** (50% probability): Llama, DeepSeek, and Qwen make open models "good enough" for 90% of use cases, eliminating frontier-model rent — this is precisely the **biggest systemic risk in the model sub-sector**

Why would a smart person not buy? Because economic returns on frontier models are uncertain, plus there's regulatory risk and a capex black hole — these concerns are legitimate, but **Alphabet is the least likely to die**: it has search advertising as its base position, and even if frontier-model rent goes to zero, it remains one of the largest advertising companies in the world.

Worst-case valuation: assume search falls 30%, Cloud growth slows to 30%, and Gemini commercialization fails → net margin falls from 25% to 18%, and market cap could fall to $2.5-3 trillion USD (versus $4.79 trillion currently). Downside of roughly -40%.

#### 4.1.4 Li Lu's perspective: civilizational-trend positioning

### 5.2 Historical analogies

**Best analogy 1: The late-1990s internet boom**
- At the time, most pure internet companies went to zero (Pets.com, Webvan)
- The true winners were infrastructure and distribution: Cisco, Intel, Microsoft, Amazon (the few that survived)
- **Implication for the model sub-sector**: pure model companies (OpenAI/Anthropic), even with sky-high valuations, may ultimately see the big platforms with "distribution moats" come out ahead

**Best analogy 2: The electricity revolution (1880-1920)**
- Edison Electric was ultimately absorbed into GE
- The true long-term winners were the grid (utilities) plus the industries that used electricity (GM, Ford)
- **Implication for the model sub-sector**: the models themselves may become commoditized, and the real AI dividend may lie in "applications transformed by AI" — so Alphabet (search + cloud + Workspace), Meta (social), and Alibaba (e-commerce) may actually be the safer exposures

**Best analogy 3: The mobile internet, 2007-2015**
- Apple and Google captured most of the value (the operating-system layer and the app layer)
- China's domestic kings: Tencent and Alibaba
- **Implication for the model sub-sector**: operating-system-level players (cloud plus AI platforms) plus China's domestic kings form a dual-track structure

  - Self-check: real enterprise AI ROI data (e.g., Meta's ad revenue +33%, Google's net income +81%) proves real returns already exist — but these companies already had strong distribution; ROI for pure model companies remains unproven

## 6. Civilizational Trend Assessment (Li Lu Framework)

### 6.1 The paradigm question

**Is this a civilizational-level paradigm shift, or a phase-level boom?**

Data points (supporting a civilizational-level shift):
- Microsoft's AI business ARR of $37 billion USD (+123% YoY)
- Anthropic's ARR of $30 billion USD (~+1,400% YoY)
- OpenAI's monthly revenue of $2 billion USD
- Alphabet's AI-model-derived revenue up 800% YoY
- ByteDance's Doubao averaging 100 trillion tokens/day

The **absolute values** and **growth rates** of these figures have already exceeded the comparable data from the 1995-2000 internet boom.

Li Lu's view: **this is confirmed to be a civilizational-level paradigm**. But **whether any single company can be held for 30 years is a separate question** — Cisco was a winner of the 1990s, yet it took 25 years after its 2000 peak to return to that level.

### 6.2 Historical analogy

The closest technology-revolution analogy: **the fusion of electricity and computing** — AI is both a new form of "energy" (intelligence that can be accessed by plugging in) and a new form of "computation" (natural language becoming a programming language).

Historical experience:
- The electricity revolution took 40 years to reach most households
- The computer revolution took 30 years to reach every desk
- The internet achieved widespread adoption in 15 years
- AI is estimated to achieve "embedding in every white-collar workflow" in 5-10 years

### 6.3 The 10-20 year endgame

**Most likely winners**:
1. **Large platforms that own "distribution"**: Alphabet (search + cloud), Meta (3 billion users), Alibaba (e-commerce + cloud), Microsoft (enterprise software)
2. **Large companies with "compute infrastructure" plus proprietary chips**: Alphabet's TPU, Meta's MTIA, Alibaba's T-Head (the Chinese version); Nvidia still dominates GPUs but its pricing power may decline
3. **A small number of pure model companies**: OpenAI (consumer + enterprise API), Anthropic (enterprise + coding) — 1-2 of these may survive

**Most likely to be disrupted**:
- Search (already underway), customer service, entry-level coding, basic translation, entry-level legal consulting, entry-level medical consulting
- Most pure model unicorns (under open-source parity)

**Winner-take-all segments**:
- Operating-system-level AI platforms (OpenAI's ChatGPT, Google's Gemini app, Alibaba's Qwen App)
- Enterprise AI workflows (Microsoft Copilot, Salesforce, Workday)

**Segments most exposed to disruption**:
- Pure API providers (if open source becomes good enough)
- Point-solution AI tools (if large models become powerful enough)
