### A. Banks (6 companies)

#### Industrial Bank (601166.SH) — PB 0.41

**Business Essence**: Core revenue comes from net interest income (67% of the total) and non-interest income. Two defining features: first, it is the "king of interbank business," with interbank assets of RMB 1.23 trillion and interbank liabilities of RMB 2.48 trillion (29% of total liabilities); second, it is pushing forward a transformation toward "green banking, wealth banking, and investment banking." Its client base is predominantly corporate.

**Moat**: A banking license plus multiple additional licenses (including Industrial Fund Management); its "Bank-to-Bank Platform" connects thousands of small and mid-sized financial institutions, creating network effects; total assets have surpassed RMB 11 trillion. However, compared with the branch networks and retail-customer stickiness of the large state-owned banks, its moat is relatively weak.

**Classification of Reasons for Low PB**:

| Factor | Classification |

The 1.08% NPL ratio and 228% provision coverage are both healthy, and a dividend yield above 5.8% provides a cushion. **Reasonably low, but a clear signal of fundamental improvement has yet to emerge.**

### B. Real Estate/REITs (4 companies)

#### China Resources Land (1109.HK) — PB 0.55

**Business Essence**: The real estate flagship of a central state-owned enterprise, driven by three engines — development sales, investment properties, and asset-light management. 2025 revenue was RMB 281.4 billion. Recurring-income business (shopping-mall rents and the like) accounted for more than half of core net profit for the first time (51.8%). It operates over 100 "Mixc"-branded shopping malls, with rental income of approximately RMB 21.1 billion (+9%) and occupancy above 97%.

**Moat**: The financing-cost advantage of a central SOE (a blended financing cost of about 3.1%, among the lowest in the industry); Mixc City/Mixc Mall are top-tier domestic commercial real estate brands; land reserves are concentrated in five core cities, accounting for nearly 80% of the total.

**Fundamental Trends**:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue (RMB billion) | 251.1 | 278.8 | 281.4 |
| Core net profit (RMB billion) | 27.77 | 25.42 | 22.48 |
| Core net profit from recurring business (RMB billion) | 9.6 | 10.3 | 11.65 |
| Shopping-mall rental income (RMB billion) | ~17.8 | 19.3 | ~21.1 |

**Reasons for Low PB**: Development-business gross margin fell from 27% to 16.8% (fundamental deterioration); the market has discounted China Resources Land alongside private developers that have defaulted (sentiment bias); the value of its recurring-income business is not fully priced in (sentiment bias); HK-share liquidity discount (structural).

**Overall Assessment**: A PB of 0.55 is undervalued. Recurring-business profit exceeds RMB 10 billion and continues to grow; if the shopping malls were valued separately on a REIT basis, the implied value would be far above book value. **This is a "quality name caught in an industry-wide sell-off."**

#### Sun Hung Kai Properties (0016.HK) — PB 0.63-0.67

**Business Essence**: One of Hong Kong's largest developers, driven by both property development and property investment (rental income). FY2024/25 revenue was HK$90.1 billion. It holds top-tier commercial landmarks across Hong Kong (including the ICC), with NAV per share of approximately HK$213.

**Moat**: The largest private agricultural land bank in Hong Kong (31 million square feet, irreplaceable); core commercial properties in prime locations; a net gearing ratio of just 15.1%, reflecting an extremely sound balance sheet. Annual rental income is HK$24.5 billion.

**Fundamental Trends**: Underlying profit has been roughly flat over three years (~HK$21.8 billion), with rental income stable at HK$24.5-25.0 billion. ROE is low (~3.2%) but stable.

**Reasons for Low PB**: Hong Kong office vacancy rates exceeding 15% and rents down 20-30% (fundamental deterioration); pressure on residential development margins (fundamental deterioration); uncertainty over agricultural-land conversion (structural); a low ROE fails to attract capital seeking return efficiency (fundamental).

**Overall Assessment**: A PB of 0.63-0.67 is somewhat justified but still undervalued. Core asset quality is extremely high and leverage is extremely low. If the Hong Kong property market continues to recover, there is 30-40% upside for valuation repair.

#### CK Asset Holdings (1113.HK) — PB 0.43

**Business Essence**: The Li Ka-shing family's real estate and diversified investment platform, which has transformed into a diversified investment company spanning real estate, infrastructure, and utilities. 2025 net profit attributable to parent was HK$10.85 billion. The sale of its UK power-grid business generated proceeds of approximately HK$22.2 billion, and the group will move to a net-cash position. Net gearing is just 2.4%.

**Moat**: Diversified asset allocation (globally spread); extremely conservative financial management; the Li Ka-shing family's asset-allocation expertise.

**Fundamental Trends**: Net profit attributable to parent has declined for three consecutive years (from HK$17.3 billion to HK$13.7 billion to HK$10.8 billion), but operating profit excluding property revaluations has been largely stable (~HK$12.0 billion).

**Reasons for Low PB**: Conglomerate discount (structural); declining net profit (fundamental deterioration — on the surface); the asset sale being interpreted as "capital flight" (sentiment bias); cancellation of the results press conference sparking discontent (sentiment bias); difficulty verifying asset NAV (structural).

**Overall Assessment**: A PB of 0.43 is undervalued but carries value-trap risk. The core issue is not poor asset quality but the market's distrust of its transparency. **A catalyst (a large-scale buyback or special dividend) is needed to unlock value.**

#### Link REIT (0823.HK) — PB 0.64-0.67

**Business Essence**: Asia's largest REIT, managing assets that include Hong Kong retail properties (former public-housing shopping malls), car parks, and properties on the mainland and overseas. FY2024/25 total revenue was HK$14.22 billion, and DPU was HK 272.34 cents (+3.7%).

**Moat**: The monopoly position of its livelihood-oriented retail properties (public-housing malls have no competing substitutes) plus a Hong Kong virtual-banking license; occupancy consistently above 97%; investment-grade ratings (A/A2).

**Fundamental Trends**: Revenue and DPU continue to grow moderately, but NAV per unit has declined for three consecutive years (from HK$74 to HK$71 to HK$63-67), mainly due to technical downward revaluations of properties. Core operating metrics remain healthy.

**Reasons for Low PB**: Pressure on REITs from the rising-rate cycle (liquidity/structural); expansion of capitalization rates for Hong Kong retail properties (fundamental deterioration — at the book-value level); exclusion from Stock Connect limiting demand (liquidity/structural); slowing DPU growth (fundamental).

**Overall Assessment**: A PB of 0.64-0.67 is undervalued. Core operations are excellent, and demand for its livelihood-oriented properties is inelastic. A rate-cutting cycle plus inclusion in Stock Connect would be direct positives. A dividend yield of 6.6-7% provides a solid cushion. **The highest-certainty name among the four real estate/REIT companies.**

### 2. Systemic HK-Share Discount Is the Common Denominator

Among the roughly 15-17 sub-book-value companies, the vast majority are Hong Kong-listed. The phenomenon of trading below book value in Hong Kong is systemic:
- **The AH premium is pervasive**: For the same company, H-share PB is typically 15-25% lower than the corresponding A-share PB
- **Foreign capital continues to underweight China**: Geopolitics and macro uncertainty are suppressing valuations
- **Insufficient liquidity**: Average daily trading volume in Hong Kong is far below that of A-shares
- **Southbound capital is providing some repair**: Since the start of 2026, Southbound Stock Connect inflows have been persistently positive, though the process remains slow

### 3. Sub-Book-Value Trading in Banking Is an Industry-Wide Phenomenon

Nearly all 42 listed A-share banks trade below book value. The core reasons are:
- A persistently narrowing net interest margin (from above 2% down to around 1.3%)
- A structural downward trend in ROE
- Market concerns over exposure to real estate and local government debt risk

That said, it is worth noting: NPL ratios are all stable or declining, and provision coverage ratios are all above 200% — the banks' underlying financial cushion is considerably thicker than what market pricing reflects.
