## II. Business Model Analysis (Duan Yongping's Perspective)

### Core Value Proposition

Kujiale uses cloud rendering plus AI technology to compress interior-design turnaround from "days to produce a rendering" to "minutes to produce a rendering," dramatically lowering the professional barrier to home design. In essence it is a **design-tool SaaS** business — it attracts designers with a free or low-cost individual edition, then charges subscription fees to B2B enterprise customers (home-furnishing brands, renovation companies).

> Duan Yongping would ask: **"Where does this business's user stickiness come from?"**

### Breaking Down the SaaS Revenue Model

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Individual customers | 433,000 paying (H1 2025) | Prospectus |
| Enterprise customers | 47,000 paying (H1 2025) | Prospectus |
| Individual paid-conversion rate | 6.8% → 9.6% (2023 → 2025) | Prospectus |
| Enterprise customer NRR | 101.6% | Prospectus |
| Key-account NRR | 110.0% | Prospectus |
| Individual customer NRR | 92.3% | Prospectus |
| Revenue mix | The top 24% of enterprise customers contribute 80% of revenue | Prospectus |

**Unit economics analysis**:
- 2025 revenue was 820 million yuan, with enterprise and individual paying customers totaling roughly 480,000
- **Average ARPU**: approximately 1,708 yuan/year (estimate)
- Key accounts contribute an outsized share of revenue — a classic "top-customer dependency" structure
- NRR above 100% shows key accounts are expanding their spend, but individual-customer NRR is only 92.3%, indicating relatively high churn

### User Ecosystem Flywheel

```
Designers use it for free → Generate a massive volume of design plans → Data accumulates (50 million+ cloud-based plans)
     ↓                                                                            ↓
Brands pay to connect ← Value of the traffic gateway ← Floor-plan library covers 90% of China's housing layouts
```

### Moat Scorecard

| Moat Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|------|------|
| Switching costs | ★★★☆☆ | Designer habits create some stickiness, but the cost of switching tools isn't particularly high |
| Network effects | ★★★☆☆ | A designer-brand two-sided network exists, but it's weaker than that of platform-type companies |
| Data barrier | ★★★★☆ | 50 million+ structured design plans and a floor-plan library covering 90% of layouts are hard to replicate |
| Brand recognition | ★★★★☆ | The No. 1 brand in home design; "Kujiale" is nearly synonymous with the category |
| Economies of scale | ★★☆☆☆ | Cloud rendering has scale effects, but R&D spend as a share of revenue remains extremely high |
| **Overall moat** | **★★★☆☆** | **Moderately above average — real barriers exist, but they aren't insurmountable** |

> **Duan Yongping's comment**: "It's doing the right thing — helping customers become more efficient. But 'doing the right thing' and 'doing the thing right' are two different matters. The key for a SaaS business is whether NRR can keep rising, and a 92.3% retention rate among individual customers shows the product isn't yet 'irreplaceable' enough."

> **Important note**: The headline "liabilities exceed assets" is mainly because, under IFRS, preferred shares are classified as financial liabilities. Once the IPO completes, all preferred shares convert into ordinary shares, the redemption liability is eliminated, and the balance sheet improves dramatically. This is a common accounting artifact for pre-IPO SaaS companies, not a sign of genuine operational distress.

### Market Size (TAM)

- China's TAM is only 6.6 billion yuan (by 2029) — the ceiling isn't especially high
- Internationalization (Coohom) is key to opening up growth room, but overseas revenue is only about 7.4% of the total

| **Meijian/Dabanjia** | <5% | Niche segments | Small scale, insufficient differentiation |

### Assessing AI Disruption

| AI Impact Dimension | Assessment | Explanation |
|-----------|------|------|
| Is AI an opportunity or a threat? | 🟡 Double-edged sword | Kujiale's in-house AI engine can strengthen the product, but AI is also lowering the barrier to entry for design tools |
| AI-generated design plans | 🟢 Opportunity | 1.1 billion design images generated with AI in H1 2025, boosting user stickiness |
| Risk of displacement by general-purpose AI | 🔴 Potential threat | If general-purpose AI like GPT-5/Gemini can directly generate high-quality interior designs, the value of specialized tools declines |
| SpatialVerse platform | 🟡 Unproven | The "spatial intelligence" concept is grand, but the path to commercialization is unclear |

> **Munger's comment**: "In China's SaaS market, Kujiale is already the 'obvious winner' in home design. The question is whether the 'pond' is big enough. A 6.6-billion-yuan China TAM with a 25% share means the ceiling is already in sight. Either internationalization succeeds, or the company expands into more spatial-design use cases — neither path is easy."

## VII. Bull vs. Bear

### 🐂 Bull Case (Optimistic Scenario)

1. **"Spatial intelligence" beyond home furnishing**: Expanding from interior design into commercial space, real-estate marketing, and gaming/VR use cases, enlarging the TAM 5-10x
2. **AI as an enhancer, not a replacement**: The proprietary AI engine creates differentiation, and AI features become a paid growth driver
3. **International breakthrough**: Coohom captures 10-20% overseas market share, doubling revenue
4. **Property market bottoms and rebounds**: A recovery in China's real estate sector reignites demand for home design
5. **NRR keeps climbing**: Key-account NRR rises from 110% to 120%+, with ARPU growing significantly

**Bull case target**: 2028 revenue of 1.5 billion yuan+, net profit of 200 million yuan+, market cap of HK$30 billion (~3x the current level)

### 🐻 Bear Case (Pessimistic Scenario)

1. **Growth ceiling**: China's TAM is limited; growth keeps decelerating from 8.6% toward 3-5%
2. **AI democratization shock**: General-purpose AI erodes the moat around specialized design tools
3. **Costly internationalization**: Overseas expansion requires heavy investment with slow payback, dragging down profit
4. **Prolonged real-estate winter**: The home-furnishing sector stays depressed for 3-5 years
5. **Pressure from early-shareholder selling**: A lock-up expiration six months after listing weighs on the share price

**Bear case target**: revenue stalls at 900 million-1 billion yuan, thin profit, market cap shrinks to HK$5-7 billion

## IX. Valuation Judgment

### Core Conclusion

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| IPO market cap (midpoint price) | ~HK$12.2 billion (~RMB 11.2 billion) |
| 2025 PS | ~14x |
| Global SaaS median PS | 6-7x |
| Autodesk/Adobe PS | 9-10x |
| **Reasonable PS range** | 8-12x (accounting for growth rate and leadership premium) |
| **Reasonable market-cap range** | HK$7.3-11.0 billion |
| **Reasonable share-price range** | HK$4.3-6.5 |

**Judgment**: The IPO pricing (HK$6.72-7.62) sits at the rich end of the reasonable range, reflecting the "Hangzhou Six Little Dragons" hype and the scarcity premium of being the "first spatial-intelligence stock."

- Buying at the **midpoint price of HK$7.17** requires the company to sustain 15%+ revenue growth over the next two years to digest the valuation
- Buying at the **floor price of HK$6.72** offers a somewhat better margin of safety, but it still isn't cheap
- **Recommendation**: mostly stay on the sidelines; wait for the share price to pull back to the HK$5-6 range post-listing before considering a position
