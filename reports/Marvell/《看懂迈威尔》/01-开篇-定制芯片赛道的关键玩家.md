## A Set of Facts That Will Change How You See Marvell

Same company, two years, market cap up from under $50 billion to $160 billion. Not on the back of a story — on the back of real results: FY2026 revenue up 42% year-over-year, data center revenue at 74% of the total, and Nvidia personally putting $2 billion into the stock.

Marvell Technology isn't in the sexiest business — it's in the most critical one: helping AWS and Microsoft design custom AI chips, and controlling 60% of the global optical interconnect market. The question is, at a $160 billion market cap and a GAAP P/E of roughly 61x, what exactly is this price betting on?

Here is the stock's trajectory over the past two years:

- Late 2024: shares broke through **$100** — the "custom AI silicon" narrative kicks off
- Mid-2025: shares climbed to **$130** — Marvell lands custom-chip contracts with AWS Trainium and Microsoft Maia
- March 2026: Nvidia announces a **$2 billion** investment in Marvell; the stock jumps 13% in a single day
- May 2026: **~$188** — market cap approaching **$160 billion**, up more than **200%** over the trailing 52 weeks

**In two years, market cap went from under $50 billion to $160 billion — a gain of more than 3x.** But if you ask most investors what Marvell actually does, few can give a clear answer.

That is what this series sets out to do: **take Marvell apart layer by layer — what it actually is, what's driving the rally, and whether it can keep climbing.**

## Who Marvell Actually Is

Marvell Technology (NASDAQ: MRVL), headquartered in Delaware, is a fabless semiconductor company — it designs chips itself and has them manufactured by TSMC.

Open up the FY2026 annual report (fiscal year ended January 2026) and the core numbers look like this:

| Metric | FY2026 | Scale assessment |
|------|--------|---------|
| Total revenue | **$8.195 billion** (+42%) | Accelerating growth |
| Data center revenue | **~$6.0 billion** (**74%** of total) | The overwhelming core |
| GAAP net income | **$2.67 billion** | First large-scale profit |
| Non-GAAP EPS | **$2.84** (+81%) | Profit growth far outpacing revenue |
| GAAP EPS | **$3.07** | Includes one-time gain from sale of automotive business |
| Operating cash flow | **$1.68 billion** | Improving cash generation |
| Current market cap (5/2026) | **~$160 billion** | Top 10 globally in semiconductors |
| Current P/E (GAAP TTM) | **~61x** (based on $188 share price) | Richly valued |
| Forward P/E (FY2027E) | **~47x** | Market is pricing in 30%+ growth |

Sources: Marvell FY2026 annual report, StockAnalysis.com

**In one sentence: Marvell is an AI infrastructure chip company with $8.2 billion in annual revenue, three-quarters of which comes from data centers, currently valued at $160 billion with a GAAP P/E of roughly 61x.**

## Three Common Misconceptions About Marvell

### Misconception One: Marvell Is a Competitor to Nvidia

This is the most common misunderstanding.

Nvidia makes **general-purpose GPUs** — one chip sold to everyone, with pricing power anchored by a platform ecosystem and CUDA developer lock-in. Marvell makes **custom ASICs** — purpose-built chips designed to each customer's specifications, one chip per customer.

The relationship between the two is more like **a bespoke tailor versus a fast-fashion retailer**:

| Dimension | Nvidia (general-purpose GPU) | Marvell (custom ASIC) |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Product | Standardized GPU, sold to everyone | Customer-specific chip, designed one-on-one |
| Customers | Thousands of companies + cloud providers | 3-4 hyperscale cloud providers |
| Pricing power | Very strong (near-monopoly position) | Limited (the customer holds the leverage) |
| Gross margin | **75%+** | **~61%** |
| Scale | Revenue **~$130 billion** | Revenue **~$8.2 billion** |

Marvell isn't a substitute for Nvidia — it's an alternative path for cloud providers who **don't want to depend entirely on Nvidia**. AWS uses Trainium not because it outperforms Nvidia GPUs, but because AWS doesn't want its fate tied to a single supplier.

**Counterpoint:** Precisely because custom ASICs are an "alternative" rather than the "optimal solution," if Nvidia cuts prices sharply or ships a much better price-performance product, cloud providers' demand for custom silicon could shrink quickly.

### Misconception Two: Marvell Is Just a "Small Company"

At a $160 billion market cap, Marvell already ranks in the global top 10 for semiconductors. But measured against the true giants:

| Company | Market cap | Multiple of Marvell |
|------|------|------------|
| Nvidia | ~$3.2 trillion | **20x** |
| Broadcom | ~$1.1 trillion | **7x** |
| Marvell | ~$160 billion | Baseline |

Marvell ranks second in the custom ASIC space, but the gap to the leader, Broadcom, is significant. Broadcom holds roughly **55%-70%** of the custom ASIC market (sources: TradingKey, SimplyWall St), versus Marvell's roughly **15%**.

**This isn't an inspirational "small company chasing the giant" story — it's a survival question of whether the runner-up can thrive.**

### Misconception Three: All AI Chip Companies Are the Same

Within semiconductors, "AI chips" actually spans at least three entirely different businesses:

| Type | Representative companies | Business model | Source of moat |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| General-purpose GPU | Nvidia | Platform + ecosystem + standard product | CUDA developer lock-in |
| Custom ASIC | Broadcom, Marvell | Purpose-built chips designed to customer spec | Design-cycle lock-in + accumulated IP |
| Optical interconnect | Marvell, Coherent | High-speed channels connecting chips together | Technology generation lead + yield barriers |

Marvell operates in the latter two simultaneously — **custom ASIC + optical interconnect**. This is the key point of differentiation versus Broadcom: Broadcom's ASIC business is larger in scale, but Marvell holds more than **60%** share in optical interconnect (source: Marvell IR).

**Counterpoint:** While optical interconnect commands a high market share, its TAM is far smaller than that of ASICs. Marvell's "differentiation" is essentially being a big fish in a smaller pond, rather than competing head-on with Broadcom in the main arena.

## Why Look at Marvell Now

Three things are happening at once:

**First, the custom ASIC market is exploding.**

Hyperscale cloud providers (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Meta) are collectively "de-Nvidia-fying" — not abandoning Nvidia, but reducing their dependence on a single supplier. Custom ASICs are one of their core strategies for doing so.

Management's numbers from the FY2026 Q4 earnings call: the custom ASIC business grew from near zero to an annualized run rate of **$1.5 billion** in a single fiscal year. Management is guiding for total FY2027 revenue above **$11 billion** (+30%+), approaching **$15 billion** in FY2028.

**Second, Nvidia has personally stepped in as an investor.**

On March 31, 2026, Nvidia announced a **$2 billion** investment in Marvell, with the two companies co-developing an NVLink Fusion interconnect solution. This signals that Nvidia does not view Marvell as a competitor, but as an "ecosystem partner" (source: NVIDIA official announcement).

**Counterpoint:** Nvidia's investment in Marvell could also be read as "turning a potential rival into a dependent" — once you've accepted Nvidia's money and joined its ecosystem, how much independence do you really retain?

**Third, the valuation is already extremely optimistic.**

At the current **~61x GAAP P/E**, **~47x forward P/E**, and **~19x P/S** — the market has already priced in 3-4 years of future growth. If everything goes smoothly, the returns could be solid; if any link in the chain breaks, the downside is substantial.

**This is the core tension at the heart of Marvell today: the growth is genuinely happening, but the price is genuinely reflecting all of it already.**
