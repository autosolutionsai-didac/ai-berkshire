# Global AI Company Valuation and Moat Panorama

> Analysis date: May 19, 2026 | Coverage: US stocks, Hong Kong stocks, A-shares (Main Board + ChiNext), STAR Market
>
> Disclaimer: This report is based on public information and valuation data and does not constitute investment advice.

---

## Key Findings

In May 2026, the global AI sector showed a **three-way divergence**:

1. **The SaaS software sector suffered a historic sell-off** (the iShares software ETF is down more than 21% year-to-date), with Adobe trading at a PE of just 14x and Salesforce's forward PE at just 13x — a once-in-a-decade bargain-hunting window
2. **AI infrastructure stocks, with earnings growing even faster, have seen valuations actually decline** — Nvidia's forward PE is only 25x, and Microsoft's forward PE is only 22x
3. **Domestic AI chip / large-model stocks remain in overvalued territory** — the STAR 50 Index trades at a PE of 156x (91st percentile historically), Cambricon at 263x, and Moore Threads / MetaX are still unprofitable

**Conclusion: The genuine value depressions are in US SaaS, Hong Kong internet platforms, and A-share security leaders — not in China's hot AI chip names.**

## Part 1: US AI Companies

### Market Background

Year-to-date in 2026, the combined capital expenditure of the "Magnificent Seven" is projected to reach $725 billion, and the AI infrastructure frenzy continues unabated. Yet the SaaS software sector has suffered a historic sell-off — the iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF (IGV) is down more than 21% year-to-date, and software-stock valuations have fallen below the S&P 500 average for the first time. This divergence has created a rare stock-picking window for value investors.

### 1. Microsoft (MSFT) — Clearly Undervalued | Moat 5/5

**Valuation:** Trailing PE 25 | Forward PE 22 | Morningstar fair value $600, currently undervalued by roughly 31%

**Business essence:** An essential-infrastructure supplier for enterprise digital operations. Revenue comes from three engines: Azure cloud computing (the world's No. 2 cloud, with the fastest-growing AI services), Microsoft 365 subscriptions (the standard for enterprise office productivity worldwide), and the Windows/LinkedIn/GitHub ecosystem. Through its deep partnership with OpenAI, AI is embedded across the entire product matrix — Azure AI services, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and GitHub Copilot.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (extremely strong):** Once an enterprise deploys the full Microsoft suite, migration costs are extremely high — data migration, employee retraining, and systems-integration rework
- **Network effects (strong):** Teams, LinkedIn, and GitHub all show clear network effects
- **Ecosystem lock-in (extremely strong):** A closed developer loop of VS Code + GitHub + Azure
- **Economies of scale (strong):** Declining marginal costs across cloud infrastructure

**Why it's undervalued:** A forward PE of 22x is below the S&P 500 average. The SaaS panic has spilled over onto Microsoft, even though Microsoft is precisely one of the biggest beneficiaries of the AI era. For a company growing 15%+ annually with the world's strongest enterprise-software moat, this is a historic low.

### 2. Meta (META) — Undervalued | Moat 5/5

**Valuation:** Trailing PE 22 | Forward PE 19 | Morningstar fair value $850, currently undervalued by roughly 28%

**Business essence:** The world's largest attention-monetization platform. Core revenue comes from digital advertising (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp), converting the attention of 3 billion+ daily active users into ad revenue through an AI-driven precision ad-targeting system. AI penetrates deeply throughout: recommendation algorithms, ad targeting, content generation, and the Meta AI assistant. Its self-developed, open-sourced Llama models have built a developer ecosystem.

**Moat:**
- **Network effects (extremely strong):** The social-network effect of 3 billion+ daily active users is the strongest type of moat
- **Data advantage (extremely strong):** Massive user behavioral data underpins the precision of the AI advertising system
- **Economies of scale (strong):** The marginal cost of the advertising platform approaches zero

**Why it's undervalued:** A forward PE of 19x is very cheap for a company with the world's largest social network and steady growth. The market harbors doubts about AI-related spending (the metaverse, infrastructure), but that spending is already translating into real returns in the form of improved advertising efficiency.

### 3. Adobe (ADBE) — Clearly Undervalued | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** Share price about $253 | Trailing PE 14 (70% below the 10-year median of 49!) | Morningstar fair value $380, undervalued by roughly 33%

**Business essence:** A subscription toll on creative tools. Adobe collects subscription fees through Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere), Document Cloud (Acrobat/PDF), and Experience Cloud (digital marketing). Adobe Firefly (AI image generation) is deeply embedded across the product line.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (extremely strong):** Professional designers have used the tools for years, and file formats (PSD, AI, PDF) have become industry standards
- **Brand (strong):** Photoshop is nearly synonymous with "image editing" itself
- **Ecosystem lock-in (strong):** Collaboration across Creative Cloud products forms a complete workflow

**Risk:** Mizuho downgraded the stock, arguing that AI-based competition is eroding the moat. Midjourney and Canva are eating into the entry-level market. But for professional-tier creators, Adobe's position remains solid.

**Why it's undervalued:** A PE of 14x is a rare low in Adobe's valuation history. The market has overreacted to the narrative of AI disrupting creative tools. TTM earnings per share of $17.18 prove that profitability remains strong.

---

### 4. Nvidia (NVDA) — Undervalued | Moat 5/5

**Valuation:** Market cap $5.48 trillion | Trailing PE 46 | Forward PE 25 | Morningstar fair value $260, undervalued by roughly 17%

**Business essence:** The arms dealer of AI compute. The data-center business generated $194 billion in revenue in fiscal 2026, up 68% year-over-year. Nearly all AI large-model training and inference depends on Nvidia GPUs. The business model combines hardware (GPUs) with software-ecosystem lock-in (CUDA).

**Moat:**
- **Technology barriers (extremely strong):** GPU architecture is 1-2 generations ahead of competitors
- **Ecosystem lock-in (extremely strong):** CUDA is the deepest moat — millions of developers and tens of thousands of AI libraries are built on CUDA, making migration costs extremely high
- **Economies of scale (strong):** R&D spending is amortized to significant effect

**Why it's undervalued:** A forward PE of 25x is quite cheap for a company growing revenue at 68% and sitting at the center of the AI revolution. 2026 capex budgets are being sharply raised (from $670 billion to $725 billion).

### 5. Amazon (AMZN) — Undervalued | Moat 5/5

**Valuation:** Trailing PE 32 (43% below the 3-year average of 56, and 60% below the 10-year median of 80)

**Business essence:** Three flywheels — e-commerce, cloud computing, and advertising. AWS is the world's largest cloud platform and a core supplier of AI infrastructure; the e-commerce platform has an unmatched logistics network; the advertising business is a high-margin growth engine. AWS Bedrock, the self-developed Trainium chip, and Amazon Q are all accelerating.

**Moat:**
- **Economies of scale (extremely strong):** The scale barriers of the e-commerce logistics network and cloud infrastructure are nearly insurmountable
- **Switching costs (strong):** AWS customers face high migration costs
- **Network effects (strong):** A two-sided seller-buyer network on the e-commerce platform

### 6. Salesforce (CRM) — Clearly Undervalued | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** Forward PE about 13 (share price down 38% year-to-date) | PS 3.5x | Average analyst price target implies roughly 49% upside

**Business essence:** The operating system for enterprise customer relationship management. Once a customer deploys the platform, migration is very difficult (customer data, sales processes, and custom configurations all live on the platform). Agentforce (AI agents) and Data Cloud have combined annualized recurring revenue of $2.9 billion, up more than 200% year-over-year.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (extremely strong):** The CRM system is a company's most central customer-data hub; switching means rebuilding every sales process
- **Data accumulation (strong):** Customer data becomes more valuable the longer it accumulates

**Why it's undervalued:** A forward PE of just 13x is a classic value-stock valuation level, with no growth premium whatsoever. Agentforce's 200%+ growth rate shows the AI transition is already paying off. The blanket SaaS sell-off has indiscriminately punished good and bad companies alike.

### 7. ServiceNow (NOW) — Undervalued | Moat 4/5

**Business essence:** The central nervous system of enterprise IT service management. Once companies use ServiceNow to manage IT tickets, employee services, and security operations, strong stickiness forms. Subscription revenue is up 19% year-over-year. Now Assist (the AI assistant) is embedded across the entire platform.

**Moat:** Extremely strong switching costs (IT operations processes are deeply bound to the platform) plus sustained growth (19% growth at a 22x PE is very attractive).

### 8. Micron Technology (MU) — Undervalued but High Cyclical Risk | Moat 3/5

**Valuation:** Market cap $817 billion | Trailing PE 34 | Forward PE 7 (extremely low)

**Business essence:** The memory supplier for the AI era. There are only three major global DRAM suppliers (Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron), an oligopoly structure.

**Risk:** Memory is a highly commoditized industry with volatile margins.

## Part 2: Hong Kong AI Companies

### Market Background

The Hong Kong AI sector shows a pattern of "internet giants as valuation depressions + pure-play AI companies as valuation bubbles." Giants like Tencent and Alibaba trade at PEs of just 15-17x (versus a US tech-stock average of 35x), while pure-play AI companies such as SenseTime and Zhipu AI remain deeply unprofitable.

### 1. Tencent Holdings (0700.HK) — The Chinese Tech Company with the Deepest Moat | 5/5

**Valuation:** Share price about HK$456 | Market cap about HK$4.3 trillion | 2026E PE about 17x | Down roughly 33% from its 52-week high

**Business essence:** A social-traffic monetization platform. Revenue comes from gaming (domestic and overseas), advertising (Video Accounts, Moments, Mini Programs), and fintech and business services (WeChat Pay, Tencent Cloud). The core asset is the social graph of Weixin/WeChat's 1.3 billion+ users, with every business built on top of this "super app" entry point.

**Moat:**
- **Network effects (extremely strong):** The social-network effect of 1.3 billion+ users is the most durable of all moat types — users will not abandon their social ties just because a competitor is a few dollars cheaper
- **Switching costs (extremely high):** Chat history, social connections, the Mini Program ecosystem, WeChat Pay habits, and Official Account subscriptions are all locked into WeChat
- **Ecosystem barriers:** WeChat Mini Programs have 7 million+ developers, and AI-agent capabilities are being embedded into the Mini Program ecosystem

**Why it's undervalued:** A 17x PE is significantly undervalued for a company with annual profit exceeding 200 billion yuan, a top-tier moat, and accelerating AI monetization. Compared with the 35x average PE of US tech stocks, there is a valuation gap of nearly 2x.

---

### 2. Alibaba (9988.HK) — An Undervalued AI Infrastructure Provider | 4/5

**Valuation:** Market cap about HK$2.6 trillion | 2026E PE about 16x

**Business essence:** The e-commerce platform (Taobao and Tmall) earns money through advertising fees and commissions — essentially "digital commercial real estate." Alibaba Cloud is China's largest public cloud provider and is transitioning from IaaS toward AI inference services. The Qwen (Tongyi Qianwen) models are integrated directly into the Taobao, Alipay, and Amap ecosystems. During the Spring Festival period, the AI shopping agent surpassed 10 million orders within nine hours.

**Moat:** Alibaba Cloud holds the No. 1 share of China's public cloud market, plus the two-sided market effect of millions of merchants on Taobao/Tmall, plus high migration costs for enterprise customers moving off its cloud services.

**Risk:** Fierce e-commerce competition (Pinduoduo and Douyin e-commerce continue to erode share), and the food-delivery war is driving up expenses.

---

### 3. Baidu Group (9888.HK) — Core Assets the Market Is "Giving Away for Free" | 3.5/5

**Valuation:** Share price about HK$136 | Market cap about HK$370 billion (about $47.3 billion) | 2026E PE about 19x

**Business essence:** Revenue comes mainly from search advertising and AI cloud services. But the real value lies in three independent assets: Kunlunxin (AI chips, already in dual-listing counseling for the STAR Market and Hong Kong), Apollo Go (the world's largest robotaxi operating platform), and the ERNIE large model/Qianfan platform.

**Key data point:** JPMorgan noted that Kunlunxin alone carries a standalone valuation of $40-49 billion, already close to Baidu's total market cap ($47.3 billion). This means the search, cloud, and autonomous-driving businesses are essentially being given away for free — a classic sum-of-the-parts discount opportunity.

**Risk:** Search advertising is being disrupted by generative AI search, and Baidu's influence in the large-model race is being squeezed by rivals such as ByteDance.

---

### 4. Kuaishou (1024.HK) — Kling AI Is Reshaping the Valuation | 3.5/5

**Valuation:** Share price about HK$55 | Market cap about HK$240 billion | PE about 10x

**Business essence:** A short-video platform earning revenue from livestream tipping, advertising, and e-commerce. The biggest variable for 2026 is **Kling AI** — its self-developed AI video-generation model, which has already surpassed $1 billion in annualized revenue and 12 million+ monthly active users, and is planning a spin-off IPO with a target valuation of $20 billion.

**Why it's undervalued:** At a PE of just 10x, the targeted Kling AI spin-off valuation ($20 billion) already amounts to roughly 65% of Kuaishou's parent-company market cap. If the Kling AI listing succeeds, the parent company's remaining businesses (short video + e-commerce + livestreaming) would essentially come for free.

### 5. Xiaomi Corporation (1810.HK) — AI Valuation Potential in the "People-Car-Home" Ecosystem | 4/5

**Valuation:** Share price about HK$31 | Market cap about HK$770 billion | PE about 17x | 20th percentile over the past 5 years

**Business essence:** Revenue comes from smartphones, IoT consumer products, internet services (advertising, gaming, and finance), and smart electric vehicles. The core model is "hardware for traffic, internet services for monetization." Xiaomi runs the world's largest consumer IoT platform, with smartphones, cars, and smart homes forming a closed "people-car-home" ecosystem.

**Sum-of-the-parts valuation:** Smartphones (HK$335 billion) + IoT (HK$246 billion) + Internet services (HK$242 billion) + Automotive (HK$212 billion) + AI/chips/robotics (HK$125 billion) = about HK$1.16 trillion, implying a share price of about HK$45 — roughly 45% upside from the current HK$31.

**Why it's undervalued:** A 15x PE plus a 6.7% dividend yield is highly attractive in a falling-rate cycle. Growth in the AI-compute business is currently barely reflected in the valuation.

## Part 3: A-Share AI Companies (Main Board + ChiNext)

### Market Background

By 2026, the logic behind A-share AI investing has shifted from "narrative-driven" to "earnings-driven." Hot tracks such as optical modules and AI chips are broadly overvalued (Zhongji Innolight's market cap now exceeds BYD's), while the genuine value depressions are companies where "AI empowers an existing core business" — a solid underlying business where AI is a tool for improving efficiency rather than the entire story.

### 1. Hikvision (002415.SZ) — The Most Noteworthy A-Share AI Name | Moat 5/5

**Valuation:** Share price about 33 yuan | Market cap about 310 billion yuan | PE (TTM) about 20x, in the low end of its five-year historical range (five-year median PE about 25x)

**Business essence:** On the surface it sells cameras, but the real money is made by "providing intelligent video solutions to governments and enterprises." Hardware is the entry point; software and services are the profit engine. 2025 net profit was about 14.2 billion yuan, up 18.5% year-over-year, with gross margin above 44%.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (extremely strong):** Security systems are safety-critical; once government and enterprise customers deploy one, they are extremely reluctant to switch
- **Economies of scale (extremely strong):** A 25.9% share of the global security market, nearly 10 percentage points ahead of second-place Dahua
- **Technology barriers (strong):** More than 7,000 patents worldwide, with an AI algorithm library covering hundreds of scenarios
- **Brand (strong):** The world's No. 1 security brand

**Why it's undervalued:** A 20x PE is near the bottom of its recent-year range. If 2026 profit grows 10-15%, the forward PE is only 17-18x. The negative impact of US sanctions is already fully reflected in the share price.

**Risk:** Restrictions from the US Entity List, and fiscal pressure on domestic local governments.

---

### 2. Dahua Technology (002236.SZ) — The Undervalued Runner-Up in Security | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** Share price about 18 yuan | Market cap about 60 billion yuan | PE about 16-18x | 52-week low of 15 yuan

**Business essence:** Similar to Hikvision. 2025 net profit is expected to be about 3.9 billion yuan, up 33.6% year-over-year. Overseas business and innovation businesses (machine vision, drones, etc.) are the growth highlights.

**Why it's undervalued:** PEG is below 0.6. Dahua also has a higher share of overseas revenue and is not on the Entity List, making it relatively less affected by US sanctions.

### 3. Unisplendour (000938.SZ) — A Low-Profile ICT Infrastructure Leader | Moat 3.5/5

**Valuation:** Market cap about 70 billion yuan | 2026E PE about 20-25x | PS only about 0.5x

**Business essence:** Through its subsidiary H3C, it provides enterprise-grade IT infrastructure (servers, storage, network equipment, cloud platforms) — "China's HPE/Cisco."

**Moat:** Clear ecosystem lock-in once enterprise network equipment is deployed, plus a leading share of the domestic enterprise-network-equipment market, plus strong recognition among government and enterprise customers.

### 4. Inovance Technology (300124.SZ) — The "Little Huawei" of Industrial Automation | Moat 4.5/5

**Valuation:** Market cap about 180 billion yuan | Static PE about 42x | 2026E PE about 26x

**Business essence:** China's leading industrial-automation company. It makes inverters, servo systems, PLCs, and other industrial-control equipment that helps manufacturing plants upgrade to automation. It holds the No. 1 share of the domestic servo-system market, ahead of foreign brands. Its founders come from Huawei, and its management culture is excellent.

**Moat:** Once industrial-automation equipment is embedded in a production line, replacement costs are extremely high (requiring a production shutdown for commissioning), plus self-developed core components such as IGBTs and encoders, plus a robotics business (planetary roller screws, bionic arms) opening new growth avenues.

**Valuation verdict:** At a 2026E PE of 26x against 25-30% growth, PEG is about 1 — fair. If the robotics business delivers incremental growth, it becomes attractive.

### 5. Hundsun Technologies (600570.SH) — The Hidden Champion of Financial IT | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** Share price about 27 yuan | Market cap about 50 billion yuan | 2026E PE about 34x

**Business essence:** China's undisputed leader in financial IT. It provides core trading systems, asset-management systems, and risk-control systems to securities firms, funds, and banks. Once a Hundsun system is chosen, switching is nearly impossible — it involves regulatory compliance, data security, and business continuity. It holds more than 50% share in the securities/fund segment. Ant Group is its largest shareholder.

## Part 4: STAR Market AI Companies

### Market Background

The STAR 50 Index's PE has reached 156.4x, in the 91st percentile historically. The combined market cap of the "AI chip six sisters" (Cambricon, Hygon, Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, and Iluvatar CoreX) has surpassed 3 trillion yuan. Overall valuation pressure is evident, but a small number of companies with "real profits + technology barriers + not-yet-fully-priced valuations" still merit attention.

### 1. Amlogic (688099.SH) — The Hidden Champion of Edge-AI SoCs | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** 2026E PE about 35-40x | Against 61% net-profit growth | PEG below 0.7

**Business essence:** Supplies multimedia smart-terminal SoC chips for smart set-top boxes, smart TVs, and AIoT devices. A fabless model (designs but does not manufacture), yielding high margins. 2025 revenue was 6.793 billion yuan, with net profit of 873 million yuan. Gross margin has risen quarter over quarter to above 40%. Shipments of its 6nm chips in 2026 are expected to exceed 30 million units.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (strong):** Once an SoC is embedded in a customer's product design, replacing it requires re-adapting the entire software ecosystem
- **Economies of scale (moderately strong):** The world's No. 2 and mainland China's No. 1 supplier of home-terminal SoCs
- **Technology barriers (strong):** Its T/V-series chips with built-in NPUs have established differentiation in edge AI inference

**This is the most cost-effective name in STAR Market AI — a PEG below 0.7 is extremely rare on a board where sky-high valuations are the norm.**

---

### 2. Bestechnic (688608.SH) — The Leader in On-Device AI Wearable Chips | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** 2026E PE about 31-35x | Profit growth of 27-35%

**Business essence:** Supplies low-power Bluetooth/Wi-Fi SoC chips for wearable devices such as TWS earbuds, smartwatches, and AI glasses — "selling chips into every device worn on the body." 2025 revenue was about 3.525 billion yuan, with net profit of 588 million yuan; gross margin rose 4 percentage points year-over-year to 38.70%.

**Moat:**
- **Switching costs (strong):** Its BES-series chips are deeply integrated into products from customers such as Xiaomi, Alibaba, and Li Auto
- **Technology barriers (strong):** Its unique combination of low-power Bluetooth audio and AI inference capability; the BES2800 is already in mass production across multiple AI-glasses products

**The AI-glasses market is on the eve of an explosive breakout, and Bestechnic is precisely positioned for it; the current valuation has not yet reflected that expectation.**

---

### 3. Montage Technology (688008.SH) — A Global Oligopolist in AI-Compute Interconnect Chips | Moat 4/5

**Valuation:** 2026E PE about 64-72x | Gross margin 65.57%

## Core Investment Principles

1. **By 2026, AI investing has shifted from "narrative-driven" to "earnings-driven"** — only companies that can convert AI into actual profit are worth holding
2. **The genuine value depression is in "AI empowering an existing core business"** — for companies like Hikvision, Tencent, and Microsoft, AI is a tool for improving efficiency rather than the entire story; their underlying business is solid, and their valuations are correspondingly more reasonable
3. **The blanket sell-off in the SaaS sector has created a once-in-a-decade bargain-hunting opportunity** — Adobe at a PE of 14x, Salesforce at a forward PE of 13x
4. **The domestic AI chip sector is broadly overvalued** — the STAR 50 Index trades at a PE of 156x (91st percentile historically); rather than chasing star names at inflated prices, it is better to focus on companies with real profits, such as Amlogic and Bestechnic
5. **Hong Kong internet giants are severely undervalued** — Tencent's 17x PE compares with a 35x average for US tech stocks, a valuation gap of nearly 2x

> A truly good investment means concentrating in high-certainty companies, not spreading capital across every name that merely "looks cheap." While others chase the stars, look for the good businesses everyone else has overlooked.
