## A set of numbers that will make you rethink the forklift industry

In 2025, China's forklift industry quietly completed a reversal: electric forklifts reached a **73.6%** share of sales, while internal-combustion forklift sales began to decline in absolute terms. At the same time, the industry's annual sales volume reached **1.4518 million units**, and China remained the world's largest forklift producer and consumer for the 14th consecutive year, with output exceeding **50%** of the global total.

A century-old industrial equipment category is undergoing a complete overhaul of its power system. And the biggest beneficiaries of this overhaul are likely two Chinese companies you've probably never heard of.

(Source: China Construction Machinery Association, Industrial Vehicle Branch, 2024-2025 data)

In this industry — with annual output value exceeding 100 billion yuan and annual sales exceeding one million units — the two Chinese companies standing at the top of the pyramid, Anhui Heli and Hangcha Group, together capture over **45%** of the domestic market.

Today we're dissecting one of them: **Hangcha Group (603298.SH)**.

## Three common misconceptions about Hangcha

### Misconception 1: Forklifts are a sunset industry

Not true.

Forklifts are basic equipment for industrial material handling — wherever there are factories, warehouses, ports, or logistics centers, forklifts are needed. The global forklift market was valued at approximately **$81.8 billion** in 2024 (Fortune Business Insights data), with an expected compound annual growth rate of about **5.6%** over the next decade.

More importantly, forklifts have two often-overlooked attributes:

1. **A "consumable" nature** — forklifts are typically replaced every 5-7 years, so this isn't a one-time sale. After a company buys a forklift, parts replacement, maintenance, and battery replacement generate ongoing revenue. The aftermarket carries gross margins as high as **30-40%**, far higher than the complete-vehicle business.
2. **Electrification is reshaping the industry** — the shift from lead-acid to lithium batteries, and from internal combustion to pure electric, means Chinese companies' advantages across the lithium battery supply chain are translating into competitive advantages in forklifts. This isn't a gradual change — it's a structural inflection point.

**But the counterargument is also worth hearing**: the forklift industry doesn't grow fast (5-6% globally), and the ceiling is limited (the global market is about $100 billion). It won't produce "10x in ten years" explosive growth. This is a stable but unglamorous business.

### Misconception 2: Hangcha is "low-end manufacturing"

This label is becoming less and less accurate.

Four years ago (2021), Hangcha's gross margin was just **15.51%** — it did look like a low-end manufacturer. But by 2025, gross margin had risen to **24.78%**, up nearly 10 percentage points in four years. Net profit grew from **908 million** to **2.191 billion yuan**, an increase of **141%**.

Two drivers lie behind this:

| Driver | Contribution | Sustainability |
|-------|------|---------|
| Product mix upgrade (electrification + high-margin overseas sales) | About 60% | Structural factor, relatively sustainable |
| Falling steel prices (cyclical factor) | About 40% | Cyclical — margins would be pressured if steel prices rebound |

(Source: Analysis and estimates based on Hangcha Group's 2021-2025 annual report data)

Hangcha formed a joint venture, Pengcheng New Energy, with CATL back in 2018 to develop lithium batteries specifically for forklifts. By 2025, it had achieved full in-house R&D and manufacturing of the "three electrics" (battery, electric drive, electric control). Hydrogen fuel cell forklifts have also been deployed, with over **400 units** in service.

**But the counterargument**: about 40% of the gross margin improvement comes from falling steel prices — if steel prices return to their 2021 highs, gross margin could pull back 3-5 percentage points. The technical barriers of electrification will also weaken as the whole industry adopts it.

### Misconception 3: Hangcha is a state-owned enterprise

**It is not.**

The actual controller of Hangcha Group is **Qiu Jianping** — a classic Zhejiang entrepreneur. Qiu Jianping holds approximately **41.34%** of Hangcha Group's shares through Zhejiang Hangcha Holding. The Hangzhou Municipal State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission holds approximately **20.39%** through Hangshi Group, making it the second-largest shareholder.

Qiu Jianping simultaneously controls Great Star Industrial (002444.SZ) and Xinchai Co. (301032.SZ), and is currently pushing forward an IPO for ZC Rubber, forming a complex "Great Star" capital network.

What does this mean? It means Hangcha's governance structure is not the "state-owned sense of security" that most people assume, but rather a structure controlled by private capital with frequent related-party transactions. This is both an efficiency advantage (fast decision-making, flexible incentives) and a governance risk (conflicts of interest, related-party transactions).

**On this point, bulls and bears disagree sharply**:

- Bulls say: since Qiu Jianping took control, Hangcha's revenue grew from 7 billion yuan to 17.7 billion yuan — the controller has demonstrated real ability
- Bears say: the "Great Star" cross-shareholding network is too complicated, and the Guozi Robotics acquisition is a textbook example of a related-party transaction

Later installments will dissect this issue in detail.
