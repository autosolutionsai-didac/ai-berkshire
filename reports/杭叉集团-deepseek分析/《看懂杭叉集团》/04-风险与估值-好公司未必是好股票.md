# A Good Company Isn't Necessarily a Good Stock

> "Understanding Hangcha Group" series · Part 04 · Risk and Valuation (Finale)
> Reading time: about 12 minutes

---

Hangcha Group: ROE of 20%, net margin of 13%, a duopoly market structure, an early lead in electrification, and accelerating globalization — with these labels attached, the market prices the stock at 15x P/E.

Not expensive. But not cheap either. Behind that 15x P/E lie four landmines: accounts receivable that have doubled in four years, frequent related-party transactions involving the controlling shareholder, a 69-year-old chairman holding both the chairman and general manager roles, and 43% of overseas revenue exposed to tariff pressure. This piece takes these risks apart one by one, then answers the question: **at the current price, is the margin of safety wide enough?**

---

## Risk One: Accounts Receivable Doubled in Four Years — the Biggest Financial Red Flag

This is the single most important finding in this series.

| Year | Accounts Receivable Turnover Days | Revenue Growth (Same Period) |
|:---:|:--------------:|:----------:|
| 2021 | **23 days** | +26.5% |
| 2022 | 31 days | -0.5% |
| 2023 | 33 days | +12.9% |
| 2024 | 45 days | +1.2% |
| 2025 | **55 days** | +5.9% |
| 2026Q1 | **58 days** | +10.4% |

(Source: Hangcha Group 2021-2025 annual reports, 2026Q1 quarterly report)

Accounts receivable turnover days surged from **23 days** in 2021 to **55 days** in 2025 — more than doubling in four years. In 2026Q1 it deteriorated further, to **58 days**.

Estimating the change in accounts receivable balance from turnover days:
- 2021: approximately **RMB 930 million**
- 2025: approximately **RMB 2.66 billion**
- Increase: **186%** — while revenue over the same period grew only **22.4%**

**What does this mean?**

Accounts receivable growth (186%) far outpaces revenue growth (22.4%), indicating the company has been substantially loosening credit terms for downstream customers in order to sustain revenue growth. Put plainly — the share of sales made "on credit" keeps rising.

This roughly **RMB 1.6 billion** of "excess receivables" has already been booked in the income statement (contributing to profit), but has not yet converted into cash. If part of it eventually turns into bad debt, it will directly hit net profit.

**Meanwhile, inventory turnover is also deteriorating**:

| Year | Inventory Turnover Days |
|:---:|:----------:|
| 2021 | **46 days** |
| 2025 | **74 days** |
| 2026Q1 | **78 days** |

(Source: Hangcha Group annual and quarterly reports)

Receivables and inventory deteriorating together means that **overall working-capital efficiency is declining**.

### The Counter-Argument

1. Overseas customers inherently have longer payment terms than domestic ones. As Hangcha's overseas revenue share rose from 20% to 43%, this naturally lengthened the overall accounts receivable turnover.
2. In 2025, credit impairment losses declined year over year, suggesting receivables quality is improving rather than deteriorating.
3. Standard payment terms for large logistics companies are typically 60-90 days, which is industry practice.

**Even accounting for these factors, the move from 23 days to 55 days is still a change that warrants a high degree of caution.** Investors need to keep tracking whether turnover days continue to deteriorate in subsequent quarters and whether bad-debt provisions increase.

## Risk Two: Governance Structure — the Shadow of the "Great Star" Empire

This is the second risk that deserves serious attention.

### Qiu Jianping's Business Empire

Qiu Jianping, Hangcha's controlling shareholder, simultaneously controls or holds stakes in multiple companies:

| Company | Qiu Jianping's Role | Relationship |
|------|----------|------|
| **Hangcha Group** (603298.SH) | Controlling shareholder (holds approximately 41.34%) | The company itself |
| **Great Star Industrial** (002444.SZ) | Chairman | Related party |
| **Xinchai Co., Ltd.** (301032.SZ) | Controlling shareholder | Engine supplier for Hangcha's internal-combustion forklifts |
| **Zhongce Rubber** | Controlling shareholder, pursuing an IPO | Related party |
| **Guozi Robotics** | Indirectly controlled through related parties | Acquired by Hangcha in 2025 |

(Source: Huafu Securities research report, Qichacha, Hangcha Group announcements)

### The Guozi Robotics Acquisition — a Textbook Related-Party Transaction

In July 2025, Hangcha's subsidiary Hangcha Intelligent acquired **99.23%** of Guozi Robotics through a capital increase and share expansion, in a deal worth approximately **RMB 398 million**.

Key details:
- The counterparties included **Kunxia Investment** (controlled by Qiu Jianping) and **Great Star Industrial** (controlled by Qiu Jianping)
- Qiu Jianping serves as a director of both Guozi Robotics and Great Star Industrial
- Qiu Jianping's daughter, **Qiu Fei**, also serves as a director of Guozi Robotics
- The transaction constitutes a related-party transaction, and the relevant parties recused themselves from voting

(Source: Hangcha Group announcement on its controlled subsidiary's acquisition of Guozi Robotics and related-party transaction, July 2025)

**Assessment**: The strategic direction of the Guozi Robotics acquisition is sound (smart logistics is a natural extension of forklifts), but the transaction structure is complex — the controlling shareholder is effectively "both buyer and seller." The complex web of cross-shareholdings widens the potential channels for value transfer.

**The counter-argument**: A related-party transaction is not the same as value transfer, and the audit by Tianjian Certified Public Accountants found no irregularities. Hangcha's performance since Qiu Jianping took control is plain for all to see (revenue grew from RMB 7 billion to RMB 17.7 billion), and the controlling shareholder's execution should not be dismissed wholesale on account of the governance structure.

**But investors must keep monitoring**: the scale of related-party transactions, whether pricing is fair, and whether the controlling shareholder is treating Hangcha as a "cash machine."

## Risk Three: An Aging Management Team — Succession Is Pressing

| Name | Age | Position | Years at the Company |
|------|:---:|------|:---:|
| Zhao Limin | **69** | Chairman and General Manager | **40+ years** |
| Jin Huashu | 47 | Deputy General Manager | 25 years |
| Li Yuansong | 48 | Deputy General Manager | 26 years |
| Xu Zhengyu | 55 | Chief Engineer | 34 years |
| Zhang Shutong | 60 | Chief Financial Officer | 31 years |

(Source: Hangcha Group 2025 annual report, management information)

Nearly the entire core management team was developed internally, with an average age of about **57**.

Key issues:
1. At 69, Zhao Limin still **holds both roles** (chairman and general manager), with no publicly disclosed succession plan
2. Succession risk in manufacturing companies has historically been underappreciated — leadership transitions are often when a company is most vulnerable
3. The company has never had a large-scale equity incentive plan, so management's interests are not tightly aligned with shareholders'

**The counter-argument**: The management team is extremely stable (core members have worked at Hangcha for more than 20 years) and has deep commitment to the company. Jin Huashu, 47, and Li Yuansong, 48, are plausible potential successors.

## Risk Four: Trade Barriers — 43% of Overseas Revenue Faces Tariff Risk

In 2025, Hangcha's overseas revenue share reached **43.38%**, concentrated mainly in Europe and Southeast Asia. Rising global trade protectionism is a risk that cannot be ignored.

**Hangcha's countermeasures**:

| Measure | Progress | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Building a plant in Thailand | Production starts 2026Q2, annual capacity of **10,000 units** | Avoids Southeast Asian tariffs, but capacity is limited |
| France joint venture (with Manitou) | Underway | A step toward localized production in Europe |
| SKD (semi-knocked-down) kit exports | Already in place | Reduces tariff costs |
| Market diversification | Expanding into the Middle East, Brazil, and other emerging markets | Spreads risk |

(Source: Hangcha Group announcements, brokerage research reports)

**Assessment**: Hangcha's direct exports to the U.S. market are already a small share, so the near-term impact is limited. But if the EU were also to impose tariffs on Chinese forklifts (there is currently no anti-dumping case specifically targeting Chinese forklifts), the blow would be much bigger — Europe is Hangcha's largest overseas market.

**The counter-argument**: Gross margin on forklift exports is about **30.7%**, so even with a 10-15% tariff added, there is still room for profit. Moreover, the Thailand plant and the France joint venture are gradually coming online, so over the long run tariff risk is manageable.

## Risk Five: Slowing Growth Plus a Possible Margin Pullback

| Year | Revenue Growth | Net Profit Growth |
|:---:|:-------:|:---------:|
| 2021 | +26.5% | +8.4% |
| 2022 | -0.5% | +8.7% |
| 2023 | +12.9% | **+73.8%** |
| 2024 | +1.2% | +17.3% |
| 2025 | +5.9% | +8.9% |

(Source: Hangcha Group annual reports)

Revenue growth slowed from 26.5% in 2021 to 5.9% in 2025. Net profit growth **has come almost entirely from margin expansion rather than revenue growth** — the sustainability of this growth pattern is questionable.

The 2026Q1 data already shows warning signs: gross margin was **22.50%**, down **2.28 percentage points** quarter over quarter; net margin was **9.44%**, down **3.63 percentage points** quarter over quarter. This could be an early sign of a steel-price rebound or intensifying competition.

(Source: Hangcha Group 2026 Q1 report)

**The counter-argument**: Q1 is typically the low point of the year, so a single quarter's fluctuation should not be over-interpreted. Excluding foreign-exchange losses, 2026Q1 net profit attributable to shareholders grew about **25%** year over year — actual operating performance was not bad at all.

**Bull scenario assumptions**: electrification accelerates + overseas expansion beats expectations + steel prices stay low
**Base scenario assumptions**: steady growth + margins hold + valuation multiple does not expand

### Margin-of-Safety Assessment

In the base scenario, the target price in 3 years is about **RMB 32**, roughly **19%** upside from the benchmark price. In the bear scenario, the downside risk is about **17%**.

**Upside and downside are roughly symmetric** — that is not an exciting risk/reward ratio. Value investors typically prefer a margin of safety of **25-30%** or more.

## Series Conclusion: A True Portrait of Hangcha

Across all four articles, the portrait of Hangcha Group looks like this:

**What it is**: one of China's forklift duopoly players, with annual revenue of RMB 17.7 billion, a net margin of 13%, and an ROE of 20%. Its business model is upgrading from "selling steel" to "selling electrification + services + intelligence," with overseas revenue exceeding 40% of the total and globalization accelerating.

**Its strengths**: a stable industry structure, the right direction on electrification, large room for internationalization, financial safety, and reasonably solid cash flow.

**Its concerns**: accounts receivable have doubled in four years, the controlling shareholder's "Great Star" governance structure is complex, management is aging, pricing power is weak, and growth is slowing.

**In one line**: Hangcha is **a good manufacturing company worth continuing to track, but one that needs a wider margin of safety**. At a P/E of 15-16x, the risk/reward isn't generous, but there's also no need to rush to sell.
