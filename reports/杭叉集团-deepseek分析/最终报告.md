> Hangcha Group is a manufacturing leader with 20%+ ROE and a 15.5x P/E — a solid but not top-tier business. ## IV. Summary of Analysis by Dimension

### 4.1 Business Model (Duan Yongping's lens) ★★★☆

**Core findings**:
- **Industrial "consumable" nature**: forklifts have an average replacement cycle of 5-7 years; the aftermarket (parts + service) contributes about 15-20% of revenue at gross margins as high as 30-40%. This is not a one-time sale
- **Revenue structure**: electric complete units 35-40% / internal-combustion complete units 25-30% / parts and aftermarket 15-20% / intelligent logistics 3-5%. Overseas revenue share is 43.38% (+27%), with overseas gross margin of ~30.7% significantly higher than the domestic ~20-22%
- **Moat score 3.28/5**: economies of scale strongest (4 stars), technology barriers medium (3 stars), switching costs medium (3 stars), brand power medium (3.5 stars), network effects weakest (2 stars)
- **Weak pricing power (2.5 stars)**: essentially no room to raise prices domestically (Heli is right next door); overseas sales rely on value-for-money rather than brand premium

> Duan Yongping's words: "**A good business model is one with a very strong moat.**" Hangcha has a moat, but it isn't wide.

### 4.2 Financials and Valuation (Buffett's lens) ★★★☆

**Core findings**:
- **Profit growth relies entirely on margin expansion**: net profit grew from 908 million to 2.191 billion yuan (+141%), while revenue grew only 22.4%. About 60% of the gross margin improvement comes from structural factors (electrification + exports), and 40% from the cyclical decline in steel prices
- **DuPont breakdown of ROE is healthy**: the 18-20% ROE comes from rising net margin (6.84% to 13.07%), not leverage. But asset turnover has fallen from 1.2x to 1.0x, dragged down by receivables and inventory
- **Warning — the biggest red flag: accounts receivable doubled in four years**: turnover days rose from 23 to 55 (deteriorating further to 58 days in Q1 2026). Estimated receivables balance rose from 930 million to 2.66 billion yuan, an increase of 186% — far outpacing the 22% revenue increase
- **Three-scenario valuation**: optimistic RMB 40.0 (+51%) / neutral RMB 31.6 (+19%) / pessimistic RMB 21.9 (-17%). The current margin of safety is about 16%, below Buffett's preferred 25-30% standard

> Buffett's words: "**Rapid growth in accounts receivable is often the result of management loosening credit policy to mask a slowdown in revenue.**"

### 4.3 Industry Competition (Munger's lens) ★★★★

**Core findings**:
- **The global forklift market is about $82 billion**; China accounts for over 50% of global output, but the duopoly (Hangcha + Heli) holds only about 8% of global revenue share — internationalization is the biggest growth pole
- **China's "two leaders, many strong players" structure is stable**, with Hangcha and Heli together holding over 45% of the domestic market, and concentration still rising
- **The electrification inflection point is confirmed**: the electrification rate jumped from 67.9% in 2023 to 73.6% in 2024, and internal-combustion forklift sales have begun to decline in absolute volume. Lithium-ization is building a structural advantage for Chinese companies
- **Competitor-by-competitor scoring**: Anhui Heli (4 stars), BYD Forklift (3.5 stars), Toyota Industries (5 stars), KION Group (4 stars). No force looks likely to disrupt the duopoly structure in the near term
- **Industry barriers**: manufacturing barriers are moderate, but brand, channel, and service network barriers are deepening. New entrants face a 3-5 year channel-building cycle

> Munger's words: "**In an industry with a stable structure, even an ordinary company can do quite well.**" That's exactly the case in the forklift industry.

### 4.4 Risk Management (Li Lu's lens) ★★★

**Core findings**:
- **Key correction**: the actual controller is **Qiu Jianping (a private individual)**, not the Hangzhou Municipal SASAC. Qiu Jianping holds about 41.34% through Zhejiang Hangcha Holding, while simultaneously controlling Great Star Industrial and Xinchai Co., and is pushing forward an IPO for ZC Rubber — forming a complex "Great Star" capital network
- **Governance risk (the highest-risk item)**: the 2025 acquisition of Guozi Robotics (398 million yuan) was a related-party transaction (the counterparty was a Qiu Jianping affiliate). The complex cross-shareholding network increases the potential channels for improper benefit transfer
- **Aging management team**: Chairman Zhao Limin, age 69, still concurrently serves as General Manager; the core team averages 57 years old, and succession is an urgent issue. There is no large-scale equity incentive plan binding management
- **Trade risk**: overseas revenue is 43.38% of the total, amid rising global trade protectionism. The Thailand plant (starting production in Q2 2026, annual capacity of 10,000 units) and the French joint-venture plant are countermeasures, but capacity is limited
- **85% probability of 10-year survival**: the industry's existence is assured, but governance risk and management succession are the biggest uncertainties

> Li Lu's judgment: "**Buying a stock means buying a company; buying a company means buying its management.**" The operating layer (Zhao Limin's team) is competent, but the "Great Star" style of actual controller Qiu Jianping discounts our confidence.

## V. Investment Thesis

### 🟢 Bull Case

1. **A stable duopoly structure**: Hangcha and Heli together hold over 45% of the domestic market; new entrants face extremely high channel barriers. Industry pricing is rational, and the probability of a price war is low
2. **High ROE from a healthy source**: the 18-20% ROE is driven by net margin, not added leverage. The 6.31% earnings yield is significantly higher than the 10-year government bond yield (2.5%)
3. **Structural tailwind from electrification**: the electrification rate of 73.6% is still rising; Hangcha's in-house three-electrics R&D and manufacturing built on an eight-year first-mover lead constitutes a cost barrier
4. **Huge room for internationalization**: global share is only about 3-4%, while China accounts for over 50% of global output. Overseas revenue grows 27% annually, with gross margin about 10 percentage points higher than domestic. Thailand and France plants are coming online
5. **Extremely diversified customer base**: no single-customer dependency risk; the industry's "consumable" nature provides stable replacement demand
6. **Ample cash reserves**: cash on hand of 4.4 billion yuan, debt-to-asset ratio of 37%, a thick financial safety cushion
7. **Valuation is not expensive**: P/E of 15.5x sits in a historically low-ish range; the neutral target price of RMB 31.6 offers 19% upside

### 🔴 Bear Case

1. **Accounts receivable doubled in four years**: turnover days rose from 23 to 55, and the receivables balance grew an estimated 186% — far exceeding revenue growth of 22%. This is the most dangerous financial signal — the company may be maintaining growth by loosening credit
2. **The growth engine is stalling**: revenue growth has slowed from 26.5% to 5.9%, with profit growth relying entirely on margin expansion rather than volume growth. The sustainability of margin expansion is questionable
3. **The shadow of the "Great Star" governance structure**: Qiu Jianping controls multiple listed companies with complex related-party transactions (the Guozi Robotics acquisition is a textbook example). The actual controller may be using Hangcha as a "cash machine"
4. **A vacuum in management succession**: the 69-year-old chairman also serves as general manager, with no clear succession plan. The core team averages 57 years old, creating concentrated retirement risk
5. **Weak pricing power**: essentially no ability to raise prices domestically; while the 13% net margin is good for manufacturing, it is heavily exposed to raw material price swings. Gross margin already fell 2.28 percentage points quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026
6. **Concentrated trade risk**: 43% of revenue overseas is concentrated in Europe and the US, and escalating trade friction would directly hit profits
7. **Insufficient margin of safety**: 19% upside in the neutral scenario versus 17% downside in the pessimistic scenario. The current price/odds are not favorable

## VIII. Disclosure of AI Research Limitations

1. **The trap of Grade-A information richness**: Hangcha is an A-share company listed for 10 years with abundant information available. But precisely because there is so much material, analysis can drift into "correct but empty" statements — all public information has already been digested by the market. This report's team deliberately built in counterarguments and non-consensus perspectives, but cannot fully avoid the "confirmation bias" that comes with information abundance
2. **Timeliness of financial data**: some data (such as the precise accounts receivable balance) is estimated from turnover ratios rather than taken directly from the annual report. Exact figures should be verified against the latest annual report
3. **Subjectivity of valuation**: the growth-rate and P/E assumptions in the three-scenario valuation are subjective judgments, and actual outcomes could deviate substantially
4. **Information asymmetry around governance risk**: information about related-party transactions in Qiu Jianping's "Great Star" network comes from public disclosures; non-public channels of benefit transfer are difficult for investors to detect
5. **Unpredictability of exchange rates and steel prices**: the two largest external variables affecting Hangcha's gross margin (steel prices and exchange rates) are inherently unpredictable. The "40% cyclical factor" figure used in this report is an estimate
6. **Handling of inconsistency among the analysis team**: the financial analyst considered accounts receivable a "major red flag," while the risk assessor considered the risk "low." This report adopts the financial analyst's view (better supported by the data), but readers should form their own judgment

## IX. Commentary on Duan Yongping's Words

> **"When I buy a company, it's always because I believe its business will keep getting better over the next 10 or 20 years. If I only think it will be good for the next 1-2 years, I won't buy it."**

Hangcha's direction of travel over the next decade (electrification + globalization + intelligence) is clear and favorable to the company. But how confident are we that it will really "keep getting better for 10 years"? We put our confidence level at about 70%. The main uncertainties lie in the governance structure and succession — these two variables carry "veto power" over the outcome.

> **"A good business model is one with a very strong moat. A moat is a monopoly — not an administrative monopoly, but a natural monopoly earned through consumer preference."**

Hangcha does not have a "natural monopoly" attribute. What it has is a kind of "industry structure dividend" (the duopoly) — you could call it a "defensive moat" (keeping others out) rather than an "offensive moat" (letting it raise prices or earn more). The value of the two is vastly different.

> **"Buying an excellent company at a reasonable price is far better than buying a mediocre company at a cheap price."**

Hangcha sits between "excellent" and "mediocre" — a solid business, healthy finances, a stable industry position, but slowing growth, deteriorating receivables, and governance concerns. Calling it "excellent" feels like a stretch; calling it "mediocre" isn't quite fair either. The current "reasonable price" of 15.5x P/E is not an exciting entry point.
