## V. Why Did He Exit? An Analysis of Possible Reasons

Li Lu has never publicly explained the specific reasons for exiting Micron. Below is an analysis of plausible reasons drawn from public information.

### Possible reason one: China's cybersecurity review (geopolitical risk)

- **March 31, 2023**: China's Cyberspace Administration announced a cybersecurity review of Micron's products
- **May 21, 2023**: China determined that Micron's products posed "serious cybersecurity risks" and barred operators of critical information infrastructure from purchasing Micron products
- **Timing strongly aligns**: Li Lu's 40% reduction in Q1 2023 coincided almost exactly with the announcement of the review, and the full exit in Q2 was completed right around when the ban took effect
- Approximately 10% of Micron's revenue comes from mainland China, and roughly 25% combined with Hong Kong — geopolitical risk had a material effect on Micron's business outlook

### Possible reason two: Downturn in the memory chip cycle

- DRAM/NAND prices fell continuously from the second half of 2022, with elevated industry inventory
- Micron's stock fell from around $93 at the start of 2022 to around $50 by year-end, a decline of nearly 50%
- Memory chips are a strongly cyclical industry; Li Lu may have judged that the cycle had peaked and chosen to exit

### Possible reason three: Reallocating capital — into bank stocks

- The failure of Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) in March 2023 triggered a sell-off in bank stocks
- While reducing his Micron position, Li Lu:
  - **Added to Bank of America (BAC)**: adding contrarily during the panic
  - **Initiated a new position in East West Bancorp (EWBC)**: new in Q1, adding another 480,000 shares in Q2
- This suggests Li Lu reallocated capital from Micron into bank stocks he judged more attractive
- This is consistent with the value-investing logic of "sell an ordinary opportunity to buy a better one"

### Possible reason four: Insufficient valuation/return expectations

- From position-building to exit, Micron's share price rose only from roughly $48 to roughly $62 — a return of about 28%
- Over a 3.5-year holding period, the annualized return was only about 7%, far below the performance of Li Lu's other investments
- By comparison, Alphabet (GOOG) and bank stocks may have offered a better risk-reward profile over the same period

> **Note**: All of the above are market speculation; Li Lu himself has never made a public statement. As a value investor deeply influenced by Munger, he almost never discusses specific buy/sell decisions publicly.

## VII. Investment Thesis Analysis — Why Did Li Lu Buy Micron?

Although Li Lu has never publicly laid out his full investment logic for Micron, the following can be inferred from his position behavior and industry analysis:

### The buy thesis

1. **Favorable industry structure**: The global DRAM market is highly concentrated (an oligopoly of Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron), with a competitive landscape far better than most other semiconductor niches
2. **Buying at the cycle bottom**: 2019 Q4 was near the tail end of a memory chip downturn; Micron's stock had fallen from over $60 in 2018 to around $40, with valuation near historic lows
3. **Durable long-term demand**: Trends such as data centers, 5G, IoT, and autonomous driving continue to drive growth in memory demand
4. **Solid financials**: Micron generates free cash flow, leads in technology, and has a deep moat

### Why did the final return end up at only 28%?

- The strongly cyclical nature of memory chips led to large price swings: rising to $93 before falling back to $50
- Li Lu did not sell at the 2021 peak (consistent with a long-term-holding philosophy, but it meant missing the exit window for a cyclical industry)
- The geopolitical black swan (China's review) accelerated the decision to sell
- In the end, he exited at a point of "having to sell" rather than "wanting to sell"

## VIII. Key Conclusions

1. **Cost basis**: approximately $48.63/share, total investment of approximately $558 million, held for approximately 3.5 years
2. **Exit price**: approximately $62/share (weighted average), total proceeds of approximately $712 million
3. **Actual return**: approximately $154 million in profit, total return of approximately 28%, annualized at approximately 7%
4. **Comparison with peak unrealized gain**: The unrealized gain exceeded $530 million at the 2021 Q4 peak (a return of nearly 100%), but the amount ultimately realized was far below that
5. **Drivers of the exit**: geopolitical risk (China's review) + downturn in the memory cycle + the emergence of a bank-stock opportunity
6. **Lesson**: For strongly cyclical industries, even when the long-term fundamentals look favorable, cyclical timing of the exit still matters; geopolitical risk is a hard-to-predict exogenous variable

> This investment ranks as "average" within Li Lu's overall track record. Compared with the 14.5 billion yuan he earned on BYD, Micron's $154 million profit looks modest. But in terms of decision quality, there was nothing wrong with the logic of building a large position at the cycle bottom and holding it — the problem was in the timing of the exit: he neither sold at the cycle peak nor waited for the second wave driven by the AI boom.
