## Preface

The technology is real, the commercialization is a blank slate, and the competitive landscape is chaotic. The first three parts laid all of that out clearly. But one question still hasn't been answered head-on: **how big are this lab's risks, really, and is it worth following for the long term?**

## Risk One: Chip Supply Cutoff — The Biggest Structural Threat

### The current situation

U.S. export restrictions on AI chips to China have kept escalating:

| Timeline | Event |
|--------|------|
| October 2022 | The U.S. banned exports of A100/H100 to China |
| October 2023 | The ban expanded to "castrated" chips such as the H800 |
| April 2025 | NVIDIA's H20 was also made subject to export licensing |
| 2025-2026 | The U.S. investigated whether DeepSeek obtained roughly **60,000 units** of NVIDIA chips (of which **20,000 units** were restricted models) through Singapore-based intermediaries (Source: U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission) |

DeepSeek's current compute base: approximately **10,000 units** of NVIDIA A100 (purchased before the 2023 ban, Source: Bloomberg), plus possible additional purchases.

**The core question**: how long will the existing NVIDIA chips last? Can domestic substitutes fill the gap?

### Huawei Ascend: a backup path, but not a perfect substitute

V4 has already been adapted for Huawei's Ascend 950 chip, achieving full-stack deployment from training to inference at the technical level. But:

| Comparison dimension | NVIDIA H200 | Huawei Ascend 950 |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Process node | 4nm | 7nm |
| HBM bandwidth | 4.8 TB/s | Not disclosed (estimated lower) |
| Ecosystem maturity | CUDA (global standard) | CANN (early stage) |
| Mass production timing | Already in mass production | Ramp-up begins in H2 2026 |

(Source: Huawei official statements, VOA Chinese, EE Times China)

**One key fact**: mass production ramp-up for the Ascend 950 won't begin until the second half of 2026. Until then, compute constraints mean DeepSeek's high-end V4-Pro inference service API pricing cannot drop substantially.

**Bull case**: DeepSeek's core competitive strength is algorithmic efficiency — even with a chip performance gap, algorithmic optimization can compensate. V3 trained a globally frontier model on H800s, proving that "you don't need the best chips to build the best model."

**Bear case**: training frontier models is becoming increasingly compute-hungry. If the next generation of models (V5 or beyond) needs 10x the compute, the gap between Ascend and NVIDIA will no longer be a magnitude that "can be made up for with algorithms." Algorithmic innovation has a ceiling, and the physical-layer gap will eventually show through.

### Verdict on chip risk

**Short term (1-2 years)**: manageable. Existing NVIDIA inventory, plus Ascend adaptation, plus algorithmic efficiency advantages, are enough to support the V4 and V4.1 iterations.

**Medium term (3-5 years)**: high uncertainty. It depends on the pace of Huawei Ascend's mass production ramp-up, whether the 7nm process can catch up to 5nm/4nm, and whether U.S. sanctions tighten further.

## Risk Two: Content Censorship and the Ceiling on Globalization

### Domestic censorship in China

DeepSeek's models comply with Chinese legal requirements and have a clear censorship mechanism on politically sensitive topics (Source: multiple independent test reports, RFA):

- On topics such as Tiananmen or Taiwan, the model refuses to answer or deletes its answer
- The censorship happens at the application layer — text is first generated and then rapidly deleted
- Censorship is strictest in Chinese and English interactions; filtering is inconsistent in other languages

### Bans overseas

| Country/Region | Measure |
|-----------|------|
| United States | Banned on government agency devices; broader bans under consideration |
| Italy | Temporarily taken down |
| Ireland, Belgium | Data security investigations |
| South Korea, Japan | Restrictive measures |
| Australia | Banned on government devices |

(Source: Secrm, CNBC, various government announcements)

### What does this mean?

**DeepSeek's globalization carries a structural contradiction**:

- The open-source model can be downloaded and used globally (it cannot be banned)
- But DeepSeek's cloud API service is restricted in multiple countries
- Enterprise customers (especially government and enterprise clients) will not use an AI service explicitly called out by their own government

**Bull case**: open source is the best "anti-ban weapon." Anyone can download the model weights and run them locally — governments cannot ban the code. DeepSeek's real influence isn't in its cloud API, but in the global developer community.

**Bear case**: influence doesn't equal revenue. Global developers using your open-source model for free doesn't mean they'll pay for your API. And the government/enterprise market — the biggest slice of AI monetization — is a market DeepSeek can barely reach outside China.

## Risk Three: Talent Attrition — The Fragility of a 270-Person Team

DeepSeek's team characteristics:

| Characteristic | Data |
|------|------|
| Total headcount | Approximately **270 people** |
| Average age | **28** |
| Education | Over 85% hold a master's degree, over 40% hold a PhD |
| Origin | Mainly from Tsinghua, Peking University, Zhejiang University, and Fudan University |

(Source: Zhihu, 36Kr)

Known departures from late 2025 through early 2026:

| Name | Departure date | Destination |
|------|---------|------|
| Luo Fuli | November 2025 | Xiaomi |
| Wang Bingxuan | Late 2025 | Tencent |
| Ruan Chong | January 2026 | DeepRoute.ai |
| Guo Daya | April 2026 | ByteDance |

(Source: 36Kr)

**The core question**: DeepSeek has no equity incentive plan. What does an unlisted lab with no option program use to retain China's top AI researchers?

**Bull case**: Liang Wenfeng's leadership and the mission-driven sense of "pure research" are DeepSeek's biggest talent magnet. Among the world's top AI researchers, some are motivated not by money but by "doing genuine frontier research." DeepSeek offers that opportunity. After the first funding round closes, an equity incentive plan may also be introduced.

**Bear case**: mission cannot be quantified or hedged. The salaries offered by ByteDance and Tencent could be 3-5x or more of DeepSeek's, plus stock options. Four core members departing within half a year is not a small proportion for a 270-person team. If iteration speed slows after V4, talent attrition could accelerate.

## Risk Four: Parent-Company Risk and Valuation Bubble

### The risk at High-Flyer Quant

| Risk factor | Description |
|--------|------|
| Return volatility | Quant fund annual returns can swing by 30-50 percentage points |
| Regulatory risk | China's regulation of quantitative trading continues to tighten |
| Conflict of interest | High-Flyer uses DeepSeek's technology for trading — does that conflict with the open-source ethos? |

### Is the valuation reasonable?

What does the 350 billion yuan valuation correspond to?

| Benchmark | Valuation | Annual revenue | Valuation/Revenue |
|------|------|--------|-----------|
| OpenAI | ~$300 billion | ~$10 billion+ | ~30x |
| Anthropic | ~$60 billion | Several billion | ~30-60x |
| DeepSeek | ~$45 billion (350 billion yuan) | **Nearly zero** | **Infinite** |

**DeepSeek's valuation is, in essence, not pricing current revenue at all — it is pricing "possible future commercialization" and "strategic technological value."**

Is this similar to the logic of the 2000 dot-com bubble? There are similarities, but also differences — the difference being that DeepSeek's technical achievements are real and its model performance can be independently verified by third parties.

## Five Possible Futures

Based on the analysis in the first three parts, here are five scenarios. Disclosure: **the probability weighting is a subjective judgment, not a precise calculation. Readers should adjust the weights according to their own judgment.**

| Scenario | Description | Key assumption |
|------|------|---------|
| "The Linux of AI" | Enormous technical influence, an unshakeable open-source ecosystem, but limited commercial value of its own | Open-source path unchanged, algorithmic lead sustained |
| Commercialization breakthrough | Finds a sustainable monetization model, becomes China's version of Anthropic | Pivots to commercialization after the first funding round, product capability catches up |
| Acquired/merged by a major tech company | Technical value recognized, but standalone operation becomes unsustainable | High-Flyer's profits decline, or Liang Wenfeng's intent shifts |
| National strategic asset | Becomes a core force of China's national AI team, gains sustained state resources | Deep involvement of state-backed funds, chip supply guaranteed |
| Gradual marginalization | Technical lead is caught up, talent attrition accelerates, High-Flyer's funding dries up | Chip gap widens plus core team disperses |

**Every scenario has real evidence supporting it, and every scenario has clear counterarguments.** This is precisely the fundamental reason DeepSeek is so hard to value — it is not a "company" that can be priced with a DCF model, but an "option bundle" stacking multiple possibilities.

## Final Verdict: Is This Lab Worth Following?

No conclusion is offered here — only a framework.

### You should follow DeepSeek if:

1. You work in the AI industry or invest in AI-related companies — DeepSeek's technical path and cost structure have a structural impact on the entire industry
2. You follow U.S.-China tech competition — DeepSeek is the best proof case of China's AI capability
3. You use AI tools — the cost-performance of DeepSeek V4 means you may get near-frontier capability at 1/10 the cost
4. You follow the evolution of open-source software — DeepSeek is redefining the boundaries of what "open-source AI" can be

### You should not be blindly bullish on DeepSeek, because:

1. **Zero revenue plus a 350 billion yuan valuation** — however good the technology, the implicit assumptions behind this valuation require many years of high growth to be realized
2. **Chip risk is real** — algorithmic efficiency has a ceiling, and the physical-layer gap will eventually show through
3. **Team fragility** — a 270-person elite team has no equity lock-up, and talent attrition has already begun
4. **Parent-company risk** — quant fund profits are not a stable cash flow
5. **Content censorship** — limits the ceiling on the global government/enterprise market

### You also should not be blindly bearish on DeepSeek, because:

1. **The technical achievements have been third-party verified** — V4 ranks alongside GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 at the frontier on multiple benchmarks
2. **The cost advantage has a compounding effect** — cheaper training leads to faster iteration leads to lower cost — this is a positive flywheel
3. **The open-source ecosystem moat is already established** — cited by 38% of new AI papers, 70,000+ stars on GitHub, 800,000+ monthly downloads on Hugging Face
4. **The Huawei adaptation lowers chip risk** — not eliminated, but there is now a backup path
5. **The first funding round brings in state resources** — both funding and chip supply guarantees are being upgraded
