## A Set of Facts That Will Make You Rethink the AI Competitive Landscape

On January 27, 2025, a name nobody had ever heard of — DeepSeek — took the No. 1 spot on the US App Store. It wasn't OpenAI, wasn't Google, wasn't any Silicon Valley company. It came from Hangzhou, was less than two years old, and had never raised a single cent of outside capital.

Then came the single largest one-day market-cap wipeout in U.S. stock market history:

- Nvidia plunged 17% in a single day, wiping out roughly **$600 billion** in market value — the largest single-day market-cap loss in U.S. stock market history (Source: Bloomberg)
- The AI sector lost roughly **$1 trillion** in total that day
- Western media dubbed it AI's "Sputnik moment" (Source: MIT Technology Review)
- The global tech industry began reexamining its belief that "only massive spending can build AI"

All of this was accomplished by a Chinese company less than two years old that had never raised a single cent of outside capital.

## What Exactly Is DeepSeek?

Let's start with the basic facts:

| Item | Detail |

| Full name | DeepSeek |
| Founder | Liang Wenfeng, born 1985, graduate of Zhejiang University |
| Founded | July 2023, Hangzhou |
| Team size | About **270 people**, average age **28** |
| External funding | From founding through April 2026: **zero** (entirely funded by High-Flyer Quant's own capital) |

In one sentence: **DeepSeek is an AI research lab funded by quant-fund profits that has produced world-frontier large language models with an extremely small team and an extremely low cost base.**

## Three Things You Must Know

### First: It's Not a Company — It's More Like a Lab

DeepSeek's founder, Liang Wenfeng, has repeatedly emphasized that DeepSeek's goal is not to make money, but to pursue "research without constraints."

This isn't just a slogan. Look at the evidence:

- API pricing is extremely low: V4's input price is **$0.30 per million tokens**, about **1/20** of GPT-5.4's price (Source: DeepSeek's official API pricing)
- All core models are **open-sourced under the MIT license** — weights and training code are all made public
- The founder has never publicly done a commercialization roadshow
- Two and a half years since founding, it has been kept alive by profit transfers from its parent, High-Flyer Quant, without seeking outside investors

**Counterpoint**: In May 2026, DeepSeek finally launched its first funding round, targeting **50 billion yuan**, with Liang Wenfeng personally contributing **20 billion yuan** and the state-backed Big Fund in talks to lead the round (Source: Sina Finance, 36Kr). This suggests the "no-funding idealism" is starting to shift. Whether this is driven by necessity or a deliberate choice still remains to be seen.

### Second: It Achieved Top-Tier Performance at 1/20 the Cost

DeepSeek V3 (released December 2024) had a training cost of just **$5.6 million**, roughly **1/20** of GPT-4's training cost (Source: DeepSeek technical paper).

V4-Pro, released in April 2026, went even further:

| Metric | DeepSeek V4-Pro | GPT-5.4 | Claude Opus 4.6 |
|------|----------------|---------|-----------------|
| Total parameters | **1.6 trillion** | Not disclosed | Not disclosed |
| Active parameters | **49 billion** | Not disclosed | Not disclosed |
| Context window | **1 million tokens** | 1 million+ | 1 million |
| LiveCodeBench | **93.5** (highest score) | Not disclosed | Not disclosed |
| SWE-bench | **80.6%** | Close | Close |
| Codeforces | **3206** | 3168 | Not disclosed |
| API input price | **$0.30/M tokens** | ~$6/M | ~$15/M |

(Source: DeepSeek official, NoteLM, OfficeChai)

The data is clear: **DeepSeek V4 has entered the global first tier on coding and reasoning benchmarks, at a price just 1/10 to 1/50 that of its competitors.**

**Counterpoint**: On math reasoning (HMMT 2026), Claude Opus 4.6 scored **96.2** and GPT-5.4 scored **97.7**, while V4-Pro scored **95.2** — a small but real gap. On MMLU-Pro, V4-Pro (**87.5%**) also trails Gemini 3.1 Pro (**91.0%**). The claim of "across-the-board superiority" is not accurate; a more precise assessment is "leading in specific domains, first-tier overall."

### Third: The "DeepSeek Shock" Changed the Entire AI Industry's Narrative

Before R1 was released in January 2025, the core belief across the global AI industry was that **scale is everything**: more GPUs, more data, and more money were what it took to build better models.

R1 disproved that belief with hard facts:

- **671B parameters, with only 37B activated** — roughly a 90% reduction in compute
- Training cost of **$5.6 million** (V3) plus an additional **~$294,000** (R1's pure-RL training)
- Performance on par with the GPT-4-generation models

How big was the shock? Just look at Nvidia's stock price — it wiped out **$600 billion** in a single day, because the market suddenly realized: "Maybe AI companies don't need to buy that many GPUs after all."

**But what happened 11 months later?** Nvidia's stock fully recovered. The reason is the **Jevons Paradox**: lower costs → lower barriers to entry → more companies and use cases adopt AI → total compute demand actually increases.

This paradox appears to hold true so far. But it doesn't erase what DeepSeek proved: **algorithmic innovation can close the compute gap.**
