## A Counterintuitive Starting Point

Among the world's most highly valued AI companies, DeepSeek is the only one that is **deliberately not making money**.

OpenAI's estimated 2025 revenue exceeds **$10 billion**. Anthropic has raised more than **$15 billion** and has begun generating significant revenue. Google DeepMind sits behind Alphabet's trillion-dollar market cap.

And DeepSeek? Its API pricing is 1/10 to 1/50 of its competitors', its core models are all free and open-source, and its founder has publicly stated it is "not pursuing profit."

So how has it survived to this point? How is it valued at **350 billion yuan**?

The answer is hidden in its parent company.

---

## High-Flyer Quant: DeepSeek's "Money Printer"

### What is High-Flyer?

| Item | Detail |
|------|------|
| Full name | Hangzhou High-Flyer Quant Investment Management |
| Founder | Liang Wenfeng (also the founder of DeepSeek) |
| Founded | 2015 |
| Nature | Quantitative hedge fund |
| Assets under management | **70 billion yuan+** (Source: Simuwang) |
| 2025 return | **56.55%** (Source: 36Kr) |
| Estimated 2025 profit | Approximately **5 billion yuan** |
| GPU cluster | Approximately **10,000 units** of NVIDIA A100 (purchased in 2023, before the chip ban) |

High-Flyer Quant is one of China's largest quantitative hedge funds. Its core capability is using AI to conduct quantitative trading — and this is also the starting point of Liang Wenfeng's decision to found DeepSeek: **while doing quantitative trading, he accumulated substantial AI compute and engineering experience, and then decided to use those resources to build a general-purpose large language model.**

### How long can High-Flyer sustain DeepSeek?

Estimating with 2025 profit of roughly 5 billion yuan:

| Item | Estimate |
|------|------|
| DeepSeek's annual operating cost | Approximately **1.5-3 billion yuan** (estimate, including compute, personnel, API subsidies) |
| High-Flyer's annual profit | Approximately **5 billion yuan** |
| Coverage multiple | **1.7-3.3x** |

It looks like it can cover the cost. **But there is a critical assumption buried here: that High-Flyer's returns are sustainable.**

Quant fund returns are extremely volatile. High-Flyer's 56.55% return in 2025 is **abnormally high** — it has previously experienced significant drawdowns as well. If High-Flyer's returns in some future year are zero or negative, DeepSeek's funding source would be cut off.

**Counterargument**: High-Flyer manages more than 70 billion yuan in assets; even if its return drops to 10-15%, annual profit would still be 7-10 billion yuan — enough to cover DeepSeek. And Liang Wenfeng's commitment to DeepSeek is a "long-term resolve" that won't be cut off by short-term volatility.

**Counter-counterargument**: tail risk in the quant fund industry is real. China's quant fund industry suffered systemic losses in 2018, and High-Flyer was no exception. Using quant fund profits to fund AI research is, in essence, **using the returns of a high-volatility asset to sustain a pure cost center** — if both hit headwinds simultaneously, the situation could turn ugly.

## The First Funding Round of 50 Billion Yuan: A Turning Point

In May 2026, DeepSeek finally launched its first external funding round since its founding. Key details:

| Item | Detail |
|------|------|
| Funding target | **50 billion yuan** (Source: Wallstreetcn, Sina Finance) |
| Liang Wenfeng's personal contribution | Up to **20 billion yuan** |
| Lead investor | The National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund ("Big Fund") in talks |
| Post-money valuation | Approximately **350 billion yuan** (~$45 billion) |
| Other potential investors | Multiple internet giants and state-owned funds |

(Source: Wallstreetcn, Sina Finance, 36Kr, PEdaily)

### What does this round mean?

**Bullish reading**:

1. Lead investment from the national Big Fund = backing from the national team, an upgrade to chip supply-chain security
2. 50 billion yuan gives ample ammunition, enough to cover 2-3 years of future compute investment
3. Liang Wenfeng personally contributing 20 billion yuan indicates extremely strong founder confidence
4. A 350 billion yuan valuation corresponds to a position in the first tier of global AI

**Bearish reading**:

1. The "idealism of not raising funds" has ended — raising funds means having shareholders, and shareholders means return expectations
2. Participation by the national Big Fund = the intrusion of political factors, which could constrain research direction
3. The 350 billion yuan valuation corresponds to a company with **zero revenue** (or near-zero revenue)
4. Liang Wenfeng's 20 billion yuan contribution could be because outside investors weren't willing to put in that much — or it could reflect control considerations

**A logic worth being wary of**: the market is anchoring DeepSeek's valuation to "OpenAI's $300 billion valuation." But OpenAI has more than $10 billion in annual revenue and 150 million+ weekly active users. What does DeepSeek have? Technical influence, an open-source ecosystem, and an option on "possible future commercialization." The valuation logic behind the two is completely different.

## Path to Monetization: Where Is It Now? Where Is It Going?

### Current revenue sources

| Source | Scale | Nature |
|------|------|------|
| API service | Extremely low (priced at 1/10-1/50 of competitors) | Deliberately low-priced |
| Enterprise customers | **3,200+** (Source: DemandSage) | Sizable scale but low unit price |
| Monthly API calls | **5.7 billion** (Source: DemandSage) | High volume, low unit price |

Rough estimate: assuming average API revenue of $0.50 per million tokens, 5.7 billion monthly calls, and an average of 1,000 tokens per call, annual revenue is on the order of **a few billion yuan** — negligible relative to operating costs and valuation.

### Potential monetization paths

| Path | Feasibility | Note |
|------|--------|------|
| Raising API pricing | Medium | Has an ecosystem lock-in base, but "low price" is core to the brand |
| Enterprise customization | Medium-high | The 3,200+ enterprise customers are a starting point |
| Government/defense | High (domestically) | Probability of government/enterprise cooperation rises after the Big Fund's investment |
| Consumer products | Low | Product capability is DeepSeek's biggest weakness |
| Licensing fees | Low | Conflicts with the MIT open-source license |

**Counterargument**: DeepSeek's "not making money" may not be a problem — if its goal is to become "AI infrastructure." Linux doesn't make money, but Red Hat (acquired by IBM for $34 billion) does. Android doesn't make money, but Google has earned profits through the Android ecosystem far exceeding its development cost. There are many successful precedents for monetizing open source.

**Counter-counterargument**: behind Linux and Android sit giants like IBM/Google with sustained investment. Behind DeepSeek sits a quant fund. The depth of resources between the two is not comparable.

## The Competitive Landscape: A Four-Way World

The global AI competitive landscape as of May 2026 can be summed up in one table:

| Dimension | OpenAI | Anthropic | Google DeepMind | DeepSeek |
|------|--------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Latest flagship | GPT-5.4 | Claude Opus 4.6 | Gemini 3.1 Pro | V4-Pro |
| Strategy | Closed source + subscription + enterprise | Closed source + safety-first | Closed source + ecosystem integration | **Open source + low price** |
| Annual revenue | **$10 billion+** | Several billion dollars | Folded into Google's revenue | **Nearly zero** |
| Total funding raised | **$30 billion+** | **$15 billion+** | Internal to Google | **Raising at a 350 billion yuan valuation** |
| Core strength | Brand + user base | Safety + long context | Data + compute | **Cost + open source** |
| Core weakness | High cost | Smaller scale | Slower productization | **Commercialization near-blank** |
| Consumer users | **150 million+ weekly active** | Tens of millions | Hundreds of millions (including Search) | **96.9 million monthly active** |

(Source: public company data, DemandSage, Bloomberg)

### DeepSeek's unique position in the competition

DeepSeek's competition with the other three is not on the same dimension.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are fighting over the **commercial market for closed-source AI** — whose model is better, who has more customers, whose subscription revenue is higher.

DeepSeek is fighting for **ecosystem influence in open-source AI** — whose model is used by the most people, who defines the open-source AI standard, who becomes "the Linux of AI."

**These two battles overlap but are not identical.** DeepSeek may lose the commercial competition to OpenAI and Anthropic, while still building a hard-to-shake position in the open-source ecosystem.

**Counterargument**: Meta's Llama series is likewise open source, and it is backed by trillion-dollar-market-cap Meta. DeepSeek's lead in the open-source race is not unassailable. Furthermore, if closed-source models continue to widen the capability gap, the appeal of the open-source ecosystem could decline.

## Anthropic's Allegation: The Distillation Controversy

In February 2026, Anthropic publicly accused DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax of carrying out a "coordinated distillation attack" against Claude — that is, extracting its capabilities through massive volumes of API calls to train their own models (Source: CNBC).

OpenAI had previously issued similar allegations.

**The essence of this controversy is**: when closed-source models are powerful enough, are open-source companies "parasitizing" the achievements of closed-source models?

DeepSeek has not publicly responded to the allegation. But from a technical standpoint:

- Distillation is a common technique in AI, and is not academically prohibited
- Large-scale commercial distillation may violate API terms of use
- If the allegation is true, some of DeepSeek's "technical lead" would need to be discounted

**This is an unresolved controversy, and no conclusion should be drawn absent further evidence. But it is a reminder of one thing: DeepSeek's technical achievements cannot simply be explained as "the purely original work of a 270-person team."**
