## Screening criteria (investment candidate screening framework)

Goal: find bottleneck companies, or companies the market is overlooking, as investment candidates.

| Dimension | Standard | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| **Bottleneck purity** | Bottleneck business >30% of revenue | The purer the better — avoid paying for an entire mine to get one diamond |
| **Irreplaceability** | Sole/oligopoly supplier | The longer it takes for an alternative to emerge, the better (2+ years is ideal) |
| **Degree of market pricing** | Whether the stock price already reflects the bottleneck thesis | P/E above 100x is likely already fully priced; overlooked dimensions exist even within large-cap companies |
| **Catalyst time window** | Any near-term confirming events | Earnings, capacity-expansion milestones, contract signings, industry supply-demand inflection points |

Each company is scored on all four dimensions (★1-5), with a maximum total score of 20.

## Framework definition

At Davos 2025, in conversation with BlackRock CEO Larry Fink, Jensen Huang defined the AI industry as a "five-layer cake":

```
Layer 5 ┃ Application layer   ┃ AI agents, autonomous driving, robotics, industry solutions
Layer 4 ┃ Model layer         ┃ Large language models, multimodal models
Layer 3 ┃ Infrastructure layer┃ AI factories, data centers, networking, cooling
Layer 2 ┃ Chip layer          ┃ GPUs, accelerators, HBM memory, advanced packaging
Layer 1 ┃ Energy layer        ┃ Power supply, transformers, nuclear energy
```

His core view: AI is not a product, it's an entire new industrial system. Every layer carries enormous investment opportunity, and the lower the layer, the more irreplaceable it is.

He calls data centers "AI factories" — "every day, they do just one thing: produce tokens." In the future, every company will have two factories: one that makes products, and one that's an AI factory.

## Layer 1: Energy — the most underrated bottleneck

**Core logic:** the endpoint of AI compute is electricity. Global data center power consumption was 485 TWh in 2025 and is projected to double to 950 TWh by 2030. Nearly half of planned US data center construction projects have been delayed or canceled — not because of a GPU shortage, but because of a power and transformer shortage.

**Bottleneck data:** lead times for large power transformers have stretched to 2-4 years; high-voltage substations run 3-5 years.

### Representative companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Eaton** | Electrical-equipment leader | Global leader in data center power distribution systems, benefiting from the transformer shortage |
| **GE Vernova** | Power equipment + gas power generation | Energy company spun off from GE, benefiting on both transformers and gas turbines |
| **Constellation Energy** | Nuclear power operator | Largest nuclear operator in the US, signed a nuclear supply agreement with Microsoft |
| **NuScale Power** | Small modular reactors (SMR) | The only NRC-certified SMR design, targeting long-term power supply for data centers |
| **TBEA (Special Transformer)** | China's transformer leader | China's transformer exports to the US surged from 1,500 units in 2022 to 8,000+ units in 2025 |

**Rationale for selection:** a 4-year transformer lead time means this bottleneck won't ease before 2028. Nuclear power is the ultimate answer for long-term data center power supply. This layer has the highest certainty, the least attention, and the most reasonable valuations.

## Layer 2: Chips — the most value-dense battlefield

**Core logic:** the AI accelerator market is projected to exceed $150 billion in 2026. Nvidia still holds roughly 75-80% share, but custom ASIC growth (+45%) has for the first time overtaken general-purpose GPU growth (+16%) — hyperscaler customers "de-Nvidia-fying" is a structural trend.

### Representative companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Nvidia** | AI GPU monopolist | FY2027 Q1 revenue $81.6 billion (+85%), data center $75.2 billion (+92%), gross margin 75%, market cap $5.2 trillion |
| **Broadcom** | King of custom ASICs | Q2 FY2026 AI revenue $10.8 billion (+143%), designs custom chips for Google/Meta/OpenAI, $73 billion order backlog |
| **AMD** | The only general-purpose GPU challenger | Q1 2026 data center revenue $5.8 billion (+57%), OpenAI chose AMD to build 6GW of compute capacity |
| **TSMC** | AI-chip manufacturing monopoly | Over 90% global share in advanced nodes, CoWoS packaging fully booked through 2026, the physical bottleneck of the entire chain |
| **SK Hynix** | HBM memory leader | 62% share of the HBM market, Samsung's share plunged from 41% to 17%; HBM is the indispensable "ammunition" for AI chips |

**Rationale for selection:** Nvidia is the standard-setter but faces erosion from custom chips; Broadcom is the biggest winner in custom chips and growing even faster; AMD is the only viable alternative; TSMC and SK Hynix hold the physical bottleneck of the entire chain. Together, these five essentially are the AI-chip layer.

## Layer 3: Infrastructure — a $725 billion arms race

**Core logic:** the four hyperscale cloud providers (Microsoft + Amazon + Google + Meta) will spend roughly $725 billion in combined capex in 2026, up 97% year over year. Not a single one is cutting spending — all are raising guidance. This money goes into servers, networking equipment, data center construction, and cooling systems.

### Representative companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Arista Networks** | AI data center network switches | High-growth AI networking revenue, 800G/1.6T Ethernet solutions, competing with Broadcom on InfiniBand replacement |
| **Vertiv** | Data center power + liquid cooling | #1 global liquid-cooling market share at 11.3%; the NVL72 architecture's mandatory liquid cooling is a core catalyst |
| **Innolight (Zhongji Innolight)** | Global leader in AI optical modules | 2025 revenue 38.2 billion yuan (+60%), net profit 10.8 billion yuan (+109%), ~50% share of 800G modules |
| **Dell Technologies** | Leading AI server shipments | AI server backlog exceeds $9 billion, deeply tied to Nvidia |
| **CoreWeave** | Pure-play GPU cloud infrastructure | 2025 revenue $5.13 billion (+170%), $99.4 billion order backlog, but total debt of $21.4 billion and a GAAP loss |

**Rationale for selection:** networking (Arista), power and cooling (Vertiv), optical interconnects (Innolight), servers (Dell), GPU cloud (CoreWeave) — together covering the entire infrastructure chain from power to compute delivery. Innolight is the Chinese company with the strongest pricing power anywhere in the AI industry chain.

## Layer 4: Models — the fastest revenue growth in history

**Core logic:** Anthropic's annualized revenue rose from $1 billion to $47 billion in just 14 months — possibly the fastest revenue growth of any company in business history. OpenAI's monthly revenue is $2 billion. The second half of 2026 will see a wave of AI-company IPOs (Anthropic has confidentially filed, OpenAI is targeting September).

### Representative companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Anthropic** | The highest-valued AI startup | Valuation $965 billion (surpassing OpenAI), annualized revenue $47 billion, #1 on the Arena leaderboard, has confidentially filed for IPO |
| **OpenAI** | AI first mover | Valuation $852 billion, annualized revenue ~$24 billion, targeting a September 2026 IPO |
| **Google DeepMind** | Most technically comprehensive | Gemini subscription revenue $1.2 billion, Google Cloud AI product revenue +800%, parent Alphabet market cap $2.1 trillion |
| **Meta AI** | Leader of the open-source path | Llama models open-source, building an ecosystem; AI has boosted ad revenue by over 20%; 2026 AI capex $125-145 billion |
| **DeepSeek** | China's strongest AI representative | V4-Pro with 1.6T parameters, math reasoning surpassing Claude, API pricing at 1/10 to 1/75 of Western prices, valuation $51.5 billion |

**Rationale for selection:** Anthropic and OpenAI are the two commercial heavyweights; Google has the most complete AI monetization loop (search + cloud + subscriptions); Meta's open-source path influences the entire ecosystem; DeepSeek proves China can match the frontier at one-tenth the cost. xAI and Mistral remain too small in scale and were not selected for now.

## Layer 5: Applications — who's actually making money from AI?

**Core logic:** for most so-called "AI concept stocks," AI revenue as a share of the total remains low. Very few companies are actually making money from AI, yet valuations are wildly divergent — Palantir trades at 105x P/E, Salesforce at just 14x.

### Representative companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Palantir** | AI decision-making operating system | Q1 2026 revenue $1.63 billion (+85%), net margin 44%, AIP Boot Camp conversion rate 75%, but P/E 105x |
| **Salesforce** | AI-agent enterprise applications | Agentforce ARR $1 billion+, 29,000 deals, FY2026 total revenue $41.5 billion, P/E only 14x — the best value AI-application play |
| **ServiceNow** | Enterprise AI workflows | Now Assist ACV target $1 billion+, subscription revenue +22%, ITSM monopoly position, P/E 25x |
| **Tesla** | Autonomous driving + robotics | 1.28 million FSD subscribers, Robotaxi already operating in Dallas/Houston, Optimus mass production targeted summer 2026, but P/E 317x |
| **Adobe** | AI creative tools | Firefly has generated 24 billion+ assets, 75% adoption among Fortune 500, but direct AI revenue is only $400 million, forward P/E 10x, the lowest of the group |

**Rationale for selection:** Palantir has the fastest-growing AI application revenue (+85%); Salesforce is the most reasonably valued large-cap AI application play (P/E 14x); ServiceNow is the clearest case of enterprise AI monetization; Tesla represents the ultimate physical form of AI (autonomous driving + robotics); Adobe defines the AI-creative track.

## Investment-perspective summary

### Investment opportunities ranked by certainty

| Priority | Layer | Logic | Representative names |
|--------|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Energy | 4-year transformer lead time cannot ease — the most certain bottleneck | Eaton, GE Vernova |
| 2 | Chips | The "standard unit of measure" for AI compute, deepest moat | Nvidia (valuation needs scrutiny), Broadcom (faster growth) |
| 3 | Infrastructure | $725 billion of capex is real money | Innolight, Vertiv, Arista |
| 4 | Models | Most explosive growth, but mostly not yet public | Wait for Anthropic/OpenAI IPOs |
| 5 | Applications | Very few companies actually making money from AI so far | Salesforce (P/E 14x), Adobe (P/E 10x) offer the best value |

### One takeaway

The lower, more physical, and more unavoidable a layer is, the higher the investment certainty. The sexiest layers (models, applications) are, ironically, the least certain.
