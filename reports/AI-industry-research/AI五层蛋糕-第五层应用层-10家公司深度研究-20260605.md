# AI Five-Layer Cake, Layer Five: Few Companies Are Actually Making Money From AI, But Someone Is Being Badly Undervalued

> Research date: June 5, 2026 | Covering 10 companies | Data as of early June 2026

---

## I. Core Logic: The Investment Truth of the Application Layer

### AI Revenue Still Accounts for a Small Share at Most AI-Concept Stocks

This is the harshest reality of the AI application layer in 2026.

Hundreds of companies in the market carry the "AI concept" label, but very few can actually produce AI revenue figures. Salesforce's Agentforce ARR reached $1.2 billion, up 205% year-over-year, but that is less than 3% of its full-year revenue of $45 billion. Adobe's Firefly ARR just topped $250 million, less than 1% of total revenue. iFlytek's large-model API and MaaS platform service revenue was 385 million yuan, just 1.4% of its 27.1 billion yuan in total revenue.

There are probably no more than three publicly listed companies worldwide where "pure AI revenue" truly exceeds 50%: Palantir (nearly 100% of revenue centers on AI decision systems), Tempus AI (whose core business is AI-driven precision medicine), and small-cap companies like C3.ai. The vast majority of so-called AI companies are, in substance, "traditional business plus AI enhancement."

This isn't a bad thing. On the contrary, it reveals the core investment logic of the application layer: **AI is a means, not an end. The companies that actually make money aren't "companies that do AI" — they're "companies that use AI to make more money."**

### Valuation Divergence Has Reached an Extreme

As of early June 2026, valuation gaps among companies within the same AI application layer are striking:

| Company | Trailing P/E | Forward P/E | Revenue Growth |
|------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Palantir | ~153x | ~85x | +85% YoY |
| Tesla | ~355x | ~175x | +16% YoY |
| Snowflake | Loss-making | ~85x | +34% YoY |
| ServiceNow | ~80x | ~32x | +22% YoY |
| Salesforce | ~22x | ~13x | +13% YoY |
| Adobe | ~16x | ~9x | +12% YoY |

(Source: Yahoo Finance, GuruFocus, MacroTrends, as of early June 2026)

Palantir's P/E is 7 times Salesforce's and nearly 10 times Adobe's. Yet Palantir's revenue (roughly $6.5 billion annualized) is only one-seventh of Salesforce's and one-quarter of Adobe's. The market prices Palantir as "the operating system of the AI era," while pricing Salesforce and Adobe as "traditional software companies undergoing an AI refresh."

This narrative gap creates enormous room for valuation arbitrage.

### The Real Opportunity: Companies Whose AI Monetization Potential Is Overlooked by Traditional Valuation Frameworks

The market is making a classic mistake: **pricing companies on "AI purity" rather than on "AI monetization capability."**

Palantir is indeed one of the purest AI companies, but its customer base is concentrated among governments and large enterprises, where procurement cycles are long and expansion speed is capped by the size of the sales team. Salesforce, by contrast, sits on the world's largest pool of enterprise CRM customers — every existing customer is a potential Agentforce buyer, and the friction cost of cross-selling is minimal. Adobe has 850 million monthly active users, and Firefly is embedded directly into existing products like Photoshop, Illustrator, and Acrobat, sparing users even the cost of switching.

It is precisely these companies that get "punished" by traditional valuation frameworks: the market sees Salesforce's +13% revenue growth and assigns it a 22x P/E, sees Adobe's +12% and assigns it a 16x P/E — while completely ignoring these companies' AI businesses growing at over 200%, and the margin-improvement potential from rising AI penetration over the next two to three years.

Conversely, Palantir's +85% growth rate is indeed staggering, but a 153x P/E means the market has already priced in five years' worth of optimistic expectations. If growth slows from 85% to 50%, the stock may not just rise more slowly — it could fall outright.

### The Benchmark Significance of Databricks' 2026 IPO

Databricks may be the most important tech IPO of the second half of 2026.

Its latest funding round valued the company at $134 billion, with a revenue run rate exceeding $5.4 billion, growing more than 65% year-over-year, and AI product annualized revenue already reaching $1.4 billion. If the IPO prices in the $150-200 billion range, corresponding to roughly 28-37x P/S, it will become a new valuation anchor for AI application-layer companies.

Databricks' significance lies not only in itself but in how it will redefine the valuation benchmark for the entire "AI data platform" category. Snowflake (current market cap roughly $90 billion, Forward P/E of 85x) will be re-examined as a result — if Databricks commands a higher valuation on the back of faster growth and stronger AI products, will Snowflake's valuation logic be further compressed, or will it rise along with the tide? This is the valuation contest most worth watching in the second half of the year.

**To summarize the investment methodology for this layer: don't chase "the purest AI company" — look for "the company with the strongest AI monetization ability that is undervalued by traditional valuation frameworks." Between Palantir at 153x P/E and Adobe at 16x P/E lies a huge gap in perception and pricing.**

### 1. Salesforce (CRM) — The Biggest Winner in Enterprise AI Agent Applications

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Salesforce is the world's largest CRM software company, with more than 150,000 enterprise customers. The core of its AI strategy is Agentforce — an AI agent platform that can autonomously execute tasks across Sales, Service, Marketing, and other scenarios. Unlike a simple chatbot, Agentforce can genuinely "replace" human work: automatically handling customer tickets, generating sales strategies, and optimizing marketing workflows.

**Key Financial Data**

- FY2027 Q1 (reported May 2026): revenue of $11.13 billion, up 13% year-over-year, beating expectations
- Agentforce ARR reached $1.2 billion, up 205% year-over-year
- Combined Agentforce + Data 360 ARR nearly $3.4 billion, up more than 200% year-over-year
- Cumulative delivery of 3.8 billion Agentic Work Units, up 111% quarter-over-quarter
- Market cap of roughly $147-156 billion, Trailing P/E of about 22x, Forward P/E of about 13x

(Source: Salesforce FY2027 Q1 earnings report, SEC 8-K filing, Yahoo Finance)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Salesforce's greatest moat isn't technology — it's the ecosystem. There are more than 10 million Salesforce-certified developers and partners worldwide, and enterprises' customer data, sales processes, and service systems are deeply embedded in the Salesforce ecosystem, making migration costs extremely high. The cleverness of Agentforce is that it isn't a separate AI product built from scratch — it layers AI capability onto existing customer relationships, which pushes customer acquisition cost toward zero.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 22x Trailing P/E and 13x Forward P/E is severely undervalued for a company whose overall revenue is growing at +13% while its AI business is growing at +205%. The market is pricing Salesforce as a "traditional software stock," completely ignoring Agentforce's potential to grow from $1.2 billion in ARR to $5-8 billion in ARR over the next two to three years. If the AI business's share of revenue rises from roughly 7% today to more than 20%, overall growth should reaccelerate to 20%+, and the P/E should logically return to 30-35x.

**Risk Factors**

- AI agent competition is fierce; Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini for Workspace are both fighting for the same market
- Overall revenue growth has slowed to 13%; if Agentforce growth falls short of expectations, the stock faces significant downside pressure
- A weak macroeconomy could lead enterprises to cut IT spending

**One-line conclusion: The world's leading player in enterprise AI agent applications, priced at a "discount" as if it were a traditional software stock — one of the biggest valuation hollows in the application layer.**

### 2. Adobe (ADBE) — The AI Creative-Tools Monopolist, Badly Misunderstood by the Market

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Adobe is the undisputed dominant force in creative software and document management; its products — Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, Acrobat, and others — face virtually no competitors at the same tier. The core of its AI strategy is Firefly, Adobe's own generative AI model, embedded directly across its entire product line — from image generation and video editing to intelligent PDF processing — bringing AI to every corner of the business.

**Key Financial Data**

- FY2026 Q1: revenue of $6.4 billion, up 12% year-over-year; Non-GAAP EPS of $6.06
- Firefly ARR exceeded $250 million, up 75% quarter-over-quarter
- AI-first ARR grew more than 3x year-over-year
- Firefly free-tier MAU exceeded 70 million, up 35% year-over-year
- MAU across the full product line exceeded 850 million, up 17% year-over-year
- Share price of roughly $253-256 (down about 39% from its high), Trailing P/E of about 16x, Forward P/E of about 9x
- A $25 billion buyback program is underway

(Source: Adobe FY2026 Q1 earnings report, SEC 10-Q filing, TIKR, 24/7 Wall St.)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Adobe's moat runs absurdly deep. 850 million monthly active users, decades-old file-format standards (PDF), and de facto industry standards for creative work (PSD, AI formats) — these aren't technical barriers, they're ecosystem barriers. Canva and Midjourney can nibble away in certain niche scenarios, but they cannot replace Adobe's position in professional creative workflows. More importantly, Adobe's compliance approach to training data (using only licensed content to train Firefly) gives it a unique legal-safety advantage with enterprise customers.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 16x Trailing P/E and 9x Forward P/E — this is Adobe's lowest valuation in a decade; its five-year median P/E is 40x. The market fears AI will disrupt Adobe (letting tools like Midjourney and Sora replace Photoshop), but the reality is the opposite: Adobe is "absorbing" AI tools into its own products, and Firefly's 75% quarter-over-quarter ARR growth is proof. A 9x Forward P/E means the market is pricing Adobe like a consumer-goods company rather than a growth software company — which is absurd.

But there is a real vulnerability worth noting: Adobe itself acknowledges that AI tools are eating into its stock-photo business (Adobe Stock). The pain of self-disruption is real.

**Risk Factors**

- The substitution effect of AI on the Stock business could spread to other business lines
- Competitors like Canva and Figma continue to chip away at the low- and mid-end market
- Overall growth is only 12%; if Firefly monetization falls behind expectations, valuation recovery could be delayed

**One-line conclusion: The software giant most unjustly punished in the AI era — buying a creative monopoly platform with 850 million MAU and accelerating AI penetration at 9x Forward P/E offers an excellent risk-reward ratio.**

### 3. ServiceNow (NOW) — The Hidden Champion of Enterprise AI Workflows

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

ServiceNow is the global leader in enterprise IT service management (ITSM) and workflow automation platforms. Its AI product, Now Assist, embeds large models into enterprise workflows to enable intelligent automation across IT operations, human resources, customer service, and other scenarios. Unlike Salesforce, which focuses on front-end CRM, ServiceNow digs deep into enterprises' "back-office" operations — one of the most solidly grounded scenarios for AI deployment.

**Key Financial Data**

- 2026 Q1: subscription revenue of $3.671 billion, up 22% year-over-year (up 19% at constant currency)
- Net new ACV for AI products grew at a triple-digit rate year-over-year
- The AI revenue target was raised 50% to $1.5 billion (mid-year target)
- The number of $1M+ Now Assist customers grew more than 130% year-over-year
- Full-year subscription revenue guidance was raised to a midpoint of $15.755 billion, implying growth of about 21% year-over-year
- Market cap of roughly $131 billion, Trailing P/E of about 80x, Forward P/E of about 32x

(Source: ServiceNow Q1 2026 earnings report, SEC 8-K filing, Futurum, GuruFocus)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

ServiceNow's moat lies in "workflow stickiness." Once an enterprise migrates core business processes — IT operations, HR workflows, customer service — onto the ServiceNow platform, the cost of migrating away is extremely high. Unlike CRM, ServiceNow manages the "nervous system" of an enterprise's internal operations; replacing it is akin to performing surgery on the entire company. Its 630 customers with $5M+ ACV are the best proof of that stickiness.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 32x Forward P/E against 22% revenue growth and triple-digit AI growth is on the pricier side of reasonable within the AI application layer — not cheap, but not a bubble either. ServiceNow's strength lies in "certainty": AI-driven enterprise workflows are a certain trend, and NOW has no true competitor of the same scale in this space. If AI revenue's share rises from roughly 10% today to 25%, overall growth could stay above 20%, making the current valuation acceptable.

**Risk Factors**

- A 32x Forward P/E leaves limited room for error; any slowdown in growth would compress the valuation
- Microsoft is embedding Copilot into Dynamics 365 and Teams, which could gradually erode ServiceNow's ITSM market
- AI investment is pressuring near-term margins

**One-line conclusion: The most certain winner in enterprise AI workflows — reasonably valued but not cheap, suited to investors seeking an "AI certainty premium."**

### 4. Tempus AI (TEM) — The Purest Bet on AI-Driven Precision Medicine

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Tempus AI is the purest publicly listed company in the AI precision-medicine field. The company has built one of the world's largest clinical and molecular databases, using AI to analyze genomic data and help physicians design personalized treatment plans for cancer patients. Its business splits into two segments: Diagnostics and Data & Applications, with the latter being a high-margin software-plus-data business.

**Key Financial Data**

- 2026 Q1: revenue of $348.1 million, up 36.1% year-over-year
- Diagnostics revenue of $261.1 million, up 34.7%; oncology test volume up 28%
- Data & Applications revenue of $87 million, up 40.5%
- MRD (minimal residual disease) testing volume of roughly 6,500 cases, up 500% year-over-year
- Full-year revenue guidance was raised to $1.59-1.60 billion, up 25% year-over-year
- Full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA is projected at roughly $65 million (its first approach toward profitability)
- Cash and marketable securities of $643.8 million
- Market cap of roughly $8.5-9.4 billion (down by half from its high)

(Source: Tempus AI Q1 2026 earnings report, BusinessWire, SEC 8-K filing)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Tempus's core barrier is data. Its volume of clinical-plus-genomic data is unmatched in the industry — the more data it has, the more precise its AI models become, attracting more hospitals to use it, which generates still more data. This is a positive feedback flywheel. The precision-medicine market exceeds $100 billion, and Tempus's current annual revenue is only $1.6 billion, a penetration rate of less than 2%.

**Valuation Assessment**

A $9 billion market cap against $1.6 billion in revenue implies a P/S of about 5.6x. Given 36% growth and an approaching profitability inflection point, the valuation isn't expensive. But Tempus's core risk is profitability — Adjusted EBITDA is only $65 million, and the company remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis. If the profitability inflection is confirmed, the stock has substantial room to recover; if profitability keeps getting pushed back, the current valuation lacks a margin of safety.

**Risk Factors**

- Precision medicine has a long commercialization cycle, and hospital procurement decisions are slow
- Dependence on health-insurance reimbursement policy creates significant policy risk
- Competition is intensifying: Foundation Medicine (a Roche subsidiary), Guardant Health, and others are all fighting for the market
- After falling by half from its high, there is still no clear timetable for profitability

**One-line conclusion: The purest name in the AI precision-medicine track, with a deep data-flywheel moat — but with the profitability inflection unconfirmed, it's a high-odds, medium-risk growth stock.**

### 5. Kingsoft Office (688111.SH) — China's AI Office-Software Leader, WPS AI Has Broken Out

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Kingsoft Office is China's leading office-software company; WPS Office has 672 million monthly active devices worldwide. The core of its AI strategy is WPS AI — an AI assistant embedded directly across the full range of office scenarios: documents, spreadsheets, presentations, and PDFs. From AI writing and AI formatting to AI data analysis, WPS AI is positioned as "the AI office assistant for every Chinese white-collar worker."

**Key Financial Data**

- Full-year 2025: revenue of 5.929 billion yuan, up 15.78% year-over-year; net profit attributable to shareholders of 1.836 billion yuan, up 11.63%
- WPS 365 business revenue of 720 million yuan, up 64.93% year-over-year (AI-driven enterprise subscriptions)
- WPS AI domestic MAU exceeded 80.13 million, up 307% year-over-year
- Average daily AI token calls exceeded 200 billion, up more than 12x year-over-year
- 2026 Q1: revenue of 1.613 billion yuan, up 23.95% year-over-year (accelerating)
- WPS 365 Q1 revenue of 244 million yuan, up 60.79% year-over-year (the fifth consecutive quarter of growth above 60%)
- Cumulative R&D spending over three years of 5.263 billion yuan
- Share price of roughly 248-262 yuan, market cap of about 115 billion yuan, P/E of about 32x

(Source: Kingsoft Office 2025 annual report, 2026 Q1 report, Sina Finance, China National Radio)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

WPS has two moats in China: a policy moat (the "Xinchuang" IT-localization push requires government and state-owned enterprises to use domestic office software), and a user-habit moat (672 million monthly active devices, with penetration in China far exceeding Microsoft Office). WPS AI's advantage is that it needs no additional customer acquisition — its 80 million AI MAU are converted directly from its existing base of 672 million users, a conversion rate of just 12%, leaving huge room to grow.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 32x P/E against 24% revenue growth (Q1), 60%+ WPS 365 growth, and 307% AI MAU growth is on the low side of reasonable among A-share tech stocks (compared with A-share AI-concept stocks that routinely trade at 80-100x P/E in the same period). The core question is AI monetization capability — WPS AI has 80 million MAU, but how high can ARPU (average revenue per user) go? If WPS AI's paid-conversion rate doubles from current levels, the WPS 365 business could grow from 720 million yuan to 2-3 billion yuan, driving a significant improvement in overall margins.

**Risk Factors**

- Competition in the AI office-software track is fierce; DingTalk, Feishu, Tongyi, and others are all building AI office products
- Overseas expansion is slow, and the company is highly dependent on the China market
- Sentiment in the A-share market swings widely, and the valuation could fluctuate sharply as the AI narrative shifts
- The 445% surge in Q1 2026 net profit was mainly driven by investment gains; growth after excluding non-recurring items was about 32%, so the true growth rate of the core business bears watching

**One-line conclusion: The most certain company in China's AI office-software track — 672 million monthly active devices plus 80 million AI MAU is a monetization goldmine, and a 32x P/E stands out for value among A-share AI stocks.**

### 6. Palantir (PLTR) — An AI Decision System With Staggering Growth, and an Even More Staggering Valuation

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Palantir is an AI decision-systems company whose products include Gotham (government/military intelligence analysis), Foundry (enterprise data integration and operations optimization), and AIP (AI Platform, its newest large-model integration platform). Palantir may be the large software company with the highest "AI purity" in the world — nearly 100% of its revenue centers on AI data analysis and decision systems.

**Key Financial Data**

- 2026 Q1: revenue of $1.63 billion, up 85% year-over-year, far exceeding expectations
- U.S. market revenue up 104% year-over-year
- GAAP net income of $871 million, a 53% net margin (quadrupling year-over-year)
- Adjusted EPS of $0.33
- Full-year revenue guidance was raised to $7.65-7.66 billion, up 71% year-over-year
- Rule of 40 score as high as 145%
- Market cap of roughly $365-385 billion, Trailing P/E of about 153x

(Source: Palantir Q1 2026 earnings report, CNBC, SEC 8-K filing, Yahoo Finance)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Palantir's moat lies in "embeddedness." Once a government agency or large enterprise deploys Palantir as its data hub and decision engine, the cost of switching away is extremely high — this involves not just technical migration but non-technical factors like security clearances and staff retraining. Especially in the U.S. defense and intelligence sectors, Palantir has secured a near-exclusive position.

**Valuation Assessment**

At a 153x Trailing P/E, even calculated against full-year revenue of $7.65 billion and a 53% net margin, the Forward P/E still sits above 50-60x. Matching 85% growth to this valuation requires assuming that growth stays above 50% for the next three years. That's not impossible, but historically almost no software company has sustained 50%+ growth for three years after surpassing $5 billion in revenue. Palantir might be the exception, but "betting on the exception" is itself a high-risk strategy.

The market isn't giving Palantir a software-company valuation — it's giving it an "operating system of the AI era" valuation. If that narrative is disproven (say, growth drops to 30%), the stock could fall by half.

**Risk Factors**

- The growth expectations implied by the valuation are extremely high, leaving almost no room for error
- Heavy reliance on government contracts means policy changes (such as defense budget cuts) could directly hit revenue
- CEO Alex Karp continues to sell shares
- If the market rotates in style (from growth to value), high-valuation AI stocks would be hit first

**One-line conclusion: The product and growth rate are beyond reproach, but a 153x P/E means the market has already priced in five years of optimistic expectations — buying at the current price means "paying for perfection."**

### 7. iFlytek (002230.SZ) — The Broadest AI Deployment in China, But Profitability Is Its Achilles' Heel

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

iFlytek is the company with the broadest AI deployment in China, with businesses spanning six major areas: smart education (33% of revenue), smart healthcare, digital government, smart automotive, and AI hardware (learning tablets and the like). Its Spark large model is the underlying technology engine, and it ranks first in the industry both in the number and value of large-model-related contracts won.

**Key Financial Data**

- Full-year 2025: revenue of 27.105 billion yuan, up 16.12% year-over-year
- Net profit attributable to shareholders of 839 million yuan, up 49.85% year-over-year; net profit excluding non-recurring items of 264 million yuan, up 40.47%
- AI platform and licensing service revenue of 1.252 billion yuan; large-model API and MaaS platform service revenue of 385 million yuan, up 263% year-over-year
- Smart education revenue of 8.967 billion yuan, up 24%; enterprise AI solutions revenue of 859 million yuan, up 33.65%
- 2026 Q1: revenue of 4.658 billion yuan, up 27.74%; net profit excluding non-recurring items up 48.29%
- R&D spending of 5.364 billion yuan, 19.79% of revenue
- Sales collections exceeded 27.4 billion yuan, operating net cash flow of 3.2 billion yuan
- Share price of roughly 48 yuan, total market cap of about 111 billion yuan (about $15.5 billion)

(Source: iFlytek 2025 annual report, 2026 Q1 report, Sina Finance, QbitAI)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

iFlytek's moat is "government/enterprise relationships plus accumulated use cases." In fields like smart education and digital government, iFlytek has spent more than a decade building deep customer relationships and product barriers. Its AI learning tablet has become a consumer hit (when users think of learning tablets, they think of iFlytek). But iFlytek's problem is that it behaves more like an "AI systems integrator" than an "AI product company" — a large share of its revenue comes from project-based solutions, which limits gross margin and scale effects.

**Valuation Assessment**

Calculated against 2025 net profit excluding non-recurring items of 264 million yuan, the P/E exceeds 400x — clearly unreasonable. Calculated against analysts' projected 2026 net profit of roughly 1 billion yuan, the Forward P/E is about 110x. For a company whose revenue growth is only 16-28%, this valuation runs rich. The core contradiction is that iFlytek's revenue scale is large enough (27.1 billion yuan), but its net margin is too low (net margin attributable to shareholders of just 3.1%, with the figure excluding non-recurring items under 1%), as R&D spending on large AI models keeps eating into profit.

**Risk Factors**

- Net margin is extremely low (under 1% excluding non-recurring items), and earnings quality is poor
- Accounts receivable is large in scale, and cash-flow quality warrants ongoing attention
- A high share of government customers means budget fluctuations directly affect revenue
- Competition in large models is fierce; Baidu's Ernie, Alibaba's Tongyi, and ByteDance's Doubao are all fighting for market share

**One-line conclusion: The company with the broadest AI deployment in China, but the price of "doing everything" is a net margin of just 1-3% — until it proves it can release profit, a 110x Forward P/E lacks a margin of safety.**

### 8. Snowflake (SNOW) — An AI Data-Cloud Platform With Improving Fundamentals, Still Expensive

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Snowflake is the leading cloud data platform, helping enterprises store, manage, and analyze data in the cloud. The core of its AI strategy is upgrading itself from a "data warehouse" to an "AI data cloud" — through products like Cortex AI and Snowflake Intelligence, it lets enterprises train and run AI models directly on the data platform, without moving data elsewhere.

**Key Financial Data**

- FY2027 Q1 (ended April 30, 2026): product revenue of $1.33 billion, up 34% year-over-year
- Total revenue of $1.39 billion, up 33%
- More than 13,600 accounts use Snowflake's AI features
- The number of Snowflake Intelligence accounts doubled quarter-over-quarter
- 779 customers with $1M+ in spend (46 net adds year-over-year vs. 26 the prior year)
- Non-GAAP operating margin expanded from 9% to 12%
- Adjusted EPS of $0.39 (up 63% year-over-year)
- Full-year product revenue guidance was raised to $5.84 billion, up 31%
- Market cap of roughly $90 billion, GAAP loss-making, Forward P/E of about 85x

(Source: Snowflake FY2027 Q1 earnings report, SEC 8-K filing, GuruFocus)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Snowflake's moat lies in "data gravity." Once an enterprise's data is stored in Snowflake, all the queries, reports, and AI models built around that data depend on the platform, making data migration extremely costly and risky. But Snowflake faces direct competition from Databricks, which has an edge on AI/ML workloads and is growing faster (65%+ vs. 34%) — Snowflake's biggest strategic pressure point.

**Valuation Assessment**

An 85x Forward P/E against 34% growth and a 12% Non-GAAP operating margin makes Snowflake noticeably more expensive than ServiceNow (32x Forward P/E, 22% growth). Snowflake's advantage is that it sits at the AI data layer — every AI application needs data infrastructure, so the theoretical TAM is enormous. But the issue is that Databricks is running faster in this space and is about to go public. If Databricks commands a higher valuation, it could put near-term valuation pressure on Snowflake.

**Risk Factors**

- Competition from Databricks is intensifying; it grows faster and has stronger AI products
- The company remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis, and its path to profitability isn't clear enough
- An 85x Forward P/E means sustained growth of 30%+ is needed to support it
- Its consumption-based pricing model makes revenue less predictable than a subscription model

**One-line conclusion: An important player in the AI data cloud, with improving fundamentals (expanding margins and steady growth), but an 85x Forward P/E lacks sufficient margin of safety ahead of the Databricks IPO.**

### 9. CrowdStrike (CRWD) — An AI Security Platform With Strong Results, Still Shadowed by Its Outage

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

CrowdStrike is the world's leading cloud-native cybersecurity platform, centered on endpoint protection, delivering one-stop security solutions through its AI-driven Falcon platform. CEO George Kurtz positions the company as "AI security infrastructure" — in an era when enterprises deploy AI at scale, protecting AI systems from attack has become a hard requirement.

**Key Financial Data**

- FY2027 Q1 (ended April 30, 2026): revenue of $1.39 billion, up 25.6% year-over-year
- ARR reached $5.51 billion, up 24% year-over-year
- Net new ARR set a Q1 record of $256 million, up 32% year-over-year
- Record operating cash flow of $591 million, free cash flow of $468 million
- Full-year net new ARR growth guidance was raised to a midpoint of 27.7%
- Non-GAAP operating income of $325.7 million (up 62% year-over-year)
- Market cap of roughly $190 billion; announced a 4-for-1 stock split
- Forward P/E estimated at roughly 55-65x

(Source: CrowdStrike FY2027 Q1 earnings report, SEC 8-K/10-Q filing, Yahoo Finance)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

CrowdStrike's moat lies in "platform stickiness plus data network effects." The Falcon platform spans 28 modules covering endpoint protection, cloud security, identity security, log management, and more, and the average number of modules customers adopt keeps rising. More customers = more threat data = more precise AI models = better detection = more customers attracted. Cybersecurity is itself one of AI's most natural application scenarios — attackers use AI, so defenders must use AI too.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 55-65x Forward P/E against 26% revenue growth and rapidly improving margins. Compared with ServiceNow (32x Forward P/E, 22% growth), CrowdStrike's valuation premium mainly reflects the "AI security is a hard necessity" narrative and its faster margin improvement. The valuation isn't cheap, but it has some justification given the non-cyclical nature of cybersecurity spending (enterprises can cut marketing budgets, but they don't dare cut security budgets).

**Risk Factors**

- The shadow of the 2024 global outage (the "Blue Screen" incident) still lingers, and brand trust needs continued repair
- Competition in cybersecurity is fierce: Palo Alto Networks, SentinelOne, Microsoft Defender
- A $190 billion market cap against $5.5 billion in ARR implies roughly 35x P/ARR, a historic high
- If growth slows below 20%, the valuation could compress sharply

**One-line conclusion: The frontrunner in the AI security track, whose consistently beat-and-raise results prove its resilience since the outage — but a 35x P/ARR means most of the good news is already reflected in the price.**

### 10. Tesla (TSLA) — Autonomous Driving Plus Robotics, the Ultimate Test of a "Faith Stock"

**Company Overview and AI Positioning**

Tesla is the world's largest electric-vehicle maker, but its AI strategy reaches far beyond cars: the FSD (Full Self-Driving) autonomous-driving system, the Robotaxi driverless ride-hailing service, and the Optimus humanoid robot. Elon Musk has repeatedly stressed that Tesla isn't a car company but an "AI and robotics company." The vast majority of its current valuation isn't pricing the automotive business — it's pricing the future imaginative space of FSD plus Robotaxi plus Optimus.

**Key Financial Data**

- 2026 Q1: total revenue of $22.4 billion, up 15.8% year-over-year
- Automotive gross margin of roughly 19% (improving)
- FSD (Supervised) active subscriptions of 1.28 million, up 51% year-over-year
- Robotaxi operates in three Texas cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston)
- The steering-wheel-free Cybercab has begun mass production and is not subject to the 2,500-vehicles-per-year autonomous-vehicle cap
- Cumulative FSD miles driven exceed 10 billion (including 3.7 billion miles on city streets)
- 2026 capital expenditure of roughly $25 billion (including AI chips and Robotaxi infrastructure)
- Market cap of roughly $1.5-1.6 trillion, Trailing P/E of about 355x
- Musk has acknowledged that Robotaxi revenue will not make a material contribution in 2026, pushing that to 2027

(Source: Tesla Q1 2026 earnings report, SEC 8-K filing, IBTimes, TradingKey)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Tesla's AI moat rests on three foundations: 1) the world's largest real-world driving dataset (10 billion miles of FSD data); 2) vertical integration (its own Dojo/AI5 AI chips, its own vehicle fleet, and its own software); and 3) brand and ecosystem stickiness. But it's worth recognizing clearly that Waymo leads Tesla by at least one to two years in autonomous-driving technology maturity — Tesla's advantage lies in scale potential, not technological leadership.

**Valuation Assessment**

A 355x Trailing P/E and 175x Forward P/E — this isn't a valuation for a car company, or even for a software company. It's a price tag on the grand narrative of "AI transforming how humanity travels."

A simple back-of-envelope calculation: based on the current automotive business alone, Tesla's reasonable P/E would be about 15-25x (benchmarked against traditional automakers), implying a market cap of roughly $70-110 billion. Its current $1.5 trillion market cap implies the market is assigning FSD, Robotaxi, and Optimus an implied valuation of more than $400 billion — while Robotaxi's 2026 revenue is close to zero, and Optimus remains purely conceptual.

That's not to say Tesla isn't worth the price. If Robotaxi truly scales up in 2027-2028 (annual revenue exceeding $10 billion), and if Optimus achieves commercialization before 2030, the current valuation might even prove too low. But that requires far too many "ifs," and each "if" carries a real chance of failure.

**Risk Factors**

- Robotaxi's scale-up timetable keeps slipping (in 2024 it was promised for 2025, in 2025 for 2026, and now for a material contribution in 2027)
- Elon Musk's political controversies (DOGE and others) continue to damage brand image and purchase intent
- Competition in the China market has reached a fever pitch, with local brands like BYD squeezing Tesla on both technology and price
- A 355x P/E means any negative news could trigger a sharp sell-off
- The Optimus robot is still at least three to five years from commercialization and remains a pure concept for now

**One-line conclusion: If you believe autonomous driving and humanoid robots will reshape human society, Tesla is the best vehicle for that bet; but a 355x P/E means you're paying for a "utopia," so the position size shouldn't be too large.**

## III. Summary: Where the Valuation Hollows Are, Who Is Most Undervalued, and Who Is Most Overvalued

### Valuation Hollow Ranking (Most Undervalued to Most Overvalued)

| Rank | Company | Forward P/E | Core Rationale |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | **Adobe** | ~9x | 850 million MAU plus 3x Firefly growth, priced by the market like a consumer product — a five-year valuation low |
| 2 | **Salesforce** | ~13x | Agentforce growing +205%, with enormous cross-sell room across 150,000 enterprise customers |
| 3 | **Kingsoft Office** | ~32x (A-shares) | 672 million MAU plus 80 million AI MAU, driven by both IT-localization and AI — the best value among A-share AI stocks |
| 4 | **ServiceNow** | ~32x | The most certain bet on AI-driven enterprise workflows, though the valuation already reflects most of the good news |
| 5 | **Tempus AI** | P/S ~5.6x | A deep data-flywheel moat in precision medicine, but the profitability inflection remains unconfirmed |
| 6 | **CrowdStrike** | ~55-65x | The AI-security-necessity logic is solid, but a 35x P/ARR sits at a historic high |
| 7 | **Snowflake** | ~85x | An important player in the data-cloud track, but facing Databricks competition and a rich valuation |
| 8 | **iFlytek** | ~110x | The broadest deployment, but margins are too thin, leaving the valuation without profit support |
| 9 | **Palantir** | ~153x | Staggering growth, but the valuation is "paying for perfection," with zero room for error |
| 10 | **Tesla** | ~355x | The ultimate AI faith stock, betting on the world after 2027 |

### Three Core Judgments

**1. The two most undervalued: Adobe and Salesforce.** The market prices them using a traditional-software valuation framework, completely ignoring their AI businesses' 200%+ growth and the cross-sell potential of their enormous user bases. At 9x and 13x Forward P/E, buying them means buying certain growth plus an AI upside option — the best risk-reward ratio among all 10 companies.

**2. The two most overvalued: Tesla and Palantir.** A 355x and a 153x P/E have already priced in the "most optimistic future" in full. That doesn't mean they're bad companies — quite the opposite, they may be the most important AI companies of this era. But investing isn't a contest of whose company is best; it's a contest of whose price is reasonable. A good company bought at the wrong price can still lose you money.

**3. The opportunity in China's AI application layer lies with Kingsoft Office, not iFlytek.** Both do AI, but Kingsoft Office has 672 million monthly active devices, WPS 365 growth above 60% for five straight quarters, and a net margin (excluding non-recurring items) above 30%. iFlytek has larger revenue (27.1 billion yuan), but its net margin excluding non-recurring items is under 1% — it is, in substance, a company that uses AI to do project integration, not a company that uses AI to build products. The former's business model scales; the latter's does not.
