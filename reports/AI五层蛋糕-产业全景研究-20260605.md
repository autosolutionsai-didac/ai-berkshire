# Jensen Huang's AI Five-Layer Cake: Who's Making Money at Each Layer?

> Research date: June 5, 2026 | Synthesis of 16 parallel research threads

---

## Framework Definition

At the 2025 Davos Forum, in conversation with BlackRock CEO Larry Fink, Jensen Huang defined the AI industry as a "five-layer cake":

```
Layer 5 ┃ Application Layer      ┃ AI agents, autonomous driving, robotics, industry solutions
Layer 4 ┃ Model Layer            ┃ Large language models, multimodal models
Layer 3 ┃ Infrastructure Layer   ┃ AI factories, data centers, networking, cooling
Layer 2 ┃ Chip Layer             ┃ GPUs, accelerators, HBM memory, advanced packaging
Layer 1 ┃ Energy Layer           ┃ Power supply, transformers, nuclear energy
```

His core thesis: AI is not a product — it is an entirely new industrial system. Every layer offers enormous investment opportunity, and the lower the layer, the less replaceable it is.

He calls data centers "AI factories" — "they do just one thing every day: produce tokens." In the future, every company will have two factories: one that makes products, and one that is an AI factory.

## Layer 1: Energy — The Most Underrated Bottleneck

**Core logic:** The ultimate constraint on AI compute is electricity. Global data center power consumption was 485 TWh in 2025 and is projected to double to 950 TWh by 2030. Nearly half of planned data center construction projects in the United States have been delayed or canceled — not for lack of GPUs, but for lack of power and transformers.

**Bottleneck data:** Lead times for large power transformers have stretched to 2-4 years; high-voltage substations now take 3-5 years.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Eaton** | Leading electrical equipment maker | Global leader in data center power distribution systems; benefiting from the transformer shortage |
| **GE Vernova** | Power equipment + natural gas generation | Energy company spun off from GE; benefiting on both the transformer and gas turbine fronts |
| **Constellation Energy** | Nuclear power operator | The largest nuclear power operator in the US; signed a nuclear power supply agreement with Microsoft |
| **NuScale Power** | Small modular reactors (SMR) | The only NRC-certified SMR design, aimed at long-term power supply for data centers |
| **TBEA** | China's leading transformer maker | China's transformer exports to the US surged from 1,500 units in 2022 to over 8,000 units in 2025 |

**Rationale for selection:** A 4-year transformer lead time means this bottleneck will not ease before 2028. Nuclear power is the ultimate answer for long-term data center power supply. This layer offers the highest certainty, the least attention, and the most reasonable valuations.

## Layer 2: Chips — The Most Value-Dense Battleground

**Core logic:** The AI accelerator market is projected to exceed $150 billion in 2026. Nvidia still holds roughly 75-80% share, but custom ASIC growth (+45%) has for the first time surpassed general-purpose GPU growth (+16%) — hyperscalers' move to "de-Nvidia-ize" is a structural trend.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Nvidia** | AI GPU monopolist | FY2027 Q1 revenue of $81.6 billion (+85%); data center revenue of $75.2 billion (+92%); gross margin 75%; market cap $5.2 trillion |
| **Broadcom** | King of custom ASICs | Q2 FY2026 AI revenue of $10.8 billion (+143%); designs custom chips for Google, Meta, and OpenAI; $73 billion order backlog |
| **AMD** | The only general-purpose GPU challenger | Q1 2026 data center revenue of $5.8 billion (+57%); OpenAI selected AMD to build 6GW of compute capacity |
| **TSMC** | Monopoly in AI chip manufacturing | Holds over 90% global share in advanced process nodes; CoWoS packaging capacity fully booked through 2026; the physical bottleneck of the entire chain |
| **SK Hynix** | Leader in HBM memory | 62% share of the HBM market, while Samsung's share collapsed from 41% to 17%; HBM is the indispensable "ammunition" for AI chips |

**Rationale for selection:** Nvidia is the standard-setter but faces erosion from custom chips; Broadcom is the biggest winner in custom silicon and is growing faster; AMD is the only viable alternative; TSMC and SK Hynix each hold a physical chokepoint on the entire chain. Together, these five companies constitute the whole of the AI chip layer.

## Layer 3: Infrastructure — A $725 Billion Arms Race

**Core logic:** The four major hyperscale cloud providers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta) will spend roughly $725 billion in combined capital expenditure in 2026, up 97% year over year. None of them is cutting spending — all are raising it. This money is going into servers, networking equipment, data center construction, and cooling systems.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Arista Networks** | AI data center networking switches | AI networking revenue growing rapidly; 800G/1.6T Ethernet solutions; competing with Broadcom as an InfiniBand alternative |
| **Vertiv** | Data center power + liquid cooling | Ranks first globally in liquid cooling with 11.3% market share; the NVL72 architecture's mandatory liquid cooling is a key catalyst |
| **Innolight** | Global leader in AI optical modules | 2025 revenue of 38.2 billion yuan (+60%); net profit of 10.8 billion yuan (+109%); roughly 50% share of the 800G market |
| **Dell Technologies** | Leader in AI server shipments | AI server order backlog exceeds $9 billion; deeply tied to Nvidia |
| **CoreWeave** | Pure-play GPU cloud infrastructure | 2025 revenue of $5.13 billion (+170%); $99.4 billion order backlog; but total debt of $21.4 billion and a GAAP loss |

**Rationale for selection:** Networking (Arista), power and cooling (Vertiv), optical interconnect (Innolight), servers (Dell), and GPU cloud (CoreWeave) — together these cover the complete chain of the infrastructure layer, from power to compute delivery. Innolight is the Chinese company with the strongest pricing power anywhere in the AI industry chain.

## Layer 4: Models — The Fastest Revenue Growth in History

**Core logic:** Anthropic's annualized revenue rose from $1 billion to $47 billion in just 14 months — quite possibly the fastest corporate revenue growth in business history. OpenAI's monthly revenue stands at $2 billion. The second half of 2026 is set to bring a wave of AI company IPOs (Anthropic has already filed confidentially; OpenAI is targeting September).

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Anthropic** | The most highly valued AI startup | Valued at $965 billion (surpassing OpenAI); annualized revenue of $47 billion; ranked first on Arena; has already filed confidentially for an IPO |
| **OpenAI** | AI first-mover | Valued at $852 billion; annualized revenue of roughly $24 billion; targeting an IPO in September 2026 |
| **Google DeepMind** | Most comprehensive technology | Gemini subscription revenue of $1.2 billion; Google Cloud AI product revenue up 800%; parent Alphabet's market cap stands at $2.1 trillion |
| **Meta AI** | Leader in the open-source approach | Building an ecosystem around the open-sourced Llama models; AI has lifted advertising revenue by more than 20%; 2026 AI capital expenditure of $125-145 billion |

**Rationale for selection:** Anthropic and OpenAI are the twin leaders in commercialization; Google has the most complete AI monetization loop (search + cloud + subscriptions); Meta's open-source approach shapes the entire ecosystem; DeepSeek demonstrates that China can match the frontier at one-tenth the cost. xAI and Mistral remain too small in scale and were not included.

## Layer 5: Applications — Who Is Actually Making Money from AI?

**Core logic:** For most so-called "AI concept stocks," AI revenue still accounts for only a small share of the total. Few companies are genuinely making money from AI, yet valuations are extremely divergent — Palantir trades at a 105x P/E, while Salesforce trades at just 14x.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Palantir** | AI decision operating system | Q1 2026 revenue of $1.63 billion (+85%); net margin of 44%; AIP Boot Camp conversion rate of 75%; but trades at a 105x P/E |
| **Salesforce** | AI agent enterprise applications | Agentforce ARR of over $1 billion across 29,000 deals; FY2026 total revenue of $41.5 billion; trades at just 14x P/E — the best value among AI application plays |
| **ServiceNow** | Enterprise AI workflow | Now Assist ACV target of over $1 billion; subscription revenue up 22%; dominant position in ITSM; trades at 25x P/E |
| **Tesla** | Autonomous driving + robotics | 1.28 million FSD subscribers; Robotaxi already operating in Dallas and Houston; Optimus set for mass production in summer 2026; but trades at a 317x P/E |
| **Adobe** | AI creative tools | Firefly has generated over 24 billion assets; 75% adoption rate among Fortune 500 companies; but direct AI revenue is just $400 million; lowest forward P/E among peers at 10x |

**Rationale for selection:** Palantir has the fastest-growing AI application revenue (+85%); Salesforce is the most reasonably valued large-cap AI application play (14x P/E); ServiceNow is the clearest case of enterprise AI monetization; Tesla represents the ultimate physical embodiment of AI (autonomous driving + robotics); Adobe is the defining company in the AI creative space.

## Investment Perspective Summary

### Investment Opportunities Ranked by Certainty

| Priority | Layer | Logic | Representative Names |
|--------|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Energy | The 4-year transformer lead time cannot be eased — the most certain bottleneck | Eaton, GE Vernova |
| 2 | Chips | The "standard unit of measure" for AI compute; the deepest moat | Nvidia (though valuation warrants scrutiny), Broadcom (faster growth) |
| 3 | Infrastructure | $725 billion in capital expenditure is real money | Innolight, Vertiv, Arista |
| 4 | Models | The most explosive growth, but most remain private | Awaiting the Anthropic/OpenAI IPOs |
| 5 | Applications | Still very few companies genuinely making money from AI | Salesforce (14x P/E) and Adobe (10x P/E) offer the best value |

### In One Sentence

The lower, more physical, and more unavoidable a layer is, the higher the investment certainty. The "sexiest" layers — models and applications — are, ironically, the least certain.
