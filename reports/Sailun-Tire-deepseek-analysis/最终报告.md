## 4. Summary of Analysis by Dimension

### 4.1 Business Model & Moat (Duan Yongping's lens)

1. **Not a "good business" by Duan Yongping's standard**: tire manufacturing is asset-heavy, highly cyclical, has low switching costs, and lacks differentiation. Duan would likely say "I can't really see where this industry's moat is" — which for him is essentially equivalent to "don't invest."
2. **Liquid Gold technology is a genuine innovation**: the world's first liquid-phase mixing chemical rubber process, certified by TÜV SÜD, achieving breakthroughs on the "devil's triangle" of rolling resistance / wet grip / wear resistance. But consumer awareness is weak, the durability of the technical edge is uncertain, and other companies can catch up.
4. **Global production footprint is the biggest moat**: five overseas bases (Vietnam / Cambodia / Indonesia / Mexico / Egypt); the first-mover advantage takes 5-10 years to build up. But Chinese tire companies are collectively going overseas, raising the risk of "overseas involution."
5. **Off-road tires (OTR) are a business highlight**: high gross margin (estimated 30%+), high technical barriers, already supplying Caterpillar and Rio Tinto — the most valuable part of the portfolio.
6. **Insufficient pricing power**: when costs rose in 2025, price increases lagged well behind cost increases, and the gross-margin trend declined.

### 4.2 Financial Health & Valuation (Buffett's lens)

1. **Revenue growth is well established**: 5-year CAGR of 19.6% (RMB 18.0 billion → RMB 36.8 billion); 2025 growth of 15.7% remains strong.
2. **The 2025 profit decline is a cyclical pullback**: gross margin fell from 28% to 25%, mainly due to rising raw-material costs plus trade friction, not deteriorating competitiveness. By 2026Q1, gross margin had already recovered to 26.86%, confirming this was a phase-driven rather than a structural issue.
3. **Cash-flow quality is good**: cumulative 5-year operating CF/net profit = 1.10. Free cash flow is temporarily tight due to overseas plant-building capex (RMB 2.5-3.5 billion per year) — this is a deliberate strategic investment, not deterioration.
4. **The balance sheet is clearly improving**: the debt ratio has fallen from 57.5% to 50.1%; BVPS has risen from RMB 3.50 to RMB 6.57 (a 17% CAGR) — leverage keeps declining even as the company grows.
5. **PE of 12.5x sits in a reasonable range**: against international benchmarks Michelin (10-13x) and Bridgestone (8-11x), Sailun's growth is higher, and its PEG (12.5/15 ≈ 0.83) is attractive. An 8% earnings yield is reasonable in the current interest-rate environment.

### 4.3 Industry Landscape & Competition (Munger's lens)

1. **Demand is perpetual**: the global market is USD 235 billion, with replacement tires accounting for 70%+, giving the category a "repeat-purchase" characteristic.
2. **Sailun ranks 2nd in domestic revenue** (RMB 31.36 billion, behind only Chemchina's RMB 38.355 billion); versus Linglong / Sentury / Triangle, it has the highest ROE (20.82%), the earliest and broadest overseas footprint, and the most pronounced technical differentiation.
3. **Natural rubber is in the early stage of a 20-year upcycle**: currently ~RMB 17,000/tonne (+24% YoY), with a supply-demand gap of roughly 400,000 tonnes. This is a structural medium-to-long-term margin pressure for tire makers, but it also raises the industry's barriers to entry.
4. **New-energy vehicle tires are the biggest structural opportunity**: EVs are heavier (+20-30%) and require more silence and lower rolling resistance → specialized tires priced 50%+ higher, with an industry CAGR of 8-10%.
5. **The industry is fragmented but consolidating**: global CR5 is below 50%, but trade barriers and scale effects are accelerating the exit of smaller players.

### 4.4 Risk & Management (Li Lu's lens)

1. **Yuan Zhongxue's strategic foresight has been outstanding**: he decided to build a plant in Vietnam in 2012 (the first in the industry), getting ahead of the US's 2015 dual anti-dumping/countervailing case by 3 years. Liquid Gold technology received a decade of sustained investment and third-party authoritative certification.
2. **Global trade protectionism is the biggest risk**: the combined US tariff rate on Chinese tires is ~270%; the combined rate on Vietnam/Cambodia exports to the US is ~71-74%. The EU, Brazil, the UK, and other countries are simultaneously closing in, directly exposing 75.9% of overseas revenue.
3. **Succession concerns**: at 71, Yuan Zhongxue remains the guiding spirit; the concert-party group holds roughly 18-20%, with a pronounced family flavor. The successor is unverified.
4. **Operational risks are manageable**: natural rubber exposure can be partly hedged through forward price-locking and Cambodia's origin advantage; currency risk has a hedging system in place; overseas political risk is low in Vietnam and moderate in Cambodia.
5. **Probability of being a top-5 global player in 10 years: 35-40%**: the tire industry won't be disrupted, and EVs are a tailwind; the real risk lies in extreme external policy shifts.

## 5. Investment Thesis (Bull vs. Bear)

### 🟢 Bull Case

1. **First-mover advantage in global production footprint**: 5 overseas bases, hard for latecomers to replicate quickly, industry-leading ability to circumvent tariffs
2. **Strong revenue-growth certainty**: 5-year CAGR of 19.6%, capacity-driven growth rather than dependent on the demand cycle
3. **Liquid Gold technology differentiation**: TÜV certified, AUTOBILD real-world testing shows performance surpassing leading international brands, constituting genuine product differentiation
4. **Off-road tires are a high-barrier track**: 5-year long-term supply contracts with Caterpillar and Rio Tinto, high margin and high barriers, substantial room for domestic substitution
5. **Reasonable valuation**: PE of 12.5x paired with 15%+ growth, PEG<1, probability-weighted target price of RMB 16.7 (+23.8%)
6. **China's rising global tire share is an irreversible trend**: the big-three's share has fallen from 56% to 35-39%, and Chinese companies are still eating into it
7. **Management has strategic foresight**: began positioning for overseas expansion and Liquid Gold ten years ago — genuinely ahead of the curve

### 🔴 Bear Case

1. **Tires aren't a good business**: asset-heavy, highly cyclical, low switching costs, weak pricing power — the business model has structural flaws
2. **Systemic escalation of global trade protectionism**: if the US imposes across-the-board tariffs on Southeast Asia, the core logic of the overseas plants would be shaken
3. **"Overseas involution" risk**: Cambodia already hosts 11 Chinese-owned tire plants; high overseas margins are being eroded by competition
4. **Natural rubber's long upcycle**: cost-side pressure over the medium-to-long term, gross margin center could shift down to 22-24%
5. **Capex black hole**: annual capex of RMB 2.5-3.5 billion, persistently negative free cash flow, rising risk during the expansion phase
6. **Receivables surged 50%**: 2024 receivables of RMB 5.086 billion grew far faster than revenue, warranting vigilance on collection risk
7. **Successor unverified**: at 71, Yuan Zhongxue remains the guiding spirit, and the governance structure carries "key-person risk"

## 8. Summary

Sailun Tire is a company worth researching, but not worth a heavy position.

It has gotten a lot right: it was the first in the world to build an overseas plant, it stuck with a decade of R&D on Liquid Gold, its off-road tires have entered Caterpillar's and Rio Tinto's supply chains, and revenue has doubled in 4 years. Within China's tire industry, Sailun's management and execution are genuinely first-tier. Sailun has a 35-40% probability of becoming a global top-5 tire company within 10 years, which would deliver handsome returns.

But it also has unavoidable structural problems: tire manufacturing requires ongoing heavy capital investment but pricing power is limited; more than 75% of revenue sits in the line of fire of global trade protectionism; the natural-rubber cost cycle is uncontrollable; and the 71-year-old founder remains its sole guiding spirit.

The current price at a PE of 12.5x is "not expensive, but not cheap enough." For growth-value investors willing to accept cyclical swings with a 3-5 year horizon, gradually building a position in the RMB 12-14 range is reasonable. But for Graham-style investors seeking a deep margin of safety, below RMB 10 is the real strike zone.

> **What Munger might say**: "If you can buy a good company at a reasonable price and hold it for the long run, you'll do fine. But you need to be sure it's a good company, and that the price really is reasonable. Is Sailun a good company? Pretty decent. Is the price reasonable? Broadly, yes. But should you bet half your net worth on it? Don't forget Cambodia now has 11 Chinese tire plants."

> **What Duan Yongping might say**: "I generally wouldn't invest in a company like this. Not because it's bad, but because there are easier businesses to choose from. If you can't find that feeling of 'greatness' in the tire industry, don't force yourself. But if you insist on investing, buy when others don't dare to — say, when the US announces a new round of tariffs and the market panics and sells off."
