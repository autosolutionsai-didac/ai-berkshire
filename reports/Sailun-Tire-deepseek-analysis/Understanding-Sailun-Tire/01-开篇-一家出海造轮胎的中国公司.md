## Why write about Sailun Tire

In 2012, a tire boss from Qingdao made a decision that baffled his industry peers: build a factory in Vietnam.

At the time, China's tire industry was booming, and no one thought "going overseas" was necessary. But Yuan Zhongxue saw something others didn't — that global trade protectionism would eventually rise, and the window for Chinese tire exports to Europe and the US would not stay open forever. This move was a full five years ahead of the rest of the industry, and it laid the foundation for what is today Sailun Tire's (601058.SH) single most important competitive advantage.

Sailun is not the largest in the industry (ZC Rubber has higher revenue), not the highest-margin (Sentury Tire is higher), and not the best-known brand (Linglong has broader consumer recognition). But it is the Chinese tire company with the widest global manufacturing footprint and the strongest tariff-avoidance capability.

**It built a factory in Vietnam back in 2012 — a full five years ahead of the industry.**

The significance of that move has only fully revealed itself a decade later, today.

## Three common perceptions about Sailun

### Perception one: "Tires are just low-end manufacturing"

This is the most widespread bias.

It's true that the tire industry is asset-heavy, strongly cyclical, and has low switching costs — it is manufacturing. But this industry has one underappreciated characteristic: **replacement tires account for more than 70% of global tire demand**. This means tires are essentially a "repeat-purchase consumable" — a vehicle needs a new set of tires roughly every 50,000-80,000 kilometers.

The competitive landscape of the global tire market is also undergoing a dramatic shift. The combined global share of the "big three" — Michelin, Bridgestone, and Goodyear — has fallen from a peak of **56%** to **35-39%** (source: CompaniesMarketCap, industry research reports). Who took that share? Chinese companies.

Sailun is one of the companies that has gone the furthest in this wave of "Chinese tire makers going global."

**Counterpoint**: The tire industry genuinely does not have a deep moat. Low switching costs mean consumers can easily switch brands, and pricing power is weak. When raw material costs rose in 2025, Sailun's gross margin fell from 27.6% to 24.7% — **it could not raise prices** — which is exactly what "no pricing power" looks like.

### Perception two: "Sailun just competes on being cheap"

Sailun is indeed cheaper than Michelin and Bridgestone. But assuming Sailun only sells cheap tires is as inaccurate as assuming Pinduoduo "just sells cheap stuff."

Sailun has two competitive weapons the market underappreciates:

**First, Liquid Gold tires.** This is Sailun's world-first "chemical compounding" technology, which breaks through the tire industry's "devil's triangle" — rolling resistance, wet grip, and wear resistance, which typically can only be optimized two at a time. Liquid Gold technology optimizes all three simultaneously. It has passed the world's strictest TUV MARK certification, with four Sailun tire models certified at once — **the best result in the history of Chinese tire companies** (source: Xinhua). In Q1 2025, replacement-market sales of Liquid Gold tires grew **50%** year over year, and the tires are now fitted on new-energy vehicle models from BYD, Geely, and others.

**Second, off-the-road (OTR) tires.** This is Sailun's most profitable segment, with an estimated gross margin of **30%+** (source: derived from research notes). It has high technical barriers and is already supplied to top global construction-equipment brands like Caterpillar and John Deere, and has entered the supply chain of mining giants such as Rio Tinto. Sailun produced its first all-steel radial giant tire in 2009, and rolled out the world's largest 53/80R63 giant tire in 2016.

**Counterpoint**: Consumer awareness of Liquid Gold technology remains weak — the vast majority of ordinary drivers don't proactively choose "Liquid Gold" when replacing tires, and brand recognition falls far short of Michelin's "quiet ride" or Pirelli's "performance" positioning. Technical leadership also needs continued validation, as other companies may catch up.

### Perception three: "Building factories overseas is just about cheap labor"

Not entirely true. The core logic behind Sailun's overseas factories is not labor cost — it's **tariff avoidance**.

The US currently levies a combined tariff rate of about **270%** on tires exported directly from China (source: China Trade Remedy Information website), which essentially blocks the path for tires made in China to be exported to the US. But tires exported from Vietnam to the US face a combined rate of about **45%**; tires exported from Cambodia to the EU can enjoy EBA tariff reductions.

Sailun's global capacity footprint is as follows:

| Base | Location | Main products | Status |
|------|------|---------|------|
| Vietnam plant | Ho Chi Minh City | 16 million semi-steel tires + 2.6 million all-steel tires + 100,000 tons of OTR tires | **Running at full capacity** |
| Cambodia plant | Sihanoukville | Semi-steel + all-steel + OTR tires, large combined capacity | **Running at full capacity** |
| Mexico plant | — | 6 million semi-steel tires | First tire produced in 2025, **ramping up** |
| Indonesia plant | — | 6 million semi-steel tires + 750,000 all-steel tires + 10,000 tons of OTR tires | First tire produced in 2025, **ramping up** |
| Egypt plant | — | Planned | Production planned to start in 2026 |

*Data source: Sailun Group official website, public disclosures, industry news*

**Five bases on five continents** — this is not simply "going to Vietnam for cheap labor." It's a global chess game laid out a decade in advance.

**Counterpoint**: The US has already started levying anti-dumping duties on tires from Southeast Asian countries. 2025 data shows the potential combined tariff rate on tires exported to the US has reached **61%** from Thailand and Cambodia, **45%** from Vietnam, and **57%** from Indonesia (source: public industry data). If the US continues to "chase down" Southeast Asian capacity, Sailun's tariff-arbitrage logic will be severely undermined. More importantly, Cambodia already has **11** Chinese-invested tire plants (source: research notes) — "overseas overcrowding" has already begun.

## A timeline worth remembering

| Year | Event | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| 2002 | Sailun founded | Starting point |
| 2009 | First all-steel giant tire produced | Entered the off-the-road tire segment |
| **2012** | **Vietnam plant construction begins** | **First Chinese tire company to build a factory overseas** |
| 2015 | US "double anti" (anti-dumping/anti-subsidy) rulings on Chinese tires | Sailun's Vietnam capacity had already avoided the impact |
| 2021 | Liquid Gold tires launched globally | Technology differentiation begins to pay off |
| 2023 | Gross margin jumps to 27.6%, net profit reaches 3.1 billion yuan | Year of profit surge |
| 2024 | Revenue 31.8 billion yuan, net profit 4.06 billion yuan (all-time high) | Peak |
| 2025 | Revenue 36.8 billion yuan (+16%), net profit 3.52 billion yuan (-13%) | Revenue up, profit down |
| 2025 | First tires produced at Mexico and Indonesia plants | Fourth and fifth global bases go into production |
| 2026 Q1 | Gross margin recovers to 26.86% | Signal of easing near-term pressure |

Of this timeline, **the 2012 decision to build the Vietnam plant** is the most pivotal decision in Sailun's history. That a tire-company chairman foresaw, a decade in advance, that global trade protectionism would rise — this strategic foresight is Sailun's most valuable intangible asset.

**Counterpoint**: Yuan Zhongxue is now **71 years old** and remains Sailun's guiding spirit and actual controller. Good strategic foresight cannot be passed down through generations automatically — **a successor has not yet been proven**.
