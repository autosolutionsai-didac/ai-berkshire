**Key observations**:
- Revenue growth has been extremely stable, more than doubling over 5 years (from 18 billion to 36.8 billion yuan) at a 19.6% CAGR, driven mainly by **overseas capacity expansion that lifted both volume and price**.
- Net profit has swung far more than revenue: profit stagnated in 2021-22, exploded in 2023-24, and pulled back in 2025. This pattern reveals an important characteristic: **Sailun is a classic cyclical growth stock** — the top line grows structurally, while profit is buffered by raw-material and trade-policy cycles.

### 1.2 Analysis of the 2023 margin jump (gross margin 18% → 28%)

2023 was Sailun's "miracle year": gross margin leapt from 18.42% to 27.64%, net margin surged from 6.52% to 12.33%, and net profit grew 132%.

**Breakdown of drivers**:

| Factor | Estimated contribution | Source of evidence |
|------|:----------:|---------|
| Falling raw-material costs (natural rubber, carbon black) | ~60% | The average 2023 tire raw-material price index fell 6.04% year-over-year (per Southwest Securities data) |
| Product mix upgrade (Liquid Gold tires, large-size PCR) | ~20% | The company's Liquid Gold tire ramped up in 2023; brand value crossed 100 billion yuan |
| Overseas plant capacity release (Vietnam phase III, Cambodia ramp-up) | ~15% | Tire output grew 27.6% in 2023; overseas plants carry higher gross margin than domestic plants |
| Falling ocean freight costs | ~5% | The 2023 SCFI composite index declined year-over-year, and export freight rates normalized |

> **Buffett's view**: Profit growth from falling raw-material costs is a "windfall" — it is not sustainable and should not be extrapolated linearly. But the product-mix upgrade and overseas capacity release are real, moat-widening improvements.

**Counter-argument**: Stripping out the raw-material cost decline, the company's true "organic" gross margin in 2023 may have been closer to 22-23% (opinion). That means the 27.58% gross margin held in 2024 actually suggests the product-upgrade contribution is genuinely being realized.

### 1.3 Analysis of the 2025 profit decline (net profit -13.3%)

In 2025 revenue kept growing, up 15.7% to 36.792 billion yuan, but net profit fell 13.3% to 3.522 billion yuan. **This is a classic "revenue up, profit down" pattern.**

**Cause breakdown**:

| Factor | Impact | Data evidence |
|------|------|---------|
| Rebound in raw-material costs | Gross margin down 2.9 pct | H1 2025 combined purchase price of the four main materials rose 0.82% YoY; Q1 rose 4.51% YoY |
| Escalating trade friction | Compressed export margins | The US expanded the scope of tariffs; the EU launched an anti-dumping investigation into Chinese tires |
| Ocean freight disruption | Higher export costs | Freight rates on some routes fluctuated amid the Red Sea crisis |
| Pressure on average selling price | Unit price edged down YoY | Q1 2025 average price of self-produced, self-sold tires was -0.40% YoY, affected by product-mix shifts |
| Overseas plants ramping up | Higher upfront depreciation | Mexico and Indonesia plants produced their first tires in 2025; construction-in-progress transferred to fixed assets, pushing up depreciation |

**Quarterly breakdown**:

| Quarter | Net profit (billion yuan) | YoY | Gross margin |
|------|:-----------:|------|:------:|
| Q1 2025 | 1.039 | +0.5% | 24.74% |
| Q2 2025 | 0.792 | -29.1% | 24.35% |
| Q3 2025 | ~1.041 (estimate) | ~-11.5% (estimate) | 24.73% (average of first three quarters) |
| Q4 2025 | ~0.650 (estimate) | ~-20.6% (estimate) | — (full-year 24.68%) |

**Key judgment**: The core driver of the 2025 profit decline is **a year-over-year rise in raw-material costs plus escalating trade barriers**, not deteriorating competitiveness. Revenue was still growing 15%+, indicating market share continued to expand. On the other hand, Q4 2025 net profit was only about 0.65 billion yuan (versus 2.872 billion yuan for Q1-Q3, 3.522 billion yuan for the full year), a notably weaker single-quarter profitability that warrants tracking the Q1 2026 recovery.

> **Q1 2026 data update**: revenue of 9.461 billion yuan (+12.5%), net profit of 1.056 billion yuan (+1.7%), gross margin of 26.86% — gross margin has recovered markedly, up 2.2 pct quarter-over-quarter and 2.1 pct year-over-year. This is a positive signal suggesting the H2 2025 margin decline may be transitory rather than structural.

**Core facts revealed by the DuPont breakdown**:

1. **Net margin is the primary driver of ROE volatility.** The 2021→2023 ROE surge (13.3% → 23.4%) was driven entirely by net margin (7.5% → 12.3%).
2. **Asset turnover has been improving** (0.80 → 0.85-0.89), reflecting rising capacity utilization and overseas plants coming online. This is a structural improvement.
3. **Leverage has been steadily declining** (2.35 → 2.00), with the debt ratio falling from 57.5% to 50.1%. This means the company is growing while simultaneously reducing financial risk — exactly the pattern Buffett favors.

> **Buffett's standard**: Buffett favors companies with high ROE (>15%) and low leverage (equity multiplier <2.5). Sailun's 2023-24 ROE of 23% combined with steadily falling leverage meets Buffett's standard. The 2025 ROE of 17%, though lower, came alongside further deleveraging, so quality has not deteriorated.

**Counter-argument**: Q1 2026 ROE was only 4.80% (annualized to roughly 19%); if raw-material prices rise again or trade friction escalates further, full-year 2026 ROE may still land in the 15-18% range rather than returning to 20%+.

### 2.2 Gross margin trend and drivers

```
2021: 18.87%  baseline
2022: 18.42%  holding steady
2023: 27.64%  ← lower raw-material costs + product upgrade + overseas ramp-up
2024: 27.58%  ← holding at elevated level (raw materials still cheap)
2025: 24.68%  ← raw-material rebound + trade friction
Q1 2026: 26.86% ← recovering (up 2.2 pct QoQ)
```

**Structural drivers of gross margin (long-term variables)**:
- **Liquid Gold tires**: an original technology that breaks the "devil's triangle" trade-off, lifting product-premium capability. This is a genuine product-strength moat.
- **Rising share of overseas capacity**: plants in Vietnam, Cambodia and elsewhere provide a dual advantage of cost (proximity to natural-rubber sources) and tariff avoidance; overseas-plant gross margin typically runs 3-5 pct above domestic.
- **Rising brand value**: ranked 10th among global tire brands by Brand Finance in 2025, with brand value of $905 million (+13% YoY).

**Cyclical drivers of gross margin (short-term variables)**:
- Natural rubber prices (30-40% of raw-material cost)
- Carbon black prices (15-20% of raw-material cost)
- Ocean freight costs
- Tariff/anti-dumping duty rates

**Core judgment**: Sailun's long-run gross-margin center of gravity is likely in the 24-26% range — above the 18% of 2021-22 (reflecting the product upgrade) but below the 28% of 2023-24 (which reflected raw materials at a cyclical low). The 26.86% recorded in Q1 2026 corroborates this judgment nicely.

### 4.2 Liquidity and short-term solvency

**Current ratio 1.13, quick ratio 0.66 — is that too tight?**

An objective analysis:
- Both ratios are indeed below the conventional "safety line" (current ratio of 2.0, quick ratio of 1.0).
- But the tire industry is **asset-heavy manufacturing**, where high inventory and receivables levels are an industry-wide feature. Peer Triangle Tire's current ratio runs about 1.5-1.8, Linglong's about 1.2-1.4 — the industry as a whole runs low.
- The **structural reason** for Sailun's low current ratio: notes payable and accounts payable (interest-free liabilities) make up a large share of current liabilities — meaning the company is using supplier financing, a reflection of operating leverage in its favor.
- **Cash on hand hit a new high of 5.964 billion yuan in Q1 2026**, ample to cover short-term financial borrowings.

> **Judgment**: Sailun's tight liquidity is a fact, but weighed against the industry's characteristics and its cash reserves, short-term solvency risk is manageable. The real risk isn't liquidity itself, but whether refinancing capacity would hold up if banks tightened credit during an industry downturn.

## VI. Margin-of-Safety Assessment

### 6.1 Buffett's Five Questions

**Question 1: Do I understand this business?**

Sailun Tire's business model is straightforward — R&D, production, and sale of tires. The competitive drivers in the industry are clear (cost, brand, channel, technology). Global manufacturing footprint is the core strategy for coping with trade barriers. **Answer: largely understandable, but requires ongoing tracking of trade-policy changes.**

**Question 2: Does it have a durable competitive advantage (moat)?**

- **Brand moat** (moderate): ranked 10th globally by Brand Finance, first in China, but still an order of magnitude behind Michelin/Bridgestone.
- **Cost moat** (fairly strong): the overseas manufacturing footprint (Vietnam, Cambodia, Indonesia, Mexico) provides a dual advantage of tariff avoidance and low-cost production. Sailun has the largest overseas capacity footprint among Chinese tire makers.
- **Technology moat** (moderate): Liquid Gold tires represent differentiated technology, certified by TÜV, but the tire industry overall moves slowly on technological change.
- **Scale moat** (fairly strong): annual revenue of 36.8 billion yuan, second in China's tire industry, top ten globally.

**Judgment**: Sailun's moat is **real and widening**, but the tire industry's moats overall are not as deep as those in consumer goods. Its global manufacturing footprint is currently its most solid moat.

**Question 3: Is management honest and capable?**

*(This question is covered in depth by the /management-deep-dive skill; observations here are limited to a financial perspective.)*

- The company's strategic execution has been strong: it entered Vietnam in 2012 (the first in the industry to do so), started Cambodian production in 2022, and produced its first tires in Mexico/Indonesia in 2025 — its globalization strategy has been **forward-looking and resolute**.
- Capital-allocation discipline: the debt ratio has fallen steadily (from 57.5% in 2021 to 50.1% in 2025), brand investment has kept rising, and R&D spending has been stable (roughly 840 million yuan in 2024).
- A point to watch: accounts receivable jumped 50% to 5.086 billion yuan in 2024 (outpacing the 22.4% revenue growth rate), and it needs confirming whether this reflects normal matching to channel expansion or a deterioration in collections management.

**Question 4: Does the price offer a margin of safety?**

At the current price of 13.49 yuan:
- PE of 12.5x, implying an 8.0% earnings yield
- Base-case 3-year target price of 16.3 yuan, an annualized return of 6.5%
- Bear-case downside of -27.9% (zero growth plus valuation compression to a 9x PE)

**Judgment**: the current price sits in a "reasonably undervalued" range. There is no enormous margin of safety (a Graham-style deep discount), but given the growth outlook it **offers reasonable investment value**. If the market falls further into the bear scenario, below 10 yuan, that would be a materially attractive buying opportunity.

**Question 5: If the market closed for five years, would I be willing to buy at this price?**

- Revenue CAGR of 10% → 36.8 × 1.1^5 ≈ 59.3 billion yuan
