## I. Probability Analysis of the Five Outcomes

### Outcome A: Alibaba exits completely, retreats to Ele.me + core e-commerce (Probability: 5%)

**Trigger conditions**:
1. Joseph Tsai / Eddie Wu changes leadership, and Jiang Fan's "three-year instant-retail strategy" is overturned
2. If Alibaba's FY27 (calendar year 2026) instant-retail losses exceed 150 billion yuan, the board calls a halt
3. Alibaba's Hong Kong shares fall below HKD 80 (currently around HKD 130), and shareholders apply pressure
4. A new round of U.S. sanctions on Chinese ADRs forces Alibaba to retrench from non-core businesses

**Why the probability is low (5%)**:
- At an internal meeting in January 2026, Jiang Fan just stated that the company "won't worry about losses for three years" — exiting would be a direct contradiction
- In Q4 2025, Taobao Shanguo's instant-retail GTV share reached **45.2%, overtaking Meituan by 0.2 pct** — the first time in seven years Alibaba has scored a win in local services, and **nobody retreats right after winning**
- Instant retail's pull on Taobao's main app DAU is structural (Taobao DAU is 457 million, +5.1% YoY); **exiting would mean bleeding out on the main battlefield**
- Alibaba holds cash of 450 billion+ yuan, enough to sustain 3-5 years of cash burn

**Impact if a real exit happens (in the unlikely event)**:
- Meituan's share price would instantly rebound to HKD 150-180 (+80%)
- JD.com would be left isolated, either taking over Ele.me or retreating itself
- The instant-retail industry would revert to a single-dominant-player structure (Meituan alone at the top)

**Assessment**: A low-probability black swan, but if it happens, it is the biggest positive catalyst for Meituan.

### Outcome B: JD.com exits completely, refocuses on 3C electronics + logistics (Probability: 15%)

**Trigger conditions**:
1. If JD.com's food-delivery losses for full-year 2026 still exceed 20 billion yuan, the board calls a halt after the Q1 2027 earnings report
2. JD.com's Hong Kong shares fall below HKD 100, and institutional investors collectively object
3. Richard Liu's "retail person" narrative is rejected by capital markets (PE falls below 7x)
4. Meituan and Alibaba join forces in a "counter-encirclement," and JD.com's food-delivery share is never able to break through 10%

**Why this probability is higher than A's (15%)**:

- JD.com CEO Sandy Xu has stated that "food-delivery investment in 2026 will be lower than in 2025" — **a prelude to retreat**
- JD.com's new businesses lost 46.6 billion yuan in 2025, severely dragging down the group's overall new-business profit margin
- JD.com's core e-commerce business is being squeezed from both ends by Pinduoduo (lower-tier markets) and Douyin (livestream commerce), **so the core business itself needs resources redeployed for defense**
- The cost structure of full-time riders plus full social-insurance contributions means "heavy operations, low margins" — financially unsustainable

**Why it isn't a higher probability**:
- Richard Liu's personal will is strong, and "delivering food himself" has become part of his personal brand
- The synergy narrative among JD Daojia, JD Logistics, and 7Fresh (the "three-kilometer life circle") requires a food-delivery entry point
- A graceful retrenchment is more politically acceptable than "announcing an exit"; JD.com is more likely to settle into a "low-investment, 5%-share" tasteless-but-tolerable state

**Possible paths for a JD.com exit/retrenchment**:
1. **Path 1 (probability 50%)**: Keep the food-delivery business but **downgrade it to a defensive posture** (5-8% share, losses cut to 10 billion yuan/year), abandoning the 30% target
2. **Path 2 (probability 30%)**: **Fold the food-delivery business into JD Daojia**, merging it with JD Logistics into an "instant delivery" business unit, downplaying the "food delivery" label
3. **Path 3 (probability 15%)**: **Acquire a smaller player** (e.g., SF Intra-city, or take 100% control of Dada Group), buying time through M&A
4. **Path 4 (probability 5%)**: **Exit entirely**, announcing the closure of the food-delivery business

- JD.com itself: share price under pressure short-term ("admitting failure"), but rebounds medium-to-long term (losses are shed)

**Key timeline milestone**: **JD.com's Q1 2027 annual report** — if food delivery still loses 20 billion+ yuan in 2026, board intervention is nearly certain.

### Outcome C: A three-way truce, reaching an implicit "no more burning subsidies" understanding (Probability: 40%)

**Why this is the highest-probability outcome**:

**Verification from historical precedent**:
1. **Didi vs. Uber China (2014-2016)**: The subsidy war burned for two years, ultimately ending with Didi absorbing Uber China at a $35 billion valuation (Uber held 5.89% of Didi). **Truce mechanism = equity swap + exit**
2. **Mobike vs. ofo vs. Hellobike (2016-2018)**: Mobike was sold to Meituan for $2.7 billion, ofo collapsed, and Hellobike absorbed the remaining market. **Truce mechanism = industry consolidation**
3. **The "thousand-group war" (2010-2014)**: Of more than 5,000 group-buying sites, only Meituan and Dianping remained, merging in 2015. **Truce mechanism = leader merger + long-tail extinction**
4. **Meituan vs. Didi food delivery (2018)**: Didi's food-delivery arm voluntarily retrenched within a year, with no explicit "truce agreement" but a de facto ceasefire. **Truce mechanism = the weaker party quietly retreats**

**How the current food-delivery war differs from history**:
- All three parties are giants, and **none can be "absorbed"** (unlike smaller players such as ofo or Didi's food-delivery unit)
- Regulators won't permit large-scale M&A (see the precedent of Alibaba's 18.2 billion yuan antitrust fine)
- **The truce can only take the form of an "implicit understanding" — each party stops predatory subsidies and drops its "absolute No. 1" slogans**

**Trigger conditions**:
1. Regulators keep signaling (already happening: the March 25, 2026 repost of "The Food Delivery War Should End")
2. All three parties disclose a "loss-narrowing inflection point" (Meituan's Q4 losses already narrowed 29%; Alibaba's CFO has hinted at "peak investment")
3. Senior executives from the three companies (Wang Xing / Joseph Tsai / Sandy Xu) meet privately (an industry norm)
4. Industry associations (China Cuisine Association, restaurant merchant representatives) publicly call for a ceasefire

**Specifics of the truce (projected)**:

| Dimension | Post-truce state |
|---|---|
| Coupon face value | Maximum subsidy per order capped at 30% of order value (vs. 50-100% currently) |
| Subsidy form | Primarily shifted to merchants (platforms move from cash discounts to traffic allocation); platform subsidy share falls below 10% |
| Take rate | The three converge to 8-12% (Meituan currently at 8%, JD.com at 5%, Alibaba in the 8% range) |
| Rider costs | Progressive inclusion in social insurance; the three form a "rider charter" |
| Share targets | None of the three publicly claims "absolute No. 1" anymore; public share competition is downplayed |

**The landscape after the truce**:
- Meituan: share stabilizes at 50-55%; CLB operating profit recovers to 35-45 billion yuan (operating margin 12-15%)
- Alibaba: share stabilizes at 35-40%; instant-retail EBITA turns positive in 2027
- JD.com: share shrinks to 5-10%; the food-delivery business becomes a "tasteless but tolerable" fixture

**The biggest obstacles to a truce**:
- Jiang Fan's "absolute No. 1" pledge has not been retracted; a public truce would mean losing face
- Meituan "holds a grudge" over Alibaba's cumulative historical losses of 150 billion yuan (Wang Puzhong: "Losing three hands of Texas hold'em and still buying more chips") — psychologically unwilling to "make peace"
- JD.com could be the "spoiler" — Richard Liu's personal will is strong and he may refuse to go along with a truce

**The most likely window for a truce**: **July-September 2026** (around the Q2 earnings disclosures, once the scale of losses at all three companies is clear)

**The impact of regulatory intervention**:

**The relationship between regulation and a truce**:

- **The more likely combination**: the truce happens first (2026 Q3-Q4), with regulatory rules arriving in 2027 as a "backstop"
- **Base-case assumption**: Truce (C) + regulatory backstop (D) = a combined 65% probability

| **Mobike vs. ofo vs. Hellobike** | 2016-2018 | Combined 10 billion+ yuan burned across the three | **Mobike sold to Meituan for $2.7 billion; ofo collapsed in a deposit run; Hellobike (backed by Alibaba) came out on top** | 2 years | **Players without Alibaba or Tencent backing die**; in the food-delivery war, JD.com lacks the boost from Tencent's traffic ecosystem, making it the most exposed |

| **Meituan vs. Alibaba's Koubei (2018-2020)** | 2018-2020 | Alibaba's Koubei plus Ele.me's Fengniao invested 20 billion+ yuan | **Meituan held its share steady**; Alibaba's Koubei business was marginalized, then reorganized as the Local Services Group after Jiang Fan took over | 3 years | **Historically, "Alibaba has fought in local services three times and never won"** — but this time Taobao Shanguo has overtaken Meituan by 0.2 pct for the first time, indicating a structural shift (the free traffic entry point from Taobao's main app) |

### Three Core Lessons from the Historical Review

**Lesson 1: All subsidy wars end, typically within 1-5 years**
- Didi vs. Uber: 2 years
- Mobike vs. ofo: 2 years
- The thousand-group war: 4 years
- Meituan vs. Ele.me: 4 years
- **The food-delivery war has already run 2 years** (2025-2026); based on historical patterns, 1-3 years likely remain

**Lesson 2: The key to winning isn't money — it's the fit with the core business**
- Didi: ride-hailing was its core business, and it won
- Meituan: food delivery is its core business, and it won
- JD.com's food delivery: 3C e-commerce is its core business, and food delivery is a new venture — a **structural disadvantage**
- Alibaba's Taobao Shanguo: e-commerce is its core business, but instant retail is a "quasi-core" business (capable of contributing trillions of yuan in incremental GMV) — **a stronger position than JD.com's**

**Lesson 3: Regulators always intervene in the final stage of a major subsidy war**
- 2014-2016, Didi vs. Uber: the Ministry of Transport stepped in with "new ride-hailing regulations"
- 2016-2018, shared bikes: local governments in many cities capped deployment quotas
- 2020-2021, community group buying: SAMR issued its "nine prohibitions," halting "group-leader incentives"
- 2026, the food-delivery war: SAMR has already reposted "The Food Delivery War Should End"

**Conclusion: For this round of the food-delivery war, the question is no longer "whether" it will end, but "how" and "when."**

| **H2 2027** | **Detailed rules capping the subsidy ratio per order** | 40% probability |

### Three Dimensions of Regulation

**Dimension 1: Subsidy cap rules**
- Modeled on the 2020 community-group-buying "nine prohibitions" template
- Possible content: "Platform subsidies per order must not exceed 30% of the product's price"
- Impact: Alibaba's "100% subsidy" model would be banned, shifting the burden back to merchants

**Dimension 2: Determining below-cost sales**
- Article 14 of the Price Law already bans "dumping at below-cost prices to squeeze out competitors"
- The amendment will clarify "how cost is determined within the platform economy"
- Impact: extreme subsidies such as Alibaba's "12 bottles of water for 1 yuan" promotion would be ruled unlawful

**Dimension 3: Transparency in algorithmic pricing**
- The draft amendment adds provisions "targeting algorithmic pricing and data-driven issues"
- Impact: differentiated pricing and "big-data price discrimination" against loyal users would be tightly controlled

### The "Degree" of Regulation

Regulation isn't meant to eliminate competition — it's meant to eliminate "irrational competition." **The most likely regulatory path is "soft constraints running in parallel with hard constraints"**:
- Soft constraints: summoning executives for talks, signaling, industry self-regulation charters (already happening)
- Hard constraints: Price Law amendment, interpretation of the Anti-Unfair Competition Law (likely to take effect in 2027)

**Net effect on Meituan**: mildly positive (constrains Alibaba's "at any cost" strategy)
**Net effect on Alibaba**: negative short term (Jiang Fan's pledge is interrupted), neutral long term (an "off-ramp" via "responding to regulation")
**Net effect on JD.com**: neutral (a low 5% take rate is itself politically correct, but share will struggle to rise further)

## IX. Conclusion

The food-delivery war is not a zero-sum game of "Meituan vs. Alibaba vs. JD.com" — it is **a necessary stage in the entire instant-retail industry's transition from "wild growth" to "regulated maturity."**

Wang Puzhong described three possible outcomes:
1. **Absorption** — already ruled out by antitrust constraints and the parity in strength among the giants
2. **A standoff followed by a truce** — the 65%-probability base case
3. **Intervention by a higher-level force** — already happened (regulators setting the tone on March 25, 2026)

**The biggest winner is the consumer**: educated by the subsidy war, instant-retail penetration has risen from 5% to 15%+, and 30-minute food delivery plus errand-running has become a way of life.
**The biggest loser is the merchant**: subsidies have broken consumers' price expectations, and dine-in traffic at full-service restaurant brands has been eroded by delivery, requiring 2-3 years to rebuild.
**The biggest winner is Meituan**: defending its moat, recovering profits, and building new growth curves — once again, a case of "nine losses, one win."
**The biggest loser may be JD.com**: with the highest certainty of a strategic failure, requiring 2-3 years to digest.

**Duan Yongping's investment maxim resurfaces**: "Doing the right thing matters more than doing things right." Meituan chose the path of "being drawn in passively but fighting back rationally" — precisely the best demonstration of doing the right thing right.
