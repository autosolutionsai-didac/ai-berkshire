# Token Relay Station Industry Deep Dive: Business Model, Profit Margins, and the Fight for Survival

> **Report date**: 2026-05-04
> **Research subject**: Third-party providers that resell/aggregate API access to large models such as OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek
> **Research dimensions**: Business model / profit margins / 6-month-1-year-3-year outlook / differentiated survival paths
> **Important disclaimer**: There is almost no credible third-party audit of domestic relay stations' transaction volume, user counts, or absconding amounts. Any figure in this report labeled "estimate" is a community-derived order-of-magnitude guess.

---

## I. Executive Summary

| Key judgment | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| **Is it a good business?** | A short-term windfall, long-term dead end — **gross margin of 20-50% but effective net margin of only 10-25%**, and the window won't last more than 3 years |
| **Who are the real customers?** | Cursor/Cline pass-through users (35%) + individual hobbyists (30%) + small-business PoC users (25%) + gray/black-market use cases (10%) |
| **6 months** | Prices keep getting squeezed; 2-3 leading players consolidate their position; the long tail keeps absconding with funds |
| **1 year** | Domestic compliance bifurcates; pure resale operators die off by more than half; those that pivot to an LLM Gateway model survive |
| **3 years** | Relay stations largely disappear as a standalone business; survivors upgrade into an "AI infrastructure layer" |
| **Key to differentiated survival** | Compliance + intelligent multi-model routing + enterprise-grade SLAs + observability + self-hosted inference |

**One-line framing**: **The token relay station is a textbook "window-of-opportunity business" — at its core, it arbitrages the double mismatch of "model vendors not entering China" and "users being sensitive to cost/speed." But three forces — model prices falling 5-10x per year, big platforms building their own overseas nodes, and the rise of domestic models — are strangling this ecosystem. Whatever survives 3 years from now will no longer be a "relay station" but an "AI Gateway."**

**The primary use case for domestic developers**: plugging a relay station's API key into IDEs such as Cursor, Cline, and Continue. This category of user **contributes roughly 35% of domestic relay stations' transaction volume** — they are not price-sensitive (spending tens to a few hundred yuan per month) but are sensitive to stability.

## III. Business Model Breakdown

### 3.1 Five profit models

| Model | Representative players | Source of gross profit | True gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Markup resale** | API2D, CloseAI | Buy at X, sell retail at 1.0-1.3X | 20-30% |
| **Top-up rebate lock-in** | laozhang.ai | "Top up 100, get 20 free" locks in prepaid funds; float earns interest + funds an absconding reserve | 30-50% (including float income) |
| **Payment-channel fee** | OpenRouter | Almost no markup; relies on a 5.5% top-up processing fee | 5-10% |
| **Enterprise subscription** | Portkey, Kong | $49-999/month SaaS fee + enterprise license | 60-80% (typical SaaS) |
| **Self-hosted inference + resale** | Together AI, Fireworks | Runs open-source models itself at costs far below vendor pricing | 40-60% |

### 3.2 Domestic gray-market sites' "float" is the real profit source

```
User tops up ¥10,000
├─ Immediately deducted as API procurement cost: ¥0 (nothing is deducted until the user actually uses it)
├─ Sitting in the account as float: ¥10,000 (the platform can earn interest on it, divert it, or "abscond" with it)
└─ Actual consumption cycle: 3-12 months

→ This "unconsumed balance" is estimated industry-wide at 40-60% of total balances
→ This is the true "cash cow" of domestic relay stations
→ It's also the root cause of the absconding wave — the more cash sitting in the account, the stronger the temptation to run
```

### 3.3 OpenRouter's "anti-markup" approach turns out to be the most stable model

**OpenRouter barely marks up tokens at all** (for some open-source models it's even cheaper thanks to wholesale pricing), and instead profits from a **5.5% credit-card/crypto top-up processing fee**.
- Advantage: users feel no psychological burden of "being marked up," which lets the platform scale quickly
- Data: monthly volume of $1-2M in early 2024 → monthly volume of $10M+ by Q4 2024 → cumulative tokens surpassed 1 trillion in 2025
- **Series A of $40M (a16z + Menlo Ventures), valuation of $500M** (2025-05)
- **Essence**: positioning itself as "the Visa for AI models" — a payment channel, not a middleman

## IV. Profit Margins and Cost Structure

### 4.1 A typical P&L for domestic gray-market sites (based on a mid-sized site with ¥10 million in annual volume)

| Item | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| API procurement cost | **60-80%** | The single largest expense |
| Servers/bandwidth/overseas circumvention lines | 5-15% | Cost of firewall-circumvention infrastructure |
| Payment channels (including AML compliance) | 3-8% | Risk fees on WeChat Pay/Alipay/USDT collections |
| Customer support | 5% | Very heavy — the community group can blow up at any time |
| **Bad debt/account-ban losses** | **5-15%** | OpenAI/Anthropic anti-abuse bans |
| **Absconding-risk reserve** | 5-10% | Self-retained buffer |
| **True net margin** | **10-25%** | Far below the headline gross margin |

### 4.2 Net margin comparison across three types of sites

| Type | Gross margin | Net margin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenRouter (compliant) | 5-10% | 3-7% | Relies on scale |
| Domestic gray-market leaders | 30-50% | 15-25% | Includes returns from float income |
| Domestic gray-market long tail | 20-30% | -10% ~ 10% | One ban wave and it's bankrupt |
| Enterprise Gateway (Portkey) | 70-80% | 20-30% | Typical SaaS |
| Self-hosted inference (Together AI) | 40-60% | 15-25% | High capital expenditure |

### 4.3 Profit margins are being squeezed by "three forces"

```
Force 1: Model API prices fall 5-10x per year
  GPT-4 Turbo $10 → GPT-4o $2.5 → GPT-4o-mini $0.15 (a 67x drop)
  Claude 3 Opus $15 → Sonnet 4 $3 (a 5x drop)
  → Markup room keeps shrinking

Force 2: Domestic model APIs are extremely cheap, leaving no margin for relays
  DeepSeek V3 at $0.27/M tokens, half-price at night at $0.135
  Qwen and Kimi are both waging price wars
  → Reselling domestic models is basically margin-free

Force 3: Model vendors are proactively fighting abuse and banning accounts
  OpenAI has run large-scale bans on Chinese IPs since June 2024
  Anthropic outright banned Chinese-controlled entities from using the Claude API in 2025
  → This raises both bad-debt costs and operating costs
```

## V. Key Events and Risks

### 5.1 An absconding case study: openai-sb (2024)

**The most notorious absconding incident in the domestic community**:
- Suddenly went offline in early 2024
- Users' unredeemed balances reportedly **exceeded RMB 10 million**
- The community group was disbanded outright
- This triggered a chain-reaction "crisis of trust," with a string of second- and third-tier sites absconding one after another

**Mechanism**: the "float" accumulated through top-up rebates keeps piling up in the account → the operator gets tempted → runs

### 5.2 Shifts in big vendors' stance

| Vendor | Stance | Impact on relay stations |
|---|---|---|
| **OpenAI** | Explicitly stays out of China but tolerates relays (a go-to-market lever) | Neutral-to-positive |
| **Anthropic** | Actively cut ties with China in 2025 | **Beneficial** (expands gray-market demand) |
| **Google Gemini** | Not available in China; stance ambiguous | Neutral |
| **DeepSeek/Qwen/Kimi** | Their own APIs are extremely cheap + they're building their own overseas nodes | **Bypasses relays** |

### 5.3 Regulatory risk

- Domestically, "unregistered calls to overseas large models" sit in a gray zone, but **enforcement is lax**
- The Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) requires domestic models to go through "generative AI service filing," but **there is no clear closure of the loophole for relaying overseas models**
- **Prediction**: clear enforcement will emerge within 12-24 months, and long-tail sites will be cleared out

## VI. 6-Month / 1-Year / 3-Year Outlook

### 6.1 Six months (end of 2026)

```
✓ 2-3 domestic leaders (at the laozhang/API2D scale) hold their position
✓ Cursor traffic keeps feeding relay stations (Cursor itself is raising prices)
✗ The long tail keeps absconding (1-2 small incidents per month)
✗ Anthropic's cutoff pushes up Claude relay prices
↑ Relaying domestic models becomes completely unprofitable and is abandoned entirely
↓ OpenRouter's monthly volume may pass $30M (annualizing to $360M)
```

### 6.2 One year (mid-2027)

```
✓ A clear compliance window emerges domestically (modeled on the "online game publishing filing" approach)
✓ Leading players attempt to pivot to an enterprise AI Gateway, with some success
✗ More than 50% of long-tail sites die off
✗ Model prices fall another 50%, pushing pure-markup profits toward zero
↑ IDEs such as Cursor and Cline start building in their own LLM Gateway, bypassing third parties
↓ Individual "carpool" sharing plans are essentially extinct
```

### 6.3 Three years (2029)

```
Relay stations essentially disappear as a "standalone business":

✗ Dead: pure resale operators, the long tail, individual carpool sharing, gray-market sites
✓ Survive and grow big:
  ├─ OpenRouter evolves into an AI infrastructure layer (akin to Stripe for payments)
  ├─ Portkey/Helicone integrate observability + cost management + RAG
  ├─ Together AI/Fireworks convert entirely to self-hosted inference
  ├─ 1-2 domestic leaders pivot to a dual track of "domestic compliant AI Gateway + overseas model proxy"
  └─ Kong AI Gateway and similar players get acquired and absorbed by companies like Cisco/IBM
```

**Whatever survives 3 years from now will not be a "relay station" but an "AI Gateway" or "AI inference platform."** This is a qualitative change, not a quantitative one.

## VII. Five Differentiated Survival Paths

### 7.1 Path A: Compliance + enterprise-grade (hardest but most valuable)

**Approach**:
- Obtain proper licenses (in China: CAICT [China Academy of Information and Communications Technology] certification + a Value-Added Telecommunications license + an Internet Culture Business Permit)
- Provide corporate invoices
- Private deployment, SLAs, SOC 2 audits
- A dual structure of an overseas entity plus a domestic agent

**Target customers**: A-share listed companies, central/state-owned enterprises, financial institutions
**Gross margin potential**: 50-70%
**Comparable benchmarks**: APIPark, Kong AI Gateway

### 7.2 Path B: Intelligent routing (the OpenRouter model)

**Approach**:
- Aggregate 100+ models and automatically route each task to the cheapest model that also meets performance requirements
- Provide fallback, load balancing, and caching
- No markup on tokens; profit comes from processing fees plus value-added services

**Target customers**: global developers, AI agent developers
**Gross margin potential**: 5-10% (thin but at large scale)
**Comparable benchmark**: OpenRouter

### 7.3 Path C: Vertical-industry packaging

**Approach**:
- Package pre-built prompts + RAG + tools for a specific vertical such as education, legal, healthcare, or customer service
- The customer isn't buying tokens, they're buying a "solution"

**Target customers**: vertical SaaS companies, content-generation companies
**Gross margin potential**: 60-80%
**Comparable benchmarks**: Cursor (for coding), Bizon AI (for customer service)

### 7.4 Path D: Observability + LLMOps

**Approach**:
- Don't buy tokens on the customer's behalf (the customer tops up directly with the model vendor)
- Provide cost monitoring, prompt version management, PII redaction, and performance monitoring
- SaaS subscription of $99-999/month

**Target customers**: mid-sized North American SaaS companies, enterprise AI teams
**Gross margin potential**: 70-80%
**Comparable benchmarks**: Helicone, Portkey, LangSmith

### 7.5 Path E: Self-hosted inference (the most capital-intensive)

**Approach**:
- Buy GPUs and build your own inference cluster
- Run open-source models such as Llama, Qwen, and DeepSeek
- Price at 50% of what the model vendor charges

**Target customers**: open-source model users, batch inference workloads
**Gross margin potential**: 40-60% (but capex is enormous)
**Comparable benchmarks**: Together AI, Fireworks AI, Replicate

## VIII. Is This Business Worth Entering Right Now?

### 8.1 Entry decision matrix

| Your situation | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **An individual looking for a small side hustle** | ❌ Don't do it | The window is too short, the absconding risk is high, and low technical barriers mean you'll get squeezed to death by competition |
| **A startup team** | ⚠️ Must plan an upgrade path from day one | Pure relay = digging your own grave |
| **A team that already has users/traffic** | ✓ Can do it, but must quickly pivot to SaaS | Use token relay to drive traffic, then ultimately sell subscriptions |
| **Inside an enterprise** | ✓ Building your own LLM Gateway is the trend | Needed for cost governance |
| **Looking to invest** | OpenRouter-type plays are reasonable targets | But the valuation is already high ($500M), and growth room needs watching |

### 8.2 Four necessary conditions to truly "scale up"

1. **Either be compliant, or stay very close to compliance** — a pure gray-market player's ceiling is around ¥50 million in annual volume
2. **Either have a channel/brand advantage** — OpenRouter has a16z's backing plus a developer-facing brand
3. **Either have technical differentiation** — intelligent routing, caching, observability, etc.
4. **Or lock in a vertical industry** — sell solutions, not tokens

**There is no way to scale up on pure "markup resale" alone — the value of this business is being rapidly diluted by the exponential decline in model prices.**

## IX. A Value-Investing Perspective

### 9.1 Is this a good business?

**Judged against 7 criteria** (drawing on Duan Yongping's/Buffett's "good business" checklist):

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brand/IP | ❌ | The relay station itself has no brand |
| Network effects | ⚠️ | OpenRouter has some (more models → more users → stronger bargaining power) |
| Switching costs | ❌ | Users can switch anytime |
| Economies of scale | ⚠️ | Present but weak |
| Long-term demand | ❌ | **A window-of-opportunity business** that largely disappears after 3 years |
| Cash flow | ✓ | Short-term cash flow is decent (thanks to float) |
| Capital allocation | ❌ | Most operators end up taking the money and absconding |

**Overall score: 2-3 / 7 — not a good business, a textbook "short-term windfall, long-term extinction" pattern**

### 9.2 Which players are worth investing in?

```
✓ Worth investing in:
  - OpenRouter-type compliant aggregators (already backed by a16z)
  - LLMOps SaaS such as Portkey / Helicone / Langfuse
  - Self-hosted inference plays such as Together AI / Fireworks (capital-intensive)

✗ Not worth investing in:
  - Domestic gray-market relays
  - Individual carpool sharing
  - Long-tail resale
```

### 9.3 Will the big vendors enter the market themselves?

| Vendor | Will it sell tokens in China itself? | Probability |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI | No | <5% |
| Anthropic | No (already cut ties) | <5% |
| Google | No | <10% |
| DeepSeek/Qwen | Building their own overseas nodes | **>50%** |
| AWS Bedrock / Azure | Domestic cloud partnerships | Medium |

**Conclusion**: overseas vendors **will not serve China directly in the short term**, so relay stations still have a window of at least 2-3 years. But domestic models building their own overseas nodes will eat into a large share of the market.

## X. Final Verdict

### One-line summary

> **The token relay station is a business of "windfall profit during the window, guaranteed death after 3 years" — at its core, it arbitrages the double mismatch of "model vendors not entering China" and "users being sensitive to cost/speed." Short-term net margins are attractive (10-25%), but three forces — the exponential decline in model prices, big vendors building their own nodes, and tightening regulation — are strangling this ecosystem. Whatever survives 3 years from now will no longer be a "relay station" but an "AI Gateway" — this is a qualitative transformation, not a simple upgrade.**

### Three concrete recommendations

1. **If you're a user**: don't hoard a large balance (absconding risk) — top up month by month
2. **If you're an entrepreneur**: the entry barrier is low → death is inevitable, so you must plan an upgrade path of "intelligent routing + enterprise-grade + vertical SaaS" from day one
3. **If you're an investor**: skip the relay layer entirely and look directly at players like OpenRouter / Portkey / Together AI that have already reached "the next stop"
