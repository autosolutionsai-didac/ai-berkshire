# Online Music Industry — Competitive Landscape and Trend Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competitive dynamics, value chain, technological change, multidisciplinary perspectives
> Core questions: What are this industry's economic characteristics? Winner-take-all, or several strong players coexisting? Will licensing costs stay elevated forever?
> Research date: June 20, 2026 (data updated through Q1 2026)

**Non-consensus view**: Market-size data varies enormously depending on scope. "Digital music" includes short-video background music, live-performance ticketing, AI music tools, and the broader pan-music economy, while "online music" refers only to streaming subscription and advertising revenue. Investors should focus on the latter — the former is prone to creating a false impression that "the industry is huge." In fact, TME (RMB 32.9 billion) plus NetEase Cloud Music (RMB 7.76 billion) equal roughly RMB 40.7 billion — the combined revenue of these two companies is already close to the entire online music market's size, indicating the market isn't as large as it might appear.

**Key gap**: Tencent Music's ARPPU is only one-quarter of Spotify's. TME's own medium-term target is only ARPPU of RMB 15 and a 25% paying ratio — implying that even management doesn't believe it can quickly close the gap with Spotify.

## III. Threat Assessment of Core Competitors

### 3.1 Qishui Music (ByteDance) — Threat level: ★★★★★

This is the biggest structural threat to Tencent Music. Not a simple competing product, but an entirely different business paradigm.

**Deconstructing the competitive logic**:

| Dimension | Traditional model (TME) | Disruptive model (Qishui Music) |
|------|----------------|-------------------------|
| User acquisition | Paid growth | Free traffic from Douyin (82% of users come from Douyin) |
| Acquisition cost | Billions of RMB per year | Near zero |
| Business model | RMB 18/month subscription | Watch ads, listen free all day |
| Content logic | Search + playlists | Algorithmic recommendation (replicating Douyin) |
| Source of hit songs | Traditional promotion | Songs made viral via short video (86% of the top 1,000 hit songs originate on Douyin) |
| User profile | All ages | Gen Z + lower-tier markets |

**Key data**:
- MAU grew from 50 million in June 2024 to 140 million in January 2026 — 180% growth in 18 months
- 20.75 million new installs in December 2025 alone, the highest in the industry
- Average daily listening time exceeds 80 minutes per user
- Douyin's music open platform's settlement revenue is projected to double in 2026 versus 2025
- Douyin already serves 1,300+ music rights partners, covering more than 100 million songs

**Qishui Music's Achilles heel**:

The economics of the free model are simple: licensing costs scale roughly linearly with user count, while advertising revenue growth is much flatter. At the 140-million-MAU level, licensing costs are an astronomical number. How long can ByteDance keep subsidizing it? That depends on two factors: (1) whether Douyin's music ecosystem remains a strategic priority; (2) ByteDance's overall profitability pressure.

**But the counter-argument is equally strong**: ByteDance's 2025 revenue exceeded RMB 700 billion (est.), making the cost of subsidizing Qishui Music trivial for the group. Moreover, Qishui Music's real value isn't its own profitability but strengthening the Douyin music ecosystem — as long as Douyin maintains 800 million daily active users, Qishui Music has a reason to exist.

### 3.2 NetEase Cloud Music — Threat level: ★★★☆☆

**Core data**:
- 2025 revenue of RMB 7.76 billion (YoY -2.4%), but online music revenue of RMB 5.99 billion (YoY +12%)
- Adjusted net profit of RMB 2.86 billion (YoY +68.2%), a marked improvement in margins
- Subscription revenue of RMB 5.05 billion (YoY +13.3%), 84% of online music revenue
- Over 90% of active users are post-90s and post-00s, with iOS DAU/MAU reaching 42%
- More than 774,000 original musicians have joined the platform

**Differentiated advantage**: NetEase Cloud Music has built the strongest community ecosystem among Chinese music streaming platforms. 50% of users browse the comment section while listening, and its "comment culture" has become a brand hallmark. This emotional stickiness is something algorithms can't replicate.

**Threats and shortcomings**:
- Total revenue is declining (dragged down by social entertainment services, down 32% YoY), and the scale ceiling is clear
- ARPPU is only RMB 7, far below TME's RMB 11.9 — commercialization efficiency is low
- Licensing costs are 75% of revenue — down 5 percentage points from 2018, but still high
- For TME, NetEase Cloud Music is more of an "existing-user diversion" than an "incremental user grab"

### 3.3 Apple Music / Spotify — Threat level: ★☆☆☆☆

**Fact**: Spotify does not operate in mainland China. Apple Music has a service presence in China but with a very small share, serving mainly high-end iPhone users. The two combined hold no more than 2% share in China (est.).

**But their strategic significance lies in**:
- Spotify's 696 million global MAU, 276 million paying users, and 40% paying ratio serve as the benchmark for valuation
- Spotify has already raised prices to $11.99/month (~RMB 87), versus QQ Music VIP's mere RMB 18/month — evidence that pricing in the Chinese market is severely underpriced
- Spotify holds a 32.9% global market share versus Tencent Music's 14.4%, and the gap is narrowing
- If the Chinese market opens further, the probability of Spotify entering, while low, is not zero

### 3.4 Ximalaya (Already Acquired by TME) — Threat level: ★★☆☆☆ (now converted to synergy)

In May 2026, Tencent Music completed the acquisition of Ximalaya for ~$2.9 billion (approximately RMB 20 billion).

**Ximalaya's assets**:
- 45.5% market share in long-form audio, 300 million all-scenario monthly active users
- 488 million pieces of audio content, 5.2 million audiobooks
- A long-standing first-mover advantage in in-car audio scenarios

**Five regulatory conditions**: no price increases, retention of free content, dissolution of exclusive licensing, no bundled sales through in-car systems, and no restriction on hosts distributing across multiple platforms. These conditions substantially narrow the room for synergy.

**Non-consensus view**: the market has generally taken a negative view of this acquisition (expensive, hard to integrate, heavy regulatory constraints), but overlooks a core logic — this is a "defensive acquisition." If ByteDance had acquired Ximalaya, and combined it with Fanqie Changting (already surpassing Ximalaya in monthly active users in the audiobook space), the ByteDance family would have formed a pincer around TME across "music + long-form audio." Spending RMB 20 billion to buy "not being encircled" insurance might not be a bad deal.

### 3.5 AI Music Startups — Threat level: ★★☆☆☆ (an indirect impact on the industry)

| Tool | Features | 2025 annual revenue |
|------|------|----------------|
| Suno | Global leader, V5 model, Studio tool | $150 million |
| Udio | Style library + Sessions tool, favored by short-video creators | Undisclosed |
| MELO Music | Optimized for Chinese-language creation, a domestic substitute | Undisclosed |

AI music tools don't directly compete with TME for users, but they are changing the economics of the industry's supply side — the number of new Chinese-language songs grew 66.2% year-over-year to 1.351 million in 2024, with AI as the main driver. Producing 7 million AI songs a day means music content is shifting from scarce to oversupplied.

## IV. Segment-Level Landscape

### 4.1 Online Music Subscriptions

| Metric | TME | NetEase Cloud Music | Industry trend |
|------|-----|-------|---------|
| Subscription revenue (2025) | RMB 17.66 billion (+16%) | RMB 5.05 billion (+13.3%) | Double-digit growth, but decelerating |
| Paywall ratio | 50%-70% (2024) | 34% | Continuing to rise |
| Membership tiers | VIP RMB 18/month + SVIP RMB 40/month | Single membership tier | TME's tiering is more mature |
| SVIP penetration | 20 million+ (~16% of paying users) | — | SVIP is the main lever for ARPPU growth |

**Munger's view**: the economic characteristics of the subscription model are excellent — high margins, predictable revenue, low marginal cost, strong stickiness. TME's subscription revenue is over 65% of its online music revenue, and NetEase Cloud Music's is over 84%. This is a classic "toll bridge" model. The question is whether Qishui Music's free model will erode users' acceptance of paying.

### 4.2 Music Social / Live Streaming

TME's social entertainment business is shrinking rapidly:
- Full-year 2025 revenue of RMB 6.18 billion, continuing to decline year-over-year
- Q4 social-entertainment MAU of 82 million (YoY -21.2%)
- WeSing (Quanmin K-Song) MAU of 36.92 million (YoY -16.6%)
- Regulation is tightening (tiered/graded management of hosts, the "Clear and Bright" special campaign)

**Conclusion**: social entertainment is no longer TME's growth engine but a declining business that needs to be managed. TME has completed its transition from a "live-streaming company" to a "music company" — online music made up over 80% of revenue in 2025, versus social entertainment exceeding 70% back in 2019. The pivot was involuntary but points in the right direction.

### 4.3 Audio Content (Podcasts / Audiobooks)

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| China's "ear economy" market size (2025) | ~RMB 611 billion |
| Audiobook market | RMB 13.4 billion, paying ratio of 5%-8% |
| Online audio user base | Projected to surpass 350 million by 2026 |
| Podcast user shift | Listening time among users 50+ up 35%, video podcasts becoming mainstream |
| Ximalaya market share | 45.5% (but being chased by Fanqie Changting) |

**Non-consensus view**: the audiobook segment looks large but has an extremely low paying ratio (5%-8%), and Fanqie Changting (ByteDance-affiliated) has already surpassed Ximalaya in monthly active users using a "free + advertising" model. The challenge TME faces after acquiring Ximalaya is how to improve audiobook monetization efficiency under regulatory constraints (no price increases, retention of free content).

### 4.4 AI Music Generation

- Adoption of domestic AI music tools reached 35% in 2024, generating over RMB 15 billion in incremental output value
- 2026 is being called "the inflection year for AI music," with tools moving from a "feature race" into the deep waters of "experience and emotional resonance"
- TME has launched "Weiyin VEMUS" and "Qiming Xing · AI Songwriting," charging a RMB 29.9 mastering fee for AI-generated works
- Chinese-language AI music creation still has bottlenecks: stilted enunciation, frequent errors with polyphonic characters, and misreadings of native styles such as classical-inspired folk music

**Munger's view**: AI music's impact on platforms is a double-edged sword. In the short term it lowers licensing procurement costs; in the long term it erodes content scarcity. When AI can generate unlimited "80-out-of-100 music," only "95-plus human creations" carry a premium — this will squeeze the livelihoods of mid-tier musicians, but the value of top-tier artists may actually rise.

## V. Industry Trends

### 5.1 Short Video's Disruption of Traditional Music Platforms (the biggest structural threat)

This is the question Munger would watch most closely — a fundamental change in consumption habits.

**Facts**:
- 86% of the top 1,000 hit songs grew up on Douyin, and 54% went viral via a cold start on Douyin
- The entry point for music consumption is shifting from "opening a music app to search for a song" to "stumbling across a song on short video"
- Qishui Music users average over 80 minutes of daily listening time, across 48 scenario-based listening modes

**Deeper implications**:

The core value of traditional music platforms is "a licensing catalog plus search" — users know what they want to hear and come to the platform to find it. But short video has changed this logic: users don't know what they want to hear, and the algorithm recommends it to them. This means:

1. **The licensing moat is devalued**: when users no longer search actively but passively accept recommendations, "whether a particular song is available" matters much less
2. **The discovery mechanism is shifting**: record labels' promotional focus is moving from music platforms to short-video platforms
3. **User habits are being reshaped**: Gen Z's music consumption habits have already been defined by short video, and this trend is irreversible

**But there's another side**: songs that go viral on short video have an extremely short lifecycle (typically only 2-4 weeks), while classic tracks on traditional music platforms have a long-tail effect. "Coming across" a song and "saving it and listening repeatedly" are two different behaviors — the latter still happens on traditional platforms.

### 5.2 Changes in the Licensing Landscape (Exclusive → Non-exclusive)

**Timeline**:
- July 2021: the State Administration for Market Regulation required TME to give up exclusive licensing
- 2021-2025: NetEase Cloud Music progressively obtained licenses from Modern Sky, EMI, China Record Corporation, Jay Chou, and others
- May 2026: TME's acquisition of Ximalaya was approved, with an attached condition requiring the dissolution of all exclusive licenses

**Impact assessment**:

The end of the exclusive-licensing era is a double-edged sword for TME:
- **Negative**: it lost its licensing moat, competitors can obtain the same content, and TME's differentiation has been weakened
- **Positive (non-consensus)**: licensing costs may actually fall. Once the exclusive-licensing era's bidding war (TME and NetEase Cloud Music bidding each other up) disappeared, the bargaining power of record labels has been diluted. TME's licensing cost as a share of revenue has fallen from roughly 89% in 2018 to roughly 73% in 2025

### 5.3 AI's Impact on Music Creation and Distribution

Already detailed in Section 4.4 above. One more key trend worth adding: AI is changing how music is "discovered." The traditional model of "editorial curation plus user playlists" is being replaced by "AI personalized recommendation." In this respect, Qishui Music (inheriting Douyin's recommendation engine) has a natural advantage.

## VI. Value Chain Analysis

### Value Chain Structure

```
Upstream: content creation      Midstream: rights management      Downstream: distribution platforms      Terminal: users
┌─────────────┐    ┌─────────────┐    ┌─────────────┐    ┌──────────┐
│ Three major labels │    │ Rights agencies /  │    │ TME         │    │ Paying users  │
│ (Universal/Sony/Warner)│→  │ collective mgmt.  │→   │ NetEase Cloud │   │ Free users  │
│ Indie labels     │    │ organizations       │    │ Qishui Music     │    │          │
│ Original musicians   │    │             │    │ Ximalaya     │    │          │
│ AI generation      │    │             │    │             │    │          │
└─────────────┘    └─────────────┘    └─────────────┘    └──────────┘
```

### Value Distribution

| Segment | Share of value | Bargaining power | Trend |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Three major labels | ~60% of licensing revenue goes to labels | ★★★★☆ (an oligopoly, but not exclusive) | Stable. The "big three" hold ~60% share of global recorded music |
| Indie labels / musicians | Revenue-share ratio lower than the big three's | ★★☆☆☆ | Rising. AI lowers the barrier to creation, and indie musicians' bargaining power is strengthening |
| Platforms (TME, etc.) | Licensing cost is 73% of revenue | ★★★☆☆ | Improving. Licensing bidding wars have eased since exclusivity ended |
| Users | RMB 18/month (VIP) | ★★★★★ | Free alternatives (Qishui) have strengthened users' bargaining power |

### In-Depth Analysis of Platform Bargaining Power

**Bargaining power toward upstream (record labels): ★★★☆☆**

Looks weak but isn't really. The three major labels hold 60% of the global recorded music market, but streaming contributes over 50% of the big three's revenue. The two sides are in a "mutually dependent" relationship — platforms can't do without content, and labels can't do without distribution. Since the non-exclusive policy took effect, the "arms race" in licensing prices has ended.

**Bargaining power toward downstream (users): ★★☆☆☆**

This is the biggest concern. Qishui Music's free model gives users a non-paying option, directly weakening TME's pricing power. TME's announcement that starting in 2026 it will no longer disclose MAU and other user data on a quarterly basis (switching to annual disclosure) itself hints that the user-level data is deteriorating.

**Non-consensus view**: many analysts compare TME's and Spotify's ARPPU gap to argue for "room to raise prices," but this overlooks a fundamental difference — Spotify has no free streaming competitor overseas (YouTube Music has a free tier but a worse experience), while TME faces Qishui Music, which offers a decent experience and is entirely free. The comparability between Chinese and American music-paying behavior has been overstated.

## VII. Summary of Industry Economic Characteristics (Munger's Framework)

### Is this industry winner-take-all, or does it favor several strong players coexisting?

**Answer: an intermediate state — "one strong player, many weak ones" is evolving toward "several strong players coexisting."**

Music streaming has network effects (more users → more data → more accurate recommendations), but this network effect isn't as strong as in social networks. The reason is that music consumption is mostly "one person listening alone" — unlike WeChat, it doesn't require "the other person to also be using it." Consequently, a music platform's moat comes more from: (1) user habits and sunk costs (playlists, favorites); (2) the completeness of the licensing catalog; (3) community stickiness (NetEase Cloud Music).

Once exclusive licensing was abolished, moat item (2) was weakened. AI music may further erode content scarcity. What remains of the moat depends on the inertia of user habits — a moat that can be slowly eroded.

### Will licensing costs stay elevated forever?

**Answer: No. They're improving, but the improvement has a ceiling.**

- TME's licensing cost as a share of revenue: ~89% in 2018 → ~73% in 2025, a 16-percentage-point decline over 5 years
- NetEase Cloud Music's licensing cost as a share of revenue: ~80% in 2018 → ~75% in 2025, a 5-percentage-point decline
- Reasons for improvement: (1) reduced bidding after the end of exclusivity; (2) growth in proprietary content (original musicians + AI generation); (3) scale effects
- The ceiling: the oligopoly position of the three major labels won't disappear, and licensing costs can't realistically fall below 50%

## VIII. Composite Scoring

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Industry growth potential | ★★★☆☆ | User penetration is already high; growth depends on ARPU gains, suppressed by free competition |
| Industry structure (favorable to the leader?) | ★★★☆☆ | Shifting from monopoly to competition; the moat is being eroded |
| Competitive intensity | ★★☆☆☆ | Qishui Music's free model is a dimensional attack, and live-streaming/short-video platforms keep siphoning off users |
| Technological disruption risk | ★★☆☆☆ | AI music's impact on content scarcity runs deep, and short video is reshaping consumption habits |
| Stability of the leader's position | ★★★★☆ | TME's position is stable in the short term (revenue and profit remain far ahead), but faces structural challenges over the long term |
| Value-chain bargaining power | ★★★☆☆ | Bargaining power toward upstream has improved, but bargaining power toward downstream (users) has been weakened by free alternatives |
| **Composite score** | **★★★☆☆** | |

**One-line conclusion**: online music is an industry with decent but deteriorating economic characteristics — the repeat-purchase nature and high margins of the subscription model are a positive, but Qishui Music's free model and AI music's impact on content scarcity are fundamental threats. Tencent Music's near-term results are solid (RMB 11 billion in profit, buybacks plus dividends), but if the pattern of "rising profit, shrinking user base" persists, it will eventually show up in earnings. Munger would say: this industry's long-term competitive equilibrium has not yet formed, and betting on it requires a larger margin of safety.
