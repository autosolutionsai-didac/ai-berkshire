# Tencent Music (TME) Investment Research Report

> Information-richness rating: **Grade A (information-rich)** — years of public listing, broad broker coverage, complete quarterly/annual reports

> This report does not constitute investment advice.

---

## 1. One-line conclusion

> Tencent Music is a severely undervalued cash-generation machine (P/B 0.57x, FCF yield 10%, net cash equal to 22% of market cap) whose profitability keeps strengthening after its business transformation — but it faces a structural contradiction: **it is making more and more money while users keep leaving, one by one.** Qishui Music's free-plus-algorithm model is reshaping the entry point for young people's music consumption, and management has chosen to stop disclosing user data after 16 straight quarters of MAU decline. The extremely low valuation provides a margin of safety, but the moat is thinning, the growth engine is shifting, and the competitive landscape is far from settled — this is not a "sit back and collect" opportunity, but a value bet that requires continuous tracking and judgment.

## 4. Summary of analysis by dimension

### Business model & moat (Duan Yongping's perspective)

1. **Revenue-mix transformation succeeded, but it was forced** — Online music rose from 30% (2019) to 82% (Q1 2026), while social entertainment shrank from 70% to 18%. This was not a proactive strategy but the right path forced by regulation and competition.
2. **Economies of scale are the strongest moat** — The copyright cost-sharing advantage from 127.4 million paying users is real (less than RMB 10 per person per month); competitors cannot replicate it. But Qishui Music doesn't need the full catalog of copyrights — a handful of hit songs is enough.
3. **The moat overall is thinning** — After exclusive copyright deals were broken up, the flywheel shifted from "copyright-driven" to "inertia-driven." Switching costs come from sunk-cost playlists and algorithmic personalization, but users are more often "too lazy to switch" than "unwilling to switch."
4. **Halting MAU disclosure is a major red flag** — Duan Yongping: "The data management doesn't want you to see is usually the data you most need to see."
5. **Assessment against Duan Yongping's standard**: this is a "decent" business, not a "great" one. It has some pricing power but limited, requires continuous investment to maintain the moat, and is not a "sit back and collect" type of business.

### Financials & valuation (Buffett's perspective)

1. **A cash-generation machine** — FCF consistently exceeds net profit, capex is only 2-3% of revenue, cumulative five-year free cash flow is ~USD 5.8 billion, and the market-cap payback period is only ~2.5 years.
2. **Astonishing margin expansion** — Gross margin rose from 30% to 44% (+14ppt over five years); operating margin rose from ~7% to ~28%, driven mainly by the shrinkage of low-margin social entertainment and the rising share of high-margin subscriptions.
3. **Valuation sits at historic lows across multiple metrics** — PE 8.65x (GAAP) / 10x (Non-IFRS), PB 0.57x, FCF yield 10%, a huge discount versus Spotify's PE of 31-49x.
4. **Net cash of RMB 21 billion equals 22% of market cap** — Equivalent to an automatic 20% discount on the purchase price, providing a solid value floor.
5. **But 2025 net profit contains padding** — RMB 2.37 billion of one-off gains (deemed disposal of an associate); excluding this, real growth is ~31% rather than the GAAP-reported 66%.
6. **ROE of only 14% is a real flaw** — Large amounts of idle cash drag down return on capital. Buffett would ask: why not return the excess cash to shareholders?

### Industry & competition (Munger's perspective)

1. **The industry has entered a steady state** — The online-music (streaming) market is worth ~RMB 40 billion, with growth slowing from 30%+ to 15%. TME plus NetEase Cloud Music combined revenue is already close to the total market size — the pie is smaller than it might seem.
2. **Qishui Music represents an entirely different business paradigm** — Near-zero customer acquisition cost (82% comes from ByteDance/Douyin traffic referral), a free model, algorithmic recommendation. It grew from 50 million to 140 million MAU in 18 months, putting it on par with NetEase Cloud Music in the second tier.
3. **TME's moat is more accurately described as QQ Music's moat** — Kugou's MAU fell 8.1% year-over-year, Kuwo fell 8%, and "wavering users" migrating to Qishui Music grew 64-73% year-over-year.
4. **Non-consensus view: the Ximalaya acquisition is a defensive acquisition** — The market widely views it negatively (paying RMB 9 billion for a loss-making company), but the core logic is preventing ByteDance's ecosystem from encircling the market with music plus long-form audio.
5. **The comparability of the China-US ARPPU gap is overstated** — Spotify doesn't face an overseas competitor that is both a decent experience and completely free; TME does.

### Risk & management (Li Lu's perspective)

1. **Management is above-average but passing** — CEO Liang Zhu completed a difficult transformation, and capital allocation (buybacks plus dividends equal to 6-7% of market cap) is above average. But he is not an innovative entrepreneur, more a career manager within the Tencent system.
2. **The biggest "invisible risk"** — It's not Qishui Music stealing users (the market has already seen that), but a paradigm shift in how music itself is consumed: as short-video background music becomes the primary way young people "listen to music," the raison d'être of standalone music apps is being fundamentally questioned.
3. **Risks already released** — Social entertainment revenue fell from RMB 19.8 billion to RMB 6.2 billion (-69%), and the antitrust rectification is complete; these two risks are largely priced in.
4. **Ximalaya is the biggest M&A bet** — USD 1.26 billion for a long-form audio company that has repeatedly failed to IPO; integration risk is high, and the risk of "diworsification" should not be dismissed.
5. **Halting user-data disclosure is a crisis of trust** — The market's 25% crash was its response to this signal. Li Lu: "When a company starts hiding its data, it's usually bad news."

## 5. Investment thesis (bull vs. bear)

### Bull case

1. **Valuation is extremely undervalued** — A P/B of 0.57x means buying below net asset value; PE<10x is the lowest tier among Chinese ADRs; net cash of RMB 21 billion provides a hard floor.
2. **Cash flow quality is excellent** — FCF consistently exceeds net profit, an asset-light model, capex of only 2-3% — nearly a "money-printing machine."
3. **Margins still have room to expand** — Further shrinkage of social entertainment will keep improving revenue quality; SVIP penetration rising from 16% to 25% could contribute incremental gains.
4. **Paid-penetration room remains versus Spotify** — Rising from 22% to 30% would theoretically add 40 million paying users.
5. **Shareholder returns are stepping up** — Buybacks plus dividends together equal 6-7% of market cap, offering a return even without share-price appreciation.
6. **If Ximalaya integrates successfully** — A combined "music + audio" platform could open a second growth curve.
7. **The Chinese-ADR discount will eventually narrow** — The Hong Kong listing provides a fallback, and policy risk is improving at the margin.

### Bear case

1. **16 straight quarters of MAU decline is a structural problem** — Not cyclical fluctuation, but a migration of user consumption habits.
2. **Qishui Music's free model is a dimensionality-reducing attack** — For young users, "paying to listen to music" itself feels counterintuitive; a free substitute erodes paid mindset from the bottom up.
3. **Management has stopped disclosing user data** — A step back in information transparency, leaving investors unable to track the single most important operating metric quarterly.
4. **Growth is clearly slowing** — Q1 2026 revenue grew only +7.3% (vs. +15.8% for full-year 2025); subscription growth slowed to 6.6%.
5. **The Ximalaya acquisition could destroy value** — RMB 9 billion for a business that has not yet proven it can sustain profitability.
6. **ARPPU is near a plateau** — RMB 11.9/month has flatlined, squeezed by consumption downgrade and free competitors simultaneously.
7. **A paradigm shift in music consumption** — Short-video background music may become the primary way young people "listen to music," calling into question the raison d'être of standalone music apps.

### Key catalysts

**Signals to add to the position:**
1. Qishui Music's MAU growth rate slows below 30% → indicates competitive intensity is easing
2. TME resumes disclosing quarterly user data → management confidence returns
3. Ximalaya turns quarterly profitable for the first time → early sign of successful integration
4. Buyback execution accelerates (>USD 200 million per quarter) → management expresses confidence with real cash
5. ARPPU breaks above RMB 13/month → pricing power is validated

**Signals to reduce the position:**
1. Qishui Music's MAU breaks above 200 million → user migration is accelerating
2. Annual paying-user count declines → the growth engine is failing
3. Gross margin falls below 40% → copyright cost bargaining power is lost
4. Management makes another large acquisition (>USD 5 billion) → capital allocation discipline breaks down
5. Deterioration in US-China relations escalates delisting risk → systemic risk

## 8. Summary

Tencent Music is a "cheap but flawed" investment case. The good side: this company earns real cash, its valuation sits at a historic low, and net cash provides a solid safety cushion. The bad side: users are leaving, the moat is thinning, management has started hiding data, and its biggest competitor is rising fast using a completely different business paradigm.

This is not a "buy and hold forever with your eyes closed" name. It's better suited to building a disciplined position at extremely low valuations while closely watching three core variables: **Qishui Music's growth rate, TME's paying-user retention, and Ximalaya's integration progress.** If user data stabilizes in the second half of 2026 and Ximalaya begins contributing revenue, a move from the current $9 to $13-16 is a reasonable path (+40% to +75%). If user attrition continues to accelerate and the acquisition fails, sub-$7 would not be surprising either.

Whether the margin of safety is sufficient depends on your judgment of one question: will the entry point for music consumption migrate from standalone apps to the short-video ecosystem?
