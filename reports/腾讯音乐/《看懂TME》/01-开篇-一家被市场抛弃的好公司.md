## A set of numbers that will make you reconsider Tencent Music

In 2025, Tencent Music turned in a solid scorecard:

| Metric | 2025 figure | Change |
|------|-----------|------|
| Total revenue | RMB 32.9 billion | +15.8% |
| Non-IFRS net profit (profit excluding one-off items such as share-based compensation) | RMB 9.6 billion | **+25%** |
| Gross margin | 44.2% | up steadily from 30% in 2021 |
| Free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex — think of it as "cash actually earned in hand") | ~RMB 9.9 billion | remains abundant |
| Net cash | RMB 38.0 billion | ~40% of market cap |
| Paying users | 127.4 million | +5.3% |

Profit up 25%, gross margin rising for four straight years, RMB 38.0 billion of cash sitting on the balance sheet — by rights, the market should be applauding.

Yet over the same period, the share price fell from a 52-week high of $26.70 to $8.47, a decline of **68%**.

Put those two facts side by side: **a company whose profit grew 25% saw its stock price fall 68%.**

What does the current valuation look like?

| Valuation metric | Value | What it means |
|----------|------|----------|
| Market cap | ~RMB 96.0 billion ($13.3 billion) | — |
| PE (Non-IFRS) | ~10x | Historic low |
| Net cash | RMB 38.0 billion (40% of market cap) | Cash on hand is nearly half the market cap |
| PE excluding cash | **~6x** | Equivalent to paying RMB 58.0 billion for a machine that earns RMB 9.6 billion a year |
| Cumulative buybacks | ~$2.5-2.6 billion | ~20% of market cap |
| Free cash flow yield | ~12% | Far exceeds bank wealth-management products |

Translated into plain language: **the market is currently pricing Tencent Music as if it were giving you the cash on the balance sheet for free, and valuing the operating business at just 6 years of profit.**

Either the market has spotted a serious problem, or the market has gotten it wrong. Figuring out which it is is the question this series sets out to answer.

## Three common misconceptions about Tencent Music

Before breaking things down, let's dispel the three most widespread misconceptions.

### Misconception one: Tencent Music is "a music player"

Most people's impression of Tencent Music stops at QQ Music, Kuwo Music, and Kugou Music — three apps for listening to songs. "Isn't it just a player?"

That impression is stuck in 2019.

In 2019, Tencent Music's revenue mix looked like this:

| Segment | 2019 share | 2025 share |
|------|----------|----------|
| Social entertainment (live-streaming tipping, etc.) | **~70%** | 18.8% |
| Online music (subscriptions, ads, etc.) | ~30% | **81.2%** |

**In six years, the revenue structure completely flipped.** The company went from a "pan-entertainment company" that earned money through live-streaming tips, to a "China-style subscription platform" driven by music subscriptions.

More importantly: as social entertainment shrank, online music grew at over 20% a year — in 2025 online-music revenue was RMB 26.7 billion, up 23% year-over-year. Overall profit rose rather than fell.

**This isn't the story of "a music player" — it's the story of a business that completely rebuilt its structure.**

### Misconception two: social entertainment decline = the company is declining

Social entertainment revenue has fallen steadily from its 2019 peak, down to just RMB 6.2 billion in 2025 — only 18.8% of total revenue. That sounds alarming: if a segment that once made up 70% has shrunk this much, can the company really be fine?

The data gives a clear answer: **yes. And better than fine.**

| Metric | 2021 | 2025 | Change |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Total revenue | RMB 31.2 billion | RMB 32.9 billion | +5% (recovering after a trough) |
| Gross margin | 30% | 44.2% | **+14 percentage points** |
| Non-IFRS net profit | ~RMB 5.0 billion (est.) | RMB 9.92 billion | **nearly doubled** |

The reason gross margin rose from 30% to 44% is simple: **social entertainment is a low-margin business (it requires revenue-sharing with streamers), while online music is a high-margin business (subscription revenue is nearly pure profit).** As the low-margin business shrinks and the high-margin business grows, blended gross margin naturally rises.

So the decline of social entertainment is, less "bad news" and more **Tencent Music completing a self-evolution — shedding low-quality revenue and replacing it with high-quality revenue.**

**Counterpoint**: social entertainment's decline is irreversible, but it hasn't fully hit zero yet — it still generates RMB 6.2 billion a year and continues to shrink. If the rate of decline accelerates, it will still drag on overall growth in the near term.

### Misconception three: something this cheap must be a "value trap"

A cash-adjusted PE of only about 6x — most people's first reaction is "you get what you pay for."

The classic hallmark of a value trap is: it looks cheap, but profits are deteriorating, the moat is disappearing, and cash is flowing out. Let's check each:

| Value-trap characteristic | Tencent Music's actual situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Are profits deteriorating? | 2025 Non-IFRS net profit +22%, Q1 2026 continuing at +4.8% |
| Is cash flowing out? | Net cash of RMB 38.0 billion, free cash flow averaging over RMB 10.0 billion a year |
| Is the moat disappearing? | This one **needs careful discussion** — covered in Part 03 |
| Is management coasting? | Cumulative buybacks of $2.5-2.6 billion (20% of market cap), 2025 dividend of $368 million |

Two of the four criteria clearly don't match a value trap, and the fourth doesn't either — management is actively buying back shares, not coasting on past success.

**The only item that warrants deeper scrutiny is the third: the moat.** MAU has declined for 16 straight quarters, while Qishui Music's MAU stands at 156 million and is growing astonishingly fast — is this simply "a batch of low-quality users leaving, no big deal," or is it "a signal that the moat is being eroded"?

This is one of the most crucial questions in the entire series. Part 03 will dig into it in detail.

## Understanding Tencent Music requires answering four questions

If you had to sum up Tencent Music's current situation in one sentence:

> **A company that is getting better, facing a rival that is getting stronger, trading in a market that is being repriced.**

The combination of these three things is what produced the anomaly of "profit up 22%, stock down 68%."

To understand this company, you need to answer at least four questions:

| Question | Corresponding part |
|------|---------|
| How long can online music's growth continue? Where's the ceiling? | Part 02 |

Each question has been written about individually before, but few people put them all together in one assessment. **A cheap valuation is only an entry condition, not a reason to buy — the real reasons for or against buying are hidden in the next four parts.**

## A small preview

Here are a few numbers, to be explained in detail in later parts. For now, just remember the scale:

| Metric | Figure |
|------|------|
| China's online-music paid-penetration rate | 24% (global average 40-50%) |
| Tencent Music ARPPU | RMB 11.9/month (Spotify ~$5.3/month, roughly RMB 38) |
| Qishui Music MAU | 156 million (March 2026, surpassing NetEase Cloud Music) |
| Qishui Music catalog | 50 million tracks (Tencent Music: 260 million) |
| Ximalaya acquisition consideration | $2.9 billion |
| Tencent Music cumulative buybacks | ~$2.5-2.6 billion (20% of market cap) |

Put these together, and what kind of company do you see?

- Paid-penetration is only half the global average — theoretically there's still room to double
- Per-user revenue is only a third of Spotify's — there's a lot of room to raise prices
- But its biggest competitor has 156 million MAU and is still growing fast — a spoiler is standing right in the path of any price hike
- It just spent $2.9 billion buying Ximalaya — either a smart move or an expensive gamble

**What do these conflicting signals add up to? Different judgments will give different answers.** That's exactly what the next four parts set out to answer.
