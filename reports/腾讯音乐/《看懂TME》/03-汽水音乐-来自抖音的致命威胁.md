# Qishui Music — A Mortal Threat from Douyin

> "Understanding TME" series · Article 03 · Competition
> Reading time: ~9 minutes

---

## A fact Tencent Music least wants to face

In March 2026, Qishui Music's monthly active users (MAU) reached **156 million** — officially overtaking NetEase Cloud Music to become China's third-largest online music platform (behind only Kugou Music and QQ Music).

Put it on a timeline:

| Time | Qishui Music MAU | Milestone |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Early 2022 | Launch | ByteDance releases a standalone music app |
| Late 2023 | ~50 million (est.) | Rapid ramp-up |
| Late 2024 | ~100 million (est.) | Growth rate near 90% |
| Late 2025 | ~140 million | Closing in on NetEase Cloud Music |
| **March 2026** | **156 million** | **Overtakes NetEase Cloud, becomes #3 in the industry** |

**Qishui Music went from zero to 156 million MAU in just 4 years.** By comparison, NetEase Cloud Music took nearly 8 years to reach that scale after launching in 2013.

The daily active user (DAU) figure is equally striking — 44.31 million, implying a DAU/MAU ratio of about 28%. That ratio indicates Qishui Music isn't a "downloaded-but-unused" app — it has a core user base that opens it every day.

The core question this article sets out to answer: **How big a threat is Qishui Music to Tencent Music, really? Is it a minor irritant or a mortal threat?**

---

## I. A full picture of Qishui Music

### Key figures

| Metric | Data | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Monthly active users (MAU) | 156 million | March 2026 |
| Daily active users (DAU) | 44.31 million | DAU/MAU ratio: 28% |
| Monthly subscription price | **8 yuan** | vs. Tencent Music's 15-18 yuan |
| Music library | ~50 million tracks | vs. Tencent Music's 260 million tracks |
| Business model | **Advertising-driven** | Paid subscription is secondary |
| User source | **82% from Douyin** | Third-party data |
| Target users | Young people, lower-tier cities | Heavily overlapping with Douyin's user base |

### What is Qishui Music, at its core?

On the surface, Qishui Music looks like a "cheap version of QQ Music" — comparable functionality, but a monthly subscription costs only 8 yuan.

But the more accurate framing is: **Qishui Music is an independent app extension of Douyin's music ecosystem.**

On Douyin, hundreds of millions of users scroll past short videos with music every day. When a user hears a song they like, the old path was "go search for it on QQ Music" — now ByteDance offers a shorter path: "tap once, go straight to Qishui Music."

That's why 82% of Qishui users come from Douyin — **it isn't competing head-on with Tencent Music for users in the music market; it's "harvesting" from Douyin's pool of 700 million daily active users.**

---

## II. What's behind Qishui Music's speed?

Reaching 156 million MAU in four years wasn't achieved through a better product than QQ Music — frankly, Qishui's product experience falls short of QQ Music's in many respects. It rests on three things:

### 1. The Douyin traffic pipeline

Douyin has 700 million+ daily active users, the single app that consumes the most user time in China. Qishui Music's customer acquisition cost is nearly zero — users hear a song on Douyin and jump over with one tap.

**An analogy**: WeChat has funneled traffic to Tencent Music for years, but music-related moments on WeChat are far less frequent than on Douyin. On Douyin, **almost every short video** has background music — a natural music-discovery scenario.

### 2. Low-price strategy

| Platform | Monthly price | Auto-renewal price |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Tencent Music (QQ Music) | 18 yuan | 15 yuan |
| NetEase Cloud Music | 15 yuan | 12 yuan |
| **Qishui Music** | **8 yuan** | **Even lower** |

**8 yuan vs. 18 yuan — more than a two-fold gap.** For users in tier-1 and tier-2 cities, this difference barely registers; but for users in tier-3 and tier-4 cities and students, it's the difference between "can afford it" and "can't afford it."

Qishui Music's low-price strategy isn't a short-term "burn cash to grab market share" tactic — **its business model is primarily ad-driven, with subscription fees not the core revenue source**. Just as Douyin itself is free, Qishui can sustain low prices indefinitely.

### 3. Entry through lower-tier cities

Qishui Music's user profile heavily overlaps with Douyin's: predominantly young users in tier-3 and tier-4 cities, price-sensitive, with music-listening habits formed through short video rather than search.

These users likely used free music-listening apps before, or Tencent Music's free tier. **Qishui Music has "intercepted" them — people who might have become Tencent Music's paying subscribers are now choosing the 8-yuan Qishui instead.**

## III. How real is the threat to Tencent Music?

### The damage that has already occurred

**The first piece of hard evidence: MAU has declined for 16 consecutive quarters.**

| Time | Tencent Music MAU (hundred million) | Change |
|------|-----------------|------|
| Q1 2022 | 6.15 | Peak |
| Q4 2023 | 5.84 | Decline |
| Q4 2024 | 5.56 | Continued decline |
| Q4 2025 | 5.28 | **-5%** |

Down from 615 million to 528 million — a loss of roughly 87 million monthly active users. Where did they go? Not all of them went to Qishui, but Qishui has undoubtedly absorbed a meaningful portion.

**The second key data point: "dual-app users" exceed 21 million.**

So-called "dual-app users" are people who have both Tencent Music and Qishui Music installed — they haven't fully left yet, but they now have an alternative. These 21 million people are a standing pool of users Qishui Music can "convert" at any time.

**The third signal: starting Q1 2026, Tencent Music stopped disclosing quarterly user data.**

When a company voluntarily stops disclosing a key metric, it usually means the metric **looks bad and is expected to keep looking bad**. Management would rather take the hit of being called "opaque" than have the market watch MAU keep declining every quarter.

### But the boundaries of the damage also need to be recognized

**The users Tencent Music is losing are mostly "low-value users" — free users or low-frequency users.**

The data evidence: MAU is falling, but paying users are rising.

| Time | MAU (hundred million) | Paying users (hundred million) | Pay rate |
|------|----------|-------------|--------|
| 2023 | 5.84 (est.) | 1.07 | ~18% |
| 2024 | 5.56 | 1.21 | 21.8% |
| 2025 | 5.28 | 1.274 | **24.1%** |

MAU is down, yet paying users are actually up — indicating that most of those who left were non-paying users. In other words: **Tencent Music is going through a "user quality upgrade" — low-value users are flowing to Qishui, while high-value users are staying and becoming more willing to pay.**

But this "good news" carries one lurking concern: **what if Qishui Music starts building out paid membership at scale?** Currently Qishui is ad-driven with paid subscriptions as a secondary function. Once Qishui starts taking paid subscriptions seriously, will the 8-yuan-vs-18-yuan price gap start eroding Tencent Music's paying user base?

This is currently the biggest unknown.

## IV. Where are Tencent Music's defenses?

Facing Qishui Music's advance, Tencent Music is not defenseless. It has four lines of defense:

### Defense line one: Depth of licensed content

| Platform | Music library size | Gap |
|------|---------|------|
| Tencent Music | **260 million tracks** | Baseline |
| Qishui Music | ~50 million tracks | **Only 19% of TME's** |

260 million tracks vs. 50 million — a five-fold gap.

For users who "listen to whatever pops up while scrolling Douyin," 50 million tracks is enough. But for deep music users with specific listening habits who need to find a particular artist's particular album, library depth is a hard barrier.

**In particular, classic Chinese-language catalogs, Japanese and Korean music, and independent-artist works are often unavailable on Qishui.** And that's precisely what paying users care about most.

### Defense line two: Super VIP premiumization

Qishui Music is taking the low-price route, Tencent Music is taking the premiumization route. The two companies' target user bases are diverging:

| Dimension | Qishui Music | Tencent Music Super VIP |
|------|---------|------------|
| Monthly fee | 8 yuan | 28-30 yuan |
| Target users | Price-sensitive, light listeners | Music enthusiasts, multi-device users |
| Audio quality | Standard | Lossless + Dolby Atmos |
| Use cases | Mainly mobile | Mobile + car + speakers + headphones |

This divergence implies: **the two may not be entirely zero-sum competitors, but rather each serving its own niche at a different price point.** Like Uniqlo and Hermès not competing in the same lane — Qishui is grabbing the "low-price tier," Tencent Music is defending the "mid-to-high-price tier."

**Counterargument**: The Uniqlo-Hermès analogy holds, but there's a large middle ground between "low-end" and "high-end." Regular members paying 15-18 yuan a month — not price-insensitive, but not enthusiasts either — could be drawn to the 8-yuan Qishui. If that middle ground erodes, Tencent Music's regular-member count could decline even as Super VIP grows.

### Defense line three: The WeChat ecosystem

Tencent Music sits behind Tencent. Music sharing on WeChat, music in WeChat status updates, music in Channels (Video Accounts) — these scenarios all naturally direct users to Tencent Music, not to Qishui.

WeChat has 1.3 billion DAU, nearly twice Douyin's. Although music-consumption scenarios on WeChat are less frequent than on Douyin, **as a social-sharing gateway, the WeChat ecosystem is a reach Qishui Music cannot match.**

### Defense line four: Tencent's broader content ecosystem

Tencent isn't just music — it also has Tencent Video (variety shows, drama soundtracks), China Literature (audiobook adaptations), and Tencent Games (game soundtracks). This content can be cross-leveraged within Tencent's ecosystem, forming a "music+" model.

**In particular, the soon-to-be-completed acquisition of Ximalaya — the combination of music + audiobooks + podcasts is something Qishui Music currently cannot offer.** This topic will be explored in the next article.

## V. Endgame scenarios for Tencent Music and Qishui Music

Three possible endgames:

### Scenario one: Each holds its ground (most probable)

Qishui Music occupies the "low-price + light-use + young user" market, while Tencent Music defends the "mid-to-high-end + deep-catalog + paying user" market. Both stabilize their respective MAU, forming a "one dominant, one strong, one ordinary" three-way split (TME #1 + Qishui #2 + NetEase Cloud #3).

Under this scenario, Tencent Music's MAU might stabilize around 450-500 million, but paying users keep growing and ARPPU keeps rising. **Limited impact on profit.**

### Scenario two: Qishui succeeds at large-scale monetization

After accumulating 200 million+ MAU, Qishui Music starts taking paid subscriptions seriously. Riding its 8-yuan low price plus Douyin traffic, it rapidly builds paying users up to 30-50 million.

Under this scenario, Tencent Music's paying-user growth could slow or even stall. The room for ARPPU improvement would also be squeezed — if a competitor sells at 8 yuan, it's hard to raise your own price to 25 yuan. **Significant impact on profit.**

### Scenario three: Qishui's growth peaks and monetization fails

Qishui Music's growth depends on Douyin's traffic funnel. If Douyin's own growth slows (its DAU is already 700 million+, leaving limited room to grow), Qishui's incremental users will also slow. Meanwhile, the limitation of a 50-million-track library becomes increasingly apparent — after users move from the "trying it out" phase into "daily use," they find many songs unavailable and may flow back to Tencent Music.

Under this scenario, Tencent Music's MAU stabilizes or even rebounds, and Qishui Music stays confined to its position as "Douyin's music player," posing no fundamental threat. **This is the most favorable outcome for Tencent Music.**

---

## VI. Conclusion: Is Qishui Music a "mortal threat" or a "minor irritant"?

Honestly: **somewhere in between, but leaning more toward "a competitor to be taken seriously" than "a mortal threat."**

Evidence supporting this judgment:

**Evidence leaning toward "must be taken seriously"**:
- MAU of 156 million and still growing — not a size that can be ignored
- 82% of users from Douyin — customer-acquisition cost near zero, and this advantage won't disappear
- 8-yuan monthly subscription — genuinely attractive to price-sensitive users
- Tencent Music's MAU has declined for 16 straight quarters — the damage is already happening

**Evidence leaning toward "not a mortal threat"**:
- Library of 50 million tracks vs. 260 million — a real weakness for deep-catalog users
- Qishui has not yet seriously pursued paid monetization — if it truly does, the results may not be favorable (its users came for free content)
- Tencent Music's paying users are actually growing — the users being lost are low-value ones
- Super VIP premiumization and the WeChat ecosystem are reaches Qishui cannot match

**The single most central judgment**: How big a threat Qishui Music poses to Tencent Music depends on what ByteDance ultimately wants Qishui to become — "Douyin's music player" (a traffic-funneling tool), or "an independent music subscription platform" (a direct competitor). The evidence currently leans toward the former, but ByteDance could change strategy at any time.
