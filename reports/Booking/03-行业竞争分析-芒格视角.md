# Booking Holdings (BKNG) Industry Competition Analysis

> Analytical lens: competitive landscape and moat
> Date: 2026-05-16

## I. OTA Industry Competitive Landscape

### Global duopoly + regional challengers

The online travel agency (OTA) industry shows a clear "duopoly + regional players" structure. Booking Holdings and Expedia Group together account for more than 50% of global online travel transaction volume.

| Company | 2025 Revenue | Market Cap | Core Markets | Differentiated Positioning |
|------|---------|------|---------|-----------|
| **Booking Holdings** | $26.9 billion | $137 billion | Europe, global | Accommodation-centric, full-trip platform |
| **Expedia Group** | $13.7 billion | $32.5 billion | North America | Multi-brand portfolio (Hotels.com/Vrbo) |
| **Airbnb** | $11.9 billion | $78.5 billion | Global | Alternative accommodation / experiential travel |
| **Trip.com** | $8.2 billion | $34.2 billion | China, Asia-Pacific | Leader in Chinese outbound travel |
| **Google** | - | - | Global | Traffic gateway / metasearch |

Sources: public company filings; [Mize OTA Market Share](https://mize.tech/blog/online-travel-agencies-market-share-across-the-world/); [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/travel-app-market/)

### Deep dive on each competitor

**Expedia: perpetual runner-up**
Expedia has a business model similar to Booking's, but execution has consistently lagged. Its revenue is only half of Booking's, its net margin is about 8% (versus Booking's 20.1%), and its market cap is only 24% of Booking's. Expedia holds some advantage in North America, but trails across globalization, margins, and technology investment. Its multi-brand strategy (Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, etc.) offers broad coverage but has also fragmented brand recognition and reduced synergy.

**Airbnb: a friend on a different track**
Airbnb pioneered the home-sharing/short-term-rental category, and while it competes with traditional OTAs, the relationship is more complementary than adversarial. Booking.com also carries substantial supply of homes and apartments, competing directly with Airbnb in alternative accommodation. But Airbnb's brand positioning leans more toward "experience" and "living like a local," and its user base differs noticeably. Airbnb's 34x P/E valuation reflects the market's recognition of its brand premium, but its revenue scale and margins both fall short of Booking's.

**Trip.com: Asia's fortress**
Trip.com holds a near-monopoly in the Chinese market, and its 8x P/E reflects the valuation discount applied to Chinese ADRs. But Trip.com is actively expanding internationally — international revenue as a share of the total rose to roughly 40% in 2025. In Southeast Asia and Japan/Korea, Trip.com competes directly with Booking (via Agoda).

**Google: the elephant in the room**
This is the force most worth watching closely. Google controls the world's search traffic gateway — in the second quarter of 2025 alone, major OTAs together spent $5.2 billion on marketing, most of which flowed to Google. In November 2025, Google announced it was developing an "agentic" travel booking tool built on Gemini AI; Booking and Expedia shares fell 4-7% that day.

Although Google has publicly stated it has "no intention of becoming an OTA," it controls infrastructure such as Maps, Flights, and Hotels search, and combined with AI capability, has the technical means to bypass OTAs and connect directly with users and suppliers. According to a Bernstein analyst report, if AI chat interfaces integrate real-time pricing and payment functionality, as many as 40% of travelers would be willing to book directly through AI.

Sources: [Skift - Google Clarifies AI Booking Plans](https://skift.com/2025/11/20/google-agentic-ai-travel-booking-no-intention-become-ota/); [HBR - Gen AI Threatening Online Travel](https://hbr.org/2026/01/gen-ai-is-threatening-the-platforms-that-dominate-online-travel)

## II. Moat Assessment

### Moat types and strength

| Moat Type | Strength | Notes |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Network effects** | Very strong | The classic two-sided-platform flywheel: a transaction base of 1.235 billion room-nights per year is nearly impossible to replicate |
| **Economies of scale** | Strong | $26.9 billion in revenue spreads technology and operating costs across a huge base, evidenced by an 87.4% gross margin |
| **Brand recognition** | Strong | In Europe, "book a hotel" is mentally equated with "Booking.com" |
| **Switching costs** | Moderate-to-strong | Supply side: small and mid-sized hotels depend on Booking for customer acquisition, with 30-50% of their bookings coming from the platform; demand side: accumulated reviews and preference data create meaningful stickiness |
| **Data barriers** | Strong | Decades of accumulated hundreds of millions of reviews, user preferences, and pricing data cannot be replicated |
| **Regulatory barriers** | Weak | Payment licensing and compliance requirements around the world raise costs for new entrants |

### Vulnerabilities in the moat

Network effects in the travel industry are not as exclusive as they are for social networks. Users can compare prices across Booking, Expedia, and Airbnb simultaneously — "multi-homing" is common. This means the moat, while wide, is not a Great Wall — more like a broad river that can nonetheless be waded across.

## III. "Inverting It" — What Would Destroy This Business?

### Scenario One: Google Actually Enters the OTA Business

If Google uses AI search to directly integrate real-time pricing and one-click booking, users might no longer need to open Booking.com at all. This would fundamentally cut off Booking's traffic source — currently, Booking's paid search marketing spend exceeds 20% of revenue, most of which flows to Google. If Google shifts from "traffic seller" to "transaction broker," Booking's customer-acquisition cost structure would be completely upended.

**Counterargument:** Google has tried multiple times over the past decade to build a travel vertical without success (Google Trips was shut down). Running an OTA requires deep supplier-relationship management and customer-service capability, which is not Google's core strength. Moreover, competing directly with OTAs would cost Google the enormous advertising revenue OTAs currently pay it, creating a conflict of interest.

### Scenario Two: Direct Hotel Bookings Continue to Rise

Large hotel chains such as Marriott and Hilton have in recent years pushed a "book direct" campaign, using loyalty points and best-price guarantees to lure users away from OTAs. If large chains succeed in raising the direct-booking share from roughly 30% today to 50%+, Booking's addressable market would shrink significantly.

**Counterargument:** Large hotel chains account for only about 30% of global accommodation supply; the remaining 70% — independent hotels and alternative accommodations — depends heavily on OTAs for customer acquisition. Booking's strength in Europe stems precisely from that market being dominated by independent hotels.

### Scenario Three: A Deep Global Recession Suppresses Travel Demand

During the 2008 financial crisis, travel spending fell about 15%; a recession of similar magnitude would meaningfully hit Booking's revenue and profit. But Booking proved its survival capability during the 2020 pandemic (when travel essentially shut down) — its asset-light model allowed a rapid recovery.

### Scenario Four: Regulatory Risk

The EU has already discussed capping the commission rates OTAs charge hotels. If major markets enact similar regulations (e.g., a 10% commission cap), it would directly hit Booking's revenue.

### Probability Assessment of the Biggest Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Time Frame |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Google AI disruption | Moderate (20-30%) | High | 3-5 years |
| Direct-booking erosion | Low (10-15%) | Moderate | Ongoing, gradual |
| Economic recession | Moderate (25-30%) | Moderate-to-high | Cyclical |
| Regulatory restrictions | Low (10%) | Moderate | Uncertain |

## IV. Final Judgment on the Competitive Landscape

**Booking Holdings occupies a "good business but not a perfect one" competitive position.** It holds the widest moat in the OTA industry, but that moat faces new challenges in the AI era.

The single most important point from a competitive standpoint: **Booking must not only beat Expedia and Airbnb, but also prove it can remain irreplaceable in an era when Google AI is reshaping how people search and book.** The Connected Trip strategy and Booking's own AI investment are the right direction — if Booking can successfully transform itself from a "booking tool fed by search-engine traffic" into a "travel super-app users open directly," its moat could actually widen in the AI era.

The outcome of this competition should become clear within the next 3-5 years.
