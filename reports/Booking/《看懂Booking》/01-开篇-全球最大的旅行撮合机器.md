## A set of numbers that will make you rethink Booking

There's a company that clears 3.38 million hotel room-nights through its platform every day, yet doesn't own a single bed. Its gross margin is higher than Kweichow Moutai's, its return on invested capital beats 95% of listed companies worldwide, it generates $9.1 billion in free cash flow every year, and it quietly plows 70% of that back into buying back its own stock — year after year.

It's called Booking Holdings. Many Chinese investors barely know the name, but in the global travel market, it is the undisputed leader.

Core numbers:

| Metric | Value | Scale comparison |
|------|------|---------|
| 2025 total revenue | **$26.9 billion** (about 194 billion yuan) | 3.3x Trip.com's $8.2 billion |
| 2025 total bookings (gross bookings value) | **$186.1 billion** | Largest in the global OTA industry |
| 2025 room nights booked | **1.235 billion room-nights** | Averaging 3.38 million room-nights transacted globally through Booking every day |
| 2025 free cash flow | **$9.1 billion** | Free cash flow margin of **33.8%** |
| Gross margin | **87.4%** | Higher than Kweichow Moutai |
| ROIC | **43.7%** | Industry median is only **3.6%** |
| Market cap (May 2026) | **~$130-137 billion** | Largest market cap among global OTAs |
| Share price (post-split) | **~$151.6** | Completed a 25:1 stock split in April 2026 |

Data sources: Booking Holdings 10-K annual report, Q1 2026 earnings report, GuruFocus, MacroTrends

Put these numbers together — **a company with an 87% gross margin, ROIC above 40%, and $9.1 billion in annual free cash flow that owns not a single hotel room and not a single airplane.**

Every dollar it earns comes from "connection" — linking millions of hotels worldwide with hundreds of millions of travelers, taking a **15-18%** commission on every transaction.

## Who exactly is Booking?

Booking Holdings operates six brands:

| Brand | Core business | Market position |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Booking.com** | Accommodation booking | Number one in Europe, the world's largest hotel-booking platform |
| **Priceline** | Flights + hotels | U.S. market, built on the "Name Your Own Price" model |
| **Agoda** | Accommodation booking | Southeast and East Asia |
| **KAYAK** | Meta-search comparison | Global flight/hotel price-comparison engine |
| **OpenTable** | Restaurant reservations | The largest restaurant-reservation platform in the U.S. |
| **Rentalcars.com** | Car rental | Global car-rental comparison |

Of these, **Booking.com contributes the vast majority of revenue**. In Europe, "Booking.com" is nearly synonymous with "booking a hotel online" — as natural as a Chinese consumer saying "let's check Trip.com."

But Booking's ambition doesn't stop there. CEO Glenn Fogel's **Connected Trip** strategy aims to turn Booking from a "hotel-booking tool" into a "travel operating system" — letting users complete every leg of a trip, from lodging to flights to car rentals to attraction tickets, on one platform. In 2025, gross bookings for the flights business reached **$16.8 billion**, up **37%** year over year; the attractions/experiences business grew roughly **80%** year over year.

## Three common misconceptions about Booking

### Misconception one: Booking is a "hotel-booking website"

This is the most superficial view.

Open the 2025 annual report and Booking's revenue breaks down like this:

| Revenue type | Amount | Share | Nature |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Merchant revenue | **$17.76 billion** | **66%** | The platform processes payment and takes a commission plus payment service fees |
| Agency revenue | **$7.97 billion** | **30%** | The traditional commission model, with the hotel collecting payment at the front desk |
| Advertising and other | **$1.19 billion** | **4%** | KAYAK/OpenTable advertising plus restaurant reservations |

Data source: Booking Holdings 10-K annual report

**Merchant revenue's share jumped from 60% in 2024 to 66% in 2025** — meaning Booking is upgrading from an "information intermediary" to "transaction infrastructure." Users pay Booking first, and Booking then settles with the hotel. This shift matters for two reasons: (1) Booking gains access to payment data; (2) it gains a "float" advantage — users pay upfront, hotels get paid later, and the cash sits with Booking in the interim.

**The essence of this business isn't "helping you book a room" — it's "an algorithmic platform matching global travel supply and demand" — earning money through commissions and payment service fees, with an 87% gross margin, higher than most software companies.**

### Misconception two: Booking and Trip.com are the same kind of company

On the surface, they do look alike — both are OTAs (online travel agencies), both earn money through commissions. But the differences are substantial:

| Dimension | Booking | Trip.com |
|------|---------|---------|
| Core market | Europe + global | China + Asia-Pacific |
| 2025 revenue | $26.9 billion | $8.2 billion |
| Gross margin | 87.4% | ~80% |
| ROIC | 43.7% | ~15-20% |
| Market cap | ~$137 billion | ~$34.2 billion |
| Valuation (forward P/E) | 14.7x | ~12x |

Data sources: respective company earnings reports, GuruFocus

**Booking's scale is more than 3x Trip.com's, and its capital efficiency (ROIC) is 2-3x Trip.com's.** More importantly, Booking's supply-side moat is deeper — Europe has a huge number of independent small and mid-sized hotels, and **30-50%** of their bookings come from Booking.com, with almost no alternative. China's hotel market, by contrast, is more concentrated (chains like Home Inns and Huazhu account for a large share), so Trip.com faces fiercer competition.

**Counterargument:** Trip.com's valuation discount (forward P/E of 12x vs. Booking's 14.7x) partly reflects the broader discount applied to Chinese ADRs rather than a gap in fundamentals. Trip.com's international business is growing rapidly, with international revenue now accounting for roughly 40% of the total. In the Asia-Pacific market, Trip.com's Trip.com-branded international arm competes directly with Booking's Agoda, and the long-term competitive landscape is still unsettled.

### Misconception three: Booking is "old economy," with no growth story left

Many investors classify Booking as a "mature-stage company" — big and stable, but with no room to grow.

The facts say otherwise:

- **2025 revenue of $26.9 billion, up 13.4% year over year** — not a low growth rate for a company with a market cap in the hundreds of billions
- **Q1 2026 revenue of $5.53 billion, up 16% year over year** — accelerating
- **Q1 2026 net income of $1.1 billion, up 225% year over year** (against a low base in Q1 2025)
- The global online travel market was worth roughly **$975 billion** in 2025 and is projected to reach **$2.19 trillion** by 2030 — the market still has room to more than double

Data sources: Booking Q1 2026 earnings report, Grand View Research

**Counterargument:** Full-year 2026 guidance calls for only "low- to mid-single-digit growth," reflecting geopolitical and currency headwinds. If the global economy enters a recession, travel — as discretionary spending — would be among the first casualties. The growth story rests on the premise that "global travel keeps trending upward over the long run" — true for the past 50 years, but that doesn't rule out a structural break over the next five.

## Understanding Booking requires answering four questions

If you had to define Booking in one sentence, the closest version might be:

> **Booking = the world's largest travel-matching algorithmic platform (with extremely strong two-sided network effects) + a free-cash-flow printing press ($9.1 billion a year) + the biggest uncertainty of the AI era (will Google step in and eat its lunch?)**

Layer these three things together and you get Booking's investment case. To understand it, at minimum you need to answer the following four questions:

But don't rush to a conclusion — **a forward P/E of 14.7x could mean "cheap enough to buy," or it could mean "the market is pricing in AI disruption risk and slowing growth."**
