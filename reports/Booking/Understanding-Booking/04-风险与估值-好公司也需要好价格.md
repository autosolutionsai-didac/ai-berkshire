## Let's Lay Out the Risks First

The first three installments covered how good Booking is. This one runs the other direction — let's lay out everything that could make this investment lose money, before we talk about price.

A good company is not the same as a good investment. Pay the wrong price and you still lose money. Before discussing valuation, let's spread the risks out on the table.

### Risk One: Google AI Reshaping the Search Gateway (Medium probability / High impact)

The previous installment covered this in detail. Here's a probability framework:

| Scenario | Probability | Impact on Booking |
|------|------|-----------------|
| Google AI goes all-in on becoming an OTA | **10-15%** | Catastrophic — Booking loses its traffic source |
| Google AI diverts part of OTA traffic | **30-40%** | Moderate — revenue growth slows, margins come under pressure |
| Google continues as an ad platform, not a direct competitor | **40-50%** | Limited — Booking's customer acquisition cost may rise |
| Booking's own AI transition succeeds and it becomes the new gateway | **10-20%** | Positive — the moat actually widens |

**Key judgment:** The probability of Google going all-in on becoming an OTA is not high (the conflict of interest is too large), but "partial diversion" is almost certain to happen. The question is whether the diversion is **10%** or **30%** — that gap has a huge impact on Booking's valuation.

**The counterargument:** The AI threat to OTAs may be overstated. Travel booking is not a purely information-matching scenario — it involves payments, refunds, rebooking, customer service, and other complex processes. AI search can help users find a hotel, but "completing the transaction plus after-sales service" still requires an OTA's infrastructure. Booking's more likely fate isn't "being replaced" but "moving from front office to back office" — margins might decline, but transaction volume won't necessarily shrink.

### Risk Two: Global Economic Recession (Medium probability / Medium-high impact)

Travel is a textbook discretionary expense. Historical data shows:

| Economic event | Change in global travel spending | Booking's performance |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| 2008-2009 financial crisis | Down about **15%** | Revenue declined but remained profitable |
| 2020 COVID-19 pandemic | Down about **60%** | Revenue plunged 55%, but did not turn unprofitable (GAAP basis) |
| 2022-2023 high inflation | Growth slowed | Revenue grew against the trend |

Sources: MacroTrends, Booking's historical filings

**Booking didn't come close to bankruptcy even in 2020's pandemic — near-total travel "zeroing out."** The asset-light model was key: no hotels to maintain, no planes to ground, so costs could be compressed quickly.

But that doesn't mean recession risk can be ignored. In 2026, geopolitical tension and tariff uncertainty have already led Booking to lower its full-year guidance to "low-to-mid single-digit growth." If the global economy truly enters a recession, Booking's revenue could decline by **10-20%**, and the stock's drawdown would be even larger.

### Risk Three: Regulatory Pressure (Low probability / Medium impact)

- The EU is discussing caps on OTA commissions
- Some cities are restricting short-term rentals (affecting the supply of alternative accommodations)
- Data privacy regulations are tightening in various countries

None of these risks have materialized yet, but they warrant continued monitoring.

### Risk Four: Valuation Compression from Slowing Growth (already happening)

Booking's revenue grew **13.4%** in 2025 and **16%** in Q1 2026, but full-year guidance is only "low-to-mid single digits." If the market decides Booking has shifted from a "growth company" to a "mature company," the valuation midpoint could compress from 20-25x P/E down to 15-18x P/E.

**The counterargument:** Even if growth slows to 5-8%, Booking remains a cash-flow machine. With a 34% free cash flow margin plus 4-5% annual share-count reduction from buybacks, per-share value naturally compounds at 4-5% a year even if the top line doesn't grow at all. For long-term holders, slowing growth is not a disaster — the key question is whether margins and capital-allocation discipline can be sustained.

## Management: An Underrated CEO and an Aggressive Buyback Machine

### Glenn Fogel's Track Record

| Decision | Timing | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Drove the merchant-model transition | 2018-present | **Excellent** — merchant revenue share rose from 30% to 66% |
| Connected Trip strategy | 2019-present | **Directionally right** — flight bookings up 37%, but profit contribution still unproven |
| Aggressive buybacks | Ongoing | **Excellent** — shares outstanding cut by more than 40% over 10 years |
| AI strategic build-out | 2023-present | **Good** — layoffs plus reinvestment in AI; direction is right, results still to be proven |
| 25:1 stock split | April 2026 | **Neutral** — improves retail accessibility, doesn't change the fundamentals |

Fogel isn't the kind of headline-grabbing celebrity CEO. He's low-key, cautious, and focused on the long term. But his track record demonstrates one thing: **this is a management team that understands capital allocation.**

### The Buyback Machine: Shareholders' "Invisible Dividend"

Booking's capital return strategy is exceptionally aggressive — a key point of contrast with many tech companies:

| Year | Buyback amount | Change in shares outstanding | Dividend |
|------|---------|-----------|------|
| 2023 | **$5.3 billion** | -6% | — |
| 2024 | **$6.0 billion** | -5% | $240 million |
| 2025 | **$6.4 billion** | -4% | $260 million |
| Q1 2026 | **$3.6 billion** | -3% | $70 million |

Sources: Booking's filings, StockTitan

**In 2025, buybacks of $6.4 billion consumed about 70% of that year's free cash flow.** Add in dividends, and **nearly all of the free cash flow was returned to shareholders.**

What does that mean? **Even if Booking's stock price doesn't rise a single cent, shares outstanding shrink by 4-5% every year — so the profit and cash flow attributable to each share you hold keeps growing at a 4-5% rate.** Add the business's own 5-10% growth, and the long-term annualized return could land around **10-15%**.

As of Q1 2026, the remaining buyback authorization stands at **$18.2 billion** — equivalent to 13% of the current market cap. Management shows no sign of stopping.

**The counterargument:** Large-scale buybacks destroy value when the stock is overvalued. Booking's buybacks over the past few years have averaged a purchase price in the $100-150 range (post-split) — if the share price falls sharply in the future, those buybacks won't look capital-efficient in hindsight. Furthermore, devoting most of FCF to buybacks leaves limited funding for AI investment and new-business expansion — if the AI arms race escalates, Booking may need to rebalance its capital-allocation priorities.

## Valuation: Three Scenarios, Three Prices

### Current Valuation Positioning

| Metric | Booking's current value | Historical comparison | Peer comparison |
|------|-------------|---------|---------|
| TTM P/E | **~25x** | 3-year average ~31x | Expedia ~19x |
| Forward P/E | **~14.7x** | Lowest range in 5 years | Airbnb ~29x |
| FCF yield | **~6.6%** | Historically medium-to-high | Trip.com ~5% |
| EV/EBITDA (Forward) | **~12x** | Reasonably low | Industry median ~14x |

Sources: MacroTrends, GuruFocus, company filings

**A forward P/E of 14.7x sits in the lowest range of the past five years.** For a company with ROIC above 40%, revenue growth above 10%, and a 34% free cash flow margin, this valuation deserves serious attention — it's either "a bargain the market has mispriced" or "the market rationally pricing in AI disruption risk and slowing growth."

### Three-Scenario Valuation

**Conservative scenario (bear case)**

| Assumption | Value |
|------|------|
| 2026-2030 revenue CAGR | 5% |
| Steady-state free cash flow margin | 30% |
| Terminal valuation multiple | 15x FCF |
| **Fair market cap** | **~$105 billion** |
| Implied post-split share price | **~$115** |
| Upside/downside vs. current | **-24%** |

Trigger conditions: global recession + significant Google AI traffic diversion + regulatory pressure on commission rates

**Base scenario (neutral)**

| Assumption | Value |
|------|------|
| 2026-2030 revenue CAGR | 8-10% |
| Steady-state free cash flow margin | 33% |
| Terminal valuation multiple | 20x FCF |
| **Fair market cap** | **~$170-190 billion** |
| Implied post-split share price | **~$185-210** |
| Upside/downside vs. current | **+22% to +39%** |

Assumptions: steady growth in global travel + Connected Trip lifting average order value + AI expanding EBITDA margin by 100-125 basis points per year + continued buybacks of 4-5% of shares outstanding

**Optimistic scenario (bull case)**

| Assumption | Value |
|------|------|
| 2026-2030 revenue CAGR | 12-15% |
| Steady-state free cash flow margin | 36% |
| Terminal valuation multiple | 25x FCF |
| **Fair market cap** | **~$280-320 billion** |
| Implied post-split share price | **~$310-350** |
| Upside/downside vs. current | **+105% to +131%** |

Trigger conditions: Connected Trip fully succeeds + accelerated penetration in Asia-Pacific + AI substantially improves operating efficiency + Booking becomes travel's "super app"

### Margin of Safety Assessment

**Using the base-case fair value of $170-190 billion, the current market cap of ~$137 billion implies roughly 22-39% upside.**

**Even in the conservative scenario, a floor valuation of ~$105 billion implies a maximum downside of about 24%** — a manageable drawdown for a top-tier platform business with ROIC above 40% and a 34% free cash flow margin.

More importantly, buybacks provide a "safety cushion": **a 4-5% annual reduction in shares outstanding is effectively management continuously "buying more on your behalf."** Even if the stock price doesn't rise, your per-share intrinsic value keeps growing at a 4-5% rate.

**The counterargument:** The core assumption underlying all three scenarios — a free cash flow margin sustained at 30%+ — may be too optimistic. If Google AI truly disrupts the search gateway, Booking's customer acquisition cost will rise sharply, and the free cash flow margin could fall to 20-25%. In that scenario, even if revenue keeps growing, margin compression would still shrink the valuation significantly. Additionally, the assumed terminal multiple of 15-25x FCF presumes Booking remains a "premium platform business" — if it gets downgraded to an "AI backend supplier," a reasonable multiple might be only 10-12x.

## Full-Series Summary: What Kind of Investment Is Booking, Really?

After four articles, the picture of Booking should now be clear.

**The good:**

- The world's largest travel-matching platform, with powerful two-sided network effects
- Gross margin of **87.4%**, ROIC of **43.7%**, free cash flow margin of **33.8%** — top-tier financial quality by global standards
- Annual free cash flow of **$9.1 billion**, more than 70% of which is returned to shareholders via buybacks
- Shares outstanding cut by more than **40%** over 10 years — excellent capital-allocation discipline from management
- Global travel penetration still has room to rise, providing a decade-plus structural growth runway
- Forward P/E of **14.7x** sits in the lowest range of the past five years

**The bad:**

- Google AI is the biggest uncertainty — it could reshape industry traffic allocation within 3-5 years
- 2026 growth guidance has slowed to "low-to-mid single digits"
- The moat is wide but not exclusive — users can compare prices, and hotels can list on multiple platforms
- The Connected Trip strategy is directionally sound but its profit contribution has not yet fully materialized
- Travel is discretionary spending and swings heavily during economic downturns

**The final judgment comes down to how you answer one core question:**

> **Do you believe that, in an era when AI is reshaping the search gateway, Booking can still remain irreplaceable?**

If your answer is "most likely yes" — then a forward P/E of 14.7x, an FCF yield of 6.6%, plus 4-5% annual per-share value accretion from buybacks, offer a decent starting point for long-term returns.

If your answer is "uncertain" — then waiting for more evidence (Connected Trip progress, the actual impact of Google AI) before deciding is a perfectly reasonable choice too.

**Investing isn't a choice between "buy" and "don't buy" — it's a trade-off between "at what price" and "what evidence do I need."**
