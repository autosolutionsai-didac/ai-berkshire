## The "War Map" of the OTA Industry

Every time you open Booking.com to book a hotel, behind the scenes is a war that's been running for twenty years — the fight isn't over hotel inventory, but over who owns the traveler's first click when they open a search box.

The landscape of the online travel industry isn't "winner take all" — it's "duopoly plus regional strongholds." Here's how the major global players compare in scale:

| Company | 2025 revenue | Market cap (May 2026) | Core market | Core advantage |
|------|-----------|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Booking Holdings** | **$26.9 billion** | **~$137 billion** | Europe, global | Largest accommodation inventory + network effects |
| Expedia Group | $13.7 billion | ~$32.5 billion | North America | Multi-brand portfolio |
| Airbnb | $11.9 billion | ~$78.5 billion | Global | Alternative accommodations + experiences |
| Trip.com | $8.2 billion | ~$34.2 billion | China, Asia-Pacific | Dominance of Chinese outbound travel |
| **Google** | **—** | **—** | **Global** | **Traffic gateway + AI** |

Sources: company filings, CompaniesMarketCap

**Notice that last row. Google's revenue doesn't come from the OTA business, but it's the sword hanging over every player in this industry.**

## Four Rivals, Four Kinds of Threat

### Rival One: Expedia — The Perpetual Follower

Expedia runs a business model nearly identical to Booking's, but its execution has always lagged:

| Metric | Booking | Expedia |
|------|---------|---------|
| Revenue | $26.9 billion | $13.7 billion |
| Net margin | 20.1% | ~8% |
| Market cap | ~$137 billion | ~$32.5 billion |
| Brand strategy | Single core brand | Multi-brand portfolio (Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, etc.) |

Source: company filings

Expedia's problem is "fragmented brand recognition and low synergy" — it has too many brands, users don't know which one to use, and internal resources compete against each other. Booking only needs the world to remember one name: Booking.com.

**Booking leads Expedia in nearly every market outside North America.** Expedia's advantage is confined to the U.S. vacation-rental market (Vrbo).

**Conclusion: Expedia is not a real threat to Booking. It's more of a follower whose market share keeps getting chipped away.**

### Rival Two: Airbnb — A Neighbor in a Different Lane

The relationship between Airbnb and Booking is more nuanced — **they compete and complement each other at the same time.**

| Dimension | Booking.com | Airbnb |
|------|------------|--------|
| Core supply | Hotels + homestays + apartments | Homestays + unique lodging |
| User profile | Business + leisure, efficiency-focused | Mostly leisure, experience-focused |
| Brand positioning | "Find the best price" | "Live like a local" |
| Valuation | Forward P/E ~14.7x | Forward P/E ~29x |

Source: company filings

Airbnb's roughly **29x forward P/E** valuation premium reflects the market's recognition of its brand distinctiveness — it's not just a "booking platform," it's a "way of traveling." But on fundamentals, Airbnb's revenue is less than half of Booking's, and its margins are lower too.

**The overlap between the two is in "alternative accommodations"** — Booking.com also carries a large supply of homestays and apartments. But Airbnb holds an irreplaceable brand mindshare in the "unique stays" category (treehouses, castles, lakeside cabins).

**Conclusion: Airbnb is more like "a neighbor in a different lane" — there's overlapping competition in the alternative-accommodations category, but overall the two are expanding the whole online-travel pie together, so the threat to Booking is limited.**

### Rival Three: Trip.com — The Asian Fortress

Trip.com is nearly a monopoly in the Chinese market, and it's actively expanding internationally:

- International revenue rose to about **40%** of the total in 2025
- In Southeast Asia and in Japan/Korea, Trip.com goes head-to-head with Booking's Agoda
- Its ~12x forward P/E reflects the discount typically applied to Chinese ADRs

**The counterargument:** The pace of Trip.com's internationalization shouldn't be underestimated. As Chinese outbound travel has recovered, Trip.com — leveraging its massive domestic user base and strong supply-chain integration — could challenge Booking's position across Asia-Pacific. In popular destinations for Chinese tourists such as Japan and Thailand in particular, Trip.com's market share is growing quickly.

**Conclusion: Trip.com is an unshakeable ruler in the Chinese market, and a rival Booking needs to take seriously across Asia-Pacific. But globally, the threat is concentrated among Chinese outbound travelers and doesn't shake Booking's foundation in Europe.**

### Rival Four: Google — The Elephant in the Room

**This is the threat most worth examining in depth.**

The other competitors Booking faces are all "peer-to-peer contests" — everyone racing within the OTA lane. Google is different — it stands at the entrance to the track, controlling "who gets seen by users" in the first place.

A key set of data points:

- In 2025, global OTAs spent a combined **$5.2 billion** on marketing in the second quarter alone, with most of it flowing to Google
- Booking's paid-search marketing spend is **more than 20%** of revenue, with most of it going to Google
- In November 2025, Google announced it was developing an "agentic" travel-booking tool based on Gemini AI; Booking and Expedia shares fell **4-7%** that same day

Sources: Skift, HBR

**Google's threat operates on two levels:**

**Level one: AI search diversion.** When a user tells Google, "find me hotels for a five-day Italy itinerary," the AI gives recommendations and prices directly — the user may never need to open Booking.com at all. According to a Bernstein analyst survey, as many as **40%** of travelers said they'd be willing to book directly through an AI chat interface.

**Level two: The ultimate disruption of agentic AI.** If an AI agent can autonomously compare prices, book, rebook, and process refunds, OTAs would be downgraded from "platforms users interact with directly" to "backend APIs that AI agents call" — and margins would be squeezed hard.

**The counterargument — Google won't lightly jump into running an OTA business:**

1. **Conflict of interest:** OTAs are among Google's largest advertising customer segments. If Google competed directly with OTAs, it would lose that enormous advertising revenue
2. **Historical precedent:** Google has repeatedly tried to build a travel vertical over the past decade without success — Google Trips has been shut down, and Google Travel's usage has fallen well short of expectations
3. **Capability gap:** Running an OTA requires deep supplier-relationship management and customer-service capabilities (refunds, rebookings, dispute handling) — that's not Google's core strength
4. **Booking's response:** Booking is investing in "agentic AI," aiming to build its own moat between users and AI. Its 1.235 billion room-nights of transaction data and hundreds of millions of reviews are a unique asset for training travel AI

**Conclusion: Google is the largest single source of uncertainty Booking faces — not because Google is certain to disrupt the OTA business, but because that possibility cannot be ruled out. How this contest plays out will become gradually clearer over the next 3-5 years.**

## Booking's Response: Become the AI, Rather Than Be Replaced by It

Booking's strategy is clear — "if you can't beat AI, become AI yourself":

| Initiative | Progress | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| AI Trip Planner | Live in 10+ countries | Conversational itinerary planning, staking a claim in the AI search gateway |
| Agentic AI | Launched late 2025 | Lets Booking's AI act on the user's behalf (rebooking, price tracking, etc.) |
| Connected Trip | Multi-category cross-booking growth of 20%+ | Locks users into the Booking ecosystem |
| Organizational restructuring | Layoffs + reinvestment in AI | Management expects AI to expand EBITDA margin by **~125 basis points** in 2026 |

Sources: AInvest, IndexBox

**The core logic: if Booking can transform from "a booking tool that search engines drive traffic to" into "a travel super-app that users open directly," it no longer depends on Google as a traffic gateway — AI would actually widen the moat instead of threatening it.**

Whether this transition will succeed is not yet a settled question.

## The Structural Growth Driver: Global Travel Is Nowhere Near Its Peak

Setting the competitive landscape aside, Booking has a tailwind too — global travel penetration is still rising.

### Three Structural Growth Drivers

**First, rising online penetration in Asia-Pacific.**

Online travel booking rates in Asia-Pacific currently stand at about **50%** and are projected to reach **75%** by 2029. The rise of the middle class in China, India, and Southeast Asia means hundreds of millions of new online travel consumers. Booking is positioned in Asia-Pacific through Agoda, but faces fierce competition from Trip.com.

Source: Grand View Research

**Second, the global online travel market is still growing rapidly.**

| Period | Global online travel market size |
|------|-------------------|
| 2025 | **~$975 billion** |
| 2030 (projected) | **~$2.19 trillion** |
| 5-year CAGR | **~17.5%** |

Sources: Grand View Research, Research and Markets

Even using a more conservative CAGR (9-10%), the market will nearly double within 5 years.

**Third, the rise of the experience economy.**

In the post-pandemic era, consumers place more value on experiences than material goods. Travel is the quintessential "experience purchase" — this trend is irreversible on a global scale.

### Risks That Could Break the Trend

The optimistic side isn't the whole picture. A few factors could interrupt growth:

- **Global recession:** Travel is discretionary spending, the first thing cut when the economy turns down. Travel spending fell about **15%** during the 2008 financial crisis
- **Geopolitical conflict:** Tensions in the Middle East and Eastern Europe affect travel demand in specific regions
- **"Anti-tourism" sentiment:** Cities like Barcelona and Amsterdam have already introduced restrictive measures
- **Climate change:** Could reshape the pattern of global travel destinations

**The counterargument:** The travel industry's long-term growth trend (a 30-year CAGR of ~7%) is very robust, but that doesn't mean every 5-year stretch goes smoothly. Booking's own full-year 2026 guidance is only "low-to-mid single-digit growth" — the growth rate really is slowing in the near term. Investors need to distinguish "long-term structural growth" from "short-term cyclical fluctuations."

## The Competitive Landscape in One Sentence

**Booking's position in the OTA industry closely resembles Google's position in search back in 2015 — the industry's number one, with a wide moat and extremely high margins. But just as Google itself now faces a search-gateway challenge in the AI era, the biggest risk facing Booking comes precisely from Google.**

This isn't a binary question of "will it be disrupted or not." A more precise framing is:

1. **How much OTA traffic will Google AI search divert?** — The most pessimistic estimates run 20-40%; the most optimistic say "very little impact"
2. **Can Booking's own AI transition succeed?** — If Connected Trip plus AI Trip Planner can turn Booking into "the user's first point of entry," AI actually becomes a tailwind
3. **What's the time frame?** — This transformation won't happen tomorrow, but it will become gradually clearer within 3-5 years

The answers to these three questions will determine Booking's future valuation midpoint.
