# Booking Holdings (BKNG) Business Model Analysis

> Analytical lens: business essence and user value
> Date: 2026-05-16

---

## Defining the Business in One Sentence

**Booking is a "transaction-matching platform" between global travelers and accommodation suppliers — the more users there are, the richer the supply becomes; the richer the supply, the more users it draws. It does not own a single hotel room, yet it takes a commission from every transaction.**

The core of this business is "connection." Booking.com connects millions of hotels, homestays, flights, and car-rental suppliers worldwide with hundreds of millions of travelers, charging a 15-18% commission every time it facilitates a transaction. The platform itself carries no inventory risk — a textbook asset-light, high-leverage model.

## Revenue Structure

According to Booking Holdings' 2025 annual report (10-K), full-year revenue was $26.9 billion, from three main sources:

| Revenue Type | 2025 Amount | Share | YoY Growth | Notes |
|---------|-----------|------|---------|------|
| Merchant Revenue | $17.76 billion | 66% | +25.5% | The platform acts as the payment intermediary for transactions, taking a commission and payment-processing fee |
| Agency Revenue | $7.97 billion | 30% | -6.5% | Traditional commission model: users pay at the hotel front desk, and Booking collects a commission from the hotel |
| Advertising and Other | $1.19 billion | 4% | +11.3% | Advertising revenue from KAYAK/OpenTable and restaurant-reservation services |

**Key trend:** Merchant revenue's share rose from 60% in 2024 to 66% in 2025, indicating Booking is accelerating the shift of transactions into its own payment system. This not only improves the user experience (prepaid rate-locking, flexible cancellation) but also increases the platform's control over transaction data, laying the groundwork for cross-selling and the "Connected Trip" strategy.

## Network Effects and Platform Stickiness

### Supply-Side Barriers
- **The world's largest accommodation inventory:** Booking.com covers accommodation suppliers across more than 220 countries and territories, spanning hotels, apartments, homestays, and other formats
- **Long-tail supply advantage:** Small and mid-sized independent hotels in Europe depend heavily on Booking.com for customer acquisition, with many hotels drawing 30-50% of their bookings from the platform, creating extremely high switching costs
- **Agoda covers Asia-Pacific:** The sub-brand Agoda has a deep supplier-relationship network across Southeast Asia and East Asia

### Demand-Side Flywheel
- **1.235 billion room-nights booked in 2025** (source: FY2025 annual report), a scale that generates a massive volume of user reviews and behavioral data
- **The review system as a moat:** Decades of accumulated hundreds of millions of user reviews form a trust asset that is nearly impossible for latecomers to replicate
- **Brand recognition:** In European markets, Booking.com is nearly synonymous with "booking a hotel online"

### The Data Flywheel
Every transaction accumulates user preference data (price sensitivity, destination preferences, advance-booking lead time), which feeds back into search-ranking algorithms to further improve matching efficiency and conversion. The rising share of merchant revenue means Booking holds more complete payment and behavioral data, providing the foundation for personalized recommendations and dynamic pricing.

## The Connected Trip Strategy: From "Booking Tool" to "Travel Operating System"

The Connected Trip strategy, driven by CEO Glenn Fogel, is Booking's key initiative to expand from single-category accommodation booking into full-trip service:

- **2025 results:** Cross-category bookings (e.g., booking a hotel plus a flight within the same itinerary) grew more than 20% year over year in transaction volume; the flights business grew 37%, with total bookings reaching $16.8 billion; the attractions/tickets business grew roughly 80% year over year
- **AI enablement:** Generative-AI trip-planning tools have launched on mobile in more than 10 countries, and Connected Trip transaction volume grew 30% year over year in the second quarter of 2025 due to AI integration
- **Strategic significance:** Increasing per-user spend and usage frequency on a single platform, raising users' switching costs

Sources: [Booking Connected Trip Strategy Analysis](https://www.ainvest.com/news/booking-holdings-ai-driven-connected-trip-execution-sparks-moat-widening-catalyst-2604/); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Travel-Technology/Booking-Holdings-q4-call-connected-trip)

## What Makes This a Good Business?

**First, asset-light and highly profitable.** In 2025, gross margin was 87.4%, operating margin was about 32.8%, and free cash flow was $9.1 billion. There's no need to build hotels or buy planes — just maintain the platform and the algorithms.

**Second, two-sided network effects form a natural moat.** More supply draws more users; more users draw more supply. Once this flywheel starts spinning, latecomers find it extremely hard to catch up. The accumulation of the review system is an irreversible time-based barrier on top of that.

**Third, the demand side has an inherent growth tailwind.** Global travel penetration is still rising, and in particular, the online-booking rate in the Asia-Pacific region is moving from under 50% today toward 75%, providing a decade-long runway of structural growth.

**Fourth, cash-flow conversion is extremely efficient.** The merchant model — where users pay upfront and hotels are settled with afterward — provides a substantial "float" advantage, akin to an insurance company's business model: collect the cash first, then provide the service.

**Fifth, management understands how to create value through buybacks.** Since 2014, shares outstanding have shrunk by more than 40%; in 2025, the company repurchased $6.4 billion of stock, returning nearly all of its free cash flow to shareholders.

**To sum it up in Duan Yongping's words: this is the "right" business — users need it, merchants can't do without it, and competitors find it very hard to replace. And the longer it operates, the higher the barrier gets.**
