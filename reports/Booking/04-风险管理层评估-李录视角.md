## I. CEO Glenn Fogel — a review of his decisions

### Background

Glenn Fogel became CEO in 2017, after more than 17 years at Booking Holdings (formerly the Priceline Group), where he was mainly responsible for global strategy and corporate M&A. He led nearly every major acquisition in the company's history: Booking.com (2005), KAYAK (2012), OpenTable (2014), RentalCars.com (2014), and Agoda. These acquisitions form the footprint of today's Booking Holdings.

Fogel is not a typical "star CEO" — his style is low-key, cautious, and long-term oriented, more a strategic "operator" than a public-facing performer.

Data sources: [Crunchbase - Glenn Fogel](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/glenn-fogel); [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/05/12/booking-holdings-is-running-ahead-of-its-own-strategy/)

### Review of key decisions

**Decision one: driving the shift to the merchant model (2018-present)**
Rating: excellent

The most important strategic decision since Fogel took over was shifting Booking.com from a pure agency model (the hotel collects payment, the platform takes a commission) to a merchant model (the platform collects payment, then settles with the hotel). This transition began in 2018, and by 2025 merchant revenue had reached 66% of the total (up from under 30% in 2018).

Significance: (1) it puts payment data in Booking's hands, laying the groundwork for personalized recommendations and Connected Trip cross-selling; (2) it gains a "float" advantage, since collecting payment upfront improves cash flow; (3) it enables flexible cancellation and other user-experience features that boost competitiveness.

**Decision two: the Connected Trip strategy (2019-present)**
Rating: in progress, right direction

Fogel laid out a "Connected Trip" vision — letting users complete every leg of a trip, from lodging to flights to car rentals to attraction tickets, on a single platform. In 2025, cross-category booking volume grew more than 20%, and gross bookings for the flights business reached $16.8 billion, up 37%. But Connected Trip today still revolves overwhelmingly around lodging, and margins in the flights and attractions businesses are lower than in lodging — whether the strategy ultimately succeeds still needs time to prove out.

Data source: [AInvest - Connected Trip Analysis](https://www.ainvest.com/news/booking-holdings-ai-driven-connected-trip-execution-sparks-moat-widening-catalyst-2604/)

**Decision three: aggressive capital returns (ongoing)**
Rating: excellent

Since 2014, shares outstanding have fallen by more than 40%. In 2025 the company bought back $6.4 billion of stock, and in Q1 2026 it bought back $3.6 billion (a single-quarter record). Remaining authorization stands at $18.2 billion. The company initiated a quarterly dividend in 2024, and the dividend grew 9.4% in 2025. It completed a 25:1 stock split in April 2026 to improve accessibility for retail investors.

There is also a tension worth watching: the company directs most of its free cash flow to buybacks and dividends, while the Connected Trip and AI strategies require ongoing investment. So far, Booking's asset-light model means it doesn't need heavy capital expenditure to stay technologically ahead, but if the AI arms race intensifies, capital-allocation priorities may need to shift.

Data source: [FinancialContent - Q4 2025 Results](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2026-3-12-booking-holdings-q4-2025-financial-results-and-25-for-1-stock-split)

**Decision four: the AI strategy (2023-present)**
Rating: good, needs continued monitoring

In late 2025, Fogel launched an organizational restructuring, cutting headcount and redirecting the savings into "agentic AI" — AI systems that can act on a user's behalf (such as automatically rebooking flights or tracking prices). The company has partnered with OpenAI and Microsoft, and its AI trip-planning tool has launched in 10+ countries.

Management expects AI-driven efficiency gains to expand adjusted EBITDA margin by about 125 basis points in 2026. This suggests AI investment is not just a cost item but is also driving operational efficiency gains.

Data source: [IndexBox - BKNG 2026 Strategy](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/booking-holdings-completes-25-for-1-stock-split-in-april-2026/)

## II. The risk of AI disrupting travel booking

### The nature of the threat

The core value of an OTA is "information aggregation plus transaction matching" — helping users find the best option among a vast supply and complete the booking. Generative AI is eroding this value from two directions:

**First, the shift in the search entry point.** When a user tells ChatGPT or Google Gemini, "help me plan a five-day trip to Italy and book my hotels," the OTA's role as the search entry point is weakened. According to a Bernstein survey, up to 40% of travelers say they would be willing to book directly through an AI chat interface.

**Second, the ultimate threat of agentic AI.** If AI agents can automatically compare prices, book, rebook, and process refunds, users may not need to open any travel app at all. This would demote OTAs from "the platform users interact with directly" to "a backend API that AI agents call," compressing margins significantly.

Data sources: [HBR - Gen AI Threatening Online Travel](https://hbr.org/2026/01/gen-ai-is-threatening-the-platforms-that-dominate-online-travel); [Yahoo Finance - AI and Online Travel](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-online-travel-disruption-reinvention-103502392.html)

### Booking's response

Booking's approach is "if you can't beat AI, become AI":

1. **Building AI capability in-house:** The AI Trip Planner has launched in 10+ countries, offering conversational trip planning
2. **Investing in agentic AI:** Launched the Agentic AI project in late 2025, aiming to let Booking's own AI act on the user's behalf
3. **Platform-level defense:** Using Connected Trip to lock users into the Booking ecosystem, reducing the risk of being replaced by an AI intermediary
4. **Data advantage:** 1.235 billion room-nights of transaction data and hundreds of millions of reviews are a unique asset for training travel AI

### Risk assessment

Short term (1-2 years): AI's substantive impact on Booking is limited, and mostly manifests as market-sentiment swings.

Medium term (3-5 years): Google's AI search may divert 10-20% of top-of-funnel traffic; Booking will need to prove that Connected Trip and its own AI can make up for that loss.

Long term (5-10 years): if agentic AI becomes the mainstream way to book travel, the OTA's role will change fundamentally. But the platform with the largest supply inventory and the best user data will still be well positioned.

## III. Civilizational trends: global travel penetration

### Travel as an inevitable product of civilizational development

From a civilizational-trend perspective, travel consumption correlates strongly with per-capita income. Once a country's per-capita GDP exceeds $5,000, travel demand starts to grow rapidly; above $10,000, outbound travel demand explodes.

The global online travel market was worth about $975 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $2.19 trillion by 2030, a CAGR of about 17.5%. Even under a more conservative estimate (CAGR of 9-10%), the market would nearly double within five years.

Data sources: [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-online-travel-booking-service-market); [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/4995185/online-travel-market-global-forecast-2025-2030)

### Three structural growth drivers

**First, rising online penetration in Asia-Pacific.** By 2029, an estimated 75% of travel bookings in Asia-Pacific will be made through digital channels (versus about 50% today). The rise of the middle class in China, India, and Southeast Asia means hundreds of millions of new online travel consumers. Booking has positioned itself in Asia-Pacific through Agoda; Trip.com is a direct competitor but also a potential partner.

**Second, the rise of the experience economy.** In the post-pandemic era, consumers increasingly prioritize experiences over physical goods. Travel is the quintessential "experience purchase," and its share of consumer spending keeps rising. This is an irreversible cultural trend.

**Third, mobile internet penetration.** In 2025, more than 52% of travel bookings worldwide were made on mobile, and mobile booking growth (CAGR of 9.9%) continues to outpace desktop. The Booking.com mobile app is one of the most-downloaded travel apps in the world.

### Counter-trend risks

- Geopolitical conflict (the Middle East, Eastern Europe) affecting travel demand in specific regions
- Climate change potentially reshaping the map of travel destinations
- The spread of remote work potentially changing patterns of business travel
- "Anti-tourism" sentiment rising in some European cities (Barcelona, Amsterdam)

## IV. Final assessment

Glenn Fogel is an underrated, excellent CEO. He doesn't chase media exposure, but he has shown vision and discipline in strategic decisions — the merchant-model transition is one of the most successful strategic moves in the OTA industry over the past five years, and the aggressive buyback program has been a faithful guardian of shareholder interests.

AI is the biggest uncertainty facing Booking, but it may also be its biggest opportunity. If Booking can use its data advantage and supply scale to build a leading position in AI travel planning, its moat will grow wider, not narrower, in the AI era.

From a civilizational-trend perspective, rising global travel penetration gives Booking a structural growth runway lasting a decade or more. This is a business that rides with the direction of human civilizational development.
