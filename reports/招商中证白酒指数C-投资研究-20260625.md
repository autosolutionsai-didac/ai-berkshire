## III. Nature of the Business — "What makes this a good business?"

### One-sentence definition

Baijiu is a China-specific, high-gross-margin consumer product business built on grain fermentation and distillation, and driven by social-etiquette culture.

### Business model canvas

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|------|------|
| Product nature | A standardized consumer product with an unlimited shelf life; the longer it is stored, the more valuable it becomes |
| Revenue model | Primarily one-time sales, but with strong repeat-purchase characteristics (banqueting, gift-giving, self-consumption) |
| Production characteristics | Asset-heavy (cellar pits, base-liquor reserves), but operationally very light (no need for sustained heavy R&D spending) |
| Pricing model | Extremely high brand premium; the ex-factory cost of high-end baijiu is less than 10% of its retail price |
| Channel structure | Distributor wholesale plus direct sales (e-commerce/i-Moutai, etc.), currently shifting toward direct sales |
| Consumption scenarios | Business banqueting (40%+), gift-giving (25%+), personal consumption (20%+), collection/investment |

### Operating leverage analysis

The baijiu industry shows pronounced operating-leverage characteristics:
- **Positive leverage**: as output rises, fixed costs (cellar-pit depreciation, base-liquor reserves) are spread across more volume, producing extremely high marginal profit margins
- **Negative leverage**: when demand falls, high-end brands can hold prices by controlling volume, preserving margins, while mid- and low-end products face simultaneous volume and price declines
- Current phase: the industry is in a period of releasing negative leverage — high-end brands remain steady, while sub-premium and mid-tier brands face severe pressure

### Follow-up question: What makes this a good business?

**In one sentence**: Baijiu is a business that "gets more valuable with age" — the older the cellar pit, the better the liquor; the older the brand, the higher the premium; the older the base-liquor inventory, the thicker the asset base — and it requires almost no R&D spending, so it cannot be disrupted by technology.

But the core problem it currently faces is: **structural change is underway on the demand side** — the business-banqueting occasion is shrinking, young people are less inclined to drink baijiu, and consumption-downgrade pressure persists. This is not cyclical fluctuation but a long-term trend driven by generational consumption change.

## IV. Moat Assessment — "Will this moat still be here in 10 years?"

### Verifying the Five Types of Moat One by One

| Moat type | Strength | Verification |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Brand/pricing power** | ★★★★★ | Moutai raised its ex-factory price by 8.55% in March 2026 (from 1,169 to 1,269 yuan), with high market acceptance. High-end baijiu brand power functions as social currency, exhibiting a "Veblen effect" (the more expensive, the more people want it) |
| **Switching cost** | ★★★☆☆ | Business banqueting carries an implicit switching cost ("serve Moutai to guests" is a social norm), but personal consumption has a low switching cost. Brand loyalty is high but not a hard lock-in |
| **Network effect** | ★★☆☆☆ | Baijiu has no classic network effect, but there is a "social-consensus effect" — the more people who recognize Moutai as social currency, the more valuable it becomes |
| **Scale effect** | ★★★★☆ | Leading distillers have significant scale advantages in brand marketing, channel coverage, and base-liquor reserves. CR5 exceeds 50% and is accelerating toward further concentration |
| **Technology/patent barrier** | ★★★★☆ | Not a patent barrier but an "irreplicable natural resource" — the microbial environment of Moutai Town, century-old cellar pits (Luzhou Laojiao's 1573 National Treasure Cellar Pit Cluster has a 450-year history). Competitors could not replicate this even with 10 billion yuan of capital |

### Moat trend

**Past 5 years: widening**
- Market concentration (CR5) has risen from roughly 40% to 50%+
- The brand power of high-end baijiu (Moutai/Wuliangye/Luzhou Laojiao) has continued to strengthen
- Small and mid-sized distillers are being eliminated at an accelerating pace, with 86% of companies seeing declining profit margins

**Forecast for the next 5 years: divergence**
- The high-end moat will likely continue to widen (Moutai's brand power may strengthen further)
- Sub-premium and regional distillers face the risk of moat erosion
- The industry's overall moat may slowly narrow due to generational shifts in consumption

### Follow-up question: Will this moat still be here in 10 years? What could destroy it?

**The moat will very likely still be here**, but it may become less important — if total baijiu consumption keeps shrinking (following a path similar to the long-term decline of Japanese sake), then even a solid moat only makes you "the biggest fish in a shrinking pond."

**Factors that could destroy the moat**:
1. The generational consumption gap (the core risk): people aged 20-35 account for only 12% of baijiu consumption
2. Deepening anti-corruption policy, further shrinking the business-banqueting occasion
3. Rising health awareness driving a global decline in spirits consumption

### Historical Analogies

| Analogy | Similarities | Outcome |
|---------|--------|------|
| Japanese sake (1975-present) | Young people not drinking, shrinking consumption occasions, seeking survival through premiumization | Output has declined continuously for 50 years, but premium brands (e.g., Dassai) still command high profit margins |
| 2012-2014 baijiu crisis | Policy shock + valuation collapse + industry destocking | Bottomed within 2 years, followed by a super bull market from 2016-2021 |
| U.S. tobacco industry (1990s-present) | Consumer base shrinking but pricing power remains extremely strong | Volume has kept declining, but leading companies have continued to generate shareholder returns through price increases, dividends, and buybacks |

**The most likely analogy is "the U.S. tobacco industry"**: consumption volume declines slowly, but the leaders sustain shareholder returns through price increases plus high dividends and buybacks. This implies baijiu may no longer be a growth story but is transitioning into a high-dividend value stock.

### Bias Self-Check

- **Narrative bias**: the current narrative that "baijiu is finished" may be excessively pessimistic, just as the 2021 narrative that "baijiu always goes up" was excessively optimistic
- **Anchoring effect**: one should not anchor to the 2021 peak to judge the current valuation as "cheap" — the industry's fundamentals have genuinely changed
- **Survivorship bias**: baijiu's excess returns over the past decade are already fully reflected in historical valuations; there is no guarantee they will repeat

### The Bear Case's Core Arguments

1. Baijiu is a "sunset industry" — output has declined for 9 consecutive years, and the trend is irreversible
2. There is a generational consumption gap; the baijiu-drinking population is aging
3. Channel inventory is severely bloated; actual sell-through is far weaker than reported revenue
4. Moutai's wholesale price keeps falling; the industry's "pricing anchor" is loosening
5. The low valuation may be a "value trap" — the PE is low because E (earnings) is about to decline

### Follow-up question: Why wouldn't smart money buy — or why would it short — this company?

Because they believe what the baijiu industry is going through is not "cyclical adjustment" but "structural decline" — the consumer base is shrinking, consumption occasions are diminishing, and while current PE ratios look low, they have not yet fully priced in the decline. If leading distillers' earnings fall another 10-20% in 2026-2027, today's 18x PE would effectively become 22-25x — not cheap at all.

## VII. Industry and Civilizational Trends — "Looking back 20 years from now"

### What stage is the industry at?

The baijiu industry **is not undergoing a civilization-level paradigm shift**; it is a textbook case of a mature industry entering a long-term contraction phase.

### Historical Technology-Revolution Analogy

Rather than resembling an emerging industry from the "steam-engine revolution," the baijiu industry more closely resembles **traditional handicraft industries in the late stage of industrialization** — its core value lies in cultural heritage and scarcity rather than technological innovation. The closest analogies are:
- **Swiss mechanical watches**: high-end mechanical watches have actually appreciated in value due to scarcity and cultural value, even under pressure from quartz watches and smartwatches
- **French wine**: global wine consumption volume is declining, but prices for top-tier appellations (Bordeaux, Burgundy) keep rising

### TAM (Total Addressable Market) Analysis

| Dimension | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 baijiu industry size | approximately 850 billion yuan |
| 2026 forecast | approximately 880-900 billion yuan (+4-5% moderate growth) |
| Output trend | declined for 9 consecutive years; 2025 volume was 3.549 million kiloliters (-12.1%) |
| Price trend | high-end steady-to-rising, mid- and low-end declining |
| Long-term ceiling | how far "falling volume, rising price" can be sustained depends on the tug-of-war between consumption upgrading and consumption downgrading |

### Key Trend Judgments

1. **Industry concentration is accelerating**: CR5 already exceeds 50% and may reach 60-70% within the next 5 years
2. **Premiumization is the only path forward**: baijiu priced under 100 yuan is dying out at an accelerating pace, while the 300-yuan-plus price band is relatively resilient
3. **Channel restructuring**: shifting from distributor-led channels toward direct sales plus online, favoring channel-controlling leaders
4. **Shift in consumption occasions**: business banqueting is shrinking, while personal tasting and collecting occasions are slowly growing

### Follow-up question: Looking back 20 years from now, what will China's baijiu industry be?

**Most likely "China's Swiss watch industry"**: output keeps shrinking, but leading brands sustain high profit margins and shareholder returns on the strength of cultural value, scarcity, and pricing power. Moutai will very likely still be one of China's most profitable consumer-goods companies, but the growth story for the baijiu industry as a whole has ended.

For index-fund investors, this means: the index's leading companies (Moutai, Wuliangye, Luzhou Laojiao, Shanxi Fenjiu) will continue to outperform the tail-end companies (Jiuguijiu, Yanghe, etc.), and the index's weighting structure will naturally optimize itself over time — but it is still inferior to holding the leading individual stocks directly.

> **Civilizational-evolution perspective**: Baijiu is a product of Chinese civilization, and its rise and fall are closely tied to shifts in China's social structure. On a 20-year timescale, baijiu will not disappear — the culture of banqueting is deeply rooted in China — but it will shift from a "universal beverage" into an "elite consumer good," following a premiumization path similar to Japanese sake.
