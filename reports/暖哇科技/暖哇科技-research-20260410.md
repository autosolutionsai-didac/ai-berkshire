## I. Business Model Analysis (Duan Yongping's Perspective)

> "Do the right things, and do things right." -- Duan Yongping

### 1.1 What Does the Company Do?

NuanWa Technology is an **insurance AI technology company** that provides insurers with AI-driven solutions covering the full lifecycle of insurance transactions. Specifically, the company has two core product lines:

- **AI Underwriting Solutions** (approximately 75% of revenue): product development, precision marketing, intelligent underwriting
- **AI Claims Solutions** (approximately 25% of revenue): intelligent claims processing, anti-fraud, health management

In addition, the company has developed seven core technology products: a medical knowledge base, a Medical Data Platform (MDP), commercial-insurance risk control, a commercial-insurance anti-fraud platform, a medical service platform, a customized insurance service platform, and an AI medical service platform.

### 1.2 Revenue Model

NuanWa Technology's revenue model is mainly **technology service fees plus transaction-based revenue sharing**:

- Providing SaaS-based underwriting/claims system services to insurers
- Charging technology service fees based on the volume of insurance cases processed
- Some revenue comes from customized product development services and risk-control consulting

**The key question Duan Yongping would ask: is this a "good business"?**

From a business-model standpoint, NuanWa Technology sits in the **middle of the insurance value chain** — it is neither the underwriter (it bears no insurance risk) nor the end consumer. This position has both advantages and disadvantages:
- **Advantage**: asset-light, technology-driven, scalable
- **Disadvantage**: limited bargaining power with upstream insurers, especially excessive dependence on its major shareholder ZhongAn

### 1.3 Moat Analysis

| Moat Dimension | Assessment | Rating |
|-----------|------|------|
| Technology barrier | Moderate barrier: the medical knowledge base plus AI risk-control models require substantial data accumulation | ★★★ |
| Data barrier | Medium: processing 250 million+ insurance cases has built up a data asset, but data ownership is questionable | ★★★ |
| Customer stickiness | Relatively high: once the core system is integrated, switching costs are high | ★★★★ |
| Network effects | Weak: network effects are not pronounced in insurance-tech B2B business | ★★ |
| Brand effect | Medium: recognized within the industry, but weak consumer-facing (to-C) brand | ★★ |

### 1.4 Differentiated Value

NuanWa's core differentiation lies in:
1. **ZhongAn heritage**: spun off from ZhongAn Insurance's health business unit, giving it an innate understanding of insurance business logic
2. **Full-stack capability**: end-to-end coverage from product design to underwriting to claims to anti-fraud
3. **Data accumulation**: having processed RMB 130 billion in claims, its risk-control models are trained on large volumes of real-world data

**Duan Yongping-perspective summary**: This does not qualify as a "great business." Customer concentration is too high (the top five customers still account for over 70% of revenue), dependence on ZhongAn is excessive (nearly half of revenue comes from its major shareholder), and bargaining power is limited. However, the technology barrier and customer stickiness do provide some protection. The core question is: **can NuanWa survive independently without ZhongAn?**

**Business Model Rating: ★★★**

### 2.5 Cumulative Losses

From 2022 through H1 2025, **cumulative losses totaled approximately RMB 718 million**.

### 2.6 Unit Economics Analysis

- Revenue growth is strong (65.5% CAGR), but losses persist
- Gross margin is declining, indicating that growth is being achieved partly by "trading price for volume"
- The company has not yet proven it can scale while achieving profitability

**What would Buffett think?**

Buffett would very likely not invest in this company. Reasons:
1. **Persistent losses**: RMB 700 million lost over three and a half years, with no clear timetable to profitability
2. **Declining gross margin**: a good business should have a stable or rising gross margin
3. **Customer concentration risk**: nearly half of revenue depends on the major shareholder, which is not genuine independent commercial capability
4. **Suspicion of an IPO as a "lifeline"**: some media outlets have questioned whether the listing is meant to raise funds to "keep the lights on" rather than to meet genuine growth needs

**Financial and Valuation Rating: ★★**

## III. Industry and Competition Analysis (Munger's Perspective)

> "Invert, always invert." -- Munger

### 3.1 Industry Size and Trends

China's health insurance market:
- 2024 health insurance premium income was approximately RMB 900 billion (estimate)
- Insurance-technology penetration continues to rise
- The application of AI in insurance (underwriting, claims, anti-fraud) is a near-certain trend

**Industry drivers**:
1. Accelerating population aging --> growing demand for health insurance
2. Medical insurance reform --> expanding room for commercial health insurance
3. Digital transformation --> growing demand from insurers to outsource technology
4. Large AI models --> another upgrade in insurance-technology capability

### 3.2 Competitive Landscape

| Company | Positioning | Revenue (2024) | Status | Core Differentiation |
|------|------|-------------|------|---------|
| **NuanWa Technology** | Insurance AI technology | RMB 944mn | Filed prospectus with HKEX | Full-stack AI underwriting + claims |
| **MediTrust Health** | Innovative pharma-insurance payment | RMB 2.035bn | Planning IPO | Dual "Smart Drug + Smart Insurance" engine |
| **Sipai Health** | Healthcare + insurance | - | Already listed on HKEX | Market cap has shrunk severely (~HKD 4 billion) |
| **Yuanxin Technology** | Pharma + insurance | - | IPO on hold | Cumulative losses of approximately RMB 2.5 billion |
| **Insurance Geek** | Enterprise group-insurance SaaS | - | Not listed | Focused on enterprise clients |

### 3.3 Munger-Style Inversion

**Under what circumstances would NuanWa Technology fail?**

1. **ZhongAn withdraws support**: ZhongAn Online is both the major shareholder and the largest customer; if the relationship deteriorates, the company's revenue could be cut in half overnight
2. **Large insurers build their own capabilities in-house**: major insurers such as Ping An, CPIC, and PICC have their own technology subsidiaries; if they build AI capabilities internally, NuanWa's addressable market among small and mid-sized insurers is limited
3. **Disruption from large AI models**: general-purpose large AI models (including vertical models for insurance) could lower the barrier to entry in insurance technology and erode NuanWa's technology moat
4. **Regulatory risk**: its subsidiary Aibang Insurance Brokerage has been the subject of repeated complaints, and tightening regulation could affect the business
5. **Continued price war**: gross margin has already fallen from 69% to 53%; if it keeps declining, profitability will remain far out of reach

### 3.4 Industry Barrier Assessment

The barriers to entry in the insurance-technology industry are **medium-to-low**:
- Technology: AI/big-data technology is becoming increasingly commoditized
- Data: data ownership belongs to the insurers, so the technology provider's data asset is not secure
- Customers: system switching costs provide some protection, but the company is not irreplaceable
- Licensing: NuanWa holds an insurance brokerage license through its subsidiary Aibang, which provides some barrier

**Industry and Competition Rating: ★★★**

## IV. Risk and Management Assessment (Li Lu's Perspective)

> "The first rule of investing is don't lose money. The second rule is never forget the first rule." -- Li Lu

### 4.1 Management Team

| Name | Position | Background |
|------|------|------|
| Lu Min | Chairman and CEO | 20+ years of insurance-technology experience; computer-science background; previously held management roles at eBaoTech and Alibaba Cloud, and served as General Manager of the Information Technology Division at the Shanghai Insurance Exchange |
| Cai Jianwei | Co-CEO | Responsible for core business areas including risk control, client management, and customer acquisition |

**Management assessment**:
- Lu Min has a solid background, with a clear career path from eBaoTech (insurance core systems) to Alibaba Cloud (technology) to the Shanghai Insurance Exchange (industry)
- However, the management team grew up within the ZhongAn system, and its ability to operate independently remains to be proven
- The dual-CEO structure may create decision-making efficiency issues

### 4.2 Ownership Structure

| Shareholder | Ownership Stake |
|------|---------|
| Lu Min (through trusts, etc.) | 31.65% |
| ZhongAn Online | 31.65% |
| Sequoia Capital | 15.90% |
| Other investors | Approximately 20.8% |

**Key risk**: Lu Min and ZhongAn Online each hold 31.65%, making the ownership structure highly balanced. If the two sides disagree, it could lead to a corporate-governance deadlock.

### 4.3 Core Risk Factors

| Risk Category | Severity | Details |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Customer concentration risk** | Extremely high | ZhongAn Online contributes about 50% of revenue, and ZhongAn is simultaneously a shareholder, a customer, and a supplier — a triple role that creates serious conflicts of interest |
| **Persistent loss risk** | High | Cumulative losses of RMB 718 million over 3.5 years, with no clear timetable to profitability |
| **Gross margin decline risk** | High | AI underwriting gross margin fell from 69% to 53%, an unfavorable trend |
| **Compliance risk** | Medium-high | Subsidiary Aibang Insurance Brokerage has been frequently subject to consumer complaints (inducing policy purchases, phone harassment, elderly customers being charged without explanation), and has been featured on the "315" consumer-rights exposure platform |
| **IPO uncertainty** | Medium | The CSRC has requested supplementary explanation of the compliance of the ownership structure, and the hearing has not yet been passed |
| **Related-party transaction risk** | Medium-high | ZhongAn Online is both a customer and a supplier, raising doubts about the fairness of related-party transactions |
| **Technology substitution risk** | Medium | Large AI models are lowering the technology barrier, and insurers may build capabilities in-house |

### 4.4 Aibang Insurance Complaint Issues (Key Focus)

NuanWa Technology's subsidiary **Aibang Insurance Brokerage Co., Ltd.** has been the target of numerous consumer complaints:
- Elderly customers being enrolled in policies and charged without their knowledge
- Text messages containing hidden policy-purchase links that trigger charges upon being clicked
- Phone harassment and recorded sales pitches
- Already reported by the 315 Financial Exposure Platform

This is not merely a compliance issue — it reflects the possibility that the company **has adopted aggressive marketing tactics under growth pressure**. Duan Yongping says "don't do the wrong thing," and this kind of customer-acquisition method clearly qualifies as "the wrong thing."

### 4.5 Regulatory Environment

- In 2023, the National Financial Regulatory Administration began investigating third-party partnership health insurance products
- Tightening regulation of the insurance-technology industry is a near-certain trend
- The CSRC has raised questions about the compliance of NuanWa's red-chip structure

### 4.6 Long-Term Development Outlook

**Optimistic scenario**:
- Successfully lists and obtains funding for independent development
- Gradually reduces dependence on ZhongAn as its customer base continues to expand
- AI technology continues to iterate, building a genuine technology moat
- Achieves profitability within 3-5 years

**Pessimistic scenario**:
- The listing fails or the valuation shrinks significantly
- A change in the ZhongAn relationship causes revenue to fall off a cliff
- Gross margin keeps declining and losses widen
- The Aibang complaint issues trigger regulatory penalties

**Risk and Management Rating: ★★**

## V. Overall Rating and Investment Recommendation

### 5.1 Four-Dimension Rating Summary

| Dimension | Analyst | Rating | Core Judgment |
|------|--------|------|---------|
| Business model | Duan Yongping | ★★★ | Full-stack insurance-AI services have value, but excessive dependence on ZhongAn means this is not a "good business" |
| Financial valuation | Buffett | ★★ | Revenue is growing quickly but losses persist, gross margin is declining, and the profit model is unproven |
| Industry competition | Munger | ★★★ | The sector has room to grow, but barriers are not high, competitors are circling, and large models could be disruptive |
| Risk management | Li Lu | ★★ | Customer concentration, persistent losses, compliance risk, and IPO uncertainty — risk factors are dense |
| **Overall rating** | | **★★☆** | **Does not currently present investment value** |

### 5.2 Core Conclusion

**One-sentence summary: NuanWa Technology is an insurance-AI company with real technical capability that has not yet proven independent commercial value; "ZhongAn dependency syndrome" is its greatest structural problem.**

**Detailed judgment**:

1. **Reasons not to invest (weighted more heavily)**:
   - Nearly half of revenue depends on major shareholder ZhongAn Online, which is not genuine market competitiveness
   - Cumulative losses of RMB 718 million over 3.5 years, with no clear timetable to profitability
   - Gross margin continues to decline (69% -> 53%), indicating the moat is not deep
   - Consumer complaints against subsidiary Aibang Insurance Brokerage raise questions about corporate culture
   - Insurance-technology companies have generally performed poorly (see Sipai Health, whose market cap has shrunk more than 90%)

2. **Reasons worth watching**:
   - The insurance-AI sector has long-term room to grow
   - Revenue growth is substantial (65.5% CAGR)
   - Customer count has grown from 62 to 163, and de-dependence is progressing
   - Management has extensive industry experience
   - Backing from top-tier VCs such as Sequoia Capital

3. **What would need to be seen to justify investment?**
   - ZhongAn's share of revenue falling below 30%
   - Gross margin stabilizing and rebounding
   - Achieving profitability in a single quarter
   - The Aibang complaint issues being systematically resolved
   - Successfully passing the HKEX hearing and completing the listing

### 5.3 Duan Yongping-Style Final Judgment

> Duan Yongping often says: "Don't do what you don't understand; don't invest in what you're not familiar with."

NuanWa Technology's core problem is not whether its technology is good, but whether **the "foundation" of this business is solid**. When 50% of a company's revenue comes from its own major shareholder, is that a genuine market-based commercial relationship, or "internal transfer pricing"? If ZhongAn decided tomorrow to build its own in-house team to do these things, what would happen to NuanWa?

**Until this question is answered clearly, investment is not recommended.**
