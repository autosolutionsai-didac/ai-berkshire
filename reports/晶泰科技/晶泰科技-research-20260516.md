## Step Two: Analyzing the Nature of the Business — Duan Yongping's "the right business"

### One-sentence definition

XtalPi is essentially **a drug-discovery outsourcing (CRO) platform company that replaces traditional lab labor with AI + quantum physics + robotics**, transitioning from "selling labor" (traditional CRO) to "selling intelligence + revenue share" (platform model).

### Business model canvas

| Dimension | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Revenue model | Service fees (short term) + milestone payments (medium term) + sales royalties (long term) |
| Customer base | Global large pharmaceutical companies (17 of the top 20) + biotech companies |
| Core product | ID4 platform (AI model cluster + robotic labs + closed-loop data) |
| Value proposition | Shortens the drug discovery cycle from 3-5 years to 1-2 years, lowering costs |
| Deliverables | Hit/lead compounds + automated experimental data |
| Repeat-purchase logic | Platform lock-in + data accumulation → continued new-project collaboration |

### Gross margin analysis

- Overall FY2025 gross margin was approximately 70% (per StockAnalysis LTM data: gross profit of HKD 622 million / revenue of HKD 893 million = 69.7%)
- Far higher than traditional CRO companies (WuXi AppTec's gross margin is approximately 35-40%)
- Reason for the high margin: AI-driven marginal costs are extremely low — once a model is trained, reuse costs approach zero
- On the other hand: the DoveTree upfront payment is essentially pure-profit revenue, which inflated the gross margin baseline

### Operating leverage

- FY2025 revenue grew 201%, but operating losses narrowed substantially → strong operating leverage is emerging
- R&D spend (FY2024: RMB 418 million) exceeds revenue (FY2024: RMB 266 million) → still in an R&D-driven stage
- Average monthly cash burn fell from RMB 62.1 million (2023) to RMB 48.1 million (2024) — under control
- Issued RMB 2.54 billion in convertible bonds in 2026 → management still believes substantial further capital investment is needed

### Duan Yongping-style question

> What makes this business good? If you could describe it in one sentence, what would it be?

**Answer**: This business's strength lies in the theoretical positive feedback loop of the "AI + data flywheel" — the more projects done, the more data accumulated, the more accurate the models become, and the more willing customers are to collaborate. But whether the flywheel is genuinely spinning at this stage, or is simply being propped up by one large deal, needs another 2-3 years to confirm.

**Objective facts**: The company has accumulated over RMB 5 billion in cumulative losses to date, and its first year of profitability in 2025 is highly dependent on a single major customer, DoveTree, which contributed 45% of revenue. Whether recurring business can be profitable on a standalone basis after excluding DoveTree remains uncertain.

## Step Three: Moat Assessment — Buffett's "Economic Moat"

### Verifying each of the five moat types

| Moat type | Assessment | Rating | Evidence |
|-----------|------|:--:|------|
| **Technology/patent barriers** | Strong | ★★★★ | Trinity of quantum physics + AI + robotics, 200+ vertical AI models, 160+ granted patents, 98% crystal form prediction accuracy, leading XtalFold algorithm |
| **Switching costs** | Moderate | ★★★ | Customer project data accumulates on the platform, creating migration costs; but CRO customers typically work with multiple providers simultaneously |
| **Economies of scale** | Potential | ★★★ | 26 million reaction data points, 200+ robotic workstations — the larger the scale, the more data and the more accurate the models; but current revenue scale remains small |
| **Network effects** | Weak | ★★ | Not a two-sided-market platform; customers do not interact directly with one another |
| **Brand/pricing power** | Nascent | ★★★ | Pfizer's blind test with 100% accuracy built the brand; large deals with Lilly/DoveTree validated it further; but brand premium has not yet translated into stable premium pricing |

### Moat trend assessment

**Past 3 years**: The moat is widening. The company is transitioning from a traditional CRO service model to a platform model; the DoveTree/Lilly deals prove out platform value, and data accumulation is accelerating the flywheel effect.

**5-year outlook**:
- Optimistic scenario: The flywheel effect materializes, more large pharma companies sign platform partnerships, and the data moat continues to deepen
- Pessimistic scenario: Giants such as Isomorphic Labs (a Google subsidiary) and Recursion accelerate their pursuit, and rapid AI model iteration erodes first-mover advantage

### Key uncertainties

1. The "depth" of the AI model moat is questionable: in a fast-iterating AI environment, today's technological lead could be matched within 2-3 years
2. The data moat depends on continuous customer-project inputs; if major customers churn, the data flywheel could stall
3. Google/Alphabet's Isomorphic Labs owns AlphaFold technology; if it commits fully to drug discovery, it could deliver a dimensionality-reducing blow to XtalPi

### Buffett-style question

> Will this moat still be there in 10 years? What could destroy it?

**Answer**: Uncertain. AI technology iterates extremely fast; today's algorithmic edge may not be an edge at all in 3 years. If a true moat exists, it should lie in the "data + wet-lab closed loop" — others may have AI algorithms, but not the accompanying 200+ robotic workstations and 26 million experimental data points. But if Google commits fully, even this barrier is not insurmountable.

### Historical analogues

| Comparable company | Similarities | Outcome |
|---------|--------|------|

**Common lesson**: The market often prices in the "potential" of science and technology platform companies too early, and it takes a long time to deliver. Along the way, valuations can undergo sharp corrections.

### Bias self-check

- **Narrative bias**: The AI drug discovery story is almost too good (raises efficiency, lowers costs, shortens cycles), making it easy to overlook delivery risk
- **Anchoring effect**: DoveTree's total contract value of USD 6.0 billion is a "potential maximum," not confirmed revenue — but the market has already treated it as a near-certain event
- **Survivorship bias**: We see the headlines about XtalPi's big deal, but we don't see how many AI drug discovery companies died or were acquired at low valuations in 2024-2025

### Munger-style question

> Where am I most likely to be wrong? Why would smart people avoid — or short — this stock?

**Answer**:
1. **Where I could be wrong**: Overestimating the certainty of the DoveTree deal's payout. USD 6.0 billion is the "maximum potential amount"; how much is actually realized depends on drug development success rates (historically under 10%)
2. **Reasons smart people wouldn't buy**: PS of 46x is 13 times more expensive than Schrödinger (PS of 3.5x), and XtalPi has not demonstrated that its platform is worth 13 times more. Dilution risk is severe (3 placements in 2 years). There is single-customer dependency syndrome.

## Step Six: Industry and Civilizational Trends — Li Lu's "Framework of Civilizational Evolution"

### Is the industry undergoing a "civilization-level paradigm shift"?

**Verdict: Yes, but at a very early stage.**

AI transforming drug discovery is a high-probability event, similar to:
- Computers transforming gene sequencing (1990s-2000s)
- Machine learning transforming protein structure prediction (AlphaFold, 2020)

But the key difference is: **AI has not yet produced a single successfully marketed drug**. This is like the internet in 2001 — the direction is right, but a bubble burst was imminent.

### Historical technology-revolution analogues

| Analogue | Similarities | Current stage roughly equivalent to |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Gene sequencing revolution | Technology platform + heavy upfront investment + long-term payoff | 2003, right after completion of the Human Genome Project |
| Internet 1.0 | New technology + massive capital inflows + most companies unprofitable | 1999-2000 |
| E-commerce platforms | Platform effects + data flywheel + loss-then-profit | 2005, Amazon (just turned profitable but not yet stable) |

### TAM analysis

| Source | 2025 market size | 2030 forecast | CAGR |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Arizton | ~$2.0 billion | $8.5 billion | 30.6% |
| Grand View Research | — | $9.2 billion | 29.6% |
| Fortune Business Insights | — | $7.9 billion | 12.2% |
| **Composite estimate** | **~$2.0 billion** | **$8-10 billion** | **25-30%** |

### XtalPi's position in the industry value chain

```
[Pharma customers] ← demand ← [XtalPi: AI + robotics platform] → uses → [cloud computing/chip suppliers]
                          ↕ competition
              [Schrödinger] [Recursion] [Insilico Medicine] [Isomorphic Labs]
```

XtalPi sits in the middle of the value chain — upstream it is affected by compute costs, downstream it is constrained by pharma R&D budgets. The upside is control over both ends; the downside is a ceiling limited by pharma companies' willingness to outsource.

### Customer/supplier concentration

- **Customer concentration**: Very high. DoveTree contributed 45% of revenue in 2025. The top 5 customers may contribute over 70% of revenue
- **Supplier concentration**: Primarily reliant on cloud computing (AWS/Alibaba Cloud, etc.), with no obvious bottleneck
- **Technology-path risk**: If large language models achieve a breakthrough in drug discovery, physics-based computational approaches could be marginalized

### Li Lu-style question

> Looking back 20 years from now, will this company be "the Standard Oil of this era" or "the flash-in-the-pan 3Com"?

**Answer**: More likely to be "the Illumina of this era" — in the gene sequencing revolution, Illumina built a platform monopoly and reaped rich rewards, but it took 15 years to truly deliver. XtalPi could become a platform-level infrastructure company in AI drug discovery. But it could also be displaced by a stronger rival (Google), like 3Com was. Probability distribution: Illumina-style success 30%, mediocre survival 40%, marginalization 30%.

### Reverse DCF: What does the current share price imply?

At the current market cap of HKD 41.0 billion, net cash of approximately HKD 7.3 billion, and an enterprise value of approximately HKD 33.7 billion.

Assuming:
- The company reaches steady state in 10 years, at a 25x PE multiple
- A 12% discount rate
- Then, the net profit required in 10 years is: HKD 33.7 billion / 25 × (1.12^10) = HKD 33.7 billion / 25 × 3.11 = **HKD 4.18 billion** (approximately RMB 3.8 billion)

This implies that the current share price bakes in: growth from RMB 258 million in adjusted net profit in 2025 to RMB 3.8 billion, i.e. **a compound annual growth rate of approximately 31%, sustained for 10 years**.

This is not impossible in theory, but requires the company to continue winning mega-deals, expand into new AI4S domains, and avoid being knocked back by competition.
