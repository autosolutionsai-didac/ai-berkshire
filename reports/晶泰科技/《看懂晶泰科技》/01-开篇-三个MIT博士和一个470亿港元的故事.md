## A set of facts that make you reconsider AI drug discovery

HK$47 billion. That's the "potential maximum value" of a partnership contract XtalPi announced in August 2025 — equivalent to the entire market cap of a small Hong Kong-listed company, compressed into a single contract clause. The day the news broke, the stock price doubled within a month.

But behind this number is a fact you need to know: **of the HK$47 billion, only $51 million (about HK$400 million) has actually been received**. The remaining HK$46.5 billion depends on how far the partnered drugs can go — and the historical clinical success rate in pharma has never exceeded 10%.

This is XtalPi: a company that has split the market into wildly divergent camps. It is the **first company to successfully "shed the P"** under Hong Kong's Chapter 18C rules (converting from a pre-commercialization specialist technology company into a normal listed company), with an IPO price of HK$5.28. In under two years since listing, its stock has traced a dramatic curve:

- IPO in June 2024: **HK$5.28**
- Low in October 2024: **HK$4.20** — broke below IPO price, market indifference
- High in August 2025: **HK$15.12** — the DoveTree $6 billion mega-deal was announced, price doubled in a month
- May 2026: **~HK$8.2** — gave back the gains, back near the starting point

**In under two years, the same company has been repriced by the market at least 3 times.**

But XtalPi is different from AI companies that trade purely on hype — it has real customers (**17 of the top 20** global pharmaceutical companies), real revenue (**803 million yuan** in 2025, up 201% year-over-year), and its **first profit** in 2025 (adjusted net income of **258 million yuan**).

The question is: is this "first profit" an inflection point, or a mirage propped up by a one-off mega-deal?

## What XtalPi is

Defined in one sentence:

> **XtalPi = a drug discovery platform that replaces traditional lab manual labor with AI algorithms + quantum physics + automated robotics.**

Breaking down the three keywords:

### 1. AI algorithms

The company has **more than 200 vertical-domain AI models**, covering core drug discovery steps such as crystal structure prediction, molecular design, and protein structure prediction. Its crystal structure prediction accuracy reaches **98%** — a figure validated in Pfizer's 2016 blind test, which put the company on the map.

### 2. Quantum physics

All three founders are MIT postdoctoral researchers in quantum physics. The company's underlying methodology isn't pure machine learning but "computation based on first-principles physics + AI." This is its core difference from most AI drug discovery companies — **others are data-driven, XtalPi is dual-driven by physics and data**.

Counter-argument: physics-driven methods are computationally intensive and costly. If pure-AI approaches (such as Google's Isomorphic Labs) catch up in accuracy, the advantage of the physics-based method could be erased.

### 3. Automated robotics

**More than 200 robotic workstations** that can run experiments around the clock, 7 days a week. This is XtalPi's biggest difference from pure-software AI drug discovery companies — it doesn't just "compute," it also "does." Once the algorithm makes a prediction, robots immediately verify it, and the data flows back to iterate the model.

**This "compute-execute-verify-iterate" closed loop is XtalPi's core narrative.**

In 2015, the three returned from MIT to start the company in China. In 2016, they took part in Pfizer's crystal structure prediction blind test, achieving **100% accuracy**, which made their name across the global pharmaceutical industry.

**One fact worth noting**: the three founders together hold only about **13.45% economic interest**, yet control **60.68% of voting rights** through a dual-class share structure. This means they have absolute control over the company, but their economic stake is not tightly bound to that of ordinary shareholders.

Counter-argument: a structure with low economic interest but high control means that during a phase when the company needs continued dilutive fundraising, the founders feel far less "pain" than ordinary shareholders do.

## The DoveTree mega-deal: what does HK$47 billion mean

On August 5, 2025, XtalPi announced a partnership agreement with DoveTree. This is **the largest single partnership by value in AI drug discovery history**.

| Term | Amount |
|------|------|
| Upfront payment (received) | **$51 million** (about HK$400 million) |
| Second payment | **$49 million** (about HK$385 million) |
| Potential milestone payments + sales royalties | **up to $5.89 billion** (about HK$46.2 billion) |
| **Total contract value** | **about $6 billion (about HK$47 billion)** |

The day the news was announced, XtalPi's stock surged, and within a month it climbed from about HK$7.5 to an all-time high of HK$15.12.

**But there is a critical cognitive trap here**:

HK$47 billion is a **"potential maximum value,"** not confirmed revenue. How much is actually realized depends on how far the drugs in the partnered pipeline can advance — and the historical success rate of drug development is under **10%**.

Put another way: treating HK$47 billion as "confirmed revenue" assumes every partnered drug successfully reaches the market. That has never happened in pharmaceutical history.

**The confirmed, received portion**: the $51 million upfront payment (about 360 million yuan), which has already been recognized in FY2025 revenue, contributing **about 67%** of the drug discovery business's revenue.

**The portion still to be verified**: the timing and conditions for receiving the second $49 million payment. If this payment is delayed or defaulted on, it would be a significant negative signal.

## The AI drug discovery track: a race no one has finished yet

To understand XtalPi, you first need to understand an industry-level fact:

> **As of May 2026, no AI-designed drug has successfully reached the market anywhere in the world.**

That's not to say AI hasn't been useful — more than **80** AI-assisted drug candidates globally have entered clinical trials. Among the fastest-progressing are:

| Drug / Company | Progress | Notes |
|----------|------|------|
| GB-0895 / Generate Biomedicines | **Global Phase III trial** (started December 2025) | The first AI-designed antibody globally to enter Phase III |
| SIGX1094 / Signet Therapeutics (incubated by XtalPi) | **Phase I trial**, 20% lesion shrinkage in the 200mg dose group | Granted FDA Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations, shortlisted for the Galien Prize |
| ISM001-055 / Insilico Medicine | **Phase II trial** | Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis |

**The most optimistic prediction**: an AI-designed drug could enter market approval around 2027. But anyone who has worked in drug development knows how much weight the word "could" carries.

Counter-argument: if no AI-designed drug has successfully reached the market within the next 3-5 years, the entire track could face a crisis of confidence similar to the 2001 dot-com bust. The valuations of AI drug discovery companies rest on the belief that success "will eventually come" — if that belief wavers, valuations could collapse.

## A preview: the essential difference between XtalPi and traditional CRO companies

Traditional CRO companies (such as WuXi AppTec) operate on a **"selling headcount"** business model — the customer pays, and the company sends chemists to run experiments for them. Gross margin is 35-40%, and growth comes from hiring more people.

XtalPi's narrative is: I don't sell headcount, **I sell a platform** — AI computes, robots execute, data accumulates, models iterate. Gross margin is 70%, and growth comes from reuse.

If this narrative holds, XtalPi's endgame looks more like **the AWS of drug discovery** — an infrastructure-level platform that anyone developing drugs has to use.

If this narrative doesn't hold, XtalPi is just an **expensive CRO company with 800 million in revenue, 5 billion in losses, kept alive by mega-deals**.
