**Report date: April 9, 2026**
**Research framework: value-chain panorama scan + Buffett-Munger-Duan Yongping-Li Lu four-master analysis**
**Information richness rating: Grade A (extremely abundant information)**

> **AI research limitations disclosure**: Nuclear power is currently one of the market's hottest investment themes, with a very strong "AI power demand + nuclear renaissance" narrative. AI analysis is highly susceptible to being swept along by optimistic consensus. This report deliberately seeks out counter-evidence — nuclear power has historically gone through two "renaissance disillusionments" (Three Mile Island in 1979, Fukushima in 2011), each collapsing precisely when the narrative was strongest. ## I. Building and Verifying the Investment Logic Chain

### 1.1 The Logic Chain

```
AI compute boom + global electrification
    → Accelerating power demand growth (data-center electricity use reaches 500 TWh by 2027, 2.6x the 2023 level)
        → Rigid demand for clean, stable baseload power (wind/solar are intermittent; carbon constraints are phasing out coal power)
            → Nuclear power is the only power source combining "zero-carbon + baseload + high energy density"
                → Global nuclear renaissance: new builds + restarts + life extensions
                    → Upstream uranium price rises + midstream equipment demand surges + downstream operators benefit
```

### 1.2 Link-by-Link Verification

| Link | Core assumption | Verifying evidence | Credibility |
|------|---------|---------|--------|
| AI → power demand | Data-center electricity consumption is surging | Gartner projects AI servers will consume 500 TWh/year by 2027; IEA confirms 2025 global electricity demand up 4% YoY | ★★★★★ |
| Power demand → nuclear necessity | Wind/solar cannot meet baseload needs | Microsoft/Google/Amazon/Meta have all signed nuclear power purchase agreements — voting with their capital | ★★★★★ |
| Nuclear necessity → global renaissance | Countries' policies are turning supportive | COP28 pledges to triple nuclear capacity by 2050; China has approved 10+ reactor units per year for 4 consecutive years | ★★★★☆ |
| Global renaissance → rising uranium prices | Uranium supply cannot keep up with demand | 2025 global uranium production of 173 Mlb vs. demand of 204 Mlb, a 31 Mlb gap; Goldman projects uranium at $91/lb by end-2026 | ★★★★☆ |
| Global renaissance → equipment demand | New-build units drive equipment procurement | China approved 10 new reactor units in April 2025, driving over CNY 200 billion in investment | ★★★★★ |

### 1.3 Events That Have Already Occurred (Not Predictions)

**Tech giants' nuclear power moves (2024-2026):**
- **Microsoft + Constellation**: signed a 20-year PPA to restart the Three Mile Island nuclear plant to power data centers (September 2024)
- **Amazon**: acquired the Talen Energy nuclear-powered data center campus (March 2024)
- **Google + Kairos Power**: signed an SMR power purchase agreement (October 2024)
- **Meta**: issued a nuclear power RFP seeking 1-4 GW of nuclear supply (December 2024)
- **Oklo + Switch**: signed a primary power supply agreement for up to 12 GW (2025)

**China's accelerating nuclear approvals:**
- Approved 10+ reactor units per year for 4 consecutive years from 2022-2025, with total investment exceeding CNY 800 billion
- In April 2025, the State Council executive meeting approved 5 projects comprising 10 units (Fangchenggang in Guangxi, Taishan in Guangdong, Sanmen in Zhejiang, Haiyang in Shandong, Xiapu in Fujian)
- As of early 2026: units in operation, under construction, and approved total 102, with combined installed capacity of 113 GW — **the largest in the world**

**Tightening uranium market:**
- Uranium prices ranged $63-$101/lb in 2025, with long-term contract prices trending steadily higher
- Major producers (Cameco, Kazatomprom) have already pre-sold years of production
- Global uranium mine output still cannot meet reactor demand; the structural gap persists

> **Duan Yongping's challenge**: The logic chain looks perfect — which is exactly what should raise the most alarm. The last nuclear "renaissance" narrative (2006-2011) was ultimately ended by the Fukushima accident. **Question: what's different this time?** Answer: AI power demand is a genuine incremental demand (tech giants have signed tens of billions of dollars in real PPAs), not the last cycle's demand that was purely fueled by policy subsidies. But nuclear construction cycles are long (5-8 years), so near-term delivery depends entirely on existing capacity.

## II. Value Chain Panorama

### 2.1 Value Chain Structure

```
Upstream: uranium mining → uranium conversion (UF6) → uranium enrichment (LEU) → fuel assembly manufacturing
         │                                              │
Midstream:   └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
         Nuclear island equipment (reactor pressure vessels / steam generators / main pumps / control rod drive mechanisms)
         Conventional island equipment (turbines / generators)
         Instrumentation and control systems (DCS / safety-grade I&C)
         Nuclear-grade auxiliary materials (valves / piping / cables / seals / support hangers)
         ↓
         Engineering, procurement and construction (EPC)
         ↓
Downstream:   Nuclear plant operation → power generation → power sales
         ↓
         Spent fuel processing → nuclear waste management → decommissioning
         ↓
Ancillary:   Nuclear safety inspection/certification → nuclear technology applications (medical/industrial irradiation) → nuclear power ETFs
```

### 2.2 Business Characteristics by Segment

| Segment | Business model | Gross margin | Competitive landscape | Barriers to entry | Cyclicality |
|------|---------|--------|---------|------|--------|
| **Uranium mining** | Selling a resource | 30-50% | Oligopoly (top 5 hold 65%) | Resources + license | Medium (tied to uranium price) |
| **Uranium conversion/enrichment** | Selling processing services | 25-40% | Oligopoly (only 4-5 firms globally) | Technology + license | Weak (mostly long-term contracts) |
| **Fuel assemblies** | Selling customized products | 20-35% | Oligopoly | Technology + certification | Weak |
| **Nuclear island main equipment** | Selling high-end equipment | 25-35% | Oligopoly (China's three major groups domestically) | Technology + certification + capacity | **Strong** (tied to approvals) |
| **Nuclear-grade valves/pumps** | Selling specialized components | 30-45% | Niche monopoly | Certification + customer stickiness | Medium |
| **I&C systems** | Selling systems + services | 35-50% | High-barrier oligopoly | Technology + safety certification | Medium |
| **Engineering/EPC** | Selling services | 8-15% | Duopoly (CNNC + CGN) | Qualifications + experience | Strong |
| **Nuclear plant operation** | Selling power (utility-like) | 45-55% | Duopoly | License (only 2 firms nationwide) | **Weak** (most stable) |
| **Spent fuel processing** | Selling services | 30-40% | Monopoly (CNNC) | Technology + license | Weak |
| **Nuclear technology applications** | Selling equipment + services | 25-40% | Fragmented | Technology | Weak |

### 2.3 Chokepoint Segments

**🔴 The biggest chokepoint: uranium enrichment**
- Only Urenco (Europe), TENEX (Russia), Orano (France), and CNNC have commercial-scale enrichment capacity globally
- After the West's de-Russification, there is a massive shortfall in Western enrichment capacity
- Centrus Energy (LEU) is the only US-approved HALEU enricher

**🟠 High-barrier segment: nuclear island main equipment**
- Reactor pressure vessels and steam generators — only a handful of firms worldwide can manufacture these
- Domestically: Shanghai Electric (95% market share in reactor internals/control rod drive mechanisms), Dongfang Electric, China First Heavy Industries
- Certification cycles of 3-5 years make new entrants nearly impossible

**🟡 Niche monopoly: nuclear-grade valves**
- Jiangsu Shentong: over 90% market share in nuclear-grade butterfly/ball valves
- China Nuclear Valve: the country's earliest nuclear-grade valve company
- High certification barriers; extremely high customer switching costs

> **Buffett's challenge**: Which segment is most like a toll bridge? **Nuclear plant operation** — only CNNC and CGN hold operating licenses nationwide, and completed nuclear plants have extremely low marginal costs (fuel costs are only ~15% of the total), resembling a hydropower-style "money printer" model. But new entrants cannot obtain licenses — this is a true "franchise right."

## IV. Four-Master Analysis of Leading Companies

### 4.1 Constellation Energy (CEG) — Leading US Nuclear Power Operator

**Business essence (Duan Yongping)**: America's largest nuclear power operator, with 31.7 GW of generating capacity (primarily nuclear), selling power to the grid and enterprise customers. Once a nuclear plant is built, marginal costs are extremely low and cash flow is very strong. Revenue rose 8% YoY in 2025, adjusted EPS rose 8%, and net margin was 9.1%.

**Moat (Buffett)**:

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★☆ | Signed a 20-year nuclear PPA with Microsoft, strong pricing power |
| Switching costs | ★★★★★ | Nuclear plants cannot be relocated; customers are geographically locked in |
| Network effects | ★★☆☆☆ | No significant network effects |
| Scale effects | ★★★★★ | America's largest nuclear operator, far ahead of the #2 player |
| Technology/license barriers | ★★★★★ | Nuclear operating license + already-built reactors are irreplicable assets |

Will the moat still be there in 10 years? **Yes.** Nuclear plants are designed for a 60-80-year lifespan; already-built plants are "perpetual assets." New nuclear plants take 8-12 years to build — this is the ultimate barrier.

**Risks (Munger)**:
- Nuclear accident risk (extremely low probability but massive impact)
- Market cap of $105 billion, PE ~35x — has already substantially priced in the AI power-demand narrative
- Electricity market price volatility could compress margins
- **Why wouldn't smart people buy?** Valuation is too rich — even Wells Fargo's $460 price target implies only ~60% upside from the current ~$290, which is high for a utility trading at 35x PE

**Management**: Joe Dominguez, CEO. Drove the Three Mile Island restart, with sharp strategic vision. **Grade B+.**

**Valuation snapshot**: PE ~35x, notably above the utilities-sector average (15-20x). The market is awarding an "AI beneficiary" premium. **On the expensive side.**

**Rating: ★★★☆☆** (a good company but already richly valued — wait for a pullback)

### 4.2 China General Nuclear Power (003816) — Leading A-Share Nuclear Operator

**Business essence (Duan Yongping)**: China's largest civil nuclear power operator, managing 28 units in operation plus 20 under construction. 2025 revenue was CNY 75.7 billion, with net profit attributable to shareholders of CNY 9.765 billion (down 9.9% YoY, mainly due to lower on-grid tariffs and increased overhaul activity). Nuclear plant operation is a classic "collect rent while sitting" business — once units are built, they generate and sell power, with fuel costs only ~15% of the total.

**Moat (Buffett)**:

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★☆☆ | On-grid tariffs are regulated, limiting pricing power |
| Switching costs | ★★★★★ | The grid depends on nuclear baseload, which cannot be replaced |
| Network effects | ★☆☆☆☆ | None |
| Scale effects | ★★★★★ | 28 units operating, 20 under construction — largest nationwide |
| Technology/license barriers | ★★★★★ | Only 2 firms nationwide hold nuclear operating licenses |

Will the moat still be there in 10 years? **Yes, and even stronger.** The 20 units under construction will come online progressively, roughly doubling installed capacity.

**Risks (Munger)**:
- Market-oriented reform of on-grid tariffs could compress profits (already visible in 2025)
- Nuclear safety accidents, though extremely low-probability, carry severe consequences
- Room for the state-owned enterprise's dividend payout ratio to rise, but also policy uncertainty
- **Why wouldn't smart people buy?** Revenue growth is slow (single digits), and net profit actually declined in 2025 — this is not a growth-stock story

**Management**: A state-owned-enterprise governance system with weak individual personality-driven leadership. Capital-expenditure discipline is sound, with projects under construction proceeding steadily. **Grade B.**

**Valuation snapshot**: Dynamic PE ~18.9x, PB ~1.75x — near the low end of its historical range. Versus Constellation's 35x PE, that's a discount of over 50%. **Reasonable to cheap.**

**Rating: ★★★★☆** (a core-position candidate — stable cash flow + capacity release from projects under construction + inexpensive valuation)

> **Li Lu's commentary**: The investment thesis for China General Nuclear Power isn't "growth" — it's "certainty." The 20 units under construction amount to increment locked in for the next 5-8 years, which is rare across any industry. Buying a utility with locked-in incremental growth, strong cash flow, and a licensed monopoly at under 19x PE is a classic value-investing target.

### 4.4 Cameco (CCJ) — Global Leading Uranium Miner

**Business essence (Duan Yongping)**: The world's second-largest uranium miner (15% of global output), listed in Canada. 2025 revenue rose 11% YoY, EPS rose 114%, net margin was 16.9%, and the debt ratio was 0.14. Uranium mining is a classic resource business — whoever has the deposit holds the power, and downstream nuclear plants have no choice but to buy.

**Moat (Buffett)**:

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand/pricing power | ★★★★☆ | Oligopoly pricing, benefits directly from rising uranium prices |
| Switching costs | ★★★☆☆ | Uranium is a standardized commodity, but long-term contracts lock in customers |
| Network effects | ★☆☆☆☆ | None |
| Scale effects | ★★★★★ | World's second-largest, McArthur River is the world's largest high-grade deposit |
| Technology/license barriers | ★★★★☆ | Mining licenses + high-grade ore sources are irreplicable |

**Risks (Munger)**:
- Large uranium price swings (from $140 in 2007 to $18 in 2016) — cyclical risk
- Kazatomprom (a Kazakh state enterprise) could ramp up production and depress prices at any time
- Geopolitical risk (the Canada-Kazakhstan-Russia triangle)

**Valuation snapshot**: Current share price ~$115, Goldman's target is $125+. PE is on the high side (high PE on a cyclical stock usually signals proximity to a cycle peak).

**Rating: ★★★☆☆** (a good company but a cyclical stock that requires timing — buying on a uranium-price pullback is safer)

| Network effects | ★☆☆☆☆ | None |
| Scale effects | ★★★☆☆ | A niche market with limited scale effects |
| Technology/license barriers | ★★★★★ | Nuclear-grade certification plus a multi-year supply track record make new entry extremely hard |

**Risks (Munger)**:
- If the pace of nuclear approvals slows, orders will dry up
- Limited market ceiling (the nuclear-grade valve market is only about CNY 5-7.8 billion per year)
- Volatile valuation, easily driven by speculative trading

**Valuation snapshot**: Market cap ~CNY 12 billion, driven mainly by the pace of nuclear approvals.

**Rating: ★★★★☆** (a niche leader with a monopoly position, suitable as a satellite holding)

## V. Industry-Level Risk Assessment (Munger's "Checklist")

### 5.1 Systemic Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Response |
|------|------|------|------|
| **Nuclear safety accident** | Extremely low (<0.1%/year) | Devastating (would stall the industry for 5-10 years) | Diversify holdings; don't overweight any single nuclear plant operator |
| **AI power demand falls short of expectations** | Medium (20-30%) | High (breaks the first link of the logic chain) | Watch actual data-center groundbreaking figures, not just plans |
| **Uranium price collapse** | Low (10-15%) | High (upstream miners' profits go to zero) | Kazatomprom's output ramp is the key variable to monitor continuously |
| **Policy reversal (anti-nuclear)** | Low (5-10%) | High | Current policy winds strongly favor nuclear power, but elections could change that |
| **SMR technology failure** | Medium-high (40-50%) | Medium (SMR companies go to zero, but doesn't affect traditional nuclear power) | Keep SMR exposure under 5% of the position |
| **Rising interest rates push up construction costs** | Medium (20-30%) | Medium | Already-built operators benefit (moat deepens); projects under construction are hurt |
| **Disruptive technology breakthrough (fusion/energy storage)** | Extremely low (<5% within 10 years) | Extremely high (long term) | No threat within 10 years |

### 5.2 Historical Analogy

**Closest analogy: the 2006-2011 "Nuclear Renaissance 1.0"**

| Comparison dimension | Last time (2006-2011) | This time (2024-2026) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Driver | Rising oil prices + carbon-emissions concerns | AI power demand + carbon neutrality + energy security |
| Catalyst | Mostly policy subsidies | **Tech giants signing real-money PPAs** |
| What ended it | Fukushima accident (March 2011) | **Unknown** |
| Uranium price path | $18 → $140 → $18 | $18 → over $100, currently in the $75-80 range |
| SMR concept | All talk, no substance | NuScale has NRC certification, but still no operating revenue |
| Ultimate winner | **Operators** (already-built plants kept printing cash) | Likely operators again |
| Ultimate loser | Uranium speculators + unbuilt projects | SMR concept stocks likely carry the biggest risk |

**Key lesson**: In the last nuclear renaissance, **operators of already-built nuclear plants** were the only sure winners — regardless of uranium price swings or policy changes, a plant that exists keeps generating and selling power. **Projects under development** and **uranium speculators** mostly lost their shirts.

> **Munger's commentary**: "Did the people selling shovels in the gold rush make money? Some did, most didn't. The people who really made money were the ones who owned the gold mine — an already-built nuclear plant is the gold mine. Future nuclear plants, future SMRs, future uranium production capacity are all 'future' — and the future has too many ways of not happening."

### 5.3 Bias Self-Check

- **Narrative bias** ⚠️: The AI-plus-nuclear-power narrative really is compelling. But a "perfect narrative" is one of the most dangerous signals in investing. Note: the 2006 nuclear narrative was also compelling.
- **Anchoring effect** ⚠️: Constellation's share price rose from $20 in 2022 to $290 in 2026, a 14x gain. It's easy to be anchored on "it can keep rising."
- **Herding effect** ⚠️: When Microsoft, Google, and Amazon are all buying nuclear power, it's hard not to follow the crowd. But what the tech giants are buying is electricity (to hedge power costs), not nuclear power stocks.

## VI. Civilizational Trend Judgment (Li Lu Framework)

### Is This a "Civilizational Paradigm Shift" or a "Passing Phase"?

**Judgment: somewhere in between, leaning toward "long-term structural opportunity."**

Nuclear power isn't a new technology — humanity has used nuclear power generation for 70 years. This isn't "inventing nuclear power," it's **"re-embracing nuclear power."** Three civilizational-scale trends are converging as drivers:

1. **Rigid power demand from the AI/compute era** — this is genuine incremental demand, not a speculative bubble
2. **Global decarbonization** — nuclear power is the only mature zero-carbon baseload power source
3. **Energy security** — after the Russia-Ukraine conflict, countries are re-examining energy independence

### The Closest Historical Technology-Revolution Analogy

**The nuclear power construction boom following the 1970s oil crisis** — also driven by energy security, with the world building nuclear plants on a large scale. Outcome: after being built, those plants did generate power and earn money for decades, but along the way there were the huge setbacks of Three Mile Island and Chernobyl. **The long-term winners were the already-built operators.**

### The 10-20 Year Endgame

- Nuclear power's share of global electricity generation rises from 10% to 15-20%
- China becomes the world's largest nuclear power nation (already is)
- SMRs may start contributing incremental capacity after 2035, but with a limited share
- Fusion power will not achieve commercialization before mid-century

### Which Segment Is Most Likely to "Win It All"?

**Nuclear plant operation** — license monopoly plus irreplicable already-built assets, a classic winner-take-all setup.

### Which Segment Is Most Likely to Be Disrupted?

**Uranium mining** — if seawater uranium-extraction technology breaks through (currently still 5-10x more costly), uranium prices could fall permanently. But this is unlikely within 10 years.

> **Li Lu's commentary**: "The nuclear power investment opportunity doesn't lie in it being 'new' — quite the opposite, its value lies in being 'old' but rediscovered. Truly civilizational investment opportunities are often not the newest technology, but something already proven effective that the market has temporarily forgotten. The investment value of China's nuclear power operators is this: you're buying, at under 20x PE, an asset with 60 years of certain cash flow — and one that's still growing at over 10% per year."
