## II. Pricing Power: The Core Difference in Moats

### Feitian Moutai vs. Eighth-Generation Puwu

| Dimension | Feitian Moutai | Eighth-Generation Puwu |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Ex-factory price | **1,269 yuan** (raised in March 2026) | 1,019 yuan |
| Wholesale price (May 2026) | **1,640–1,680 yuan** | 760–780 yuan |
| Terminal retail price | ~1,539 yuan (official) / at or above wholesale price | ~809 yuan (as low as 719 yuan on e-commerce) |
| Wholesale/ex-factory ratio | **129–132%** (premium) | **75%** (inverted) |
| Distributor profit per bottle | **300–400 yuan profit** | **70–200 yuan loss** |
| Price increase in 2026? | **Already raised** (+8.6%) | Explicitly says no price increase under consideration |
| Price-increase history | 10 price increases since listing; ex-factory price up 5.8x | No substantive increase in years |

**This table is a microcosm of the moat gap between the two companies.** Moutai's wholesale price runs 29–32% above its ex-factory price, and distributors are scrambling for supply; Wuliangye's wholesale price runs 25% below its ex-factory price, and distributors lose money on every bottle sold. One is a seller's market with demand exceeding supply; the other is a buyer's market with supply exceeding demand.

## III. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown of the Moat

### Layer One: Brand

| Dimension | Moutai | Wuliangye |
|------|------|--------|
| Brand positioning | **China's only "social hard currency"** | Leader in the 1,000-yuan price tier |
| Irreplaceability of occasion | Extremely high — "not serving Moutai at a business banquet signals you don't take it seriously" | Moderate — can be substituted by Guojiao 1573 or Jiannanchun |
| Financial-asset property | Yes — can preserve/appreciate in value and be liquidated | None |
| Mindshare monopoly | **No competitor** in the 2,000-yuan-plus price band | Many competitors in the 1,000-yuan price band |
| Brand scarcity | **Irreplicable** (triple scarcity of production region + craftsmanship + history) | Replicable (the technical barrier for nong-xiang/strong-aroma craftsmanship is lower than for jiang-xiang/sauce-aroma) |

**Moutai's brand moat isn't just "strong" — it's in a class of its own.** In China's baijiu industry, Moutai is the only brand that combines consumer-goods, social, and financial-asset properties simultaneously. Wuliangye has only the consumer-goods property.

### Layer Two: Production-Capacity Constraints

| Dimension | Moutai | Wuliangye |
|------|------|--------|
| Core craft | Sauce-aroma, the "12987" process (1-year production cycle + 5 years of aging) | Strong-aroma, 60–90 day fermentation cycle |
| Aging period | **At least 5 years** (this year's production can't be sold until 2031) | 1–3 years |
| Dependence on production region | **Extremely high** — Moutai Town's core-region microbial environment cannot be replicated | Moderate — Yibin has advantages but is not the sole production region |
| Difficulty of expanding capacity | **Extremely high** — triply constrained by production-region area, microbial environment, and aging period | Moderate — 100,000 tons of new capacity already under construction |
| Current sellable capacity | ~47,000 tons/year | ~27,000 tons/half-year (premium tier) |
| Designed capacity utilization | **126%** (running above rated capacity) | ~70% (has headroom) |

**Moutai's capacity constraint is a natural, unbreakable moat.** A 5-year aging period means that even if Moutai doubled all its capacity today, the incremental liquor wouldn't be sellable until 2031. This "time lock" means no competitor can catch up quickly just by spending money. Wuliangye's strong-aroma craft also requires aged fermentation pits, but with a shorter fermentation cycle, lower aging requirements, and easier capacity expansion, its moat is an order of magnitude shallower.

### Layer Three: Pricing Power

| Dimension | Moutai | Wuliangye |
|------|------|--------|
| Current pricing power | **Extremely strong** — demand exceeds supply; even after price increases, wholesale price stays well above ex-factory price | **Almost none** — wholesale price is below ex-factory price, unable to raise prices |
| Price-increase frequency | 10 increases over 25 years since listing | No substantive increase in years |
| Room for further increases | Ample — ex-factory price at 1,269 yuan, wholesale at 1,640+, still room to grow | None — ex-factory price of 1,019 yuan is already above the 780-yuan wholesale price |
| Benchmark | Global luxury goods (Hermès gross margin 71%, Moutai 92%) | Domestic baijiu peers |

### Layer Four: Financial Texture

| Metric | Moutai | Wuliangye | Meaning |
|------|------|--------|------|
| Gross margin | **92%** | 77–81% | A bottle of Moutai costs about 60 yuan to make and sells for 1,269 yuan |
| Net margin | **48–50%** | 22–36% | Nearly 50 yuan of every 100 yuan Moutai earns is profit |
| ROE | **~30%** | 6.9% (a clean-out year) / ~25% (a normal year) | Moutai's return on capital is higher and steadier |
| Capex needs | Extremely low | Low | Both are asset-light, but Moutai is even lighter |

| Capacity barrier | ★★★★★ (5-year aging + irreplicable production region) | ★★★ (aged pits have value, but expansion is easier) |

## IV. Which One Is More Severely Undervalued?

### Moutai: Moderately Undervalued

| Dimension | Data |
|------|------|
| Current PE | ~21x (TTM) / ~20x (forward) |
| Historical PE center | 25–30x |
| Current PE / historical center | **70–84%** (below the historical mean) |
| Dividend yield | **~4%** |
| Margin of safety | 178.0 billion yuan cash + zero debt + 79% payout ratio |

**Reason for undervaluation:** the industry adjustment cycle + the first-ever simultaneous decline in revenue and net profit + a backlog of social inventory + a governance shadow over management (three chairmen investigated in five years)

**Is the undervaluation justified? Partly.** The industry adjustment is real, but Moutai's fundamentals (92% gross margin, demand exceeding supply, continued price increases) have not been impaired. Paying 20x PE for a company with a 92% gross margin, 30% ROE, zero debt, and a 4% dividend yield leaves limited downside risk even if the industry doesn't recover.

### Wuliangye: Deeply Undervalued on Paper, but for Well-Founded Reasons

| Dimension | Data |
|------|------|
| Current market cap | ~358.0 billion yuan |
| PE on adjusted 2025 net profit | ~40x (distorted) |
| PE on institutions' forecast 2026 net profit of ~36.0 billion yuan | **Forward PE ~10x** |
| Dividend yield | **~5.3%** |
| Buybacks + shareholder purchases | 11–15 billion yuan (very aggressive) |

**Reasons for undervaluation:**
1. **The 30.3-billion-yuan accounting error** — a one-time clearing of the channel-overstock bubble, exposing years of inflated revenue recognition
2. **Two consecutive chairmen brought down** — Li Shuguang (2025) and Zeng Congqin (February 2026), a collapse of governance trust
3. **CSRC investigation** — if it is determined to be a "disclosure violation," administrative penalties may follow
4. **Wholesale-price inversion** — ex-factory price of 1,019 yuan vs. wholesale price of 760–780 yuan; distributors sell at a loss
5. **Overtaken by Fenjiu** — 2025 net-profit ranking: Moutai first, Fenjiu second, Luzhou Laojiao third, Wuliangye fourth
6. **Declining share of the strong-aroma category** — the strong-aroma category's market share has fallen 10 percentage points in 2 years, squeezed by both sauce-aroma and light-aroma baijiu

**Is the undervaluation justified? Mostly.** Wuliangye's undervaluation isn't a market mistake — it's the market pricing in a **governance collapse plus a channel crisis plus a competitive disadvantage in its category**. Every one of these problems is structural, not something that can be resolved in a quarter or two.

## VI. Investment Judgment

### If You Can Only Buy One: Buy Moutai

**Reasons:**
1. **The moat gap is qualitative, not just quantitative** — Moutai's moat is "irreplicable" (production region + 5-year aging + brand monopoly), while Wuliangye's moat is "erodable" (low technical barrier for strong-aroma craft + intense competition in the 1,000-yuan price tier)
2. **Pricing power is night and day** — Moutai is raising prices, while Wuliangye's wholesale price is inverted. This is the most direct expression of the fundamental gap between the two companies
3. **Governance risk is fundamentally different** — Moutai has had corruption issues historically, but its core product "can sell well without depending on management." Wuliangye's 30.3-billion-yuan incident exposes a systemic governance flaw, and rebuilding trust will take years
4. **Stronger downside protection** — Moutai at 20x PE + 4% dividend yield + 178.0 billion yuan cash + 92% gross margin has limited downside even if the industry continues to adjust. Wuliangye's 10x forward PE looks cheap, but the reliability of the denominator (the 36.0-billion-yuan profit forecast) is questionable

### Wuliangye Isn't Uninvestable, But You'd Be Betting on Three Things Happening at Once

1. A mild outcome from the CSRC investigation (determined to be an accounting error rather than a disclosure violation)
2. The wholesale price stabilizing and recovering above 900 yuan in the second half of 2026
3. New management genuinely driving channel reform and restoring market confidence

If all three happen, Wuliangye at 10x forward PE could double. But if any one of them fails to materialize, the current price may not be the bottom.

### One-Line Summary

> **The gap between Moutai and Wuliangye isn't the gap between "number one and number two" — it's the gap between "having pricing power and not having it."** Moutai raised its price 8.6%, and its wholesale price still runs 30% above its ex-factory price — proof of a monopolistic brand. Wuliangye's ex-factory price is 1,019 yuan against a wholesale price of 780 yuan — proof that its brand power isn't strong enough to support its price. During the baijiu industry's adjustment period, companies with pricing power can safely weather the cycle; companies without it can only pray for an industry recovery.
