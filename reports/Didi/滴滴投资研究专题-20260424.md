## 0. Four-sentence core conclusion

### Q1: What kind of company is Didi?
**A wounded super-leader.** 2025 revenue **RMB 226.7 billion (+9.6%)**, adjusted net profit **RMB 7.86 billion (+41%)**, core order volume 18.24 billion, average daily orders 40 million (peak above 65 million), China market share **70-73%**, cash + short-term investments RMB 55.7 billion, 12 consecutive quarters of double-digit order growth. The international business (Brazil's 99 + Mexico) has posted positive EBITA for 2 straight years, and the autonomous-driving subsidiary — valued at US$5.0 billion — is raising an independent funding round.

### Q2: What is fair value?
**Four-method composite valuation of US$38.0-47.0 billion** (neutral case US$40.0-50.0 billion). Current OTC market cap for DIDIY is roughly US$25.0-28.0 billion, implying a **PS of just 0.85-1.0x** (a quarter of Uber's 3.5-3.7x), reflecting a 25-40% China-concept discount plus a 5-10% data-security "heart condition" discount. **Upside is 50-90%** (neutral case) / 100-150% (bull case).

### Q3: When will it relist?
**A Hong Kong Stock Exchange main-board IPO is the most likely path (60% probability)**, with the base case being a **listing in Q1-Q2 2027** (40% probability). The bottleneck isn't the Hong Kong exchange itself — it's the **dual regulatory gate** (Cyberspace Administration of China + CSRC, 9-18 months total approval time). IPO pricing is estimated at US$34.0-45.0 billion (roughly half the US$67.0 billion valuation at the 2021 NYSE listing). **Market rumors point to a target valuation of HKD 600 billion ≈ US$77.0 billion**, but this diverges more than 50% from the financial valuation of US$40.0-50.0 billion.

### Q4: What are the key risks?
**Three gray rhinos:**
1. **Mandatory driver social-insurance enrollment** (weight 35%) — if it follows the path taken with food-delivery riders, annual costs could rise RMB 20-50 billion, **directly wiping out a full year's net profit**
2. **Data-security "heart condition"** (weight 40%) — the core business is inherently data-intensive; a single incident could trigger an app takedown and a valuation collapse
3. **Prolonged IPO approval** (weight 25%) — the dual regulatory gate takes 9-18 months

**Key takeaways**:

- The aggregator model's biggest threat isn't order poaching — it's **suppressing take rate** and **shifting pricing power** away from platforms
- 2024-08: the industry-wide "commission cap (27% ceiling)" move was made against this backdrop

| Ant Group | 2020-11 | 7.1 billion | **Has not restarted its IPO to this day** |

### 7.2 Three ongoing risks (by weight)

#### Risk 1: Data compliance — a "permanent heart condition" (weight 40%)
- The core business is inherently data-intensive
- Any data leak/hack/employee data sale could trigger a renewed review
- **A single incident could lead to an app takedown and an irreversible valuation collapse**
- The essence of the 2021 incident wasn't "listing violations" — it was a **data-sovereignty red line**: Didi holds nationwide road-mapping data and vehicle-movement records around sensitive military zones

#### Risk 2: Mandatory driver social-insurance enrollment (weight 35%)
- **3-4 million** full-time drivers (comparable to the social-insurance path taken with delivery riders)
- **Estimated annual cost increase of RMB 20-50 billion**
- **Would directly wipe out Didi's full-year net profit** (roughly RMB 7.9 billion in 2025)
- This is a **gray rhino**

#### Risk 3: Extended IPO approval timeline (weight 25%)
- The dual gate takes 9-18 months
- The 2023-03 Filing Administration Measures + Article 7 of the 2022 Cybersecurity Review Measures
- The so-called **"Didi clause"**: mandatory review for any platform with over 1 million users seeking a listing abroad, effectively tailored to Didi

### 7.3 Three-scenario probabilities

| Scenario | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| **Mild (base case)** | **70%** | Hong Kong IPO approved H1 2027 + gradual social-insurance rollout |
| Harsh | 20% | New data incident/US-China deterioration/driver-related black swan → IPO delayed 12-24 months |
| Loose | 10% | US-China agreement + valuation rebound |

### 7.4 Evidence of regulatory thaw

- **2023-01-16**: App restored to app stores (**a formal signal that the rectification passed**)
- **2023-07-12**: Premier Li Qiang chaired a platform-economy roundtable: "support the platform economy"
- **2023-12**: Central Economic Work Conference included "standardized, healthy, sustained development of the platform economy" for the first time
- **2024-07**: Third Plenum called for "promoting innovative development of the platform economy"
- **In 2025, the "anti-involution" focus is on food delivery/e-commerce, not ride-hailing**

## 10. Tallying the three moats against the three risks

### Moats (positive)

1. **Two-sided network effects**: a pool of 40 million daily orders, driver wait times under 2 minutes
2. **Algorithms + data**: 12 years of accumulated dispatch/pricing/ETA models
3. **Brand as verb**: "Didi" is synonymous with "hail a ride"
4. **Capital structure**: Tencent 17% + Alibaba 15% + Uber 10% + SoftBank 20% = no credible rival with comparable backing
5. **First-mover in electrification**: 68% of mileage electrified, delivering a cost tailwind

### Risks (negative)

1. **Driver social insurance** (RMB 20-50 billion/year in added cost)
2. **Data security** (a single incident could be fatal)
3. **Aggregator encroachment** (Amap squeezing take rate)
4. **International expansion losses** (2025 Q4 EBITA of -RMB 3.44 billion)
5. **Autonomous-driving disruption** (Pony.ai/WeRide pose a 5-10 year challenge)

### Overall assessment

**Didi is a "wounded super-leader"**:
- Core business cash flow is stable
- Balance sheet is solid (RMB 55.7 billion in net cash)
- International operations have a growth story (Brazil + Mexico)
- Autonomous driving offers optionality

But **it is not "an undervalued great company"** — it is a **"discounted OK company"**:
- The discount is named **Chinese regulation + driver social insurance + aggregator encroachment**
- If the Hong Kong IPO and a robotaxi spin-off both land, that would mark a valuation re-rating inflection point
- If any black swan resurfaces, there is still downside room on the OTC price
