## I. Autohome (ATHM): The benchmark IPO for China's vertical auto platforms

### 1.1 Basic listing information

| Item | Data |
|------|------|
| Listing date | December 11, 2013 |
| Listing venue | New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) |
| Ticker | ATHM |
| Offer price | $17/ADS |
| First-day closing price | $30.07 (up 76.88%) |
| IPO proceeds | approx. $133 million |
| Market cap at first-day close | approx. $3.17 billion |

### 1.2 Financials at the time of IPO

- **Revenue for the first three quarters of 2013**: RMB 831 million (up 62.6% year-on-year)
- **Net profit for the first three quarters of 2013**: RMB 334 million (up 96.7% year-on-year)
- **2010-2012 revenue**: RMB 253 million → 433 million → 733 million, a 70.2% compound annual growth rate
- **2010-2012 net profit**: RMB 80 million → 135 million → 213 million, a 62.7% compound annual growth rate
- **Core revenue mix**: advertising revenue accounted for 74% (RMB 618 million in the first three quarters of 2013), with the remainder from dealer services

**Full-year 2013 estimate**: extrapolating from the first-three-quarters trend, full-year revenue was roughly RMB 1.1-1.2 billion and net profit roughly RMB 450-500 million. At the first-day market cap of $3.17 billion (approx. RMB 19.5 billion), that implies a P/E of roughly 16-18x and a P/S of roughly 16-18x.

### 1.3 Post-IPO performance and market-cap trajectory

| Milestone | Share price (USD) | Market cap ($B) | Key events |
|----------|-------------|---------------|---------|
| Dec 2013 IPO | 17→30 | ~3.17 | First-day price surged 77% |
| 2014-2016 | Sustained rally | 5.0-8.0 | China's auto market grew rapidly |
| 2018 | ~90 | ~11.0 | Revenue RMB 7.233 billion, up 16.5% YoY |
| Feb 2021 peak | ~138 | ~17.0+ | All-time high |
| Mar 2021 | Secondary listing in Hong Kong | HK ticker 2518 | HKEX market cap approx. HK$91.3 billion |
| 2024 | Decline | ~3.5-4.0 | Revenue RMB 7.04 billion, net profit RMB 1.68 billion |
| May 2026 | ~19 | ~2.4 | Market cap sharply reduced |

### 1.4 Revenue-mix evolution (2024)

| Business segment | Revenue (RMB, billions) | Share | YoY change |
|----------|-------------|------|---------|
| Media services (advertising) | 1.52 | 21.6% | -18.6% |
| Leads-generation services | 3.14 | 44.6% | +0.8% |
| Online marketplace and other | 2.38 | 33.8% | +8.1% |
| **Total** | **7.04** | **100%** | **-2.0%** |

### 1.5 Key takeaways

1. **Listing during the high-growth phase is the best window**: Autohome went public while revenue was compounding at 70% annually and profit was growing rapidly, and it earned a very high valuation premium.
2. **Vertical platforms have a ceiling**: after listing, Autohome's market cap rose from $3.17 billion to as much as $17 billion before eventually falling back to $2.4 billion, indicating that a single vertical platform's long-term growth potential is limited.
3. **The evolution from advertising to leads to transactions**: revenue mix shifted from advertising (74% at IPO) to leads services (45% currently), a common evolutionary path across the industry.
4. **Current valuation reflects an industry trough**: Autohome's current market cap of only $2.4 billion, a P/E of about 10x and a P/S of about 2.1x, reflects the market's pessimistic expectations for the growth prospects of traditional auto vertical platforms.

## II. Bitauto (BITA): Lessons from IPO to going private

### 2.1 Listing and development

| Item | Data |
|------|------|
| Listing date | November 17, 2010 |
| Listing venue | New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) |
| Ticker | BITA |
| Offer price | $12 |
| IPO proceeds | approx. $100 million |
| Market cap at first-day close | approx. $400 million |

### 2.2 Key financials

- **2010 revenue**: under RMB 460 million
- **2016 revenue**: nearly RMB 5.8 billion, a 52.5% compound annual growth rate
- **Peak share price**: $91.28, reached in 2014
- **2019 net profit margin**: -8.89% (versus 38% for Autohome in the same period)

### 2.3 Going-private process

| Time | Event |
|------|------|
| September 2019 | Received a take-private acquisition proposal |
| June 2020 | Completed the take-private transaction at $16/ADS, total deal value $1.1 billion |
| Post-privatization | Taken over by a buyer consortium led by Tencent |

### 2.4 Key lessons

1. **Early to the race, late to the finish**: Bitauto listed earlier than Autohome but was ultimately overtaken by it entirely, showing that first-mover advantage is not reliable in the internet industry.
2. **Profitability is the decisive dividing line**: Bitauto suffered chronic losses with a net profit margin of -8.89%, while Autohome maintained a 38% net profit margin — the gap between their market caps widened from comparable to a 10x difference.
3. **Going private was a "dignified exit"**: the $1.1 billion take-private price was far below Autohome's contemporaneous market cap of over $10 billion — in essence, a low-price acquisition.
4. **Tencent's strategic logic**: Tencent acquired Bitauto to fill a gap in its automotive ecosystem, not because it saw Bitauto's standalone value.

| Net profit | -- | $20.05 million (-58%) | $5 million |

### 4.3 Current valuation

- **Market cap, May 2026**: $581 million
- **Revenue scale**: ~$720 million
- **P/S**: approximately 0.8x
- **EV/EBITDA**: approximately 10x at the time of the spin-off listing

### 4.4 Key takeaways

3. **Market cap has shrunk sharply**: from $1.95 billion at listing to $581 million, reflecting the fate of low-growth traditional platforms.
4. **A cautionary example for Dongchedi**: Cars.com's struggles stem from its lack of a content ecosystem and technological innovation — it is a pure classifieds-advertising platform.

### 5.2 IPO-rumor timeline

| Time | Rumored plan | Outcome |

| 2021 | Considering a Hong Kong listing, raising over $100 million | Did not materialize |
| August 2024 | Planned to list in the U.S. within the year, raising approx. $200 million | Unconfirmed |

### 5.3 Key takeaways

2. **The predicament of the asset-heavy model**: a used-car transaction platform (C2C/C2B/B2C) differs from an asset-light information platform — it requires heavy offline investment, making profitability difficult.
3. **Limited reference value for Dongchedi**: Guazi's business model (used-car transactions) differs substantially from Dongchedi's (auto content + leads), but the fact that it has "remained unlisted for so long" is itself a cautionary signal.
4. **Regulatory and market-environment uncertainty**: U.S. regulatory scrutiny of Chinese concept stocks, U.S.-China relations, and similar factors are common obstacles for all Chinese internet companies seeking to list in the U.S.

## VIII. Inferring Dongchedi's listing path

### 8.1 Most likely listing path

**Path: Hong Kong Main Board IPO**

Rationale:
1. **Hong Kong already selected**: in February 2026, it was confirmed that a Hong Kong listing was being considered, with Citigroup and Goldman Sachs selected as sponsors.
2. **Avoiding U.S. regulatory risk**: as ByteDance's first listing project, choosing Hong Kong avoids the scrutiny that Chinese concept stocks face in the United States.
3. **Hong Kong is friendly to new-economy companies**: weighted voting rights (WVR) structures already have well-established precedents on the Hong Kong exchange.
4. **Paving the way for ByteDance's further spin-offs**: if Dongchedi's Hong Kong IPO succeeds, it will provide a template for spinning off other ByteDance businesses (such as Douyin e-commerce and Feishu/Lark).

## IX. Core risks and points to watch

### 9.1 Valuation risk

1. **Autohome's "cautionary tale"**: its market cap fell from $17 billion to $2.4 billion, an evaporation of more than 85%, suggesting that the long-term valuation center for auto vertical platforms may be far below the optimistic expectations at IPO.
2. **Private-market valuation bubble**: Chehaoduo's $10 billion valuation has still failed to translate into a public listing, showing that private-market valuations do not necessarily hold up in public markets.
3. **Hong Kong liquidity discount**: Hong Kong-listed shares typically carry a 10-30% liquidity discount relative to U.S.-listed shares.

### 9.2 Business risk

1. **Revenue undisclosed**: Dongchedi has not yet disclosed specific financial data, so investors cannot judge its true profitability.
2. **Relationship with ByteDance**: although legally independent, it remains highly dependent on ByteDance's traffic, technology, and data ecosystem, and the fairness of related-party transaction pricing is questionable.
3. **Slowing industry growth**: growth in China's auto market is slowing, and Autohome's revenue has declined for two consecutive years.
4. **Uncertainty of the CPS model**: the shift from CPT to CPS is an innovation, but it also means revenue is directly tied to auto transaction volume, making it more cyclical.

### 9.3 Structural risk

1. **The "benchmark pressure" of ByteDance's first IPO**: if Dongchedi's listing underperforms, it will affect ByteDance's spin-off plans for other businesses.
2. **The overall environment for Chinese concept stocks**: although choosing Hong Kong avoids part of the risk, uncertainty in U.S.-China relations will still affect international investor sentiment.
3. **Intensifying competition**: ongoing competition from Autohome and Bitauto (Tencent-affiliated), plus the auto-content ecosystems being built by short-video platforms such as Douyin and Kuaishou themselves.

| Key benchmark | Autohome's 2013 IPO ($3.17 billion first-day market cap) |

### 10.2 Core variables investors should watch

1. **Revenue and profit figures disclosed in the prospectus**: this is the only reliable basis for assessing valuation reasonableness.
2. **Arrangements for related-party transactions with the ByteDance ecosystem**: whether the pricing of traffic procurement, technical services, and data sharing is fair.
3. **Conversion rate and average order value under the CPS model**: this is the core point of differentiation from Autohome's traditional model.
4. **The user-growth curve**: whether DAU/MAU growth can be sustained, especially after Douyin's traffic support diminishes.
5. **Autohome's market-cap trend**: as the most direct comparable company, Autohome's valuation will be an important reference for Dongchedi's pricing.

### 10.3 Summary of historical patterns

From the five comparable cases, the following patterns can be distilled:

- **Listing during a high-growth phase yields the highest valuation**: Autohome achieved a 16x P/S at IPO while growing at a 70% rate, and CarGurus achieved a 4-7x P/S while growing at a 65% rate.
- **Vertical platforms have a clear ceiling**: in every case, the gap between peak market cap and later-stage market cap is enormous, showing that high valuations are not sustainable.
- **Profitability is the fundamental determinant of long-term value**: Autohome (38% net margin) versus Bitauto (-8.89% net margin) — this decided their entirely different fates.
- **The transition from information to transactions determines the upper bound**: pure information/advertising platforms (Cars.com) have limited growth, while platforms that move into the transaction layer have far greater potential.
