## What does a 0.65x P/EV multiple mean?

RMB 1.5 trillion of embedded value, and the market is willing to pay only RMB 1 trillion for it — a 35% discount. This isn't just a "cheap" label; it's a question waiting to be answered: **what is the market discounting?**

Ping An's current A-share P/EV is about **0.65x**, and its H-share multiple is even lower, at about **0.58x**. This discount isn't random noise — it's built up from three layers of logic, each backed by real fundamentals.

Historically, Ping An's A-share P/EV has traded within these ranges:

| P/EV range | Historical period | In hindsight |
|----------|-----------------|------------|
| 1.5-2.0x | 2017-2018 | Market overoptimism |
| 1.0-1.5x | 2019-2020 | Reasonably high |
| 0.8-1.0x | 2023 | A reasonable range |
| 0.6-0.8x | 2022, 2024-2026 | Historic bottom zone |
| Below 0.6x | Extreme low in October 2022 | Panic-driven undervaluation |

**A P/EV of 0.65x sits at a historically very low level.** But "historically low" doesn't mean "bound to rebound" — if the fundamentals are deteriorating, low valuations can persist for a very long time (Japanese insurers are a cautionary tale).

The key question becomes: **what is the market discounting?**

---

## What is the market worried about? Unpacking the valuation discount

Ping An's P/EV discount is made up of three layers:

### Layer one: the falling-interest-rate discount

This is the single most important driver of the discount.

China's 10-year government bond yield fell from **3.6%** in 2018 to about **1.7%** in 2026. For an insurer, this means:

1. **Yields on newly added fixed-income assets keep declining:** most of Ping An's RMB 6.49 trillion of insurance funds are allocated to bonds and time deposits. As older assets mature, the yields available on new assets keep getting lower.
2. **Net investment yield under pressure:** the 2025 net investment yield was **3.8%**, down 0.4 percentage points year-on-year, versus a 10-year average of **4.8%**.
3. **Negative-spread risk:** if investment yields stay below policies' guaranteed crediting rates for a long time, the insurer is effectively "losing money selling insurance."

**Management's position:** "There is no negative-spread problem in either new or in-force business." Their rationale: the product mix has shifted toward participating (dividend-paying) policies (with guaranteed rates as low as **1.25%-1.75%**), and mortality and expense margins now make up a larger share of profit, reducing reliance on the interest spread.

**The market's doubts:** management says there's no negative spread, but the market isn't buying it. The investment-yield assumption implied by the current 0.65x P/EV is only about **1.3%-1.7%** — in other words, the market is pricing Ping An as though it can only earn a 1.7% investment yield going forward. The actual comprehensive investment yield in 2025 was **6.3%**.

**If you believe Ping An can sustain an investment yield above 4% over the long run, 0.65x P/EV is clearly undervalued. If you believe interest rates will grind toward zero the way they did in Japan, the current valuation may still not be cheap enough.**

Sources: Soochow Securities in-depth research report, Industrial Securities commentary, Jiemian News

### Layer two: the diversified-financial-conglomerate discount

Pure life insurers (such as AIA) typically trade at a higher P/EV than diversified financial conglomerates. The reasons:

1. **Business complexity:** diversified financial groups are harder to value, and the market tends to discount what it "doesn't fully understand"
2. **Bank drag:** Ping An Bank contributes about 18% of profit, but bank stocks trade at only 0.5-0.7x P/B, dragging down the overall valuation
3. **Technology/asset-management losses:** losses in the asset-management segment and thin profit in the technology segment are both "valuation burdens"

**Comparison with AIA:**

| Dimension | AIA | China Ping An |
|------|---------|---------|
| P/EV | **1.12x** | **0.65x** |
| Business mix | Pure life insurance | Life + P&C + banking + technology |
| Market positioning | High-end | Mass market |
| Management transparency | High | Medium |
| Interest-rate sensitivity | Medium (diversified across markets) | High (concentrated in China) |
| Real-estate exposure | None | Some (though being wound down) |

AIA's P/EV is **1.7 times** Ping An's. Of that gap, roughly 30%-40% is arguably justified (the diversified-conglomerate discount plus China interest-rate risk), but the remaining 60%-70% may reflect excessive pessimism.

**The counterargument:** AIA operates across multiple Asia-Pacific markets, so its interest-rate risk is naturally diversified; Ping An is concentrated in the China market, so its interest-rate risk is concentrated too. AIA also carries no banking baggage, no China Fortune Land Development history, and a higher-quality agent force. The 1.7x gap may not be "excessive pessimism" but rather "reasonable pricing."

Sources: Eastmoney, Eastmoney research reports

### Layer three: the confidence discount

This layer is the least quantifiable — but may be the most important.

- **The scar left by China Fortune Land Development:** the RMB 54 billion blow-up happened four years ago, but once investor trust is damaged, it takes a long time to rebuild
- **Ma Mingzhe's age:** at 68, the market considers succession planning insufficiently clear
- **Insurance's "unsexy" image:** in an era dominated by AI, new energy, and semiconductors, insurance stocks naturally struggle to attract incremental capital
- **The A/H price-gap lock-in effect:** the A-share/H-share price gap isn't large (about 5%-10%), but weaker liquidity in Hong Kong weighs on the overall valuation

## Sum-of-the-parts valuation: breaking Ping An apart

Since the market is "discounting" the diversified-financial-conglomerate structure, let's break Ping An into pieces and value each separately.

### Method one: life insurance EV + other businesses

| Segment | Valuation method | Valuation (RMB billion) | Notes |
|------|---------|------------|------|
| Life & health insurance | 0.8x life-insurance EV | 928.6 × 0.8 = **742.9** | Conservatively assigned 0.8x (vs. AIA's 1.12x) |
| P&C insurance | 15x net profit (estimated at RMB 20 billion) | **300** | P&C's COR is strong; benchmarked to industry level |
| Ping An Bank | 0.6x P/B (net assets of about RMB 460 billion) | **276** | Current valuation midpoint for bank stocks |

| **Degree of undervaluation** | | **~17%** | |

### Method two: P/EV + NBV growth

| Scenario | Assumption | Reasonable P/EV | Target market cap (RMB billion) | Implied A-share price | Upside from current RMB 58 |
|------|------|---------|----------------|------------|-------------------|
| Optimistic | Sustained high NBV growth + stabilizing rates | 1.0x | ~1,550 | ~**RMB 86** | +48% |
| Neutral | Moderate NBV growth + investment yield holds | 0.8x | ~1,240 | ~**RMB 69** | +19% |
| Pessimistic | Sharp rate decline + NBV growth slows | 0.55x | ~850 | ~**RMB 47** | -19% |

### Method three: dividend-yield-implied valuation

If Ping An is priced as a "high-dividend stock":

- 2025 dividend per share: **RMB 2.70**
- At a required **5%** dividend yield: implied price of **RMB 54** (close to the current price)
- At an accepted **4%** dividend yield: implied price of **RMB 67.5** (+16%)
- At an accepted **3.5%** dividend yield: implied price of **RMB 77** (+33%)

Given that Ping An's dividend has grown for 14 consecutive years, assuming 5% annual dividend growth going forward, dividend per share could reach about **RMB 3.12** in three years. At a 4% dividend yield, that implies a price of **RMB 78** three years out. Add roughly **RMB 8.5** of cumulative dividends over three years, and total return comes to about **49%**.

**The key premise behind this calculation is that dividend growth continues.** If falling rates drag profits down persistently, dividend growth could stall.

## The essence of the market's disagreement

Pull all of the above together, and the market's disagreement on Ping An really comes down to one question:

> **Will China's interest-rate environment "turn Japanese"?**

**If it doesn't (rates stabilize at 2%-3%):**
- Ping An's investment yield can be sustained above 4%
- The EV assumption (4.0%-4.5%) is conservative, and true EV is being understated
- 0.65x P/EV is severely undervalued; a reasonable P/EV should sit at 0.8-1.0x
- Combined with a 4.8% dividend yield and NBV growth, annualized returns could reach **15%-20%**

**If it does (rates approach 0%-1%):**
- Ping An's investment yield could fall to 2%-3%
- The investment-return assumption embedded in EV (4.0%) may be too high, and true EV may need to be discounted by 20%-30%
- The negative-spread problem in the in-force book could shift from "nonexistent" to "real"
- 0.65x P/EV may not actually be cheap — Japanese insurers have traded at 0.3-0.5x P/EV for a long time

**This is a macro question, not a company question.** If you're confident in China's macro economy (GDP growth holding at 4%-5%, inflation returning to around 2%), Ping An is most likely undervalued. If you believe China is following Japan's path, then no Chinese insurer should be bought — not just Ping An.

## An angle that's easy to overlook: Ping An vs. "putting your money in the bank"

In the current interest-rate environment, many conservative investors' alternative is to park their money in bank deposits or government bonds.

| Choice | Annualized return | Risk |
|------|---------|------|
| 3-year bank time deposit | **~2.0%** | Virtually risk-free |
| 10-year government bond | **~1.7%** | Virtually risk-free |
| China Ping An (dividend only) | **~4.8%** | Share-price volatility + possible dividend cuts |
| China Ping An (dividend + valuation recovery) | **~10%-15%/year (over 3 years)** | Depends on rate trends and earnings delivery |

**If you hold for 3 years, worst case (pessimistic scenario):** the stock falls to RMB 47 (-19%), but cumulative dividends over three years of about RMB 8.5 bring the actual loss down to about **-4%**. Dividends provide meaningful downside protection.

**If you hold for 3 years, neutral scenario:** the stock rises to RMB 69 (+19%), plus about RMB 8.5 of cumulative dividends, for a total return of about **34%**, or roughly **10%** annualized.

**This isn't a "moonshot" story — it's a "high-certainty compound return" story, on the condition that you can tolerate short-term volatility and have your own view on where interest rates are headed.**

## Conclusion of this piece

| Question | Answer |
|------|------|
| Is Ping An expensive? | A P/EV of 0.65x sits at a historic low; unless you believe China is "turning Japanese," it's on the cheap side |
| How cheap is it? | Sum-of-the-parts valuation shows ~17% undervaluation; sell-side consensus target price is 35% higher |
| Is downside protection adequate? | A 4.8% dividend yield plus 14 consecutive years of dividend growth provides a meaningful safety cushion |
| What are the catalysts? | Interest rates stabilizing, continued NBV growth, confirmation of the bank's earnings inflection |
| What is the biggest risk? | Sustained rate declines shrinking EV, and negative-spread risk materializing |

**In one line: a 0.65x P/EV provides an ample margin of safety — but whether that margin of safety is "enough" depends on your view of interest rates.**
