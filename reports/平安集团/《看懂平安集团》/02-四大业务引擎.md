# The Four Business Engines

> *Understanding Ping An Group* series · Part 02
> Reading time: about 15 minutes

## Engine one: life insurance — from "human-wave tactics" to an "elite-force strategy"

An insurance company cuts its core sales force from 1.2 million people down to 350,000, then announces the "reform succeeded" — that sounds like self-inflicted harm.

But the data tells a different story: over the same period, NBV grew from a trough of RMB 21.1 billion to RMB 36.9 billion, and per-agent productivity kept hitting new highs. The logic of the life-insurance reform is counterintuitive — and it's the starting point for understanding Ping An's valuation.

### Agents: cut by 850,000, yet profit doubled

This has been the most drastic transformation in China's insurance industry over the past five years.

| Year | Ping An agent count | Y/Y change | NBV (RMB billion) | NBV growth |
|------|--------------|---------|------------|---------|
| 2019 | ~1.2 million | — | ~49.5 (estimate) | — |
| 2020 | ~1.02 million | -15% | ~43.2 (estimate) | -13% |
| 2021 | ~600,000 | -41% | ~37.9 (estimate) | -12% |
| 2022 | ~450,000 | -25% | ~28.9 (estimate) | -24% |
| 2023 | ~360,000 | -20% | 21.1 | -27% |
| 2024 | ~360,000 | flat | 28.5 | +35.6% |
| 2025 | **351,000** | -3.3% | **36.9** | **+29.3%** |

Sources: China Ping An historical annual reports, Securities Times, The Paper

**Agent headcount fell from 1.2 million to 350,000 — a 70% reduction. Yet 2025 NBV (RMB 36.9 billion) is nearly double the 2023 trough (RMB 21.1 billion).**

What does this mean?

Per-agent productivity has been rising rapidly. Per-agent NBV grew 17.2% year-on-year in 2025 — a textbook case of "volume down, quality up": underqualified agents are weeded out, and those who remain sell higher-value products to higher-net-worth clients.

**The counterargument:** agent headcount is still falling (-3.3% y/y in 2025), which suggests the "bleeding" hasn't fully stopped. Can rising per-agent productivity keep going? If another wave of attrition hits the remaining 350,000 agents, NBV growth could stall. And agent recruitment has not yet recovered, which raises doubts about whether life insurance's "blood-generating capacity" is still intact.

### Bancassurance: 2025's biggest surprise

If the agent channel is "slow and steady," the bancassurance channel was 2025's breakout.

- H1 2025 bancassurance NBV growth: **+169%**
- First-nine-months bancassurance NBV growth: **+170.9%**
- Bancassurance has become the number-one driver of NBV growth

**Why did the bancassurance channel suddenly take off?**

1. **Falling rates + maturing high-yield assets:** Bank wealth-management yields dropped below 3%, and as large volumes of deposits matured, customers needed a new "stable-return" product — participating (dividend-paying) insurance became the substitute
2. **Ping An Bank's channel advantage:** Ping An is the only financial group that simultaneously owns a large bank and a large insurer, giving it a natural edge in bank-insurance coordination
3. **Product-mix support:** Ping An made participating insurance its flagship product for 2025-2026, with premium volume up more than **40%** year-on-year

Sources: KAIYUAN Securities research report, Sina Finance, Guancha.cn

**The counterargument:** part of the bancassurance channel's rapid growth relies on the "deposit migration" effect triggered by falling rates. That effect is one-off — once deposit rates fall low enough, the migration effect will fade. Also, bancassurance NBV margins are typically lower than those of the agent channel, so the high growth rate may come at the expense of "value content."

### NBV margin: from "chasing volume" to "building value"

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| NBV margin (on standard premium basis) | 19.2% (estimate) | 22.7% | **28.5%** |

Sources: China Ping An 2025 annual report, The Paper

NBV margin rising from 19% to 28.5% means every RMB of premium collected is generating steadily more value. This wasn't achieved by selling more low-priced products — it came from a combination of an improved product mix (more high-value participating and protection-type products) and better expense control (expense ratios falling as the agent force shrank).

**But there's a warning sign:** Q4 2025 NBV alone fell **71.4%** year-on-year. The headline full-year figure of +29.3% masks a sharp deterioration in Q4. This could be a base-effect issue (a high Q4 2024 base), or it could be a sign that growth has peaked. NBV growth slowed further to +20.8% in Q1 2026, confirming the deceleration trend.

Source: Industrial Securities research report

## Engine two: P&C insurance — the quiet profit engine

P&C insurance gets less attention than life insurance, but it's becoming an increasingly important piece of Ping An's profit structure.

### Combined ratio: from underwriting losses to underwriting profit

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| P&C combined ratio (COR) | **100.7%** | 98.3% | **96.8%** |
| Auto insurance COR | — | 98.1% | **95.8%** |
| Insurance service revenue (RMB billion) | — | 320.2 | **338.9** (+5.8%) |

Sources: China Ping An 2025 annual report, Securities Times

A COR below 100% means "underwriting itself is profitable" — no need to lean on investment income for support. Falling from 100.7% to 96.8%, a 3.9-percentage-point improvement over three straight years, is a remarkable turnaround.

**Why has COR kept improving?**

1. **More granular auto-insurance pricing:** Ping An uses massive driving-behavior data (from Ping An P&C and the Ping An Auto Owner app) to price precisely — good drivers pay less, high-risk drivers pay more
2. **Optimizing non-auto lines:** cutting non-auto lines with high loss ratios and focusing on more profitable categories
3. **Technology-driven cost reduction:** AI claims handling and smart loss assessment reduce operating costs

**The counterargument:** a COR of 96.8% is already excellent, leaving limited room for further improvement. And in years with major catastrophes (typhoons, floods), claims can swing COR sharply — the 100.7% COR in 2023 was partly driven by natural disasters. P&C profit is less stable than life-insurance profit.

## Engine three: banking — from drag to inflection

Ping An Bank is Ping An Group's "controversial business."

### The past two years: sustained pressure

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Revenue (RMB billion) | 164.7 | 137.4 | 132.7 (estimate) |
| Net profit attributable to shareholders (RMB billion) | 46.5 | 44.5 | 42.7 (estimate) |
| Net interest margin | — | — | Under pressure |

Sources: Ping An Bank historical annual reports, Yiping Grade

Ping An Bank's revenue and profit fell for two straight years, 2024-2025, mainly due to:

1. **Continued net-interest-margin compression:** loan rates falling while deposit costs stay sticky, squeezing the bank's "spread" income
2. **Actively shrinking high-risk retail lending:** Ping An Bank was once known for aggressive retail lending (consumer loans, credit cards), and once asset-quality problems surfaced it deliberately pulled back
3. **Falling non-interest income:** hit by capital markets and distribution-business trends

### Q1 2026: inflection confirmed?

In Q1 2026, Ping An Bank delivered results that surprised the market:

- Revenue turned **positive** year-on-year
- Net profit up **+3%** year-on-year
- The first "double increase" in both revenue and net profit in three years

Sources: Ping An Bank Q1 2026 report, Yiping Grade, NetEase Finance

**Is this a genuine inflection?**

The optimistic view: net interest margin has bottomed, asset quality is improving, and retail business is regaining momentum — fundamentals really are turning for the better.
The cautious view: one quarter of data can't define a trend; Q2 and Q3 still need to confirm it.

**The counterargument:** Ping An Bank's strategic positioning within the group has never been entirely clear. Is it trying to be the "king of retail banking" or a "diversified financial connector"? Its frequent strategic shifts (from aggressive retail expansion, to deliberate contraction, to renewed push) suggest management still hasn't settled on a direction. With net interest margins declining across the entire banking sector, it's hard for Ping An Bank to stand apart.

## Engine four: technology / health & elderly care — the biggest "long-dated check"

Ping An's investment in technology and health & elderly care is enormous — but the payoff remains uncertain.

### Technology segment

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Large-model API calls | **8.18 billion** |
| AI use cases deployed | **650+** |
| Technology patents | Industry-leading (exact figures not disclosed in detail in the annual report) |
| Technology segment operating profit | Turned from loss to profit in 2025 |

Source: China Ping An 2025 annual report

Ping An's technology footprint includes:
- **Ping An Technology:** the AI foundation and large-model capability that empowers the group's business lines
- **OneConnect (Jinrong Yizhangtong):** technology services exported to external financial institutions
- **Ping An Good Doctor:** online medical care plus health management, with 23 million paying users

**The core question:** 8.18 billion large-model API calls and 650 use cases look impressive on paper — but how much do they actually contribute to group profit? When does technology investment translate into a measurable profit improvement?

Management's answer: technology's value shows up as "cost reduction and efficiency gains" rather than standalone profit. For example, AI claims processing lowers P&C's operating costs, and smart underwriting improves life insurance's underwriting precision — these benefits are "internalized" in the profit improvements of each business line.

That logic makes sense, but it can't be quantified — you'll never know how much of that 96.8% COR is attributable to technology.

### Health & elderly care segment

Ping An's "health and elderly care" strategy is a long-term story:

- **Ping An Good Doctor:** building a service network spanning online, in-store, in-home, and device-based ("four channels") care
- **Peking University Healthcare Group:** proprietary medical resources
- **Health and elderly-care communities:** premium senior-living communities bundled with high-end life-insurance products
- **External partnerships:** more than 10,000 healthcare institutions

The logic: as China's aging accelerates (the population aged 65+ now exceeds **15%** of the total), Ping An is using an "insurance + health & elderly care services" bundle to strengthen the stickiness and retention of its insurance products through service.

**The counterargument:** Ping An Good Doctor has run losses for a long time since its IPO, health and elderly-care communities require tens of billions of yuan in investment, and the profit model still isn't clear. If no profit inflection is visible within five years, the market won't price in this story.

## Tallying the four engines

Put the four engines side by side:

| Engine | Current status | 3-year outlook | Key variables |
|------|---------|--------------|---------|
| Life insurance | High NBV growth, but decelerating | Moderate growth (+10%-20%) | Agent productivity vs. headcount, durability of the bancassurance channel |
| P&C insurance | COR steadily improving | Holding at 96%-98% | Catastrophe-year volatility, auto-insurance competitive landscape |
| Banking | Inflection signal just emerging | Slow recovery | Net-interest-margin trajectory, retail loan quality |
| Technology/health care | Turned from loss to profit | Slowly contributing to profit | Validating the profit model, return on investment |

**The optimistic reading:** three of the four engines are improving (life, P&C, banking), and one is moving from loss to profit (technology). The overall direction is upward.

**The pessimistic reading:** life insurance's NBV growth is slowing (Q4 collapsed 71%), the banking inflection rests on just one quarter of data, and technology's contribution to profit is negligible. The core life-insurance engine faces structural pressure from falling rates, and the growth ceiling may arrive soon.

**Both readings are backed by data. That's Ping An's contradiction — it isn't a simple "good" or "bad" stock, but one that's "pointed in the right direction, at an uncertain speed."**
