## V. Industry Trends: Three Major Trends Reshaping the Competitive Landscape

### Trend 1: Smartification — from single-product intelligence to scenario intelligence
- **Haier's strategy**: the Sanyiniao whole-home smart ecosystem, emphasizing "scenarios" rather than "products"
- **Midea's strategy**: the Midea Home app as a platform, connecting various smart devices
- **Key difference**: Haier emphasizes offline experience stores, while Midea emphasizes its online platform

### Trend 2: Premiumization — the inevitable choice of consumption upgrading
- **Data support**: Casarte's 2025 revenue grew 15%, with an average price three times the industry average
- **Midea's response**: launched the COLMO brand, though its brand premium still falls short of Casarte
- **Core tension**: premiumization brings higher gross margins but sacrifices the pace of scale expansion

### Trend 3: Going global — from exports to local operations
- **Haier's advantage**: the most complete global footprint (acquisitions of GE Appliances, Candy, etc.)
- **Midea's strategy**: focused on emerging markets such as Southeast Asia and the Middle East
- **Risk warning**: overseas business margins are typically lower than domestic ones (Haier's overseas net margin is ~4% vs. ~8% domestically)

**Munger perspective commentary:**
> "Forecasting is not the key factor in investing; the key is knowing what you don't know."
Smartification carries the greatest uncertainty — technology standards are not yet unified and consumer acceptance is unclear. Haier has invested heavily in its scenario-based ecosystem, but whether it can ultimately form a defensible business model remains to be seen.

## VII. Value-Chain Analysis: The Harsh Reality of Uneven Value Distribution

### Value-chain distribution (using refrigerators as an example):
1. **Upstream raw materials** (steel sheet, compressors, chips, etc.): **gross margin 15-20%**
2. **Midstream manufacturing** (brand owners such as Haier/Midea): **gross margin 25-30%**
3. **Downstream channels** (online platforms, offline stores): **gross margin 20-25%**
4. **Installation services**: **gross margin 40-50%**

**Analysis of Haier's control over the value chain:**
- **Upstream**: gains bargaining power through procurement scale, but core components (compressors, chips) still depend on outside suppliers
- **Midstream**: manufacturing is highly digitized; the Qingdao Connected Factory is an industry benchmark
- **Downstream**: channel control is relatively weak, with high dependence on platforms such as JD.com and Tmall
- **Services**: Goodaymart (Rizhun) Logistics is a differentiating advantage, but the services business remains limited in scale

**Midea's comparative advantages:**
1. **Upstream extension**: high in-house supply ratio of Meizhi compressors (a joint venture with Gree)
2. **Downstream control**: Anda Logistics covers the whole country, with stronger channel-penetration capability
3. **Cost control**: across-the-board cost advantages from scale effects

**Key vulnerability:**
Haier's control over **upstream core components** is insufficient. Variable-frequency compressors and smart chips for high-end refrigerators still depend on Japanese/German suppliers. This is a strategic risk hidden beyond what appears in the financial statements.

## VIII. Haier vs. Midea Competitive Strategy Differences: A Clash of Two Philosophies

### Table 6: Comparative Analysis of Strategic Paths

| Dimension | Haier Smart Home (scenario-ecosystem driven) | Midea Group (efficiency driven) | Long-term win-rate assessment |
|------|----------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Core logic** | **User-experience centered**<br>building a smart-home ecosystem | **Cost-efficiency centered**<br>scaled production and sales | Each has its own strengths |
| **Product strategy** | **Premiumization + bundled suites**<br>Casarte + Sanyiniao scenario solutions | **Full coverage + value for money**<br>complete product line from low-end to high-end | Midea is more steady |
| **Channel strategy** | **Experience stores + scenario displays**<br>emphasis on offline experience conversion | **Omni-channel + deep distribution**<br>comprehensive online and offline coverage | Midea is more comprehensive |
| **Technology innovation** | **Application innovation + scenario innovation**<br>smart-home connectivity protocols | **Engineering innovation + process innovation**<br>manufacturing-efficiency gains | Haier is more forward-looking |
| **Globalization** | **Brand acquisitions + local operations**<br>multi-brand global matrix | **Own-brand + OEM exports**<br>focused on exporting manufacturing advantage | Haier goes deeper |
| **Financial performance** | **Higher margin but slower growth**<br>ROE 16.98% | **Scale advantage, faster growth**<br>ROE 19.70% | Midea performs better |

### Strategic risk assessment:

**Haier's risk points:**
1. **High execution difficulty for the ecosystem strategy**: requires coordinating a large number of partners, with high coordination costs
2. **Clear ceiling on premiumization**: Casarte's penetration in tier-1/tier-2 cities is already relatively high
3. **High cost of educating demand for scenarios**: it takes time for consumers to shift from buying products to buying scenarios

**Midea's risk points:**
1. **Weak brand-premium capability**: struggles to break through in the high-end market
2. **Questioned innovation capability**: often criticized as a "follower" rather than a "leader"
3. **Diversification dilutes resources**: an overly long list of business lines may spread management attention too thin

**Munger's ultimate question:**
> "In business, we often find that the winning systems are those that were forced to carry heavy burdens from the start, because those burdens later became insurmountable moats."

Haier's "heavy burdens" are: 1) enormous investment in premiumization; 2) the complexity of integrating global acquisitions; 3) the long build-out cycle of the scenario ecosystem. But if these succeed, they will all become **moats that are hard to cross**.

Midea's "heavy burdens" are: 1) organizational bloat risk from its sheer scale; 2) a fixed brand image resulting from its low-price strategy; 3) the management complexity of a diversified business.

**Assessment of Haier Smart Home's competitive position in the home appliance industry:**

**Strengths (moat sources):**
1. **Absolute leadership in refrigerators** (40%+ share) — the deepest moat
2. **High-end brand Casarte** (30-50% premium) — brand moat
3. **First-mover advantage in globalization** (multi-brand local operations) — geographic moat
4. **Scenario-ecosystem layout** (Sanyiniao smart home) — ecosystem moat (potential)

**Weaknesses (vulnerable points under attack):**
1. **Weak air-conditioner business** (share <20%) — losing in the largest single-product market
2. **Small-appliance gap** — insufficient cash-flow stability
3. **Below-average profitability** (net margin 6.67% vs. Midea's 9.75%)
4. **Persistent valuation discount** (P/E 10x vs. Midea's 13.6x)

**Munger-style investment judgment:**

**Positive factors (meeting Munger's standards):**
1. **Competing within its circle of competence**: Haier has a clear "structural advantage" in the refrigerator segment
2. **Brand moat**: Casarte's premium image requires a long time to build and is hard to replicate
3. **Global footprint**: acquisitions and integration are difficult, but once successful they form entry barriers

**Negative factors (that would make Munger wary):**
1. **Competing in a weak area**: the air-conditioner business is clearly at a disadvantage
2. **Overly complex strategy**: the scenario ecosystem involves too many variables, with high execution risk
3. **Insufficient profitability**: ROE persistently below Midea's, with capital efficiency needing improvement

**Final recommendation:**

From a **Munger-style competitive-analysis perspective**, Haier Smart Home's competitive position in the home appliance industry shows **clear structural divergence**:

- **In refrigerators and the high-end market**: it holds a **strong moat** and a solid competitive position
- **In air conditioners and small appliances**: it is at a **clear disadvantage**; a strategic retreat or seeking partnerships is advisable
- **In the new smart-home track**: it holds a **first-mover advantage**, but the business model has not yet been fully validated

**Investment implications:**
Investing in Haier is, in essence, a bet on the moat of its **refrigerator business** and on the **success of its premiumization strategy**. Investors need to closely track:
1. Whether refrigerator market share remains stable
2. Whether the Casarte brand premium can be sustained
3. Whether the scenario ecosystem can achieve commercial breakthrough

The home appliance industry has entered an **era of stock-market competition**; the future winner will not be the one with the largest scale, but the one with **the deepest moat in its chosen niche**. Haier has chosen a strategic path of "depth" over "breadth," which aligns with Munger's principle of "competing within one's circle of competence," but is extremely difficult to execute.

> "For a ship sailing blindly, any wind is a headwind." — Charlie Munger
Haier needs to clarify: should it continue reinforcing its core refrigerator advantage, or spread resources to catch up on its air-conditioner shortfall? Does its current "full-category + scenario-ecosystem" strategy risk spreading resources too thin?
