## For every 100 yuan of sales, Haier earns only 6.7 yuan, while Midea earns 9.8 yuan

Number one in global large-appliance retail volume for 17 consecutive years. A P/E of 10x, a dividend yield of 5.6%. On paper, it looks like a bargain.

But flip open the financial statements and another side surfaces: the net margin is only two-thirds of Midea's, the gross margin has dropped nearly 5 percentage points in two years, and in Q1 2026 both revenue and net profit declined year-over-year. It's cheap, but the cheapness has a reason. This series exists to take that "reason" apart.

Let's start with a set of 2025 figures to re-introduce Haier Smart Home:

| Metric | 2025 figure | Scale assessment |
|------|------------|---------|
| Global revenue | **302.3 billion yuan** (+5.7%) | Crossed 300 billion for the first time |
| Net profit attributable to parent | **19.55 billion yuan** (+4.4%) | Fifth consecutive year of growth, but growth is slowing |
| Share of overseas revenue | **51.1%** | Exceeds domestic revenue for the first time — the only A-share appliance company to do so |
| Global large-appliance retail volume ranking | **No. 1 for 17 consecutive years** | Source: Euromonitor International |
| Casarte 10,000-yuan-plus refrigerator share | **44%** | Near-monopoly at the high end |
| Casarte 10,000-yuan-plus washing machine share | **75%** | Near-monopoly at the high end |
| Global lighthouse factory count | **13** (most in the world) | Certified by the World Economic Forum |
| P/E (TTM) | **~10x** | 5-year historical low percentile |
| Dividend yield | **~5.6%** | Committed to a payout ratio of at least 60% for 2027-2028 |

But don't jump to conclusions yet. Set it next to Midea:

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Gap |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Revenue | 302.3 billion | **458.5 billion** | Midea is **51.7%** higher |
| Net profit | 19.55 billion | **43.95 billion** | Midea is **124.7%** higher |
| Net margin | **6.67%** | **9.75%** | Haier is 3.08 percentage points lower |

**Haier's revenue is 66% of Midea's, but its net profit is only 44% of Midea's.** For every 100 yuan of product sold, Haier earns only **6.7 yuan**, Midea earns **9.8 yuan**, and Gree earns **15 yuan**.

That's the core contradiction at the heart of Haier Smart Home: **global-leading scale, industry-lagging profit efficiency**. How you interpret this contradiction directly determines your view of the stock.

## What exactly is Haier Smart Home

The most accurate one-line definition of Haier Smart Home is:

> **Haier Smart Home = a global white-goods empire (seven brands + 35 industrial parks + 163 manufacturing centers) + a still-unproven smart-home ecosystem story (Three-Winged Bird) + a stock priced by the market at an "efficiency discount."**

Let's unpack these three pieces.

### Piece one: the global brand matrix

Haier is not just "Haier." It owns seven brands, each covering different markets and price tiers:

| Brand | Positioning | Market covered |
|------|------|---------|
| **Haier** | Mass-market flagship brand | China + global |
| **Casarte** | High-end | China |
| **Leader** | Young, value-for-money | China |
| **GE Appliances** | North America mainstay | North America |
| **Fisher & Paykel** | Ultra-premium | Australia/New Zealand |
| **AQUA** | Japan + Southeast Asia | Asia-Pacific |
| **Candy** | European market | Europe |

This "multi-brand, localized operation" globalization model is **unique** among A-share appliance companies. Midea relies mainly on its own-brand exports and OEM manufacturing abroad, and Gree's overseas revenue is only 18% of its total. Haier is the only Chinese appliance company **whose overseas revenue exceeds its domestic revenue**.

**Counterpoint**: A multi-brand matrix also means **higher management complexity and difficulty compressing the expense ratio**. Seven brands each maintain their own R&D teams, marketing spend, and channel systems — this is one of the structural reasons Haier's expense ratio is **19%** (far above Midea's **15.5%**).

### Piece two: Casarte — Haier's most valuable asset

If you could pick only one most valuable thing about Haier Smart Home, the answer isn't its "world No. 1" scale — it's **Casarte**.

A few key figures:
- Casarte revenue was **2.2 billion** in 2016 and roughly **38 billion** in 2025 (source: brokerage research estimate) — about a **17x** increase over 10 years
- Offline market share for refrigerators priced above 10,000 yuan: **44%** (source: 2025 annual report)
- Offline market share for washing machines priced above 10,000 yuan: **75%** (source: 2025 annual report)
- Market share for air conditioners priced above 15,000 yuan: **53%** (source: 2025 annual report)
- Brand value: **92.8 billion yuan** (source: Euromonitor International)

Casarte's position in China's high-end appliance market resembles Kweichow Moutai's position in the high-end liquor market — **the mindshare among consumers has already been established**. Walk into a store and look at refrigerators priced above 10,000 yuan, and there's a good chance the sales associate's first recommendation is Casarte.

**But here's a key fact**: Casarte accounts for only about **~13%** (estimate) of Haier Smart Home's total revenue. In other words, Haier's business with the most pricing power contributes only one-eighth of revenue. The remaining 87% comes from mass-market brands and overseas businesses with limited pricing power.

**Counterpoint**: Midea's high-end brand COLMO has grown faster than Casarte in recent years, and the gap is narrowing. The high-end market has a limited ceiling, Casarte's penetration in tier-1 and tier-2 cities is already high, and room for further growth is questionable.

### Piece three: Three-Winged Bird — a story whose value is still unproven

Since 2020, Haier has been pushing "Three-Winged Bird" (San Yi Niao) — a "scenario brand" that attempts to shift from selling individual appliances to selling whole-home smart-living solutions.

**Management's narrative**: from selling refrigerators to selling a "food IoT network," from selling washing machines to selling a "clothing IoT network" — customers are buying not a product but a "lifestyle."

**The reality in the financials**: Three-Winged Bird contributes **less than 5%** (estimate) of revenue, cumulative investment has exceeded **10 billion yuan**, and the profit model remains unproven. The repeat-purchase rate for smart-home scenario packages is only **~15%** (estimate).

**Bull case**: The direction is right. Smart-home penetration is currently only 15%, leaving huge room for future growth. Haier has a first-mover advantage in scenario-based ecosystems.

**Bear case**: Consumers are far more willing to pay for the function of a single product than for a "scenario." Huawei's and Xiaomi's smart ecosystems have stronger compatibility and lower switching costs. Haier's "scenario premium" may be a false proposition.

## Five-year financial trend: stable growth, worrying efficiency

| Year | Revenue (billions) | Net profit (billions) | Gross margin | Net margin | ROE |
|------|-----------|-------------|--------|--------|-----|
| 2021 | 227.1 | 13.08 | **31.23%** | 5.81% | 17.3% |
| 2022 | 243.6 | 14.71 | 31.33% | 6.05% | 16.8% |
| 2023 | 274.2 | 16.60 | **31.51%** | 6.40% | 16.9% |
| 2024 | 286.0 | 18.73 | 27.80% | 6.85% | 17.7% |
| 2025 | 302.3 | 19.55 | **26.66%** | 6.67% | 17.0% |
| Q1 2026 | 73.69 | 4.65 | **25.34%** | 6.52% | — |

Source: Haier Smart Home annual reports 2021-2025, Q1 2026 quarterly report

A few key signals:

1. **Revenue is growing steadily**: a 5-year CAGR of **7.4%** — neither fast nor slow, a normal level for the white-goods industry
2. **Net profit is growing faster**: a 5-year CAGR of **10.6%**, indicating margins are slowly improving
3. **The persistent decline in gross margin is the biggest red flag**: down from **31.51%** to **26.66%**, a drop of **4.85 percentage points** in two years. This means Haier's products are losing pricing power, or cost pressures cannot be passed downstream
4. **Q1 2026 saw both revenue and profit decline**: revenue **-6.87%**, net profit **-15.22%** — the first year-over-year quarterly revenue decline in 6 years. Mainly driven by the impact of North American tariffs, but it also exposes insufficient business resilience

**Counterpoint**: Q1 2026 profit rose **113%** quarter-over-quarter versus Q4 2025, suggesting Q4 was the true bottom and Q1 is already recovering. You shouldn't look only at year-over-year comparisons — you need to look at the trend.

Here's a set of valuation figures first; Part 04 will expand on this in detail:

| P/E (TTM) | **~10x** | ~13.5x | ~7-8x |

| Dividend yield | **~5.6%** | ~5.1% | — |
| Free cash flow yield | **~8%** | — | — |
| 5-year P/E percentile | **2.58%** (extremely low) | Low-to-mid | Historic low |

Source: public market data, April-May 2026

Put it all together, and here's the picture of Haier Smart Home you see:

- The world's No. 1 in white-goods scale, with the most complete brand matrix
- Casarte holds a near-monopoly in the high-end market
- A P/E of only 10x, at a 5-year low percentile
- A dividend yield above 5%, with a three-year commitment to raise it
- **But** net margin is only two-thirds of Midea's
- **But** gross margin has dropped nearly 5 percentage points in two years
- **But** Q1 2026 saw both revenue and net profit decline
- **But** the expense ratio is 19%, far above Midea's 15.5%

**It's cheap, but the cheapness has a reason.** Whether that reason is a "temporary headwind" or a "structural flaw" is exactly what the next 3 parts of this series will answer.

## A one-line comparison with Midea and Gree

Before you read the next three parts, remember the core differences between the three companies:

| Company | One-line portrait | Investment essence |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Midea Group** | An efficiency-driven, full-category appliance giant | What you're buying is management efficiency and execution |
| **Gree Electric** | A cash cow with a single dominant category — air conditioners | What you're buying is single-category monopoly and dividends |
| **Haier Smart Home** | The most globally deepened brand-matrix company | What you're buying is global footprint and premiumization potential |

Each of the three companies has its own strengths, but the market prices them very differently: Midea trades at roughly **13.5x** P/E, Haier at roughly **10x**, and Gree at roughly **7-8x**. The logic behind this pricing gap is the single most worthwhile thing to dig into in this entire series.
