## A 27.8 billion yuan goodwill overhang, and gross margin down nearly 5 points in two years

A company carries 27.8 billion yuan of goodwill on its balance sheet, its gross margin has fallen for two straight years, both revenue and net profit declined year-over-year in Q1 2026 — and yet its P/E ratio is only 10x.

Is this cheapness real, or a trap? This final installment answers directly.

---

## I. Three risks that must be confronted head-on

Before discussing valuation, let's lay out the biggest risks clearly. Because Haier's "cheapness" is not without a cost — the market's 10x P/E has its reasons.

### Risk one: the persistent decline in gross margin — the most alarming signal

| Year | Gross margin | Change |
|------|--------|------|
| 2021 | 31.23% | — |
| 2022 | 31.33% | +0.10pct |
| 2023 | **31.51%** | +0.18pct |
| 2024 | 27.80% | **-3.71pct** |
| 2025 | 26.66% | **-1.14pct** |
| 2026Q1 | **25.34%** | **-1.32pct** |

Source: Haier Smart Home 2021-2025 annual reports, 2026Q1 quarterly report

**Down 4.85 percentage points in two years.** This is the single most important number in the entire report.

What does a declining gross margin mean? It indicates that Haier's products are losing pricing power — either competition is intensifying and forcing price cuts, or rising raw-material costs cannot be passed on to consumers, or the product mix is deteriorating (with a rising share of lower-margin products).

Breaking down the specific causes:
1. **A "four-way squeeze" on raw materials**: in 2025, copper prices rose **+40%**, R32 refrigerant rose **+80%**, plastics rose **+40-50%**, and aluminum rose **+20%** (source: commodity-price data)
2. **A rising share of overseas revenue**: overseas gross margin is only about **12%**, far below the domestic **42%**; the overseas share rose from 49% to 51%, automatically dragging down the overall gross margin
3. **Intensifying competition**: Midea, Xiaomi and others continue to pressure the low-to-mid-end market
4. **Gree can raise prices, Haier can't**: Gree's average air-conditioner price rose **+2.15%** in 2025 against the market trend, while Haier had no choice but to follow the market down — this shows Gree has pricing power and Haier does not

**The bull case**: raw-material prices are cyclical, and copper won't keep rising forever. Cost-side pressure is expected to ease in H2 2026. A declining gross margin does not equal declining competitiveness — Haier's revenue is still growing.

**The bear case**: companies with a genuine "franchise" can pass rising costs on to consumers (like Moutai or Apple). Haier cannot do this — which shows its pricing power is limited. If gross margin cannot stabilize, any improvement in net margin is wishful thinking.

### Risk two: revenue and net profit both declined in Q1 2026 — a cyclical trough or a structural inflection?

The Q1 2026 numbers have put the market on edge:

| Metric | 2026Q1 | Year-over-year | Quarter-over-quarter vs. Q4 |
|------|--------|------|---------|
| Revenue | 73.69 billion | **-6.87%** | — |
| Net profit | 4.65 billion | **-15.22%** | **+113%** |

Source: Haier Smart Home 2026Q1 quarterly report

This marks Haier's **first year-over-year quarterly revenue decline in 6 years**. The main cause is tariff pressure hitting the North American market — Americas revenue was **79.9 billion** (**26.4%** of total revenue), so once North America runs into trouble, the impact shows up directly on the income statement.

**The key question**: is this a "cyclical trough," or "the start of a structural deterioration"?

Evidence supporting a "cyclical trough":
- Profit grew **113%** quarter-over-quarter versus Q4 — Q4 was the real bottom
- Excluding the North America impact, Haier's overall operating profit grew a combined **10%+**
- GE Appliances' price index keeps rising, and it still holds the #1 share position in North America
- Domestic trade-in subsidy policy continues to be rolled out

Evidence supporting a "structural inflection":
- This is the first negative year-over-year quarterly revenue print in 6 years — an unwelcome precedent
- North American tariff risk is not short-term, but long-term
- Gross margin continues to slide, down to **25.34%** — with no sign of stabilizing

**The current data are insufficient to draw a conclusion**. We need to wait for **2026Q2** data to confirm — if Q2 revenue turns positive and gross margin stabilizes, that would indicate Q1 was the cyclical trough; if Q2 continues to deteriorate, the situation needs to be reassessed.

### Risk three: a 27.8 billion yuan goodwill overhang

| Metric | Data | Note |
|------|------|------|
| Total goodwill | **27.8 billion yuan** | **9.2%** of total assets, **22%** of net assets |
| Main source | GE Appliances (2016 acquisition) | Acquisition price of roughly **$5.4 billion** |
| Impairment trigger condition | Continued deterioration of the North America business | Stalling growth at GE Appliances would be the warning sign |

Source: Haier Smart Home 2025 annual report

**What does 27.8 billion yuan of goodwill amount to?** It's equivalent to about a year and a half of Haier's net profit. If an impairment were ever triggered, the hit to earnings would be devastating.

**The bull case**: GE Appliances currently remains the #1 player in North American major appliances by share (**24.5%**), operations are normal, and impairment risk is manageable. 80% of its products are manufactured domestically in the US, so tariff exposure is limited.

**The bear case**: GE Appliances' 2025 Americas revenue grew only **0.4%** — growth has essentially stalled. If the US economy enters a recession or tariffs escalate further, deterioration in the North America business and a goodwill impairment could hit at the same time. At 22% of net assets, that 27.8 billion is a ticking time bomb.

## II. Other risks worth watching

### R&D spending declined for the first time

Haier's 2025 R&D spending was **10.7 billion yuan**, down **5.3%** year-over-year — the first decline since 2013. The drop in R&D spending may reflect a short-term choice by management under profit pressure (cutting R&D to protect profit), but over the long run it will erode competitiveness.

**The bear case**: Midea's 2025 R&D investment is larger, and is focused on efficiency gains and industrial-internet initiatives. While Haier cuts R&D, its competitor is doubling down — the gap could widen further.

### A complex shareholding structure

The Haier Group holds roughly **42%** of Haier Smart Home's shares through a multi-layered holding structure, while management holds only **1.2%** (versus **3.5%** for Midea's management). This means:
1. The alignment between management's interests and shareholders' interests is weaker than at Midea
2. The Group may prioritize its own overall interests over those of the listed company's shareholders
3. Related-party transactions total **15 billion yuan** (5% of revenue)

### Management's narrative is spread too thin

Management has talked up too many storylines: "ecosystem Haier + AI-native organization + big health + robotics + new energy + embodied intelligence" — but the ratio of R&D spending to sales expense is only **0.3**, meaning the business is still, in essence, "channel- and marketing-driven" rather than "technology-driven."

## III. Positive factors: why some are bullish

### Positive factor one: valuation sits at a historic low percentile

| Valuation metric | Current value | 5-year percentile | Note |
|----------|--------|---------|------|
| P/E (TTM) | **~10x** | **2.58%** | Only 2.58% of the past 5 years have been cheaper than now |
| P/B | **1.63x** | On the low side | Historical median is roughly 2.0-2.5x |
| Dividend yield | **~5.6%** | Very high | With a commitment to raise the payout ratio to 60% |
| Free-cash-flow yield | **~8%** | — | China's 10-year Treasury yield is only **1.7%**, a spread of **625bp** |

Source: publicly available market data, April-May 2026

**Buying the world's #1 white-goods maker at 10x earnings** — from a historical-percentile standpoint, the current price has already priced in most of the pessimism. Even in a pessimistic scenario (zero growth and a P/E holding at 10x), a three-year holding period would not lose money (dividends alone would deliver roughly 5.6% a year).

### Positive factor two: extremely solid cash flow

| Metric | 2025 |
|------|--------|
| Operating cash flow | **26 billion yuan** |
| Operating cash flow / net profit | **1.33x** |
| 5-year average OCF/NP | **~1.46x** |
| Net cash position | Cash and equivalents of **47.6 billion** > interest-bearing debt of **32.1 billion** |

Source: Haier Smart Home 2025 annual report

The profit Haier earns is real cash — operating cash flow has exceeded **130%** of net profit for five straight years, a top-tier result among A-share manufacturers. The net-cash position means there is no financial risk.

### Positive factor three: shareholder returns are improving

- 2025 payout ratio of **55%**, with a dividend of **0.89 yuan** per share
- Commitment: a payout ratio of no less than **58%** in 2026, and no less than **60%** in 2027-2028
- A-share buyback: a plan of **3.0-6.0 billion yuan**, with an average buyback price of **25.25 yuan** (**23%** above the current share price)
- D-share buyback and cancellation: about **55 million shares** repurchased and cancelled to date

Management is putting real money behind buybacks at a level 23% above the current share price — a signal of aligned interests.

### Positive factor four: strong growth in emerging markets

- Middle East and Africa: **+56%**
- South Asia: **+23%**
- Southeast Asia: **+20%**

Source: Haier Smart Home 2025 annual report

The rise of the global middle class is a long-term trend. Haier established an early presence in emerging markets, and if those markets keep growing rapidly, they could partly offset the slowdown at home and in North America.

### Positive factor five: the long-term value of Casarte

Casarte grew from **2.2 billion yuan** to roughly **38 billion yuan** over 10 years, holding **44%** share of refrigerators priced above 10,000 yuan and **75%** share of washing machines in that price tier. The trend toward premiumization is irreversible — China's long-term consumption-upgrade trend provides fertile ground for Casarte's continued growth.

## IV. Valuation judgment: three scenarios

We will not give a specific "target price" — because any precise target price is false precision. Instead, here are three scenarios, so you can form your own judgment.

### Scenario one: optimistic (improving net margin + valuation re-rating)

- **Assumptions**: gross margin stabilizes in H2 2026, expense ratios gradually decline, net profit grows **8-10%** annually
- **EPS in 3 years**: about **2.67 yuan** (at 8% growth)
- **Target P/E**: **14x** (back to the historical median)
- **Target price in 3 years**: **~37 yuan**
- **Implied upside**: **+82%** (from a starting price of 20.52 yuan)
- **Annualized return**: **~22%** (including dividends)

**Trigger conditions**: 2026Q2 gross margin recovers to 26%+, North American tariff situation becomes clear, net margin breaks above 7%

### Scenario two: neutral (moderate growth + modest valuation re-rating)

- **Assumptions**: gross margin stabilizes slowly, net profit grows **5-6%** annually
- **EPS in 3 years**: about **2.45 yuan** (at 5% growth)
- **Target P/E**: **12x**
- **Target price in 3 years**: **~29 yuan**
- **Implied upside**: **+44%**
- **Annualized return**: **~13%** (including dividends)

**Trigger conditions**: domestic trade-in subsidies continue, emerging markets sustain high growth, expense ratios improve modestly

### Scenario three: pessimistic (low growth + valuation holds steady)

- **Assumptions**: gross margin keeps declining, North American tariffs escalate, net profit grows **0-2%**
- **EPS in 3 years**: about **2.12 yuan** (zero growth)
- **Target P/E**: **10x** (holding at the current level)
- **Target price in 3 years**: **~21 yuan**
- **Implied upside**: **+3%**
- **Annualized return**: **~7%** (almost entirely from dividends)

**Trigger conditions**: gross margin below 25% for two consecutive quarters, negative domestic revenue growth for two consecutive quarters

### Summary of the three scenarios

| Scenario | Annual growth | Target P/E | 3-year target price | Annualized return (incl. dividends) |
|------|--------|---------|-----------|------------------|
| Optimistic | 8-10% | 14x | **~37 yuan** | **~22%** |
| Neutral | 5-6% | 12x | **~29 yuan** | **~13%** |
| Pessimistic | 0-2% | 10x | **~21 yuan** | **~7%** |

**Key conclusion**: even in the pessimistic scenario, Haier's downside is limited (dividends alone provide roughly 5-6% a year). But the upside case requires fundamentals to actually improve — if gross margin cannot stabilize, the optimistic scenario will not materialize.

## V. Valuation comparison with Midea and Gree

| Metric | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Gree Electric |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| P/E | **~10x** | ~13.5x | ~7-8x |
| Net margin | 6.67% | 9.75% | ~15% |
| ROE | 16.98% | 19.70% | ~21% |
| Growth rate | 4.4% | 14.0% | -10.9% |
| Dividend yield | ~5.6% | ~5.1% | higher |

**The market's pricing logic**:
- **Midea** (13.5x): highest efficiency, fastest growth, fullest product range — deserves a premium
- **Haier** (10x): strong globalization but low profit efficiency — an efficiency discount
- **Gree** (7-8x): highest profit margin but growth has peaked — a growth discount

The differences in P/E across these three companies are not the market making a mistake — the market is pricing in different risks and growth expectations.

**If you had to pick just one** — this is the practical question many investors actually face:

| Preference | Best fit | Rationale |
|------|--------|------|
| Efficiency and comprehensiveness | **Midea** | Best efficiency, fastest growth, fullest product range |
| High dividends and certainty | **Gree** | Highest profit margin, most generous dividends, lowest valuation |
| Globalization and premiumization potential | **Haier** | Deepest global footprint, strongest premium brand in Casarte |

There is no "absolute best" choice — it depends on what you value most.

## VII. Final-chapter summary

### What kind of investment is Haier Smart Home?

Pulling together the analysis from across this series, the essence of a Haier Smart Home investment can be summarized as:

> **Buying an above-average-quality global white-goods leader at a cigar-butt price — with downside protected by dividends (~5.6%) and upside contingent on an improvement in net margin. This is a "defensive allocation," not an "aggressive, concentrated bet."**

### The core contradiction

The core contradiction Haier currently faces is: **the most complete global brand matrix in white goods and the deepest scale-based moat in the industry, versus the lowest net margin in the industry and a gross margin that keeps sliding.**

The scale is real, the brand is real, the globalization is real — but profit efficiency lags peers by far, and the gap is widening.

### Who is this investment suited for?

| Investor type | Recommendation | Rationale |
|------|------|------|
| Seeking high dividends + low volatility | Can allocate | 5.6% dividend yield + a 10x P/E means limited downside |
| Seeking long-term compounding growth | Wait and watch | Without an improvement in net margin, compounding growth is hard to achieve |
| Seeking a short-term catalyst | Watch the Q2 data | A stabilizing gross margin would be the biggest catalyst |
| Indifferent about the white-goods sector | Favor Midea instead | Higher efficiency, faster growth, fuller product range |

### One final word

Haier Smart Home is China's most successful globalized appliance company. From the Qingdao Refrigerator General Factory in 1984 to today's revenue surpassing 300 billion yuan, overseas revenue exceeding domestic revenue, and 17 consecutive years as the world's #1 major-appliance brand — this is a business history worth respecting.

But investing is about the future, not the past. **A price around 20 yuan provides an adequate safety cushion, but "safe" does not mean "richly rewarding."** The ultimate return on this investment comes down to one thing: whether Haier can convert its position as the world's #1 by scale into profit efficiency that matches that scale.

The data of the past five years do not fully support that it can. But the price has already fallen cheap enough that even if the answer turns out to be "it cannot," the losses would not be large.

**That is Haier Smart Home — an investment that is "cheap, but cheap for a reason." You'll need to judge for yourself whether that reason is temporary or permanent.**
