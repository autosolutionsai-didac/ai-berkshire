# Li Lu — Compilation of Speeches and Interviews

> Li Lu, born 1966 in Tangshan, is the founder and chairman of Himalaya Capital. He holds three degrees from Columbia University: a bachelor's in economics, an MBA, and a JD. He manages the Munger family's assets. Assets under management are approximately $15 billion. He is a member of the Columbia University Board of Trustees and a trustee of Caltech.

**Editor's note**: This piece organizes, in chronological order, the core content and original quotes from Li Lu's public speeches, interviews, and writings, staying as close to the original text as possible. Sources are noted at the end of each section.

## I. Preface to the Chinese Edition of *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (2011)

> Source: [Himalaya Capital official website](https://www.himcap.com/cn/publications)

### First encounter with Munger

"More than twenty years ago, as a young Chinese student who had come to the United States alone, I never imagined that I would end up in the investment business."

"Exactly twenty years ago (2003), Charlie and I had a conversation lasting several hours in Charlie's library in Santa Barbara. That conversation opened the investment partnership between us that has lasted the twenty years since."

### On Munger

"Munger's success rests entirely on investing, and the success of his investing rests entirely on self-cultivation and learning. This has nothing to do with trading in power or money — he achieved his success in the cleanest possible way."

"Charlie achieved this level of success purely through wisdom. Having gotten to know him up close, I feel his success was simply inevitable."

## II. On Modernization (2014/2018)

> Source: An article published on the Himalaya Capital official website

### The three-stage theory of civilization

Li Lu divides the evolution of human civilization into three stages:

- **Civilization 1.0 — hunter-gatherer civilization**: human survival depends on nature; the economy is a zero-sum game
- **Civilization 2.0 — agricultural civilization**: land is the core means of production; economic growth is extremely slow
- **Civilization 3.0 — technological civilization**: centered on the free-market economy plus modern technology, producing compounding economic growth

"The core feature of Civilization 3.0 is this: the free-market economy combined with modern technology produces sustained, compounding growth. This has never happened before in human history."

### China's positioning

"China is currently in 'Stage 2.5' — a consolidation period following the takeoff of industrialization."

The core contradiction: a vast gap between "the reality of compounding economic growth and the slow pace of change in society's psychological and political modes of governance."

### On U.S.-China relations

"Standing at the level of all humanity, we should think about the shared future of the human race. U.S.-China relations are not a zero-sum game, but rather a win-win collaboration within the framework of Civilization 3.0."

> Source: Himalaya Capital official website; *Civilization, Modernization, Value Investing, and China* (CITIC Press, April 2020)

## III. Peking University Guanghua Speech: The Prospects for Value Investing in China (Oct. 2015)

> October 23, 2015, Room 202, Building 1, Guanghua School of Management, Peking University

### The four basic concepts of value investing

"There are only four concepts in value investing. The first three all come from Benjamin Graham, Buffett's teacher; the fourth is Buffett's own unique contribution."

**First, a stock is partial ownership of a company, not merely a piece of paper to be bought and sold.**

"Buying a stock means buying the company — what you're buying is a portion of ownership in that company. This idea looks simple, but very few people actually invest according to this principle."

**Second, understand the concept of Mr. Market.**

"The market exists to serve you, not to guide you. Mr. Market gives you a quote every day; you have the right to take advantage of it, and you also have the right to ignore it."

"In the short run, the market is a voting machine; in the long run, it is a weighing machine."

**Third, the essence of investing is forecasting the future, and forecasts can never be 100% correct — hence the need for a margin of safety.**

"A margin of safety means the price you pay is far lower than the value you receive. The less confidence you have in your own judgment, the larger a margin of safety you need."

**Fourth, the circle of competence.**

"The most important thing about the circle of competence isn't how big it is, but knowing exactly where its boundary lies."

"What an investor really needs is an understanding of the companies they invest in. You don't need to understand many companies — you only need to truly understand a few."

### Does value investing work in China?

"Some people believe value investing only applies to the U.S. market and not to the Chinese market. This view is completely wrong."

"Value investing applies universally, because its underlying logic — buying undervalued, high-quality assets — holds in any free market."

"Over the past three or four decades, China's economy has created wealth on a scale unprecedented in human history. Such a dynamic economy is bound to produce a large number of excellent companies. For value investors, this is a tremendous opportunity."

### Advice for young people

"If you truly understand the logic of value investing, you'll find it's not just an investment method — it's a philosophy of life."

"Start early, find an area you can truly understand, build your circle of competence, and then wait patiently for opportunities."

"Everyone craves a five-fold return in one year, while overlooking the ten-thousand-fold returns that come from compounding."

> Source: [Peking University Guanghua School of Management official website](https://www.gsm.pku.edu.cn/undergraduate/info/1053/5832.htm); [Speech PDF](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5ef3c7300432b40ed865991a/5f0936c4321954706fa9eca2_%E4%BB%B7%E5%80%BC%E6%8A%95%E8%B5%84%E5%9C%A8%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E7%9A%84%E5%B1%95%E6%9C%9B-%E6%9D%8E%E5%BD%952015-10-23%E5%8C%97%E5%A4%A7%E6%BC%94%E8%AE%B2.pdf)

## IV. Peking University Guanghua Speech: The Practice of Value Investing (2019)

> Source: Peking University Guanghua School of Management's "Value Investing" course

### Unity of knowledge and action

"The logic of value investing is extremely simple — simple enough that almost anyone can understand it. But the number of people who can truly practice what they understand may be less than 5%."

"Why can't most people do it? Because of human nature. Greed, fear, herd behavior, and short-termism in human nature all work against the principles of value investing."

### A deeper elaboration on the circle of competence

"Building a circle of competence takes a great deal of time and effort. You need to study a company the way an industry expert studies an entire industry."

"You don't need many good ideas. If you can find ten good ideas in a lifetime, that's enough to make you very wealthy."

### On selling

"The best investment is one you never need to sell. If you've bought into a truly excellent company, the best strategy is to do nothing at all."

### Fund fee structure

Himalaya Capital practices the "Buffett formula" — no management fee, the first 6% of returns are fee-free, and only 25% performance fee is charged on returns above that threshold.

"Our interests are completely aligned with our investors'. We only make money when our investors make money."

> Source: [Full text on Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/96977331); Peking University Guanghua School of Management

## V. In Memory of My Mentor Charlie Munger (Nov. 2023)

> November 30, 2023, the day Charlie Munger passed away

### The final farewell

"I was on a business trip in Asia on Tuesday when I got a call from Charlie's family telling me Charlie was near death. I flew back to the U.S. immediately."

"On the plane, I made one last call to Charlie. A nurse held the phone to his ear, and I said my final few words. I don't know whether he heard them."

### Twenty years as partners

"Exactly twenty years ago, on Thanksgiving Day, Charlie and I had a four- or five-hour conversation in Charlie's library in Santa Barbara. From that day on, we became investment partners."

"For the twenty years that followed, we had dinner together every Tuesday. Nothing was off limits — investing, history, science, life. In more than twenty years of actual time spent together, Charlie never once let me down."

### A modern scholar-official

"Charlie was the embodiment of a modern scholar-official — cultivating himself through honesty, virtue, and diligence, and building wealth through wisdom and true knowledge in a mutually beneficial way."

"Charlie gave me a real, living role model in life. Once you have a real person as your role model, you have to accept the complexity of that person. But Charlie never let me down on that front either."

### Good Enough

"One of Charlie's catchphrases was 'good enough.' He didn't pursue perfection — he pursued 'good enough.' That was true both in investing and in how he lived his life."

"Charlie always firmly believed that the U.S. and China should cooperate permanently. That is one of the precious legacies he left the world."

> Source: [Himalaya Capital official website](https://www.himcap.com/cn/publications); [Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20231130A05CNZ00)

## VI. Exclusive Interview: On Munger, Musk, and AI (Nov. 2024)

> November 28, 2024, on the one-year anniversary of Munger's death. Source: Tencent News · Smart Investor

### The four levels of Munger's rationality

"Charlie's 'rationality' was not simply logical thinking — it contained four levels:
1. **Universal wisdom** — distilled from historical cases
2. **Lattice-work thinking** — integrating knowledge across disciplines to form multiple mental models
3. **The psychology of human misjudgment** — identifying and avoiding systematic cognitive errors
4. **Grounding in common sense** — repeating approaches that have been proven to work"

### The collision between Musk and Munger

"Musk and Charlie were extremely similar in their rational thinking. Charlie's 'universal wisdom thinking' and Musk's 'first-principles thinking' are essentially the same thing — both are a form of scientific thinking, which is to say, a form of physics-style thinking."

"At one long lunch, Charlie, Musk, another person, and I discussed batteries and various other scientific topics. The two of them were on the same wavelength about a great many things."

"But their business judgment differed on how they viewed risk — **Musk will act as soon as he sees a 5% chance of success, while Charlie might need an 80%-plus probability of success before acting**."

### Three judgments on AI

**1. A generation-defining technology:**
"The impact of AI is all-encompassing, but the specifics are still very hard to pin down at this point."

**2. Existential risk:**
"AI brings hope, but it may also bring existential risk. A species with intelligence beyond human intelligence might view humans as tools, the way humans treat other species. When a more intelligent species can evolve on its own without needing human control, humanity faces a real threat."

**3. The need for global governance:**
"A governance framework shared by all of humanity must be established. Government and the private sector need to cooperate across the board."

### Sticking with value investing

"In the short term some people have surpassed Buffett and Munger, but over the long term, no one has yet matched or surpassed them. This shows that value investing is genuinely hard."

"Value investors will always be a minority. More and more people like to use the term, but fewer and fewer actually practice it."

> Source: [Tencent News exclusive interview](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20241214A056UO00); [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2024-12-14/doc-inczmiev4860265.shtml)

## VII. Peking University Guanghua Speech: Global Value Investing and Our Era (Dec. 2024)

> December 7, 2024, the 10th-anniversary salon of Peking University Guanghua School of Management's "Value Investing" course. A 30,000-character keynote speech. Source: Tencent News, Sina Finance

### The confusions of our era

**On the domestic front:**
- "Youth unemployment has reached 20%, reflecting a crisis of confidence among private enterprises"
- "Real estate accounts for 60% of household wealth, and a sharp shrinkage in asset values has hit consumption"
- "A 'neijuan' (involution) culture under deflationary conditions, with economic policy mismatched between the supply side and the demand side"

**On the international front:**
- "The U.S. has fundamentally questioned its own role in international society, shaking the postwar international order"
- "The global trade system faces uncertainty"

### China's problem within the framework of civilization

"China is in 'Stage 2.5' — a consolidation period following the takeoff of industrialization. Every country that has gone through industrialization faces the same predicament."

**Three outdated-mindset traps:**
1. **The land mindset**: "In the 3.0 era, it is market scale and the flow of factors of production — not land and population — that determine economic growth"
2. **The virtual-versus-real economy dichotomy**: "'Shifting from the virtual economy back to the real economy' is a false concept; software, gaming, and the like are already inseparable from the real economy"
3. **The government's role**: "It should shift from a command-and-control model to a republican, deliberative, service-oriented model"

### The middle-income trap and how to escape it

"A 3.0 economy requires the full exchange and circulation of all factors of production throughout the economy."

**Current bottlenecks:**
- "Personal consumption accounts for only 40% of GDP, while the savings rate is close to 50%"
- "The state-owned banking system cannot allocate capital effectively and lacks a credit system"

**Hong Kong's strategic value:**
"Hong Kong possesses all the elements of a modern capital market — a complete system of institutions, law, and credit mechanisms. Similar to the relationship between the Netherlands and England back in the day, China can use Hong Kong to build a truly modern financial system."

**A practical direction:**
"Raising personal consumption from 40% to India's level of 60% would put economic growth on a sustainable path."

### Redefining the concept of wealth

"Wealth is the share of purchasing power you hold within the entire economy, not an absolute number."

"In a continuously growing 3.0 economy, static wealth (cash, real estate) will relatively shrink. True wealth comes from holding equity in companies that have the capacity to grow."

### Six basic principles of value investing

1. **A stock is proof of ownership**, not merely a trading chip
2. **Mr. Market provides a service rather than guidance**; in the short term, price often deviates from value
3. **A sufficient margin of safety** is a precondition for long-term holding
4. **Clearly define the boundary of your circle of competence**; invest only in companies you truly understand
5. **Fish where the fish are**; you don't need to master every macro parameter
6. **The goal is to hold the best companies in the most dynamic economies**, maintaining and growing purchasing power

### Lessons from history

"Graham, Keynes, and Templeton all discovered value investing during the Great Depression and wartime. Those times were far harsher than today's environment. Macro hardship is precisely when value investing shows its advantages."

### Core conclusion

"The macro environment is something we must accept; the micro environment is where we can make a difference."

"Investors don't need to accurately forecast the macro outlook — instead they should find mispriced, excellent companies amid the turmoil, and sustain their share of purchasing power in the global economy through long-term holding."

"True value investors are not parasites — they are key to the price-discovery function of capital markets, allowing savings to circulate effectively into social productivity."

"For an economy as vast as China's, many of the moments that truly trigger enormous change tend to happen by accident."

> Source: [Tencent News transcript of the speech](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20241226A03XAF00); [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-01-02/doc-inecnynp0487245.shtml); [Full video on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1XN6GYbE7E/)
