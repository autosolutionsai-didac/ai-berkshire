## 2. Verification of Munger's Original "19x" Quote

### 2.1 Munger's Verified Public Remarks About Li Lu

**Daily Journal 2019 Annual Meeting** (2019-02-14, Los Angeles):
> "I have given money to only one outside manager in my entire life, and that's Li Lu. I've got one investment, one outside manager that I trust to run money for me. And that's Li Lu. **He's hit it out of the park**."

**Daily Journal 2020 Annual Meeting** (2020-02-12), introducing Li Lu at the meeting:
> "Li Lu is here. I just saw his face in the audience. **He's the most successful investor in this whole damn room**."

**Another quote traced by the media after Munger's passing**:
> "We made unholy good returns for a long, long time... **That $88 million has become four or five times that**."
> (corresponding to Munger's 2004 investment of $88M in Himalaya's new fund growing to approximately $400M)

### 2.2 Tracing the Origin of "19x in 16 Years": Conclusion

- **Exact source: unverifiable (N/A)**
- Exhaustive review of the public transcripts from the Daily Journal 2018/2019/2020 annual meetings plus English-language feature collections from Acquirer's Multiple, Latticework Investing, Worldly Partners, and Kingswell → **no match found**
- **Most likely origin**: A "derived figure" calculated within Chinese-language circles from Munger's "$88M became $400M+" (roughly 4.5-5x, over approximately 15 years from Q4 2004 to 2019) combined with Himalaya's 26% annualized return since 1998, or a secondhand retelling of the "2003 (met Li Lu) to 2019 (16 years)" time window
- **Conclusion**: "19x in 16 years" **cannot be cited as Munger's original statement**. If it must be cited, it should be labeled "as retold by Chinese-language media; primary source unverified"

### 2.3 Mathematical Derivation If "19x in 16 Years" Were True (Scenario Comparison Only)

- 19^(1/16) − 1 ≈ **20.18% annualized** (geometric mean)
- If this range were valid, it would correspond to the **2004-2019 new-fund period** (Munger's capital), not the entirety of Himalaya since 1998

### 2.4 Interpretation

- The Li Lu performance Munger publicly praised **refers, in the overwhelming majority of contexts, to the "2004 new fund" in which the Munger family's $88M grew to ~$400M+**, not to Himalaya's overall group performance
- That performance figure is **net of fees** (WSJ 2010 explicitly states "net of operating costs")
- BYD's substantial contribution **shows up both in Himalaya's performance and in Berkshire's BYD stake**—Berkshire bought 225M shares at HK$8 in 2008-09, totaling HK$1.8 billion, on Li Lu's recommendation to Munger/Buffett. **Berkshire's gains on that position are not counted in Himalaya's performance**

### 🚨 Key Finding: A Crack in the Data

If we go strictly by **Munger's own disclosure of "$88M becoming 4-5x"** (~15 years), that works out to **roughly 10-11% annualized net of fees**, **essentially in line with the S&P 500**.

**This is sharply at odds with the "20%+ excess alpha" narrative circulating in Chinese-language circles**.

This implies: **unless the "$400M" in Munger's remark was a snapshot at some point before any sales, with further unrealized gains still to come**, **Li Lu's true long-term net-of-fees alpha on Munger's money may be lower than commonly assumed**.

### A More Reasonable Overall Assessment

- **1998-2010 (12 years)**: Himalaya returned ~26% annualized net of fees (WSJ primary source) → excess of **~24 percentage points/year over the S&P 500, a huge alpha**
- **2004-2009 (5 years)**: the new fund returned ~36% net of fees, **an extremely large alpha** (the S&P was near 0 over the same period)
- **2010-2023 (13 years)**: **data is opaque**. If Munger's "4-5x" figure covers the full 2004-2019 span, then **alpha over the most recent decade may have narrowed significantly**

### Performance

- WSJ 2010: "36% net of fees over the new fund's first 5 years from Q4 2004"
- **Himalaya has not publicly disclosed any performance figures after 2010**

All claims of "30% annualized over 25 years" are blog-derived estimates and **should not be cited as precise figures in research reports**.

### The "19x in 16 Years" Problem
This widely circulated "Munger said" quote in Chinese-language circles—**no precise match can be found in any English-language primary source**.

The closest actual Munger statements:
- "hit it out of the park"
- "$88M became 4-5× that"

**Working backward from the latter**: Munger's own money returned only **~10%** annualized from 2004-2019—**on par with the S&P**!

There is a clear gap between these two sets of figures.

**The rigorous approach**: when citing Li Lu's performance, anchor to **"1998-2010, ~26% annualized net of fees (per WSJ disclosure)"**, and **stop applying "19x" or "30% over 25 years"**.

### An Honest Range for Alpha
- **1998-2010**: alpha over the S&P ≈ **+24 percentage points/year** (an enormous excess)
- **2004-2009**: alpha over the S&P ≈ **+36 percentage points/year** (an enormous excess)
- **2010-2025**: **opaque; no alpha claim should be made**

### BYD as a Single Source of Alpha
Within Li Lu's long-term track record, the BYD contribution may **account for the overwhelming majority**—recommended to Munger/Buffett in 2008, divested for US$300 million+ in cash in 2021, with peak unrealized gains of 20-30x.

**This implies that, excluding BYD, Himalaya's replicable alpha needs to be discounted in any assessment**.
