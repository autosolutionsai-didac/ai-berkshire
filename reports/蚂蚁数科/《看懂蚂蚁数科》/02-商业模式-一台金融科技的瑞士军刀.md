## 5.5 billion yuan in revenue, five product lines of wildly different scale

A company with roughly 5.5 billion yuan in 2024 revenue that just reached breakeven — yet whose valuation range spans from 8 billion to 200 billion yuan. That gap isn't the product of anyone's delusion; it exists because the question of "where exactly does this 5.5 billion come from, and how does it grow" has never been clearly answered.

This piece goes through the five product lines one by one: how much each contributes, which one is the steady ballast collecting rent, which one is the growth engine fighting in a red ocean, and which one is nothing more than a long-dated option.

---

## Five product lines: ballast, growth engine, and options

Start with the big picture. Below is a breakdown of Ant Digital Technologies' five product lines (2024 revenue estimated at approximately 5.5 billion yuan, based on the CEO's public remarks and industry data — low confidence):

| Product line | Estimated 2024 revenue | Share | Role | Growth outlook |
|--------|--------------|------|------|---------|
| SOFAStack + mPaaS (cloud middleware) | approximately **1.8 billion** | 33% | Ballast | Steady growth |
| AntChain BaaS + privacy computing (Morse) | approximately **1.2 billion** | 22% | Ballast | Steady growth |
| AntShield (intelligent risk control) | approximately **1 billion** | 18% | Undervalued core asset | Moderate growth |
| AI services (Agentar, etc.) | approximately **500 million** | 9% | Growth engine | High growth (100%+) |
| ZOLOZ (overseas eKYC) + RWA/Web3 | approximately **700 million** | 13% | Option | High growth overseas, RWA at a very early stage |
| Other | approximately **300 million** | 5% | — | — |

**Note**: The breakdown above is an estimate based on industry data and the competitive landscape. Ant Digital Technologies has not disclosed revenue by product line, so confidence is low.

Below, each line is broken down in turn.

---

### Line one: SOFAStack + mPaaS (cloud middleware) — the ballast

**What it is**: SOFAStack is Ant's self-developed financial-grade distributed middleware; mPaaS is a mobile development platform. In simple terms, it's a toolkit that helps banks build the foundation of their IT systems.

**Why it matters**: This line covers **130+** financial institutions (source: Ant Digital Technologies' official website), including nearly every major state-owned bank and joint-stock bank. Once SOFAStack is embedded in a bank's core systems, switching costs become extremely high — one of Ant Digital Technologies' hardest moats.

**How it makes money**: Subscription fees plus project-based delivery. Large clients (state-owned banks) sign contracts worth tens of millions of yuan a year; small and mid-sized banks, millions of yuan.

**Scale comparison**: Benchmarked against Hundsun Technologies (2025 revenue of approximately 5.783 billion yuan, market cap of approximately 60 billion yuan), SOFAStack + mPaaS is still less than half of Hundsun's size, but growing faster.

**Counterargument**: Huawei Cloud is encroaching across the full stack, pushing up from IaaS into PaaS, and holds the No. 1 position in financial cloud infrastructure with 28.9% market share (source: IDC). CEC Fintech has won the top spot in banking IT solutions for seven straight years and serves 600+ financial institutions. SOFAStack's middleware business faces a pincer squeeze — Huawei Cloud above, CEC Fintech below.

---

### Line two: AntChain + privacy computing — the technology crown, on too small a track

**What this is**:

- **AntChain**: a consortium-chain BaaS service that helps enterprises build blockchain infrastructure. Its market share of **26.5%** has ranked No. 1 for 4 straight years (source: IDC). It holds **5,000+** blockchain patents, the most of any company globally.
- **Morse privacy computing**: helps institutions perform joint modeling and data analysis while keeping data within its original domain. Its market share of **36.7%** has ranked No. 1 for 3 straight years (source: IDC).

**The awkward part about these two**:

The technology genuinely leads. But look at the market size:

| Track | 2024 China market size | Ant Digital Technologies' share | Corresponding revenue |
|------|-------------------|-------------|----------------|
| Blockchain BaaS | approximately **1.9 billion** | 26.5% | approximately **500 million** |
| Privacy computing | approximately **1 billion** | 36.7% | approximately **360 million** |
| **Total** | **approximately 2.9 billion** | — | **approximately 860 million** |

**Combined, these two tracks add up to a market of only 2.9 billion yuan.** Ant Digital Technologies has already captured nearly a third of it. That means:

1. There's limited room left to grow share further — it's already the outright leader
2. Growth can only come from the market itself expanding
3. In 2024, the privacy-computing sector saw only 1 financing deal and 4 newly registered companies industry-wide — the industry is undergoing a brutal shakeout

**One-line verdict**: The undisputed king of a small track, but the track's own ceiling means it can never become the core engine driving the company's valuation.

**Counterargument**: RWA tokenization could break through that ceiling. If the global tokenizable asset pool reaches BCG/McKinsey's projected **$2 trillion to $16 trillion**, AntChain — as an early mover in institutional-grade on-chain infrastructure — could see its revenue ceiling completely rewritten. But that "if" is a big one: the current global on-chain RWA market is only about **$30 billion** (source: RWA.xyz). The gap between $30 billion and $2 trillion is 67-fold, and a great deal could go wrong in between.

### Line three: AntShield (intelligent risk control) — the undervalued core asset

**What this is**: AntShield is Ant Digital Technologies' intelligent risk-control product, helping financial institutions detect fraud and assess credit risk.

**Why it's "undervalued"**:

AntShield is rarely discussed on its own, but it may be Ant Digital Technologies' **single largest revenue contributor**:

- IDC's May 2025 report: AntShield holds a **15.7%** share of China's retail-credit intelligent risk-control market, ranked No. 1
- Forrester rating: ranked **No. 1** among APAC anti-fraud solutions
- China's retail-credit risk-control market was worth approximately **65.4 billion yuan** in 2024 (source: IDC)
- Applying the 15.7% share, AntShield-related revenue could be in the **1 billion+** range

**Core edge**: AntShield's data moat comes from more than a decade of anti-fraud data accumulated by Ant Group across Alipay, Huabei, and MYbank. That data asset is nearly impossible for competitors to replicate.

**Counterargument**: AntShield's data moat has a compliance boundary — how much of Alipay's transaction data can legally be shared with Ant Digital Technologies? As enforcement of the Personal Information Protection Law and anti-monopoly law tightens, this data pipeline could narrow. In addition, Tongdun Technology (valued at **9.2 billion yuan**, with **10,000+** clients), as a purely independent third party, appears more "neutral" than an Ant-affiliated vendor to some clients.

### Line four: AI services (Agentar) — a growth engine in the most brutal competition

**What this is**: Agentar is Ant Digital Technologies' enterprise-grade AI agent development platform, letting financial institutions build agents with no code. Core products include:

- **Agentar Agent Platform**: holds CAICT's top-tier certification; rated "Comprehensive Pick" by IT Home/CSDN
- **Agentar-Fin-R1**: a financial reasoning LLM (fine-tuned from Qwen3) that outperforms DeepSeek-R1 on financial benchmarks
- **Agentar-Scale-SQL**: topped the global BIRD-SQL leaderboard, with query accuracy of **92%**
- **LingDT-2.6-flash**: an efficient inference model for enterprises, cutting token consumption by **90%**

**Lighthouse case**: Bank of Ningbo is the flagship client for Ant Digital Technologies' AI services:

| Scenario | Result | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Knowledge Q&A (KBase) | Complex Q&A accuracy rose from 68% to **91%** | Xinhua Net, Oct 2025 |
| Content recommendation | Accuracy **+35%**, recall **+40%** | same as above |
| Bill/document recognition | Error rate fell from 0.8% to **0.05%**, cutting annual losses by **30 million yuan** | internal research notes |

**Why growth is fast**: In H1 2025, Ant Digital Technologies reached large-model cooperation agreements with nearly **30** financial institutions, and both the financial and new-energy sectors achieved **100%** growth (source: CEO, Bund Summit 2025). In February 2026, it established a "Large Model Technology Innovation Department" to coordinate ToB commercialization of the Bailing large model.

**Why the competition is fiercest here**:

AI agents are Ant Digital Technologies' most brutally contested product line, and every rival is a heavyweight:

| Rival | Core weapon | Threat level |
|------|---------|---------|
| Baidu AI Cloud | No. 1 in IDC's financial-LLM market share at 12.2%; ERNIE 4.5 | Extremely high |
| Tencent Cloud | Tens of thousands of financial clients; seven flagship financial agents | Extremely high |
| Hundsun Technologies | Monopoly-like position in financial IT + LightGPT (trained on 400 billion tokens of financial data) | Extremely high |
| Huawei Cloud | Pangu 5.5 + No. 1 financial-LLM market share (Frost & Sullivan) | High |
| DeepSeek | Doesn't compete directly, but erodes application-layer value from underneath | High |

**Counterargument**: Topping BIRD-SQL is an academic-benchmark achievement, and the bridge from there to commercial willingness to pay hasn't been proven. BloombergGPT also led on financial benchmarks in its day, and its commercialization ultimately came close to failing. China Construction Bank's "Ark Program" has already adapted 16 versions of general-purpose LLMs, completely independent of Ant Digital Technologies; China Merchants Bank chose Alibaba's Tongyi model, not Ant Digital Technologies, as its primary partner. Major banks are increasingly following an "open-source foundation + build in-house" path that bypasses every commercial LLM vendor.

### Line five: ZOLOZ (overseas eKYC) + RWA/Web3 — the option

**ZOLOZ**: Ant Digital Technologies' overseas eKYC (electronic identity verification) product, covering **14** countries and **740 million** users. Its flagship client is Malaysia's Touch 'n Go (covering 75% of user registrations). The global eKYC market was worth approximately **$13.7 billion** in 2024 and is projected to reach **$63 billion** by 2033 (an 18% CAGR, source: Straits Research).

**RWA/Web3**: This is the piece with the biggest imagination space — and the most uncertainty:

- A **60 billion yuan** (approximately **$8.4 billion**) new-energy asset tokenization plan, already connected to **14 million** devices (source: Bloomberg/The Block)
- **Jovay** Layer 2 public chain: 100,000 TPS / 100ms latency (launched in Dubai, April 2025)
- **DT Tokenization Suite**: a full-stack Tokenization-as-a-Service platform (launched September 2025)
- **Anvita**: launched at Cannes in April 2026, letting AI agents trade autonomously on-chain (source: CoinDesk, April 2026)
- Currently applying for stablecoin licenses in Hong Kong, Singapore, and Luxembourg

**Key turning point**: On April 10, 2026, Hong Kong issued only **2** licenses in its first batch of stablecoin licenses (HSBC + AnchorPoint) — **Ant was not among the winners** (source: Sina Finance, April 12, 2026). This means the optimistic narrative around the stablecoin line has already been substantially discounted.

**Counterargument**: The global on-chain scale of RWA tokenization currently stands at only about $30 billion, and Ant Digital Technologies' 60 billion yuan asset tokenization plan has no clearly defined fee model — a 2-5% platform fee? A technology service fee? Or transaction commissions? The revenue implications differ enormously across models. With the first batch of stablecoin licenses having passed it by, the value of the RWA option needs to be reassessed.

## A critical revenue-quality question: related-party transactions

Ant Digital Technologies' revenue has an unavoidable question — **how much of it comes from within the Ant Group system?**

Industry estimates put the share of related-party transactions at **30-40%** (source: industry-internal verification, medium confidence). That implies:

| Basis | Estimated 2025 revenue | Meaning |
|------|---------------|------|
| Management basis (including related-party transactions) | approximately **10-15 billion** | Used in the CEO's public statements |
| Third-party basis (excluding related-party transactions) | approximately **6-9 billion** | Closer to the true independent customer-acquisition capability |

**Why this matters**:

- Related-party transactions must be disclosed in an IPO prospectus, and investors typically apply a valuation discount to them
- If Ant Group ever decides to reduce internal procurement (or switch to another supplier), this portion of revenue could disappear
- Benchmarked against Hundsun Technologies (full-scope 2025 revenue of approximately 5.783 billion yuan), Ant Digital Technologies' third-party revenue of 6-9 billion yuan is already in the same order of magnitude as Hundsun's

**Counterargument**: Related-party transactions aren't necessarily a bad thing. MYbank, Ant Consumer Finance, and Alipay under Ant Group are all genuine financial businesses with real technical demand for Ant Digital Technologies' products. Related-party transactions do not equal fake revenue — the key question is whether the share can keep declining.

## Core features of the business model

Looking at all five lines together, Ant Digital Technologies' business model has three notable features:

### Feature one: high per-customer value, low customer count

Ant Digital Technologies looks more like Palantir (high per-customer value, government/large-enterprise clients) or Hundsun Technologies (financial IT monopoly-like position) than an internet subscription software business.

The estimated average revenue per customer is approximately **420,000 yuan** (5.5 billion yuan / 13,000 customers), but the distribution is highly uneven — a comprehensive solution for a top-tier bank might run into the tens of millions, while long-tail clients might pay only tens of thousands.

### Feature two: revenue structure shifting from "project-based" to "subscription + pay-for-performance"

CEO Zhao Wenbiao's push for a "pay-for-performance" model is a notable innovation worth watching. The Bank of Ningbo case (30 million yuan in annual loss reduction shared proportionally) shows that Ant Digital Technologies is willing to tie its revenue to clients' actual business outcomes.

This implies **higher customer stickiness** (good results mean continued payment) but also **greater revenue volatility** (poor results mean payments stop).

### Feature three: cross-selling among the five lines

Ant Digital Technologies' playbook is to first win a customer through one line (say, SOFAStack middleware or Morse privacy computing), then gradually expand into AI agents, blockchain, and other product lines.

This "land-expansion" strategy depends on **depth of customer relationships** — and in the financial industry, that happens to be exactly where Ant Digital Technologies is strong. With 100% coverage of state-owned banks and 60%+ coverage of regional banks, there is enormous room for cross-selling.

## One-line verdict

> **Ant Digital Technologies' business is "the packaged output of a financial-grade technology stack" — each of the five lines plays its own role, but no single one alone can support a premium valuation narrative. The ballast (middleware + blockchain + privacy computing) is highly certain but has a low ceiling; the growth engine (AI) is growing fast but fighting a brutal war; the option (RWA/Web3) has huge imagination space but is at a very early stage. Taken together, it's more like a "fintech Swiss Army knife" — every blade is useful, but none of them is a killer weapon.**
