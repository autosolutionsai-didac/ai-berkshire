## Why Is Ant Digital Technologies So Hard to Value

Ant Digital Technologies is No. 1 in blockchain BaaS, No. 1 in privacy computing, No. 1 in retail-credit risk control. These "No. 1" claims are real. But between "genuinely No. 1" and "worth how much," there sits a giant black box: almost none of the company's core financial data has been publicly disclosed.

The first three articles covered what Ant Digital Technologies is, how it makes money, and how strong its competitors are. That brings us to the core question: **what is it worth?**

This question is hard not because valuation methodology is inadequate — it's because **the underlying data is almost entirely a black box**.

| Key data needed for valuation | Ant Digital Technologies' actual situation |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Revenue | Undisclosed; the CEO's public remarks suggest an inferred RMB 5.5-8.5 billion |
| Profit | Undisclosed; all that is known is that it "broke even in 2024" |
| Revenue breakdown by product line | Undisclosed |
| Related-party transaction share | Industry estimate 30-40%, unofficial |
| Customer renewal rate (NDR) | Undisclosed |
| Operating cash flow | Undisclosed |
| External-financing valuation anchor | **Does not exist** (no outside investors brought in) |

**A company that has not even publicly disclosed its revenue will inevitably produce a fuzzy valuation.** The right mindset for reading this piece isn't "find a precise number" but "understand the logic and drivers behind the valuation range."

## The Valuation Bounds: From RMB 8 Billion to RMB 200 Billion

Different methods and different bases produce valuations spanning a disturbingly wide range:

| Valuation method | Valuation range (RMB) | Core assumption | Confidence |
|---------|-------------------|---------|--------|
| Reverse-derivation from employee equity | Approximately **RMB 8 billion** | One-split-into-four mapping of 1.4% × the Group's RMB 571 billion | Medium (reliable data, but understates standalone value) |
| Industry-median method | **RMB 22-30 billion** | Median P/S of 3.7x across 22 comparable companies × RMB 6-8 billion revenue | Medium |
| Comparable-company P/S method | **RMB 40-68 billion** | Third-party revenue of RMB 8 billion × P/S 6-8x | Medium |
| Sum-of-the-parts method | **RMB 56.7-104.2 billion** | Each of five business lines benchmarked separately, including a 30-40% holding-company discount | Low |
| Parent-company back-solve (Ant International valued conservatively) | **RMB 45-122 billion** | Worked backward after stripping out Ant International | Low |
| Market-rumored valuation | **RMB 150-200 billion** | Requires all three of the AI + SaaS + Web3 scarcity narratives to be delivered | Extremely low |

**This table is itself the most important conclusion**: Ant Digital Technologies' valuation is not a single "point" but a **huge range**, depending on which assumptions you choose to believe.

### Why Is the Employee-Equity Basis Only RMB 8 Billion?

Ant Group completed its "one-split-into-four" structural adjustment in March 2024, and completed the segment mapping of employee equity incentives in March 2025, allocating Ant Digital Technologies **1.4%** of the Group's equity. Working backward from the Group's RMB 571 billion valuation, Ant Digital Technologies' internal accounting fair value is only about RMB 8 billion.

This figure **clearly understates** Ant Digital Technologies' standalone value — because the 1.4% mapping ratio is an internal accounting allocation within the Group, not market-based pricing. Departing employees are subject to mandatory buyback at this price, which is also the core reason multiple departing employees publicly protested in 2025.

### Why Does the Market Rumor a Valuation of RMB 150-200 Billion?

Reaching a valuation of RMB 150 billion+ requires **simultaneously** satisfying the following conditions:

1. Hong Kong stablecoin license approval + a scale-up of RWA volume
2. Third-party revenue share > 50% (related-party transactions falling below 30%)
3. Ant International's successful Hong Kong IPO driving a sibling valuation premium
4. The AI + SaaS + Web3 triple-scarcity narrative being delivered

**As of May 2026, none of the above four conditions has been verified.**

## Fair Valuation Range: RMB 40-68 Billion

Cross-validating multiple methods, **the market-based valuation range that comes closest to "fair" is RMB 40-68 billion (median approximately RMB 52 billion)**.

Here's how this number is derived:
- Third-party comparable revenue of approximately **RMB 8 billion** (2026 estimate, excluding related-party transactions)
- Referencing Hong Kong-listed tech companies' P/S multiples of **6-8x** (Hundsun Technologies 8.3x, Kingdee 6.9x)
- After applying an unlisted-company discount, a related-party-transaction discount, and a liquidity discount, taking the median

| Tier | Valuation | Implied assumptions |
|------|------|---------|
| Conservative | **RMB 30 billion** | Related-party transactions > 40% + Hong Kong liquidity discount + parent-company IPO delay |
| Neutral | **RMB 52 billion** | Third-party revenue of RMB 8 billion × P/S 6.5x |
| Optimistic | **RMB 90 billion** | Ant International's successful IPO + AI narrative delivering + related-party transactions falling to 30% |

**Cross-check against a comparable**:

An interesting reference point — **Qulian Technology** (a blockchain unicorn expected to file in Hong Kong in the second half of 2026) is valued at approximately **RMB 40 billion**, with only about **RMB 1.3 billion** in 2025 revenue, implying a P/S of roughly **30x**. Ant Digital Technologies' revenue is **6.5 times** Qulian's, yet its valuation is comparable — which means either Qulian is significantly overvalued, or Ant Digital Technologies is undervalued, or both.

**Counter-argument**: the RMB 40-68 billion valuation range assumes a P/S of 6-8x is reasonable. But if Ant Digital Technologies' growth rate slows from 50%+ to 20-30% as its revenue base grows, the P/S should converge toward Hundsun Technologies' 8x or even lower. Moreover, the historical discount applied to spun-off fintech subsidiaries (see "cautionary precedents" below) may push the true transaction price below the theoretical valuation.

### The Hong Kong Stock Exchange's Hard Constraint

There's a fact many people overlook: **the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has never approved an independent IPO by a subsidiary whose unlisted parent has not itself listed.**

This means: either Ant Group's overall IPO or Ant International's IPO going first is a **precondition** (not a sufficient condition) for Ant Digital Technologies' independent IPO. Whichever of the two happens first, plus a 12-18-month observation period, equals the earliest possible date for Digital Technologies to file.

## Three Cautionary Precedents: The Fate of Spun-Off Fintech Subsidiaries

Ant Digital Technologies is not the first tech subsidiary spun off from a large financial group. At least three companies have walked a similar path before it — **none has succeeded**.

| Company | Parent | Listing date | Post-listing performance | Lesson |
|------|--------|---------|-----------|------|
| **Ping An OneConnect** | Ping An Group | NYSE, 2019 | Stock fell from $10 to below $2, delisted in 2024 | High related-party-transaction share + difficulty acquiring customers independently |
| **JD Digits** | JD Group | Withdrew its 2020 STAR Market IPO | Never listed | Tightened regulation + disputes over business positioning |
| **Lufax** | Ping An Group | NYSE, 2020 | Market cap fell from a peak of $47 billion to below $5 billion | P2P regulation + related-party transactions + deteriorating profitability |

**The shared lesson of all three**:

1. **Related-party transactions are valuation poison** — investors assign extremely low multiples to revenue that comes from "parent-company transfusion"
2. **Standalone customer-acquisition ability is unproven** — once out from under the parent's halo, growth rates fall off a cliff
3. **Spun off ≠ independent** — management's decision-making power, technical resources, and customer relationships remain heavily dependent on the parent

**How likely is Ant Digital Technologies to avoid the same fate?**

Here are the key differences:

| Dimension | Ping An OneConnect / Lufax / JD Digits | Ant Digital Technologies |
|------|--------------------------|---------|
| Technical differentiation | Weak (generic financial IT) | Moderately strong (dual No. 1 in blockchain + privacy computing) |
| Regulatory environment | Faced severe regulation | Relatively stable (though the financial holding license remains pending) |
| Related-party transactions | High (> 40-50%) | Medium-high (estimated 30-40%) |
| Overseas business | Almost none | Has some (ZOLOZ + RWA + Hong Kong overseas headquarters) |
| **Overall assessment** | | Better than its predecessors, but has not escaped the same category of difficulties |

**Counter-argument**: Ant Digital Technologies differs from the first three in a fundamental way — the first three companies' core products (P2P, financial IT, consumer-finance technology) had low barriers to entry and faced regulatory headwinds, whereas Ant Digital Technologies' core products (blockchain BaaS, privacy computing, financial AI agents) have higher technical barriers and sit in a policy-favored direction (data-element economy, AI industrialization, Xinchuang domestic substitution).

## Three Layers of Uncertainty

Across this entire series, the core problems facing Ant Digital Technologies boil down to **three layers of uncertainty**:

### Uncertainty One: Revenue Independence

**Related-party-transaction share** is the largest drag on the confidence of Ant Digital Technologies' valuation.

If related-party transactions exceed 50%, it means Ant Digital Technologies' true standalone growth capability is overstated, and every valuation needs a 20-30% haircut. The current industry estimate is 30-40% — not catastrophic, but still some distance from "clean."

**Verification point**: related-party-transaction disclosure in the IPO prospectus. Until then, this number can only be guessed at.

### Uncertainty Two: The Ceiling of the Track

Ant Digital Technologies' current core tracks (BaaS + privacy computing) add up to only about a **RMB 3 billion** market. Being No. 1 in these small ponds cannot support a premium valuation narrative.

The only directions that could break through the ceiling are **RWA/Web3** and **AI agents** — but the former's entire on-chain global scale is only about USD 30 billion, and the latter's competition is brutal.

**Verification points**: the RWA platform's annual transaction volume surpassing RMB 5 billion; Agentar surpassing 100 financial-institution customers with annual revenue exceeding RMB 2 billion.

### Uncertainty Three: Exit Path

Ant Digital Technologies' probability of an independent IPO is only **50%**, at the earliest **listing in 2029**. If the parent company's IPO continues to stall and the financial holding license continues to go unapproved, Ant Digital Technologies could remain stuck for a long time in a state of "having a valuation but no way to realize it."

**Verification points**: Ant International's successful Hong Kong IPO (the bellwether); Ant Digital Technologies completing an independent financing round and bringing in outside investors.

## The Ant Digital Technologies We End Up Seeing

Across these four articles, the complete picture of Ant Digital Technologies looks like this:

**What it is**: the core vehicle through which Ant Group's technology spills over and gets monetized, holding the No. 1 position in niche tracks such as blockchain BaaS, privacy computing, and retail-credit risk control, with about RMB 5.5 billion in 2024 revenue and having reached breakeven.

**What it is not**: it is not a foundation large-model company (it depends on the Group's Bailing model); it is not OceanBase (already independent); it is not Alipay (a completely different business).

**Its strengths**: the deepest engineering capability for financial scenarios nationwide (66 scenario categories, CAICT Level-5 certification); high switching costs (deeply embedded in 130+ financial institutions); strong brand mindshare ("proven within Alipay"); first-mover positioning in RWA/Web3.

**Its weaknesses**: its core tracks are relatively small (BaaS + privacy computing together under RMB 3 billion in market size); the AI track is brutally competitive and depends on the Group's foundation model; the related-party-transaction share is unclear; the exit path is uncertain; disclosure is extremely opaque.

**The core tension it faces**:

> **Ant Digital Technologies is a subject whose fundamentals are real but whose valuation anchor is missing.** Its core value genuinely exists — a financial-grade technology stack, No. 1 positions across multiple tracks, and continued transfusion from the parent. But its valuation dilemma is equally real — no external financing to establish pricing, a black box around related-party transactions, no successful listing precedent among spun-off fintech subsidiaries, and a limited ceiling in its core tracks.

## Final Thoughts

Having written these four articles, my understanding of Ant Digital Technologies can be condensed into one sentence:

> **Ant Digital Technologies has achieved genuine No. 1 positions in a few niche fields — that is not hollow. But between "No. 1 in a small track" and "a large company," the gap is not one of technology — it is the chasm between track size and commercial validation. AI and RWA are the bridges across that chasm, but the bridge is not yet built.**

For ordinary readers, what's most worth continuously tracking about Ant Digital Technologies isn't its valuation figure — which remains a guess until it lists — but these three things:

1. **Agentar's commercialization progress**: the financial AI-agent track is being rapidly reshuffled; can Ant Digital Technologies go from "No. 1 in academic benchmarks" to "No. 1 in commercial revenue"
2. **Regulatory progress on RWA tokenization**: Hong Kong's stablecoin license, positioning in Singapore/Luxembourg, and the growth rate of global on-chain RWA scale
3. **The outcome of Ant International's IPO**: this is the bellwether for Ant Digital Technologies' own listing, and its success or failure will directly determine Digital Technologies' exit path

If clear positive signals emerge on these three fronts over the next 2-3 years, Ant Digital Technologies will be worth revisiting in depth. Until then, staying attentive, staying patient, and not rushing to a conclusion is the most rational stance.
