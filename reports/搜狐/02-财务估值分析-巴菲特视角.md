**Key observations**:
- Revenue has shrunk roughly 30% cumulatively over five years ($835M to $584M), with continued pressure on the core business
- 2025 GAAP net income was $394M and Non-GAAP net income was $234M — but **stripping out a one-time $285M reversal of accrued Changyou withholding tax**, 2025 Non-GAAP results were actually a net loss of $51M (a 40% narrowing from 2024's -$83M)
- **At the true operating level, Sohu has posted a loss every year since 2021**; 2025 marks a turn toward breakeven but the business is not yet self-sustaining

## IV. Net-cash discount analysis (**a non-consensus focus**)

### 4.1 Verifying the core cigar-butt characteristics

Graham's classic definition of a "cigar butt": **market cap < net current asset value (NCAV)**. Sohu meets this criterion, and to an extreme degree:

| Dimension | Standard | Sohu Currently | Meets Criterion? |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Market cap < net cash | Yes | $410M < $1,164M | Yes — extremely so |
| Cash > 50% of total assets | Yes | ~70%+ | Yes |
| Interest-bearing debt extremely low | Yes | $36M | Yes |
| Ongoing but manageable losses | Borderline condition | Non-GAAP loss of -$51M | Marginal |
| Core business operating cash flow positive | Bonus factor | Games business OCF positive | Yes |
| Major shareholder buying back stock | Strong signal | $150M buyback completed | Yes, strongly |

**Conclusion**: Sohu is an **A-grade cigar butt**, further confirmed by "management actively buying back stock in the market" — a double confirmation in both the Graham and Duan Yongping sense.

### 4.2 Comparison with similar net-cash-discount cases

| Company | Net Cash / Market Cap | Outcome |
|------|-----------|------|
| Esprit Holdings (330.HK) | 1.5-2.0x | Long-term value trap, continuously burning cash |
| Long Fu Green (106.HK) | 1.3x | Slow value release, mainly via dividends/buybacks |
| **Changyou (CYOU)** | **1.5x (before going private)** | **Taken private by Sohu itself in April 2020 at $10.80/ADS, a 68% premium** |
| **Sohu (SOHU)** | **2.84x** | **Currently — cheaper than Changyou was before it went private** |

**The strongest comparable is Sohu's own former subsidiary, Changyou**: Charles Zhang has already executed the playbook of "taking a net-cash-discounted subsidiary private" once before, so the path is well-trodden.

### 4.3 Value-trap risks (must be heeded)

1. **Cash mostly sits within the onshore VIE structure**: the bulk of the $1,200M is held in RMB within mainland China, and dividends/repatriation are subject to foreign-exchange controls plus a 10% withholding tax
2. **Management has not historically distributed cash**: despite the huge net cash pile, Charles Zhang has never paid a regular dividend (the 2025 one-time $202M dividend is in fact highly unusual in Sohu's history and needs confirming)
3. **Core business continues to shrink**: the advertising business could shrink to near zero, and the games business depends on a single aging IP (*Tian Long Ba Bu*)
4. **China-concept delisting risk**: the HFCAA shadow has eased but not disappeared

## VII. Financial analysis of a potential take-private

### 7.1 Capital required for a 100%-premium take-private

| Item | Amount |
|------|------|
| Current market cap | $409.81M |
| Total cost of a 100%-premium buyout | $819.64M |
| Zhang already holds 35.8%; needs to acquire the remaining 64.2% | ≈ $526M |
| Company's available net cash on hand | $1,163.73M |
| **Net cash remaining after the take-private** | **+$637M** |

**Conclusion**: the company's own cash could fund a 100%-premium take-private in full, with **$637M in cash left over for Charles Zhang**. Financially, this would be an **almost risk-free negative-price acquisition**.

### 7.2 Assessing Charles Zhang's incentive to go private (referencing the Changyou precedent)

| Dimension | Changyou (2020 Going-Private) | Sohu Currently |
|------|----------------|---------|
| Net cash / market cap before going private | ~1.5x | **2.84x (even more extreme)** |
| Major shareholder stake | Sohu held 70%+ | Zhang holds 35.8% |
| Cost of maintaining the listing | High | High (a 25-year-old China-concept stock) |
| Regulatory/audit costs | High | Extremely high (HFCAA) |
| Valuation not recognized by the market | Yes | Yes (more severe) |
| **Outcome** | **Went private at $10.80/ADS (68% premium)** | **Theoretically an ideal candidate** |

**Countervailing factors**:
- Charles Zhang's personal style leans toward a "graceful exit" rather than active capital engineering
- A take-private would require financing and dealing with minority-shareholder litigation
- The window for China-concept take-privates is affected by U.S. listing regulation

**Probability assessment**: a take-private is a **5-10% probability tail event**, but if it happens, based on the Changyou precedent's 60-100% premium, it would imply $25-32/ADS.

### 8.3 Final judgment from a Buffett perspective

Buffett moved on from cigar-butt investing long ago in his later years, but his 1950s experience with Dempster Mill and Sanborn Map applies here:
- **A discount of assets to market cap does not disappear on its own — it needs a catalyst or active distribution**
- **Management is the key variable**: Charles Zhang has already demonstrated sincerity through the 2025 $352M in returns and the completed $150M buyback, far better than PDD-style "hoarder" management
- **The risk is time**: with every additional year of waiting, cash outflows from the operating business will erode part of the margin of safety

**Position sizing suggestion** (if allocating):
- No more than **3-5%** of a portfolio (poor liquidity, high single-name risk)
- Holding period: **18-36 months** while waiting for a catalyst
- Stop-loss: below 50% of net current asset value per share (i.e., below $22 one should not stop out — rather, add to the position)

### 8.4 Compatibility with the Duan Yongping / Li Lu perspectives
- **Duan Yongping**: would likely pass, on the grounds of "not understanding the Chinese gaming industry + Charles Zhang not being obsessive enough"
- **Li Lu**: might be interested, given the "enormous margin of safety plus aligned management interests," but would be concerned about liquidity
- **Graham himself**: **would buy** — this is a textbook cigar butt
