## I. Management Assessment

### 1.1 Pony Ma (CEO / Chairman of the Board) — Product Instinct and Strategic Resolve

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| **Age / tenure** | 54 years old, founder, 28 years |
| **Shareholding** | ~8.72% (~805 million shares), worth approximately HKD 408.9 billion |
| **Circle of competence** | A product-manager-type CEO; a technical background, but true talent lies in user insight |
| **Core decision quality** | The "self-revolution" from QQ to WeChat is etched into the annals of Chinese business history |

**Review of key historical decisions:**

| Decision | Timing | Outcome | Rating |
|------|------|------|------|
| Incubating WeChat, allowing internal "horse-racing" (parallel competing teams) | 2010-2011 | WeChat became a super gateway with 1.385 billion MAU, cementing dominance of the mobile-internet era | ★★★★★ |
| Strategic pivot from "doing everything" to "connection only" | 2014 | Invested in JD.com/Meituan/Didi, abandoned self-run e-commerce/O2O — a major leap in strategic clarity | ★★★★★ |
| Accelerating overseas game expansion | 2020-2025 | 2025 overseas gaming revenue reached 77.4 billion yuan (+33%), breaking USD 10 billion for the first time | ★★★★ |
| "Cost reduction and efficiency" strategic retrenchment | 2022 | Headcount fell from 108,500 to 105,400; profit growth of 36%; successfully weathered the downturn | ★★★★★ |
| AI investment pace: admitting it was "slow" | 2024-2025 | Yuanbao MAU only 41 million vs. Doubao's 226 million — a clear lag at the AI application layer | ★★☆☆☆ |
| Doubling AI investment in 2026 | 2026 | In progress — 2025 AI new-product investment was 18 billion yuan, set to at least double in 2026 | To be verified |

**Pony Ma's greatest strength is "knowing what not to do."** In 2022, facing an industry-wide downturn, he decisively cut non-core businesses and reined in the pace of investment. But sluggishness in AI strategy is the biggest demerit of recent years. Pony Ma himself has publicly admitted: "Our overall pace was actually slow — it wasn't until the end of 2024 that our AI products, Yuanbao and the Hunyuan large model, were transferred to CSIG, and only then did they start doing this properly."

> Li Lu: "The most important thing about the circle of competence is not how big it is, but knowing exactly where its boundary lies." Pony Ma's strength lies precisely here — he knows what he is not good at, which is why he chose to invest rather than build his own e-commerce and O2O operations. But the problem is that AI may not be a field that can be "outsourced" to the investment portfolio.

**Capability rating: ★★★★☆ (4.5/5)** — Strategic vision and execution are first-rate; the AI-pace misstep costs half a star.

### 1.3 The Succession Problem — The Biggest Hidden Risk

**Current state:**
- Pony Ma is 54, in good health, and can likely lead the company for at least another 5-10 years
- Martin Lau is 52, but has already stepped down from the board — an ambiguous signal
- Among the next generation of management, no "star successor" has yet emerged
- At the end of 2025, a new AI Infra department was established, led by Yao Shunyu (a former Tsinghua scholar), but this is a technical rather than a managerial line

**Benchmark comparison:**

| Company | Succession arrangement | Assessment |
|------|---------|------|
| Apple | Jobs → Cook (groomed 5 years in advance) | ★★★★★ |
| Microsoft | Ballmer → Nadella (internal selection) | ★★★★★ |
| Alibaba | Jack Ma → Daniel Zhang → Joe Tsai / Eddie Wu | ★★★☆☆ |
| Tencent | **No clear plan** | ★★★☆☆ |

> Li Lu: "Integrity is the most important quality in management. No matter how smart a dishonest management team is, it isn't worth investing in." But harder than integrity is succession — how do you ensure an excellent management culture endures beyond the founder?

Alibaba's succession experience shows that even with ample preparation, a handover can still lead to strategic confusion. Tencent has not even reached the "preparation" stage yet.

**Succession risk rating: ★★★☆☆** — Not a major issue in the medium term (within 5 years), but a significant long-term hidden risk.

## II. Regulatory Risk

### 2.1 Internet Antitrust

| Event | Impact | Current status |
|------|------|---------|
| 2021 antitrust crackdown (industry-wide) | Tencent fined 500,000 yuan (symbolic), required to relinquish exclusive music licensing rights | ✅ Rectification completed |
| Undisclosed-acquisition penalty for China Literature's acquisition of New Classics Media | 500,000 yuan fine | ✅ Resolved |
| "Interoperability" requirement | WeChat relaxed restrictions on external links | ✅ Adapted, limited impact |

**Current assessment:** the harshest antitrust period (2021-2022) has passed. In 2025-2026, policy tone has shifted toward "promoting development." But caution is warranted: the regulatory cycle is cyclical, not linear. The next tightening is only a matter of time.

### 2.2 Game Licensing and Minor Protection

- 1,771 game licenses were issued in 2025 (a five-year peak), a clear loosening of approvals
- The share of gaming time by minors has fallen to 0.2%, and regulatory pressure has largely been released
- **But a new risk has emerged:** in February 2026, rumors circulated online about a "tax hike on internet value-added services." Although this was confirmed to be baseless, it briefly triggered a sharp stock drop, showing that the market is extremely sensitive to tax-policy news

### 2.3 Financial Regulation (WeChat Pay / Licaitong)

- The People's Bank of China's "Measures for the Administration of Data Security in the Business Sector" took effect in June 2025, raising requirements for payment data security
- Tencent has proactively been "de-risking" — fintech revenue growth (8%) is significantly lower than its other business lines, reflecting management's intent to control financial leverage
- Businesses such as Licaitong and Weilidai operate within a prudent regulatory framework, with no major compliance incidents

### 2.4 Data Security and Privacy

- In 2025, China's data governance shifted from a "principles framework" to an "enforceable interface," raising compliance costs
- As one of China's largest data holders, Tencent faces the greatest compliance pressure
- But this also means compliance capability forms a barrier — smaller competitors find it harder to meet the bar

**Regulatory risk overall rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)** — The worst period has passed, but Chinese regulation can never be taken lightly.

> Li Lu: "China is in 'Stage 2.5' — a consolidation period after the takeoff of industrialization." Regulatory swings are an inherent feature of this stage, and investors must factor this into their long-term expectations.

## III. Competitive Risk: Assessing the ByteDance Threat

This is the part of the report that most needs a "contrarian check." Market consensus holds that "WeChat is irreplaceable" — but the real threat is often not replacement, but marginalization.

### 3.1 AI Application Layer: Tencent Is Clearly Lagging

| Metric | Tencent Yuanbao | ByteDance Doubao | Gap multiple |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Monthly active users (February 2026) | 41 million | 226 million | **5.5x** |
| 30-day retention rate | ~30% | ~45% | 1.5x |
| Ranking (AI products) | 18th | 1st | — |

**This data is alarming.** Tencent, sitting on WeChat's 1.385 billion MAU, has an AI product ranked 18th? Yuanbao's low retention rate suggests insufficient product strength, not a distribution problem.

Pony Ma's own diagnosis: "Our pace was indeed slow — the AI product wasn't formally handed to CSIG until the end of 2024."

### 3.2 The Battle for User Time

| Platform | DAU | Core competitive strength |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Douyin | ~700 million | Algorithmic recommendation + content ecosystem |
| WeChat Video Accounts | ~400 million+ | Social recommendation + private-domain traffic |
| WeChat (overall) | ~1.385 billion | Social relationship graph |

Video Accounts' DAU of 400 million+ is already strong, but its eCPM is only 63 yuan, far below Douyin's. This monetization-efficiency gap means that even as users consume short-video content within WeChat, the ad revenue Tencent captures from it remains lower than ByteDance's.

### 3.3 Assessing the Threat Level

**Short term (1-3 years): limited threat.** WeChat's social relationship graph is its deepest moat, and ByteDance cannot break through it.

**Medium term (3-5 years): the threat escalates.** If AI becomes the new user gateway (similar to the role search engines once played), and Doubao continues to lead in AI, it could gradually erode Tencent's position in "information access."

**Long term (5-10 years): the biggest uncertainty.** Will AI change the underlying logic of socializing? If AI agents can replace humans in completing most social needs (booking, recommendations, communication), WeChat's "relationship-graph moat" could be bypassed rather than replaced.

> Li Lu: "In the short run, the market is a voting machine; in the long run, it is a weighing machine." The market's current pricing of Tencent's AI lag seems insufficient — investors still believe "WeChat can catch up later," but what if that assumption turns out to be false?

**Competitive risk rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)** — Not a near-term problem, but potentially a key variable determining Tencent's long-term positioning.

## IV. Business Risk

### 4.1 Gaming Dependence

**2025 revenue structure:**

| Business segment | Revenue (RMB) | Share | Growth |
|---------|------------|------|------|
| Value-added services (gaming + social) | 369.3 billion | 49% | +16% |
| — of which: domestic gaming | 164.2 billion | 22% | +18% |
| — of which: overseas gaming | 77.4 billion | 10% | +33% |
| Marketing services (advertising) | 145.0 billion | 19% | +19% |
| Fintech and business services | 229.4 billion | 31% | +8% |

Gaming (domestic + overseas) totals approximately 241.6 billion yuan, or 32% of total revenue. Given that gaming margins are far higher than fintech and cloud services, **gaming's contribution to profit may exceed 45%**.

### 4.2 Honor of Kings: World — High Expectations, High Risk

"Honor of Kings: World" launched on PC on April 10, 2026, after five years of development, and is a "bet-the-house" product for Tencent Games:

- **Opportunity:** the Honor of Kings IP has a base of 139 million DAU; first-month gross billings are estimated at 1.5-2.5 billion yuan
- **Risk:** there is a large gap between open-world RPG and MOBA user profiles; migrating the IP carries "character-identity conflict" risk; a pure cosmetics-based monetization model is not well validated in the Chinese market
- **Key question:** can MOBA users' social relationships migrate to the open world? If not, this game amounts to an entirely new customer-acquisition challenge

### 4.3 Video Accounts Monetization Uncertainty

- GMV of 433.0 billion yuan (+75%) — stunning growth, but the base is still far below Douyin's e-commerce business
- eCPM of 63 yuan, only about one-third of Douyin's
- Video Accounts functions more as a **defensive product** — preventing user time from leaking to Douyin, rather than an offensive profit engine
- If Video Accounts e-commerce fails to close the gap with Douyin, market growth expectations for it may need to be revised down

**Business risk rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)** — Revenue diversification is best-in-class among Chinese internet companies, but profit concentration in gaming remains high.

## V. Geopolitical Risk

### 5.1 The Pathways Through Which US-China Relations Affect Tencent

| Risk pathway | Probability | Impact |
|---------|------|------|
| US sanctions Tencent (a TikTok-style scenario) | Low (5%) | Extremely high — overseas gaming revenue and the investment portfolio would be directly damaged |
| Escalation of semiconductor/AI chip bans | Medium (30%) | Medium — affects the pace of AI infrastructure investment, but Tencent is not a chip-design company |
| Persistent Hong Kong stock valuation discount | High (70%) | Medium — PE structurally below comparable US stocks (Tencent 18x vs. Meta 25x+) |
| Restricted overseas investment exits | Medium (20%) | Medium — affects SOTP valuation, but Tencent has already been reducing its overseas exposure |

### 5.2 The Structural Problem of the Hong Kong Valuation Discount

Tencent's PE (TTM) is ~18x vs. a 10-year median of 27x. Is this discount an "opportunity" or the "new normal"?

**The "opportunity" case:** profit growth of 17%, FCF of 182.6 billion yuan, ROE of 24% — these fundamental metrics rank among the best of any global large-cap tech company.

**The "new normal" case:** Hong Kong stock market liquidity discounts, China policy risk premiums, and continued foreign-capital outflows — these structural factors are unlikely to reverse in the short term. In 2026, US-China relations remain in a pattern of "tightened restrictions in sensitive areas, selective cooperation in non-sensitive areas."

> Li Lu: "Think about humanity's shared future from the perspective of humankind as a whole. US-China relations are not a zero-sum game." But as investors, we must acknowledge: even if Li Lu's judgment proves correct in the long run, short- and medium-term political friction will still affect asset pricing.

**Geopolitical risk rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)** — Uncontrollable, but Tencent is already proactively de-risking its overseas exposure.

### 6.1 Ownership Structure

| Shareholder | Stake | Role |
|------|---------|------|
| Prosus/Naspers | ~23% | Largest shareholder, continuing to reduce its stake |
| Pony Ma | ~8.72% | Founder and CEO |
| Martin Lau | ~0.58% | President |
| Other management and employees | ~3-5% (estimated) | — |
| Public shareholders | ~65% | Includes a large number of institutional investors |

**Core governance issue: Tencent has no dual-class share structure.** This means Pony Ma cannot exercise absolute control over the company with only an 8.72% stake. The upside is greater "democracy"; the downside is that if Prosus continues to sell down, the company could face barbarians at the gate.

### 6.2 History and Trend of Prosus's Stake Reduction

| Period | Action | Change in stake |
|------|------|------|
| 2001 | Naspers invests USD 34 million in Tencent | 46.5% |
| 2018 | First stake reduction | From 33.2% → 31.2% |
| 2022 | Launches an "indefinite reduction program" to fund its own buybacks | Accelerating decline |
| 2024 | Continues selling | Down to ~25% |
| 2025 | Continues to reduce | Down to ~23% |
| Q1 2026 | Sold 13 million shares | ~23% (current) |

**Key judgments:**
- Prosus's stake reduction is a **certain source of selling pressure**, roughly 10-15 million shares per quarter
- But the pace is moderate — at the current rate, a complete exit would take more than 10 years
- Prosus CEO Fabricio Bloisi has publicly stated his intent to build "over USD 100 billion in value" outside of Tencent, indicating a clear and long-term motive for the reduction
- Tencent's large-scale buybacks have to some degree offset Prosus's selling pressure (the 2024 buyback of HKD 112.0 billion vastly exceeds the scale of Prosus's stake reduction)

**Governance structure rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)** — Prosus's stake reduction is a chronic bleed, not fatal but a persistent drag.

## VII. Long-Term Durability Assessment (Li Lu's Perspective)

> Li Lu: "The best investment is one you never need to sell. If you buy into a truly excellent company, the best strategy is to do nothing at all."

So, is Tencent such a company that you "never need to sell"?

### 7.1 What Will Tencent Look Like in Ten Years?

**Base case (55% probability):** Tencent remains one of China's most valuable internet companies, but permanently transitions from a "high-growth company" to a "stable-growth value company." WeChat remains a super gateway, but its growth rate falls to single digits. Gaming stays in the global top three, with overseas revenue exceeding 40% of the total. Annual profit of 400-500 billion yuan, with dividend-plus-buyback returns of 3-5%. Something like today's Microsoft — steady but unexciting.

**Optimistic case (25% probability):** the AI catch-up strategy succeeds, and WeChat + AI forms a new generation of super app. Video Accounts e-commerce approaches the scale of Douyin. Overseas gaming enters a high-growth phase. Annual profit surpasses 600 billion yuan, and the valuation returns to a 25x PE.

**Pessimistic case (20% probability):** AI-gateway migration, declining user time, and a population peak converge simultaneously. WeChat isn't "replaced," but shifts from "must-open" to "occasionally opened." Gaming faces a shift in cultural tastes (changing Gen Z preferences). Annual profit stalls at 250-300 billion yuan. Valuation falls to 12-15x PE, becoming "China's IBM."

### 7.2 What Could Disrupt WeChat?

**The probability of direct replacement is extremely low (<5%).** The social relationship graph of 1.385 billion MAU is one of the deepest network-effect moats in human history. No one will move all their friends and family to another app just because it looks nicer.

**But the probability of indirect erosion is moderate (30%).** The pathway:
1. AI agents become the primary gateway for personal assistance
2. Users' "intents" (finding a restaurant, booking tickets, seeking advice) are fulfilled directly through AI, no longer passing through WeChat mini-programs
3. WeChat is reduced to a "pure messaging tool," losing its ecosystem-platform premium
4. Analogy: QQ did not "die" in the PC era, but it was marginalized in the mobile era

### 7.3 Will AI Change Socializing?

**Short term (1-3 years): no.** The core need of socializing is building relationships with "real people," which AI currently cannot replace.

**Medium term (3-7 years): edge-case scenarios will be eroded.** Companion-type AI (like Character.AI-style products) may pull away part of young users' social time. But this is more "entertainment consumption" than a "replacement for socializing."

**Long term (7-15 years): extremely high uncertainty.** If AGI (artificial general intelligence) is achieved, humanity's mode of socializing could fundamentally change. But this assumption lies outside the circle of competence of any investment analysis.

> Li Lu: "The impact of AI will be all-encompassing, but the specifics are still hard to pin down right now." For variables that cannot be predicted, a value investor's strategy is to demand a larger margin of safety.

### 7.4 Does Tencent Possess "Evergreen" Genes?

**Evidence supporting "evergreen" status:**

| Evidence | Explanation |
|------|------|
| The "self-revolution" from QQ to WeChat | A globally rare platform-level self-disruption, proving organizational learning ability |
| The evolving investment ecosystem | From "doing everything" → "invest + partner" → "retrench + select," showing extremely strong strategic flexibility |
| 28 years of management stability | Core team average tenure of 20+ years, among the strongest of any global tech company |
| Continuously rising gross margin | From 43% in 2022 → 56% in 2025, indicating competitiveness is strengthening rather than declining |
| Continuously rising R&D investment | R&D of 85.75 billion yuan in 2025, 11.4% of revenue, a record high |

**Evidence against "evergreen" status:**

| Evidence | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Half a step slow on AI | Yuanbao ranked 18th, suggesting the organization's "innovation DNA" may be waning |
| A calcifying WeChat ecosystem | WeChat's product iteration is becoming increasingly conservative — the "super app" is turning into a "bloated app" |
| Talent attrition | While Tencent poaches AI talent from ByteDance, it is also being poached from in the opposite direction |
| China's population peak | The domestic user ceiling has been reached (1.385 billion MAU is nearly equal to China's entire mobile-internet population) |
| Founder's divided attention | Pony Ma's reduced public appearances may imply declining day-to-day engagement |

### 7.5 Tencent's Evolutionary Ability Through the Lens of "Compounding Knowledge"

> Li Lu: "Knowledge accumulates bit by bit. When you use the right method to do the right things, you find that the accumulation of knowledge and the growth of the economy are the same — both grow through compounding."

Tencent's "knowledge compounding" is reflected in:

1. **The compounding of social understanding:** from QQ → WeChat → WeCom → Video Accounts, Tencent's understanding of "connecting people" has continued to deepen. This accumulated knowledge does not reset to zero because one new product fails.

2. **The compounding of game industrialization:** from licensing → co-operation → self-development → going overseas, Tencent's gaming industrialization system has accumulated over 20 years and now stands at a top global level. Overseas gaming revenue breaking USD 10 billion for the first time in 2025 was no accident.

3. **The compounding of investment judgment:** the industry insight accumulated from 800+ investments is Martin Lau's team's single greatest information advantage.

4. **But knowledge accumulation in AI is severely insufficient:** in the large-model era, Tencent lacks the foundational technical accumulation that Google (DeepMind), Meta (LLaMA), and ByteDance (Doubao) possess. Is doubling investment in 2026 enough? That remains questionable.

**Long-term durability rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)** — WeChat's moat is unlikely to collapse within 10 years, but a transition from "growth stock" to "value stock" may already be underway.

## IX. Li Lu's Ultimate Questions

> "The logic of value investing is very simple — simple enough that almost anyone can understand it. But the number of people who can truly practice what they know may be less than 5%."

If Li Lu were assessing Tencent, he would ask:

1. **"Do you really understand this company?"** Tencent's business complexity is the highest among Chinese internet companies. Gaming, social, advertising, finance, cloud, AI... can you form an independent judgment on the competitive landscape and future trend of every single business?

2. **"Is the margin of safety big enough?"** At a current PE of 18x against a 10-year median of 27x, it looks cheap. But if profit growth slows from 17% to 8%, is this PE still cheap? Is a DCF margin of safety of 26% sufficient for a company facing AI uncertainty?

3. **"Can you actually hold on?"** Tencent has plunged 40-60% multiple times in its history (2018, 2021-2022). If you sell during a crash, every "long-term hold" analysis becomes meaningless.

4. **"Is there a better alternative?"** Given the same capital, would deploying it into a company you understand more deeply, with higher certainty and a larger margin of safety, be a better choice?

> Li Lu: "You don't need many good ideas. If you can find ten good ideas in a lifetime, that's enough to make you very wealthy."

**Final conclusion:** Tencent's management quality is five-star, but the external risks facing an investment in Tencent (regulation, geopolitics, AI competition) bring the overall risk rating down to 3.8/5. For value investors who can withstand volatility, understand the Chinese market, and have an investment horizon of 5+ years, the current price offers a reasonable margin of safety. But it must be acknowledged: this is not a "buy with your eyes closed" investment — it requires continuous tracking of the AI competitive landscape, regulatory developments, and management succession progress.
