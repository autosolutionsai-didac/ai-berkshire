# In the AI Era, Is Tencent Winning or Losing?

> *Understanding Tencent* series · Episode 05 · AI
> Reading time: about 11 minutes

---

## A set of numbers worrying the market

In April 2026, Tencent's share price fell from HK$522 to HK$467, down 10.47% in 14 days. The loudest narrative behind this was:

**Tencent is losing to ByteDance in AI.**

The core comparison underpinning this narrative is this data set:

| AI app | MAU | Source |
|---------|-----|------|
| Doubao (ByteDance) | **345 million** | QuestMobile + multiple public disclosures from ByteDance |
| **Yuanbao (Tencent)** | **57.35 million** | Disclosed in Tencent's 2025 annual report |

Doubao's MAU is roughly **6x** Yuanbao's. If the AI era comes down to an "AI assistant MAU war," Tencent really has lost badly.

> **Why only these two are listed**: third-party estimates of MAU for AI assistants such as Ernie Bot, Tongyi Qianwen, and Kimi vary enormously (a 2-3x spread between sources at the same point in time), and the methodologies differ across companies (standalone app / web / embedded / cumulative registrations). This section uses only the two most credible data points as a narrative anchor. The other AI assistants sit somewhere between "tens of millions and hundreds of millions" and **generally lead Yuanbao** — there is industry consensus on this point, though this piece does not attempt to estimate exact figures.

But is that the only lens through which to view the AI era?

This piece sets out to answer: **what exactly has Tencent lost, what has it won, what has it missed, and what has it gained?** This is not a simple "bullish vs. bearish" call — it is a story of divergence.

## I. AI's impact on Tencent's businesses: sharp divergence

Tencent is not a single "AI concept stock." It has a dozen-plus business lines, and AI's impact on each is completely different.

Ranking these businesses by "degree of AI impact":

| Business segment | AI impact | Rating | Key driver |
|---------|---------|------|---------|

| Online advertising | **Substantially strengthened** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Miaosi AIGC penetration at 65%; AIM+ cuts manual operations by 80% |
| Weixin Search | **Revolutionary strengthening** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Daily searches up from 333 million to 1 billion+; AI search MAU at 160 million |
| China Literature | **Substantially strengthened** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Nearly a thousand AI comic-drama titles; IP fanning out across multiple formats |
| Domestic gaming | Strengthened | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Hunyuan game visuals, AI NPCs, anti-cheat data moat |
| Overseas gaming | Strengthened | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Industrial-grade AI pipelines at Supercell/Riot/Epic |

> **Scoring methodology**: the star rating (out of 5) is a subjective assessment of each business's "structural benefit intensity" from AI, based on three dimensions — (1) whether AI can generate incremental revenue or cost savings; (2) whether Tencent's existing endowments in that business (data, entry points, relationship chains, etc.) can be reused; (3) competitive positioning versus peers. **This is a subjective score based on public information and business structure, not a precise forecast — it is meant only as a rough gauge of magnitude.**

**This is the real picture of AI's impact on Tencent — not a simple "win/lose," but a divergence across 15 business segments.**

## II. Underappreciated winner #1: advertising

Tencent's advertising revenue reached 145 billion yuan in 2025, **up 19% year over year** — a top-tier growth rate among Chinese internet companies.

AI is what's driving this growth:

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| 2025 ad revenue | **145 billion yuan (+19%)** |
| Estimated 2025 Weixin Channels ad revenue | About 37.3 billion yuan (+48%) |
| Miaosi AIGC penetration among advertisers | **65%** |
| Average daily creative assets generated by Miaosi | 3 million |
| Share of WeChat Store ads originating from Miaosi | **47%** |
| Weixin Channels monetization rate | 1.8% (vs. Douyin's 6.1%) |

Here's the story behind these numbers:

**1. Miaosi — Tencent's in-house AIGC tool**

Miaosi is Tencent Advertising's AIGC platform, which automatically generates creative images, videos, and copy based on an advertiser's keywords. In just two years, **65% of Tencent advertisers are using it**. That means:

- Advertisers' creative production costs have plunged
- Advertising ROAS (return on ad spend) has risen sharply
- Advertisers are willing to allocate more budget to Tencent
- Tencent's advertising eCPM has increased

**2. AIM+ — Tencent Advertising's agent**

AIM+ is an AI ad-buying assistant for advertisers that **cuts manual operations by 80%** — where dozens of parameters once had to be adjusted by hand, AI now adjusts them automatically.

**3. Weixin Channels still has 3-4x monetization headroom (an order-of-magnitude judgment, not a promise)**

Weixin Channels' monetization rate is roughly 1.5-2.5% (midpoint ~1.8%) versus Douyin's roughly 6-8% (midpoint ~6.5%) — a purely mathematical gap of about 3-4x. That gap is a fact, but whether it can be realized depends on how mature the e-commerce closed loop becomes — see the discussion of disclosure-methodology changes in Episode 02 on the moat.

**Advertising is the segment where AI dividends are being realized fastest and with the highest degree of certainty.**

## III. Underappreciated winner #2: Weixin Search

The 2026 numbers for Weixin Search are startling (sources: Tencent public events + Pengpai News reporting, third-party estimation methodology):

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Average daily searches (early 2024) | 333 million |
| Average daily searches (early 2026) | **1 billion+ (up roughly 200% year over year)** |
| AI search MAU (early-2026 basis) | **about 160 million** |

What does this mean?

**Weixin Search's daily search volume has already surpassed Baidu.** That's because, after integrating AI, Weixin Search evolved from "finding articles" into "finding answers + finding services + finding products."

Why does Weixin Search have such a strong AI advantage? Because it has **eight years of accumulated, arguably the highest-quality Chinese-language corpus on the internet — Official Accounts.**

What's distinctive about Official Accounts?
- **The largest pool of long-form Chinese content on the internet** — the single most important material for AI training and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- **Real-name verified authors** — an order of magnitude more trustworthy than the open internet
- **Quality-first** — readers are willing to pay for, follow, and share this content
- **Full scene coverage** — from medicine, law, and finance to daily life, relationships, and education

Doubao, Ernie Bot, and Kimi all lack this. **Whatever they can crawl from the open web is far lower quality than eight years' worth of accumulated WeChat Official Account content.**

This is why Tencent doesn't need to build "China's ChatGPT" to secure a place in the AI era — **it holds a corpus no one else has.**

## IV. A severely underappreciated winner #3: China Literature

China Literature (56.32% owned by Tencent) is a severely underappreciated hidden winner of the AI era.

Why?

China Literature has tens of millions of authors and millions of web-novel IPs. Under the traditional model, extracting value from an IP goes: web novel → comic adaptation → animation adaptation → TV adaptation. Each step requires huge amounts of manpower and time, and the conversion rate is low.

**In the AI era, IP value extraction can fan out across multiple formats at once.**

| China Literature AI initiative | Data |
|--------------|------|
| AI comic dramas (AI-generated short animated series) | **nearly 1,000 titles** |
| 2025 AI comic drama revenue | **topped 100 million yuan** |
| Cost of converting a web novel into an AI comic drama | 1/10 of the traditional method |
| Conversion time | 1/20 of the traditional method |

China Literature is the only Tencent-affiliated content asset **whose moat is clearly being deepened by AI** — because its "raw material" (web-novel IP) is nearly inexhaustible.

## V. A genuine loser #1: Tencent Cloud

There's no glossing over it: Tencent Cloud has **structurally fallen behind** in the AI era.

| Dimension | Alibaba Cloud | ByteDance Volcano Engine | Huawei Cloud | Tencent Cloud |
|------|--------|---------|--------|--------|
| General cloud IaaS market share (2025Q2) | 26.8% | — | — | **7.9% (5th)** |
| AI cloud market share (2025H1) | 35.8% | 14.8% | 13.1% | **7.0% (4th)** |
| MaaS market share (2025H1) | 27% | 49.2% | — | **outside the top 5** |

Tencent Cloud finally achieved scaled profitability in 2025 — that's good news. But the bad news is:

**Cloud's "second AI growth curve" has been claimed by ByteDance's Volcano Engine and Alibaba Cloud.**

Why?

1. **Tencent Cloud started late** — founded in 2014, five years behind Alibaba Cloud
2. **Lacking an e-commerce use case** — Alibaba has Taobao, ByteDance has Douyin; their cloud businesses have internal demand driving them
3. **Weak enterprise sales culture** — Tencent has a consumer-facing culture, and its B2B sales organization has been slow to build
4. **Hunyuan hasn't produced a killer app** — Tencent has lost ground at the MaaS layer

Tencent Cloud is unlikely to turn things around within five years — that needs to be honestly acknowledged.

But there's an important **reframing** to keep in mind:

> **Don't value Tencent Cloud as a standalone cloud company. Think of it as "self-use + ecosystem" — infrastructure supporting the WeChat ecosystem, global game distribution, and internal AI enablement.**

Under this framework, Tencent Cloud's "market-share ranking" isn't the key metric. **What matters is whether it can serve WeChat Store, Weixin Channels, Hunyuan, and Enterprise WeChat well.**

## VI. A genuine loser #2: Tencent Music

Tencent Music Entertainment (TME) has a moat that looks unbreakable: **its licensing deals with the three major record labels** (Universal, Sony, Warner).

But AI is quietly diluting that moat.

| AI music generation data | Data |
|----------------|------|
| Suno's daily AI song output (2025 third-party estimate, order of magnitude) | **millions of tracks per day** (estimates from different sources range widely, from hundreds of thousands to millions) |
| Share of new tracks added to Deezer (European music platform) in April that were AI-generated or partly AI-generated (company disclosure) | **about 44%** |
| AI music buyout price | roughly **1/10** the price of a track by a human artist |

What does this mean?

**The scarcity of music copyright is being diluted by AI.** If users don't care whether a song was "created by a real person," music platforms become less dependent on rights holders, and their bargaining power declines.

Tencent Music will still make money, but the "width" of this moat is slowly being eroded by AI.

## VII. The key question: Yuanbao lost — can Tencent still win?

Back to the number we opened with: Doubao's 345 million MAU versus Yuanbao's 57.35 million. Will this gap reverse in the future?

**Realistic assessment: based on observed traffic-referral patterns and team execution pace, a reversal looks difficult, though it can't be ruled out.**

Doubao's lead is commonly attributed to three factors (industry retrospectives, not all rigorously attributed):
- ByteDance's traffic advantage (referrals from Douyin and Jinri Toutiao)
- The execution strength of ByteDance's AI team
- Switching costs once user habits have formed

Paths by which Tencent could claw back ground include deep integration with the WeChat ecosystem and agent-ification plus RAG built on Official Account corpus — but the pace at which these paths might pay off is something to observe, not something predictable.

**But does this really matter as much as it seems?**

Let's ask a more fundamental question: **what does the AI-assistant MAU race actually determine?**

If you believe it determines "the entry point to the AI era" — then it matters a great deal.
If you believe it's "just another search-engine rivalry" — then it doesn't matter nearly as much.

Tencent management's actual choice has been the latter — inferable from its capex structure, product portfolio, and executives' public statements. This strategy has been summarized externally as **"AI in All"**:

> **Not AI for AI's sake, but AI folded into every dish already on the menu.**

In practice, this means:
- **Not joining the "arms race"** — Tencent's 2026 AI capex of 110-130 billion yuan (vs. ByteDance's 200 billion and Alibaba's 120 billion)
- **Pragmatically integrating DeepSeek** — Yuanbao runs directly on DeepSeek-R1 rather than fighting it out with Hunyuan
- **Treating Hunyuan as a strategic reserve** — not chasing #1 in MaaS, but making sure it stays in the game
- **Focusing AI on advertising, gaming, and the WeChat ecosystem** — the arenas where Tencent can actually win

Is this strategy right? It depends on your yardstick:
- If you believe "the winner of the AI era is the technology leader" — Tencent isn't doing enough.
- If you believe "the winner of the AI era is whoever monetizes best" — Tencent is doing the right thing.

> **The counterargument has to be stated plainly**: Doubao's 345 million MAU isn't an isolated number. ByteDance is building an e-commerce closed loop around Doubao (Oceangine + Douyin e-commerce + AI product selection/generation/customer service), ad-buying tools (Jimeng / Ocean Engine), and agent workflows. If these closed loops come together, **MAU converts into an entry point for — and fulfillment rights over — the agent economy**, at which point the "AI in All" strategy could be proven wrong. This is the single biggest downside risk to Tencent's path, and it shouldn't be waved away just because one is inclined to believe "AI isn't only about MAU."

## VIII. Working backward: will the AI agent economy bypass WeChat in 5-10 years?

The most worrying long-term risk is this question.

**Scenario**: in the future, users no longer open apps — they tell an AI assistant "order me some takeout." The assistant finds the mini program itself, places the order itself, and pays itself — **all without ever going through WeChat.**

If this scenario materializes, WeChat's value as an entry point gets bypassed.

**Is this a real threat? Yes.**
**Will it happen? Based on the four counterarguments below, a subjective estimate puts the probability as low (on the order of 10-15%, not a precise calculation).**

Why is the probability not high?

### 1. An AI assistant has to pay for things — it can't bypass the payment rails

China's payments market: WeChat Pay at 59.7% + Alipay at 36.2% = 96%. **Any AI assistant will ultimately have to go through one of these two channels.**

### 2. An AI assistant has to reach services — it can't bypass the merchant ecosystem

China has 4.3 million+ mini programs living inside WeChat — **the "fulfillment interface" merchants have already built.**

### 3. An AI assistant has to use social relationships — it can't bypass the WeChat relationship graph

"Send my mom a message for me" — how would AI do that? It still has to go through WeChat.

### 4. The AI assistant itself may simply be a new form of WeChat

WeChat's Yuanbao, WeChat's Doubao integration, WeChat's intelligent assistant — **the form of the entry point may change, but its carrier is very likely to still be WeChat.**

Flip these four points around, and the conclusion is:

> **When AGI arrives, WeChat won't be bypassed — it will be needed even more deeply. AGI can only run "on top of WeChat," not "around it."**

This is the strongest rebuttal to the bear case on Tencent and AI.

## IX. A five-year view: five possible scenarios

Pulling together everything above, Tencent's AI-era outlook over the next five years can be roughly grouped into five scenarios. **This section only lists the scenarios and their trigger conditions — it does not attempt probability-weighted calculations**, because assigning a probability to each scenario is a purely subjective judgment with no objective basis for "proving" one framework is correct; forcing out an "expected return of +X%" would give readers a false sense of precision.

| Scenario | Key trigger condition | 5-year share-price direction |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **A. AI-empowered growth** | WeChat becomes a super-agent entry point + Weixin Channels catches up to Douyin + Tencent Cloud's AI revenue booms | Sharply up |
| **B. Moderately positive (base case)** | AI delivers 10-15% efficiency gains and incremental revenue; gaming/advertising gross margins improve moderately | Moderately up |
| **C. AI-neutral** | Capex rises but returns fall short of expectations | Flat to slightly up |
| **D. Losing the AI race** | ByteDance's Doubao erodes WeChat + agents bypass Tencent's ecosystem | Moderately down |
| **E. AGI disruption** | AGI arrives in 2028-2030 | Extreme outcomes at either end (depending on who controls the entry point) |

Readers should estimate the probability of each scenario themselves and calculate their own expected value — that is the useful exercise. This piece won't do that on the reader's behalf.

But there is one **structural conclusion** worth remembering: given Tencent's current asset quality (1.4 billion WeChat users + 14 evergreen games + a 1-trillion-yuan investment portfolio), scenarios A, B, and C would not produce significant downside. Only the two "disruptive" scenarios, D and E, carry genuine downside risk — and D and E require not a small tweak to the probabilities but changes at the level of "the rules of the game being overturned." **Judging whether that kind of change occurs matters far more than judging its probability.**

## XI. A perspective on "restraint"

Distilling Tencent's AI path down, the most accurate summary is really just one word: **restraint.**

- Not joining the "arms race" — capex of 110-130 billion yuan, well below ByteDance's
- Not fighting it out with Hunyuan — Yuanbao connects directly to DeepSeek
- Not forcing its way to #1 in MaaS — keeping a card in reserve without burning cash to get there
- Putting all its AI effort into arenas it can actually win: the WeChat ecosystem, advertising, gaming, e-commerce

This restraint isn't a lack of ambition — it's **an acknowledgment of its own endowments.**

What are Tencent's endowments? 1.4 billion WeChat users, 14 evergreen games, the world's largest game publisher, China's deepest payment scenarios, and eight years of accumulated Official Account corpus. **WeChat and QQ are themselves world-class consumer products — Tencent has no shortage of consumer-product capability.**

What is Tencent not good at? **Building a standalone app from scratch, outside the WeChat/QQ ecosystem, to go head-to-head with an established market leader** (such as trying to make Yuanbao independently beat Doubao); and competing on B2B sales in public cloud.

**Good strategy isn't "win everything" — it's "know which race you can win."** In other words, know the boundaries of your circle of competence.

Tencent's biggest asset in the AI era **is not the Hunyuan foundation model, but the "already-built entry point + fulfillment network" formed by 1.4 billion WeChat users.** Once general-purpose foundation models become commoditized, what's genuinely scarce is the combination of "distribution + payments + fulfillment" — and that combination belongs uniquely to WeChat.

## Key takeaways from this piece

1. **AI's impact on Tencent isn't a simple win/lose — it's a sharp divergence across 15 business segments.**
2. **Winners**: advertising (Miaosi AIGC penetration at 65%), Weixin Search (daily searches over 1 billion), China Literature (AI comic dramas topping 100 million yuan in revenue), gaming (AI cutting costs and boosting efficiency).
3. **Losers**: Tencent Cloud (structurally falling behind), Tencent Music (copyright being diluted), Tencent Video (losing share to short dramas), Yuanbao (losing to Doubao).
4. **Tencent's AI strategy is "AI in All"** — not AI for AI's sake, but applying AI where it can actually win.
5. **AGI won't bypass WeChat — AGI actually needs WeChat** — payments, entry points, fulfillment, and the relationship graph all live within WeChat.
6. **The five-year view lists five scenarios** (A: empowerment / B: moderately positive / C: neutral / D: losing the race / E: AGI disruption) — this piece does not attempt a probability-weighted calculation, leaving that estimate to the reader.
