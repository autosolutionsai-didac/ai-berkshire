## I. What Is an "Evergreen Game"? Tencent Has 14

The biggest myth in the gaming industry is that games are a hit-driven business — a title is popular for a year or two, then fades.

Tencent runs a different kind of business: **evergreen games** — titles that stay popular for more than 10 years and reliably generate RMB 4+ billion in revenue every year.

Tencent's 2025 annual report formally confirmed 14 evergreen games (the bar: for mobile titles, annual revenue ≥ RMB 4 billion and average quarterly DAU ≥ 5 million; for PC titles, DAU ≥ 2 million).

The industry's general belief that "games are a hit-driven business" stems from the fact that most game companies have only one or two evergreen titles — NetEase has its Fantasy Westward Journey series and Onmyoji, miHoYo has Genshin Impact and Honkai: Star Rail — while **Tencent has 14**.

That order-of-magnitude gap means Tencent's gaming business isn't a single-hit story — it's a **multi-engine, compounding cash machine**.

## II. Honor of Kings: Why Is It Still Setting New Highs After 11 Years?

The "counterintuitive champion" of China's gaming industry is Honor of Kings:

| Metric | 2025 Data |
|------|------------|
| Launch year | 2015 |
| Domestic DAU | **139 million** (a world record for any game) |
| Global MAU | **260 million** (highest of any game worldwide) |
| 2025 global revenue | **$2+ billion** (approx. RMB 14 billion, per Sensor Tower) |
| 2025 performance | Topped the global mobile game revenue chart in **8 out of 12 months** |

Eleven years in, and DAU is still setting records. That's an almost counterintuitive phenomenon in the gaming industry. There are three reasons:

### 1. It's No Longer Just a Game — It's a Social Relationship

Honor of Kings has embedded itself in the "let's squad up" vocabulary of college dorms, office WeChat groups, and family chats across the country — it functions as a **social activity**. Once a game becomes a social activity, retention is locked in.

### 2. The KPL Pro League = A Professional Sports Ecosystem

The KPL (King Pro League) has run more than 10 seasons, cultivating several generations of players, coaches, casters, and fan communities. **Esports extends a game's lifecycle by 10-plus years.**

For reference: League of Legends launched in 2009 and is now 17 years old; World of Warcraft launched in 2004 and is still running 21 years later. **Esports and tournament ecosystems tend to significantly extend the lifecycle of PVP games.**

### 3. Skin Pricing Power — the Core of the Money Machine

Honor of Kings' skin pricing ladder runs from ordinary Epic skins (in the RMB 88-168 range) all the way up to Legendary-tier skins (a full set with Star Essence accessories runs about RMB 800-1,000), and limited-edition skins sell out every time they return.

Value investing has a hard-and-fast test for a good business — **"if you raise the price and customers don't leave, that's pricing power."** Honor of Kings' skin ladder climbing year after year, with limited editions selling out every time they return, is the most direct proof of that standard.

## IV. Breaking Out Overseas — From "Top Tier Globally" to a 7.7% Starting Point

**Let's first correct a commonly repeated claim**: Tencent is widely called the "world's largest game publisher," but that ranking comes from Newzoo consolidating Riot at 100%, Supercell at 84%, and Epic's 31.5% equity stake all into Tencent's figures. If you instead look at each company's own disclosed gaming-segment revenue — Sony's gaming segment was about $31.7 billion in fiscal 2025, Microsoft Gaming (including Activision Blizzard) was about $25 billion, and Tencent's total gaming revenue was about $34.5 billion — the three companies trade the lead within a **$25-35 billion range**, so "top tier globally" is the more solid description.

Tencent's overseas gaming revenue was **RMB 77.4 billion in 2025 (crossing $10 billion for the first time)**, up 33% year over year. But the revenue figure itself isn't the point — what matters is what share that represents against the global overseas market as the denominator, and where that share could go in five years. This section answers that.

First, a look at the portfolio of assets — Tencent's overseas "ammunition depot":

| Company | Stake | Flagship Titles | Strategic Position |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| Riot Games | **100%** | League of Legends, Valorant, Teamfight Tactics | Wholly owned subsidiary, valued at approx. $23 billion |
| Supercell | **~84%** | Clash of Clans, Clash Royale, Brawl Stars | Equity-method affiliate, valued at approx. $15 billion |
| Epic Games | **~31.5%** | Fortnite, Unreal Engine | Equity-method affiliate, valued at approx. $22.5 billion (per Disney's 2024 investment) |
| Krafton | **13.5%** | PUBG | Financial investment; Korea-listed, market cap approx. $8.9 billion |
| Funcom / Techland / Embark | Controlling or large stakes | Dune: Awakening, Dying Light, The Finals | In-house premium (buy-to-play) titles |

What makes this portfolio special?

**Tencent hasn't just "made an overseas game" — it has rounded up several of the top IPs in the entire global gaming industry.**

- League of Legends (PC + mobile) — one of the biggest esports IPs in the world;
- Valorant — the number one PC shooter community globally;
- Clash of Clans — 13 years evergreen;
- Fortnite — 650 million cumulative active accounts, $20+ billion in cumulative revenue;
- PUBG — the originator of the global battle royale genre.

And nearly all of these companies were acquired or invested in by Tencent **between 2011 and 2018** — a roughly **15-year "global game IP land grab."**

Take the most impressive example: **Tencent acquired Riot Games step by step over 7 years, in three stages, for a cumulative investment of about $700 million** — a small initial stake in 2008, a controlling stake (about 92.78%) added in 2011, and the remaining shares acquired for about $440 million in 2015 to reach 100% ownership. By 2026, Riot is valued at about $23 billion — **a cumulative investment of about $700 million against a current stake worth $23 billion, roughly a 33x return over a span of about 18 years.**

(Note: The commonly cited claim of "$400 million turning into $23 billion, a 58x return" refers only to the valuation implied by the 2011 controlling-stake deal, not Tencent's cumulative investment. This piece consistently discloses figures on a cumulative-investment basis, to avoid overstating management's capital-allocation track record.)

Note: Newzoo's China figure of $49.8 billion is broadly consistent in magnitude with CNG (Gamma Data)'s RMB 350.8 billion (≈$50.1 billion) — the former is simply the dollar-converted figure. Sources: Newzoo Global Games Market Report 2025; CNG (Gamma Data), "2025 China Gaming Industry Report."

**The numerator** — Tencent's 2025 financial results:

| Dimension | Tencent Revenue | Market Denominator | Implied Share |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Domestic | RMB 164.2 billion | RMB 350.8 billion | **~47%** |
| Overseas | $10.7 billion (≈RMB 77.4 billion) | $139 billion | **~7.7%** |
| Global (incl. China) | $34.5 billion (≈RMB 241.6 billion) | $188.8 billion | **~18%** |

Three numbers worth noting:

- **Domestic share at 47% is already near the ceiling** — gaining even one more percentage point will be difficult;
- **The overseas 7.7% is where Tencent's real 5-10 year growth headroom lies** — this is the number that should be the starting point for the Tencent gaming story, not "world's number one";
- **2024 overseas share is estimated at about 6% (back-calculated from the +33% growth rate), rising 1.5 percentage points in a single year in 2025** — a rare one-year gain in the history of Tencent's overseas share.

### 4.2 Where Could Tencent's Overseas Share Go in Five Years?

Separate "how the market grows" from "how Tencent grows," then combine them:

| Year | Overseas Market (scenario) | Tencent Overseas Revenue (scenario) | Implied Share |
|------|----------------|------------------|---------|
| 2024 actual | ~$134 billion | $8.0 billion (back-calculated from +33%) | ~6% |
| 2025 actual | $139 billion | $10.7 billion | **~7.7%** |
| 2030 neutral case | ~$170 billion | ~$28.5 billion (≈RMB 200 billion) | **~16-17%** |
| 2030 conservative case | ~$170 billion | ~$20 billion (≈RMB 140 billion) | ~12% |

The core assumption behind the neutral case: Tencent's overseas share doubles over five years. For reference, Sony and Microsoft's global console-era share has long held steady in the 15-25% range — **this target is equivalent to "Tencent, via a PC + mobile + cross-platform path, reaching a global share on par with the console giants" — aggressive, but not far-fetched.**

But **the 2025 growth rate of +33% absolutely cannot be extrapolated linearly**: growing the base from $8.0 billion to $10.7 billion is a different proposition from growing it from $10.7 billion to $28.5 billion — natural decay in existing titles becomes more pronounced, and new games are needed to keep the baton moving.

### 4.3 What Would Drive That +10pp Share Gain? Four Paths

**Path 1: Unlocking value from existing assets (highest certainty)**

- **Supercell** — Brawl Stars surged back to its historical peak in 2024 (annual revenue estimated at about $3 billion), the new title *Mo.co* launched globally in 2025, and *Squad Busters* continues to operate. **Supercell's recovery is the single biggest contributor to 2025's overseas +33% growth.**
- **The Riot lineup** — League of Legends: Wild Rift's penetration outside China, Europe, and the US hasn't peaked yet; Valorant's console version, launched in August 2024, has entered a growth ramp; Teamfight Tactics continues to deliver steady overseas output.
- **PUBG Mobile** — 8 years evergreen, generating a double benefit for Tencent via both direct operations and its 13.5% Krafton stake; growth is steady but off a large base.
- **Epic (31.5% equity-method stake)** — Fortnite and the UEFN creator economy flow into Tencent's investment income in proportion to its stake, **not directly into revenue** (something the market easily overlooks).

**Path 2: Self-developed titles going overseas (highest uncertainty, but also the biggest upside)**

- **Delta Force's** global version launched in April, with registrations topping 10 million within 4 days — but retention and revenue curves through Q2-Q3 2026 need to be watched; registration numbers are not the same as long-term paying users.
- **Honor of Kings (overseas version)** — launched in Brazil and the Middle East in 2024, cracking the local top 10 in revenue in the Middle East in its first month, though still weak in Europe and the US. This is a key test case for whether a Chinese MOBA can truly succeed overseas.
- **DnF Mobile overseas** — repeatedly topped Korea's bestseller chart after launch, with China, Korea, and other overseas regions expected to take turns driving growth from 2025 to 2027.
- **Honor of Kings: World (Project Centaurus)** — an open-world version, announced at Gamescom 2025, expected to launch in 2026-2027.
- Risk: Chinese high-DAU developers have historically had a low hit rate going overseas (Honor of Kings overseas has been relaunched multiple times, and Game for Peace's overseas role was taken over by PUBG Mobile) — this is the strongest counter-evidence against this path.

**Path 3: The premium (buy-to-play) studio pipeline (an underrated dark horse)**

Over the past five years, Tencent has taken controlling stakes in a string of mid-size European and American studios, aiming to fill the gap in AAA buy-to-play titles:

| Studio | Stake | Main Pipeline | Launch Timing |
|--------|------|---------|---------|
| Funcom | Controlling stake | *Dune: Awakening* (Dune IP) | Launched June 2025 |
| Embark | Controlling stake | *The Finals* / *ARC Raiders* | Launched / 2025 |
| Techland | Controlling stake | *Dying Light: The Beast* | 2025 |
| Sharkmob / Klei / Don't Nod | Minority stakes | Multiple titles | Ongoing |

The advantage of buy-to-play is that a single title is more easily accepted by mainstream overseas players; the weakness is a low hit rate, and these titles don't compound into evergreens. **Currently they still contribute only a single-digit percentage of Tencent's overseas revenue** — whether that can break through to 10%+ is a key thing to watch in 2026-2028.

**Path 4: Capability upgrades + regional rebalancing**

- **AI localization** — multilingual translation, voice-over, and cultural adaptation compress what used to be a 6-12 month overseas localization process into 1-3 months. This directly accelerates the expansion of PUBG Mobile, Delta Force, and Honor of Kings into second-tier markets in Latin America, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia.
- **Regional structure** — current overseas revenue relies on North America (Riot / Supercell / Epic), Korea (DnF), and global (PUBG). **Weak spots**: Japan (almost no presence outside IP tie-ins), Europe (weak own brands). **Blue oceans**: the Middle East and Latin America — the two fastest-growing regions per Newzoo, where Tencent has already stepped up investment significantly in 2024-2025.
- **The window for large-scale M&A has essentially closed** — regulators blocked Tencent from increasing its stake in Ubisoft in 2018, and Microsoft's acquisition of Activision Blizzard went through multi-country scrutiny in 2022; both precedents make it nearly impossible for Tencent to pull off "another Riot-sized" acquisition. **That means future gains in overseas share will have to come mainly from operations, not capital** — a fundamental shift in Tencent's overseas story.

### 4.4 Counterarguments: The Odds the Overseas Story Doesn't Work

To avoid a preset conclusion, the counterarguments that could invalidate the scenario above must be laid out clearly:

1. **Self-developed titles have historically had a low hit rate overseas** — Honor of Kings overseas has been relaunched multiple times, and after launching in Brazil and the Middle East, downloads grew but revenue stayed weak. If Delta Force's global version and Honor of Kings: World both underperform, the contribution from self-developed overseas titles becomes an empty shell, and the +10pp share gain in the 2030 scenario has nothing to stand on.
2. **Supercell has already returned to its historical peak** — whether 2026-2027 brings another hit or a pullback from these highs, following Brawl Stars' surge, is a key variable for the overall overseas picture. Supercell's historical hit cadence runs about one every 3-4 years, and the next dry spell may not be avoidable.
3. **US-China tail risk** — Riot (100%), Epic (31.5%), and Krafton (13.5%) could all be forced into divestiture under an extreme US-China decoupling scenario. If that happened, Tencent's overseas share would fall back to **4-5% overnight**. This is a real but low-probability tail risk.
4. **AI overseas capability isn't exclusive** — miHoYo, NetEase, ByteDance, and Lilith are also using AI to accelerate their overseas expansion; Tencent's localization advantage isn't a structural monopoly.
5. **Overseas publishing rights reverting** — the overseas publishing rights for titles like PUBG Mobile stem from partnerships (Tencent + Krafton), and those contracts could go unrenewed or see higher fees when they expire.

## V. Studio Horse-Racing — The Core of Tencent's Organizational Capability

Tencent's gaming strategy is often joked about as "making everything, racing everything." That's actually its most core organizational capability.

### Domestic: The Four Major Studio Groups

| Studio | Head | Flagship Titles | Style/Positioning |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| **TiMi** | Yao Xiaoguang | Honor of Kings, League of Legends: Wild Rift, Honor of Kings: World | MOBA, major IP titles |
| **LightSpeed & Quantum (Photon)** | Chen Yu | Game for Peace, Delta Force, PUBG Mobile | FPS, shooters |
| **Aurora** | Yu Haipeng | Moonlight Blade, QQ Speed | MMO, casual |
| **Morefun** | Zhang Hanjin | Naruto Mobile, Xiyou Kingdom | IP adaptations, anime-style |

### Overseas: Autonomy for Controlled Studios

Riot Games (Los Angeles), Supercell (Helsinki), Epic Games (North Carolina) — **Tencent's approach is to provide resources without interfering.**

Portfolio companies consistently describe Tencent as "the best strategic shareholder in the gaming industry."

### Why Does the Horse-Racing Mechanism Matter?

The four major studios often greenlight competing projects in the same genre (such as FPS), and whichever version wins in the market prevails. Delta Force vs. Valorant: Source Energy Operation is a product of this horse-racing.

The cost of horse-racing is that some projects are bound to fail (sunk costs).
The benefit of horse-racing is that Tencent almost never misses a mainstream genre.

ByteDance's Nuverse gaming business has been in continuous strategic retreat since 2024, cutting multiple product lines (the industry has offered various explanations, including game-license supply, the hit rate of self-developed titles, and organizational restructuring — this piece does not attribute it to any single cause).

## VI. Why Does the Market Undervalue Tencent Gaming?

With the facts above established, back to the question: **by how much is Tencent Gaming undervalued?**

Using a SOTP (sum-of-the-parts) framework for a rough back-calculation (read the notes on methodology carefully):

- Tencent's current total market cap is approximately **RMB 3.76 trillion** (as of 2026-05-04);
- After subtracting the roughly **RMB 1.04 trillion** fair value of its external investment portfolio, the implied valuation of the operating business is about **RMB 2.7 trillion**;
- The gaming business contributed **RMB 241.6 billion in revenue in 2025, accounting for 32% of total revenue** (per financial reports);
- Tencent does not disclose profit by segment separately; sell-side estimates put gaming's contribution to the group's operating profit in the **45-55% range** (for order-of-magnitude reference only);
- Working backward from this, **Tencent's gaming business accounts for roughly RMB 1.2-1.5 trillion of market cap** — but it must be stressed: this is an "implied" figure under SOTP assumptions, not a standalone market price.

A side-by-side look at leading gaming companies (note: most of these are also mixed-business companies, so the comparison is only a rough reference, not a strict price-to-sales multiple):

| Company | 2025 Gaming Revenue | Market Cap (approx.) | Market Cap / Gaming Revenue |
|------|--------------|-----------|---------------|
| **Tencent (implied gaming portion)** | **RMB 241.6 billion** | ~RMB 1.3 trillion (implied) | **~5.4x** |
| NetEase | ~RMB 85 billion | ~RMB 500 billion | ~5.9x |
| Nintendo | ~RMB 58 billion (converted) | ~RMB 580 billion | **~10x** |
| EA | ~RMB 57 billion | ~RMB 300 billion | ~5.3x |
| Take-Two | ~RMB 40 billion | ~RMB 230 billion | ~5.8x |

Tencent Gaming's implied multiple sits on the lower end among global gaming leaders, which doesn't fully match the quality of its asset base — "world's largest revenue scale + a 14-title evergreen matrix + an AI option + accelerating overseas growth." Whether that gap represents a premium or a discount is left for the reader to judge.

There are three common explanations for the market's low pricing of the gaming business:

### 1. The Regulatory Shadow Hasn't Fully Lifted

Although **1,771 game licenses** were issued in 2025 (a 5-year high) and minors' share of gaming time is only **0.2%**, showing a substantive easing of the regulatory environment, market memory moves slowly — the two game-license episodes in 2018 and 2021 saddled the gaming industry with a "regulatory discount" for a while. That discount is still being slowly worked off.

### 2. The US-China Black Swan

Tencent's stakes — Riot at 100%, Epic at 31.5%, Krafton at 13.5% — are all in companies registered or listed in the US/Korea. In an extreme US-China decoupling scenario, these assets could be forced into a sale. This is a real but low-probability tail risk.

### 3. Uncertainty Around AI Investment

Tencent's AI capital expenditure was **RMB 79.2 billion in 2025 (+57% year over year)**, with plans to double again in 2026. In the short term this weighs on profit; in the long term it's the biggest variable in empowering the gaming business.

## VII. What AI Brings to the Gaming Business

The most underrated point: gaming could be one of AI's biggest application scenarios — and Tencent happens to be the largest gaming company.

### Content Production: Significant Room for Cost Reduction (Exact Magnitude Still to Be Seen)

- **Hunyuan 3D Studio**: at the Tencent Hunyuan 3D 1.0 launch event, Tencent disclosed that efficiency improved about **100x** for a specific 3D asset-generation workflow (note: this is a single data point, not evidence that the entire development pipeline speeds up 100x; Tencent has not disclosed a unified figure for the actual improvement in development cycle time)
- **AI level design**: automatically generating levels, storylines, and dialogue
- **AI localization**: automatic multilingual translation, accelerating overseas expansion
- **Possible effect**: compressing development cycles from 4-5 years to 2-3 years — a direction broadly shared across the industry, though Tencent hasn't given unified quantitative guidance

### Gameplay: Upgrading the Experience

- **AI NPCs**: NPC behavior tailored to each player (Honor of Kings' "Lingbao" is already live)
- **AI teammates**: filling in the match experience during off-peak hours (Game for Peace already uses DeepSeek)
- **AI coaching**: dynamically adjusting difficulty based on player skill level

### UGC Ecosystem: Disruptive

- Yuanmeng Star + Oasis Genesis (Game for Peace's UGC mode)
- Players use AI tools to create their own levels and gameplay
- **Goal**: replicate the Roblox / Fortnite Creative model
- Once it takes off, UGC content-creation costs approach zero, **extending evergreen lifespans by another 10 years**

Gaming is the segment most certain to show returns within the "AI in All" strategy — because Tencent has the most players, the most gameplay formats, the most studios, and the most IP.

## VIII. What Will Tencent Gaming Look Like in Five Years?

Putting all the threads together, here is a scenario for Tencent Gaming five years out (note: the following is a scenario projection based on current trends, not a forecast, and certainly not a promise; uncertainty over a 5-year horizon in the gaming industry is extremely high):

| Year | Domestic (RMB bn) | Overseas (RMB bn) | Total (RMB bn) | Overseas Share of Global |
|------|------------|------------|-------------|---------------|
| 2025 (actual) | 164.2 | 77.4 | 241.6 | ~7.7% |

The domestic business is settling into slow, steady-state growth (mature-market comparisons include NetEase and Activision); overseas is the core source of incremental growth, but phenomenon-level products like PUBG Mobile have already passed their fastest growth phase, and new overseas increments will come more from the pacing at which the Riot lineup (Valorant, League of Legends: Wild Rift) and buy-to-play studios (Funcom, Embark) deliver — **the 5-year compound growth rate will decline as the base gets larger, and the 2025 +33% should not be extrapolated linearly.**

## Key Takeaways

1. **14 evergreen games together form the stable base of RMB 241.6 billion in gaming revenue** — this density of an evergreen matrix is unmatched in China's gaming industry.
2. **Honor of Kings' DAU is setting records 11 years in** — driven by its social nature, esports ecosystem, and skin pricing power.
3. **Overseas revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, yet overseas market share is only 7.7%** — the Riot, Supercell, and Epic global IPs, built up over 15 years, are entering their payoff phase; the 2030 neutral-scenario share reaches 16-17%, driven by four paths — unlocking existing assets, self-developed titles going overseas, the buy-to-play pipeline, and capability/regional upgrades — with the largest M&A window now closed.
4. **The horse-racing mechanism** is the core of Tencent Gaming's organizational capability; the relative retreat of competitors like ByteDance's Nuverse in self-developed games serves as a reverse reference point for observing this organizational edge (the specific attribution is left to the reader).
5. **Tencent Gaming's implied market-cap-to-revenue multiple sits on the low end among global gaming leaders** — whether that reflects a missing premium or a fair valuation is something to judge for yourself, weighing the AI option, overseas acceleration, and regulatory tail risk.
6. **Gaming revenue has room to roughly double in five years, but the growth curve will taper** — the neutral scenario is around RMB 400-500 billion, and this is not a promise.
