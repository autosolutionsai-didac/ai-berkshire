## Three Failed Wars to Replace WeChat

If you remember only one thing, remember this:

**Over the past 10 years, the three Chinese internet companies with the strongest execution all tried to build "the next WeChat." All three failed.**

- **August 2018**, ByteDance launched Duoshan. This was ByteDance's product managers' highest-spec answer in their attempt at close-friends social networking — Douyin traffic funneling + red envelopes + video chat + a rush to give friends free RMB 0.88 red envelopes — backed by RMB 10 billion in promotional spending. Peak monthly active users reached 13 million before it was quietly discontinued in 2021.
- **2013**, Alibaba launched Laiwang. Jack Ma personally rallied employees, declaring: "Any employee who doesn't add 100 external friends on Laiwang is not a good employee." Billions of yuan were poured in, the app was eventually renamed Diandianchong, and after that no one remembered it.
- **2011**, NetEase teamed up with China Telecom to launch Yixin. It leveraged the carrier's free-SMS advantage. Three years later, it disappeared without a trace.

Alibaba had payments, ByteDance had algorithms, NetEase had carrier resources — together, the three covered essentially every advantage you could imagine for building a social network. **All three lost to the same thing: the relationship graph that already existed inside WeChat.**

This is the essence of Tencent's moat.

---

## The Relationship Graph: The "Digital Asset" You Can't Migrate

Why can't the relationship graph be replicated? Because it isn't a product — it's a **digital asset an individual has spent ten years accumulating**.

Open your own WeChat and run a thought experiment: take stock of what you've stored inside it.

- **400 to 2,000 contacts** in your address book
- **30 to 200 group chats** you've joined
- **Dozens to hundreds of official accounts** you follow
- **Mini programs, merchants, and articles** you've bookmarked
- **Chat logs, files, and photos** spanning years
- **Bank cards, social security, medical insurance, and health codes** you've linked
- **Thousands of WeChat Pay transactions** you've made

None of this **can be migrated with a single click**, and almost none of it **can be bought with money**. To switch to a new social app, you'd need everyone above to switch with you — that's not a product problem, it's a **collective action problem**.

Economics has a concept called the **network effect**: the more nodes, the greater the network's value. WeChat's value isn't simply the sum of its 1.4 billion users — it's the **total sum of the actual interpersonal connections among those 1.4 billion people**.

Third-party estimates of WeChat's average friend count vary considerably, with a common range of 200 to 400 people. Even using a conservative estimate of 250 people, the total number of relationship pairs that WeChat carries comes out to roughly:

> 1.4 billion × 250 ÷ 2 ≈ **175 billion pairs**

For anyone else to build a "new WeChat," they'd need **every one of these hundreds of billions of relationships to be re-established from scratch** — and each pair only counts once "both sides have migrated over."

ByteDance didn't manage it. Alibaba didn't manage it. NetEase didn't manage it. Will the next challenger?

## The Complete List: 7 Assaults Over 15 Years

The opening covered three attempts, but the fuller list of failures is even longer — it starts a month before WeChat itself was even born.

| Year | Challenger | Product | Final Fate |
|------|--------|------|---------|
| 2010 | Xiaomi | MiTalk (Miliao) | Launched a month before WeChat, initially hotter, then overtaken and declined once WeChat leveraged QQ's traffic funnel |
| 2011 | NetEase + China Telecom | Yixin | Ceased operations |
| 2013 | Alibaba | Laiwang | Renamed repeatedly, eventually vanished |
| 2018 | ByteDance | Duoshan | Discontinued in 2021 |
| 2018 | Luo Yonghao | Bullet Messenger / Chat Bao | Shut down after burning through its funding |
| 2019 | Wang Xin | Matong (Toilet MT) | Banned immediately upon launch |
| 2019 | Zhang Yiming | Feiliao | Ceased operations within a year |

15 years, 7 major assaults, **all of them failures**.

The MiTalk story is especially illustrative — it launched a month before WeChat and **briefly led in early monthly active users**. But MiTalk had no pre-existing relationship graph to build on, while WeChat could directly import the relationship network of hundreds of millions of people straight from QQ, **overtaking MiTalk within three months**. This case has since been cited repeatedly as reverse evidence of how relationship-graph portability determines the fate of standalone social apps.

If there is a business that the smartest product managers in all of China, backed by the largest pools of capital in all of China, have repeatedly tried and failed to crack over 15 years — that business has a name: **a moat**.

## The Flywheel of the WeChat Ecosystem: How Many Buildings Have Been Built on One Plot of Land

If the relationship graph is the foundation, how many buildings has WeChat already built on top of it?

```
Social networking (1.4 billion MAU)
    ↓ Relationship graph accumulates
WeChat Pay (>90% penetration)
    ↓ Transaction data accumulates
Mini Programs (800 million+ DAU, 949 million MAU, 4.3 million+ mini programs)
    ↓ Closed-loop services
Channels (600 million+ DAU)
    ↓ Content retention + ad monetization
Official Accounts (the largest high-quality Chinese-language corpus on the internet, built up over 8 years)
    ↓ AI search + Agent entry point
Search / Sou Yi Sou (1 billion+ daily searches)
    ↓ Find content → find services → find products
WeChat Stores (2024 GMV +192%; 2025 disclosure shifted to structural metrics)
    ↓ E-commerce closed loop
    → Back to social sharing, payment, mini programs again... the flywheel accelerates
```

With every building added, **users are locked in more tightly, and revenue per user rises**.

Consider a comparison to understand just how "super" WeChat's super-app status really is:

| Dimension | WeChat | Douyin | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| MAU | **1.402 billion** | ~700 million | WeChat covers nearly every smartphone user in China |
| Time spent per user | ~80-100 minutes/day | ~120-150 minutes/day | Douyin leads |
| Usage frequency | **Dozens of times per day** (fragmented) | Longer per session | WeChat is a "utility"-type product |
| Payment scenarios | **Full coverage, online and offline** | In-app only | WeChat Pay penetration >90% |
| Commercial ecosystem | **4.3 million+ mini programs** | Fewer than 500,000 Douyin mini programs | WeChat's maturity is 5-10x greater |
| Relationship graph | **Strong (acquaintances)** | Weak (interest-based recommendations) | This is a qualitative difference |

Douyin has indeed captured users' **time**, but it has not captured their **identity** — on Douyin, a user is a "viewer" who leaves once done watching; on WeChat, a user is a "resident" who lives there.

The commercial value of these two types of relationships is worlds apart.

## Channels: Offense or Defense?

Next, let's challenge a piece of conventional wisdom.

The mainstream market narrative holds that **Channels is Tencent's biggest offensive growth engine, positioned to rival Douyin**.

But reasoning from the product's actual form, a more sensible interpretation may be that **Channels is, in essence, more of a defensive product, and should not be valued as "the second Douyin."**

There are three reasons:

**1. Its algorithm lags Douyin's by 1.5-2 years.** This is an open secret. Channels' recommendation system started later, and both its recommendation precision and retention rate trail Douyin's.

**2. Its content ecosystem relies on the relationship graph rather than the algorithm** — this is both WeChat's strength and its ceiling.
- Strength: high trust, strong conversion rates (many brand advertisers favor Channels).
- Ceiling: its content pool is smaller than that of public, algorithm-driven ecosystems.

**3. Channels' greatest value is "defending WeChat's usage time against erosion by Douyin"** — not "becoming the next Douyin."

The two perspectives imply a substantial valuation gap:

| Perspective | Valuation Approach | Reasonable Valuation for Channels |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Mainstream consensus | Benchmarked against Douyin's trillion-yuan e-commerce GMV | RMB 500 billion to 1 trillion |
| Defensive perspective | Defensive product + moderate monetization | RMB 300-500 billion |

**The point isn't which valuation is correct — the point is that even under the more conservative view, Channels is still a cash machine not to be underestimated.**

Let's look at the specific data:

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Channels DAU | **600 million+** (third-party estimate) | The 2025 annual report did not disclose a specific figure separately; public data moved from 490 million in December 2023, to an inferred 550 million in 2024, to 600 million+ in 2025-2026 |
| 2024 ad revenue growth | +60% | Cailian Press |
| 2025 estimated ad revenue | Approximately RMB 37.3 billion (+48%) | Third-party estimate |
| Monetization rate (ad revenue / user time spent) | **Approximately 1.5-2.5%** (median ~1.8%) | Third-party estimates including CBNData |
| Douyin monetization rate for reference | **Approximately 6-8%** (median ~6.5%) | Same as above |
| Magnitude gap | **Approximately 3-4x** (range-based, not a single point) | — |
| WeChat Stores 2024 GMV growth | **+192%** | WeChat Open Class |
| WeChat Stores 2025 disclosure basis | **Tencent shifted to structural metrics**: brand-driven GMV growth reaching 4.3x the platform average, monthly active merchants +1.7x, GPM +1.5x | WeChat Open Class / 2025 annual report |

> **Note**: Monetization rate is an unofficial metric, and estimates vary considerably across third-party research (approximately 1.5-2.5% for Channels, approximately 6-8% for Douyin); the figures in the table are for order-of-magnitude reference only.

Monetization rate converging from a median of ~1.8% toward the 4-5% range is a **potential path** — if realized, Tencent's ad revenue would have roughly another full doubling of upside. But this is a "path," not a "promise": Douyin's median level was earned through a complete e-commerce closed loop plus a high-precision recommendation algorithm, and whether Channels can complete this path requires ongoing observation of three specific metrics: **absolute GMV scale, brand-driven GPM growth, and monthly active merchant growth** — these are verifiable anchors that Tencent's financial reports or the WeChat Open Class will disclose.

**The change in WeChat Stores' disclosure basis deserves a note of its own**: the +192% GMV growth for WeChat Stores in 2024 was a highly eye-catching number, but starting in 2025 Tencent **stopped disclosing total year-over-year GMV growth**, shifting instead to structural metrics such as "brand-driven GMV growth / monthly active merchants / GPM." This change in disclosure strategy has prompted various interpretations within the investment community (such as growth normalizing off a larger base, or avoiding simplistic comparisons with Douyin's e-commerce business), and Tencent has not given an official explanation for the specific motive — **that judgment is left to the reader**. Regardless of the reason, the fact remains: **the "total scale" figure for Channels' e-commerce will be harder to obtain directly going forward, requiring inference from a patchwork of structural metrics and sell-side estimates** — which is itself a signal that valuation difficulty is rising.

## Five Years From Now, Ten Years From Now: Will the Moat Still Be There?

Before answering this question, consider a counterexample first.

Facebook (Meta) was considered an "irreplaceable social media hegemon" in the 2010s, yet by the 2020s young people flocked overwhelmingly to TikTok and Instagram. The Facebook flagship brand's presence in young people's minds fell off a cliff.

So will WeChat follow in Facebook's footsteps?

#### Five-Year Horizon (2031)

| Moat | Status in 5 Years | Disruption Risk (Qualitative Assessment) |
|--------|----------|---------------------|
| WeChat social networking | **Stronger** — MAU could reach 1.5 billion, ecosystem more closed | Very low |
| Gaming | **Flat to slightly stronger** — overseas revenue may overtake domestic | Low |
| Payments | **Flat** — duopoly maintained once regulation stabilizes | Low |
| Advertising | **Stronger** — AI-driven + Channels inventory release | Low |
| Cloud / enterprise services | **Uncertain** — depends on the payoff from AI investment | Medium |

> **Note**: "Disruption risk" in the table is a subjective, qualitative assessment based on business structure, moat type, and historical data — it is not a probability figure.

**Within 5 years, the probability of WeChat's social moat being disrupted is close to zero** — at least under the existing interaction paradigm.

#### Ten-Year Horizon (2036)

The only variable truly worth worrying about is **whether AI agents change how people interact with the internet**.

Consider this: if in the future users no longer open apps but instead tell an AI assistant "order me some takeout," WeChat's gateway value gets bypassed.

This threat is real, but its magnitude is overstated, for two reasons:

**1. WeChat itself can become the AI gateway.** Yuanbao is already embedded in the WeChat ecosystem, and Sou Yi Sou's 1 billion+ average daily searches represent an enormous advantage in both corpus and user habit.

**2. The relationship graph cannot be replaced by AI.** If an AI assistant sends a message to your mother on your behalf, your mother still needs to see "what you said" — and the network that message flows through is still WeChat.

In other words: **socializing is a basic human need**, and WeChat solves the problem of connecting people to people. Unless an entirely new hardware form factor emerges that makes the phone disappear, a social network of this magnitude will not be replaced by a new app.

But there is **one genuine risk** worth flagging in advance:

**WeChat could shift from a "super app" to "infrastructure" — still used by everyone, but with limited room for growth, and its valuation multiple could re-rate downward.**

This resembles Facebook's fate: **the social moat remains deep, but the market narrative has shifted from social networking to AI/the metaverse**. Tencent may likewise face this medium-to-long-term risk of "gradually being re-rated by the market from a growth stock to a value stock."

But note: **a downward re-rating of the valuation multiple does not equal the company getting worse**. A company whose P/E falls from 30x to 15x while its EPS still grows 10% a year can still deliver long-term shareholders a solid return. That's the math Episode 08 will work through.

## A Reverse Thought Experiment

Let's close with a reverse deduction:

**Suppose AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) really does arrive — will WeChat disappear?**

Asking it the other way around may be more illuminating — once AGI arrives, **will WeChat actually be bypassed, or will it be relied upon even more deeply**?

AGI **can only run "on top of WeChat," it cannot "bypass WeChat"** — because:

1. For an AI assistant to send a message, it must send it somewhere the recipient can actually receive it (= WeChat);
2. For an AI assistant to make a payment, it must connect to a payment channel (= WeChat Pay at 60%, Alipay at 36%);
3. For an AI assistant to reach services, it must plug into the mini-program ecosystem (= 4.3 million merchants live here);
4. For an AI assistant to know user preferences, it must know where the user is (= 1.4 billion people are inside WeChat).

The stronger AGI becomes, the deeper its dependence on gateways and fulfillment — and gateways and fulfillment are **precisely what WeChat uniquely owns**.

If this reasoning holds, **AGI won't kill WeChat — it will instead make WeChat more valuable**.

But it's important to honestly flag the **boundary conditions** of this logic — it assumes AI agents still run on top of the "existing app + smartphone" ecosystem as their underlying layer. If a genuinely AI-native interaction paradigm emerges, this chain could be bypassed. At least three potential paths can be identified:

- **New hardware replacing the phone**: AI glasses, AI pins, brain-computer interfaces, and the like could make the "open the app" action disappear, restructuring gateway logic entirely;
- **OS-level agents + universal payment protocols**: an operating-system-level AI assistant calling merchant APIs directly, combined with stablecoins, central bank digital currencies, or more open payment protocols, could bypass the WeChat Pay/Alipay duopoly;
- **Cross-app relationship protocols**: driven by interoperability regulation of the kind already seen, relationship graphs could be forcibly opened up, or partially migrated to new protocols such as end-to-end encrypted, decentralized instant messaging.

The probability of these three paths materializing over the next 10 years is not high (a combined order of magnitude of roughly 10-15%), but they **do genuinely exist**. This is the most distant, and most worth watching, tail risk to WeChat's moat — and it's why "AGI makes WeChat more valuable" cannot be treated as an unconditional conclusion.

## Key Takeaways From This Episode

1. **The relationship graph is a digital asset that cannot be migrated** — the essence of WeChat's moat.
2. **All 7 challenges over 15 years failed** (MiTalk, Yixin, Laiwang, Duoshan, Bullet Messenger, Matong, Feiliao) — moat depth validated with real money.
3. **The WeChat ecosystem flywheel now spans 7 layers**: social networking → payments → mini programs → Channels → official accounts → search → WeChat Stores.
4. **Channels is primarily defensive, secondarily offensive** — a 3-4x monetization improvement is a visible path, but realizing it requires the e-commerce closed loop to mature.
5. **The moat looks nearly worry-free over 5 years**; the real variable over 10 years is AI agents — under the base-case scenario, "AGI makes WeChat more valuable," but watch for the three tail-risk paths of new hardware, OS-level agents, and interoperability regulation.
