## A Question That Will Make You Rethink Tencent

If someone told you:

> "I run a company that's sitting on **RMB 1,015.8 billion** of equity assets, with a portfolio that includes Pinduoduo, ByteDance, Spotify, Sea Limited, Universal Music, Epic Games, WeBank, Kuaishou, Supercell..."

Would you assume this is Buffett's Berkshire?

It's not. These are the notes to Tencent's financial statements.

On a look-through economic-interest basis, **Tencent's external investment portfolio is worth roughly RMB 1.04 trillion** (about **US$148.8 billion**). That's equivalent to a standalone investment portfolio larger than most Chinese fund management companies.

But here's the odd part: when you buy Tencent stock, almost no one reminds you that "you're buying this trillion-yuan asset at a discount." The market typically applies roughly a 30% discount when valuing this portion of the assets (a common sell-side SOTP assumption, not an official figure — Section 7 of this piece breaks down five reasons why).

Why? This piece is dedicated to unpacking exactly that.

> **Important premise (to avoid double-counting)**: Tencent's equity holdings fall into two categories:
> - **Consolidated subsidiaries** (Riot Games at 100%, Miniclip, China Literature at 56%, WeSure, etc.) — their revenue/profit is already consolidated into Tencent's core-business financial statements, and **is already reflected in the P/E multiple**. It cannot be added on again separately.
> - **Unconsolidated external investments** (financial investments + associates) — this is the true scope of the RMB 1.04 trillion "portfolio."
>
> The "portfolio" discussed in this piece refers specifically to **the second category**. The value of consolidated subsidiaries like Riot was already covered in Part 03 (the gaming piece) and will not be recalculated here.

But one thing needs to be said upfront: **ByteDance is a private company, and Tencent's exact stake has never been disclosed through any public channel**. Every number in this section is an **estimated range** inferred from historical financing rounds, not a precise figure. What we can be confident about is the "order of magnitude," not the "exact number."

Whether ByteDance's ultimate valuation is US$400 billion or US$600 billion, **Tencent's stake cannot possibly be on the order of US$50 billion**.

Why does this clarification matter? Because it bears directly on **your core understanding of Tencent's valuation** — using the mistaken "US$50 billion" figure would overstate Tencent's portfolio valuation by roughly RMB 300.0-400.0 billion.

## V. Different Accounting Treatments — Same Category, Wildly Different Impact

Something many people don't realize: **different investments on Tencent's books are subject to different accounting treatments, and the impact on the income statement varies enormously.**

| Category | Holdings | Does Profit/Loss Flow Through Tencent's Income Statement | Form |
|------|------|----------------------|------|
| **Fully consolidated** (subsidiaries) | Riot, Miniclip, Funcom, Sharkmob, China Literature, WeSure | ✅ **Yes** — 100% of revenue/profit/loss is consolidated | Control >50% |
| **Associates** (equity method) | Epic, Supercell, WeBank, Kuaishou, Krafton (under some classifications) | ⚠️ **Partially** — only recorded via a single "share of associates' profit/loss" line | Significant influence, 20-50% |
| **Financial assets, FVOCI** | Krafton, Kakao Games, some long-term holdings | ❌ **Does not flow through the income statement** — fair-value changes hit equity only | Long-term strategic holding |
| **Financial assets, FVTPL** | PDD, Sea, Spotify, UMG, ByteDance, Snap, Bilibili, Meituan | ⚠️ **Flows through the income statement, but reflects market-price swings** — not operating profit | Financial investment, <20% |

### A Simplified Formula for Tencent's Net Profit

```
Tencent GAAP Operating Profit (already includes all consolidated subsidiaries: Riot / China Literature / Miniclip / WeSure, etc.)
   ├─ Domestic Games + International Games (including all profit from consolidated subsidiaries such as Riot/Miniclip)
   ├─ Marketing Services (Advertising)
   ├─ FBS (Fintech and Business Services)
   └─ Value-Added Services (social subscriptions, video memberships, China Literature 56% consolidation, etc.)
   = About RMB 241.56 billion in 2025

+ Net interest income (interest income - interest expense)
+ Fair-value changes on investments (FVTPL holdings such as PDD/Sea/Spotify/ByteDance/AI startups) ⭐
+ Share of associates' profit/loss (Epic/Supercell/WeBank/Kuaishou, etc., by equity-method share)
+ Net gains on disposal/deemed disposal of investments
- Impairment losses (goodwill, long-term investments, etc.)
- Income tax expense
- Non-controlling interests (the portion, e.g., China Literature's 44%, attributable to non-controlling shareholders)
═══════════════════════════════════════════
= Tencent GAAP Net Profit Attributable to Shareholders = About RMB 224.84 billion in 2025
```

⭐ **This line item (fair-value changes on investments) is the core source of the gap between Tencent's GAAP net profit and its Non-IFRS net profit.**

**Watch out for a common mistake**: the "operating profit" line above already includes the full profit of **all consolidated subsidiaries** such as Riot, China Literature, and Miniclip — do not add a separate line below for "consolidated-subsidiary profit," or you'll be double-counting. This is exactly the accounting reason this piece keeps stressing that "Riot is already consolidated and not part of the portfolio figure."

For example: if PDD's share price rose +50% in a given year, Tencent's booked Pinduoduo equity of about US$20.0 billion × 50% ≈ US$10.0 billion (roughly RMB 72.0 billion) of fair-value change would flow into GAAP net profit. The reverse is equally true — in 2024, PDD actually fell about 33% ($146→$97), which dragged Tencent's GAAP profit down by several tens of billions of yuan. But whichever way it moves, this is **paper wealth / paper loss**, not operating cash flow.

**So to assess Tencent's core business, you need to strip out the investment noise in GAAP net profit.** Which metric to use depends on how rigorous you want to be — there are two levels:

| Rigor | Metric | 2025 Figure | Meaning |
|--------|------|----------|------|
| Cleanest | **GAAP operating profit** | **RMB 241.56 billion** | At the "operating" level, fair-value changes on investments are naturally excluded; share-based compensation (SBC) and intangible-asset amortization remain in |
| Market convention | **Non-IFRS net profit** | **RMB 259.63 billion** | Starts from GAAP net profit, adds back share-based compensation, amortization, and impairments, then strips out investment items |

> **The "P/E of 14.5x" and "core-business P/E of 11x" figures cited elsewhere in this series both use the Non-IFRS net profit basis — that's market convention, not an absolute truth. A stricter value investor would deduct **share-based compensation (SBC = Share-Based Compensation, the stock the company grants employees, roughly RMB 28.0-30.0 billion a year)** back out of Non-IFRS profit, bringing "true core-business profit" down to about RMB 230.0 billion — even lower than GAAP operating profit, because GAAP operating profit doesn't include the pro-rata share attributable to associates/joint ventures.**

Tencent's 2025 GAAP net profit was RMB 224.8 billion, versus Non-IFRS net profit of RMB 259.6 billion — a gap of **RMB 34.8 billion**. That gap nets out primarily from items such as **share-based compensation +RMB 28.0 billion, intangible-asset amortization +RMB 6.0-8.0 billion, impairment provisions +RMB 10.0-18.0 billion, and fair-value changes on investments -RMB 5.0 to -15.0 billion** — the exact figure for each line should be taken from the annual-report notes.

## VII. Why the Market Applies a 30% Discount to the Portfolio

Back to the core question: Tencent has RMB 1 trillion of equity assets on its books — why does the market discount them by 30%?

Five reasons:

### 1. Valuations Are Inherently Opaque

The true value of the unlisted portion (Riot/Epic/Supercell/ByteDance/WeBank, etc.) is hard to pin down precisely — private-market valuations often carry liquidation preferences and earn-out clauses, and the gap between book value and realizable value can run 30-50%. The market tends to "apply a discount just to be safe."

### 2. A Stake Is Not the Same as Cash

Tencent's roughly 13.76% Pinduoduo stake is a passive financial investment — **it can't just be sold at will**; a large disposal would hit the share price. Riot, a 100%-owned subsidiary, **could theoretically be spun off, but in practice is simply part of Tencent's core business and won't be sold separately**.

### 3. Uncertainty Around Dividend-Style Disposals

Tencent has made two major dividend-style disposals historically: JD.com in 2021 and Meituan in 2022. While this kind of in-specie distribution is shareholder-friendly, shareholders passively accept whatever timing and price result — and **the market dislikes this kind of uncertainty**.

### 4. Tail Risk from US-China Relations

Holdings such as Riot (US), Epic (US), and Krafton (South Korea) could face forced disposal under an extreme US-China decoupling scenario. **This is a real but low-probability tail risk** — yet the market still prices in a discount for it.

### 5. A Mismatch in Valuation Logic

The most important one. **Tencent's core business is valued on a P/E basis, while the portfolio is valued on an NAV basis — there is no standard model for combining the two.** Sell-side analysts' DCF models rarely fold the portfolio's full NAV in — so the portfolio's hidden value is frequently overlooked.

## IX. A Simple Method for Estimating "the Discount the Portfolio Implies for the Core Business"

Pulling all of this together, here's a simplified estimate (read the notes on methodology carefully):

```
Tencent's current total market cap: about RMB 3.76 trillion (as of 2026-05-04, roughly US$520.0 billion)

Subtract:
- Fair value of the investment portfolio (counted at 70% of book value, i.e. assuming a 30% discount):
  US$148.8bn × 70% ≈ US$104.0 billion ≈ RMB 750.0 billion
- Net cash of RMB 107.1 billion ≈ US$15.0 billion
- Combined hard-asset cushion: about US$119.0 billion (roughly RMB 860.0 billion)

= Implied market cap of the core business: RMB 3.76 trillion - RMB 0.86 trillion ≈ RMB 2.90 trillion

Core business 2025 Non-IFRS net profit: about RMB 259.6 billion (from Tencent's 2025 annual report;
Non-IFRS already excludes fair-value changes on investments)
→ Implied core-business P/E: about 11x
```

> **Methodology reminder**: the "core-business P/E of about 11x" above is a "pure core-business implied valuation" obtained by counting the RMB 1 trillion portfolio at 70% of book value plus net cash, and then stripping both out. The "full-market-cap P/E of 14.5x" used elsewhere in this series is simply total market cap ÷ Non-IFRS EPS, with no assets stripped out. The two are not mathematically contradictory — they're different slices of the same company: the former is closer to "pure core business," the latter closer to "the market's quoted price." **The assumed discount (30% / 50% / 70%) will materially change the calculated core-business P/E — adjust it to match your own degree of conservatism.**

> **A note on cross-company comparisons**: directly comparing "core-business P/E of 11x" against "global internet leaders' P/E of 25-30x" is **an unfair mismatch of methodology** — other companies also hold cash and investment portfolios, and their published P/E ratios likewise haven't stripped those assets out. For a fair comparison, either use Tencent's **full-market-cap P/E of 14.5x** against other companies' full-market-cap P/E, or apply the same "strip out cash + portfolio" adjustment to every company. This section does not attempt that cross-company comparison, to avoid false precision.

**Conclusion**: if you believe Tencent's portfolio can recover at least 70% of book value, the **implied core-business P/E under the SOTP framework is about 11x** — a cheap-looking implied multiple for a company with a 56% gross margin, an extremely deep moat, and Non-IFRS net profit up 17% year over year. If you assume a more aggressive haircut for the portfolio (say, worth only 50% or even 30% of book), the implied P/E rises correspondingly to 12-13x.

But be aware: **11x is a "derived implied value" under the SOTP assumption, not the market's actual pricing.** The quote the market actually shows is 14.5x (based on full market cap); the 3.5x gap between the two reflects the market's doubts about "whether the portfolio is really worth 70% of book." Whether that doubt is justified is left to the reader to judge.

## X. A Sequence of Judgment That's Often Confused

Two opposing arguments frequently surface around "Tencent's investment portfolio":

- One camp says: "At the current price, Tencent's RMB 1 trillion portfolio is essentially thrown in for free — that's a massive margin of safety."
- The other camp says: "A 30% discount on the portfolio's valuation is the norm — you shouldn't expect it to really be worth book value."

Both camps have a point. But the more correct **sequence of judgment** is this:

**Step one: first judge whether the core business is worth buying.** The core business (the Weixin ecosystem + games + advertising + FBS) must independently pass your investment criteria — a sufficiently deep moat, sufficiently strong profits, a reasonable valuation. If the core business isn't worth buying, no matter how valuable the portfolio is, you shouldn't buy Tencent for it (you'd be better off just buying PDD or Spotify directly).

**Step two: treat the portfolio as a bonus, not the core rationale.** Once the core business clears the bar, the portfolio is "icing on the cake" — it gives you an extra margin of safety, an extra hidden pocket of upside, but **it is not the core reason to buy Tencent**.

If you reverse the order — buying Tencent purely because of the portfolio's valuation — you're most likely making a mistake:

- The portfolio's fair value moves with the market, and a decline there will drag Tencent's share price down with it
- A large disposal will hit the share price, and the timing is not something you control (see the market's reaction to the 2022 JD.com and Meituan dividend-style disposals)
- Under the tail risk of US-China relations, some overseas assets could be forced into disposal

**The conclusion is simple**: look at the core business first, the free gift second. Only when both steps pass are you actually buying Tencent — not a "grab-bag ETF" disguised as Tencent.
