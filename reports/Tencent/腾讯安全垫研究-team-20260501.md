# Tencent "What If I'm Wrong" Downside-Cushion Research Report

> **Core question**: If a Tencent purchase turns out to be a mistake, how far could it fall in the worst case? How much hard-asset and hard-cash-flow downside protection is there?
> **Research framework**: a four-role parallel research team (Buffett balance sheet + Duan Yongping cash flow + Munger SOTP + Li Lu extreme stress test)
> **Data as of**: 2026-05-01
> **Current share price**: HKD 467.80 / total market cap ≈ HKD 4.21 trillion ≈ USD 540 billion
> **Data confidence**: Grade A (4 analysts, 55 WebSearch queries combined; primary data sourced from the 2025 annual report and HKEX first-hand disclosures)

---

## 1. One-Sentence Conclusion

**At the current entry point, Tencent's "mathematically expected worst-case decline" is roughly -15% to -18% (HKD 383-398); the worst single black-swan scenario is -47% (HKD 248); an extreme joint scenario has less than a 5% probability over 5 years of reaching -57% (HKD 200-230). Over a 10-year holding period, buybacks plus dividends alone are enough to absorb a -50% paper loss. This is a name with "a fairly solid floor that will not bankrupt you overnight."**

- Long term (10 years): shareholder returns plus EPS growth **fully absorb a -50% paper loss**
- Extreme case: the hard-asset liquidation floor is HKD 103 = -78% (theoretical limit)

- Even if the share price were to halve, the annual shareholder-return yield would automatically rise from 2.7% to 5.4%, triggering management's "increase buybacks" reflex

### 3.3 The Hard-Asset Cushion

| Item | As of 2025-12-31 | USD equivalent | Share of market cap |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|
| Net cash | RMB 107.1 billion | $157B | **2.9%** |
| Investment portfolio (listed + unlisted) | RMB 1,015.8 billion | **$1,488B** | **27.6%** |
| (Net cash + investment portfolio × 70%) | RMB 818.2 billion | **$1,193B** | **22.0%** |

Per-share liquidation value: **HKD 103/share** (i.e., if the share price falls to HKD 103, "hard realizable assets × a 70% discount" alone would fully support it)

**This 22% hard floor exceeds that of the U.S. tech "Magnificent Seven" (typically under 15%)** — but it is still not sufficient to constitute a Graham-style "deep margin of safety" on its own; it must be backed by DCF support from the core business's cash flow.

**Key insight**: the investment portfolio is a hidden gold mine the market is treating with a "half-discount" — the unlisted portion (Riot / Epic / Supercell, carried at book value) is likely worth 30-50% more in reality.

## 6. The "Time Cushion" — the Most Underrated Cushion

Based on an annual shareholder return of 2.9-5% (buybacks + dividends + EPS growth, compounded):

| Holding Period | Cumulative Shareholder Return (conservative, 4%/year compounded) | Can It Offset a 30% Decline? | Can It Offset a 50% Decline? |
|---------|--------------------------|--------------|--------------|
| 5 years | 21.7% | Partially | No |
| 10 years | 48% | **Fully** | Nearly |
| 15 years | 80% | Fully | **Fully** |

**Core insight**: over a 10-year holding period, **even if the share price fell 50% the very next day after purchase**, 10 years of cumulative shareholder return plus EPS growth (a conservative 6%/year) would erase that "paper loss" by year 10.

> This is the concrete meaning of "time as a cushion" — the mathematical foundation of Li Lu's "hold for the long term" philosophy.

## 10. Precise Answers to the User's Questions

### Q: If a Tencent purchase turns out to be a mistake, what cushions exist?

**A: Four cushions, ranked by thickness**:

1. **Gold: the cash-flow cushion (thickest)**
   - FCF yield of 4.4%, annual shareholder return of 2.9-5%
   - Gross margin across all three core businesses rose in tandem to 56.4% (an all-time high)
   - Even with zero growth in the core business, 5 years of buybacks plus dividends would recover 14-28% of the market cap
   - **Core protection**: over a long holding period, time will automatically absorb any decline

2. **Silver: the SOTP discount cushion (moderate)**
   - Current market cap of $540 billion vs. a median SOTP of $660.5 billion, a discount of roughly **22%**
   - Games and FBS are systematically undervalued
   - **Core protection**: valuation recovery

3. **Bronze: the hard-asset cushion (22% of market cap)**
   - Net cash of $157B plus an investment portfolio of $1,488B
   - Better than the U.S. tech "Magnificent Seven" (typically under 15%)
   - **Core protection**: provides a floor in an extreme liquidation scenario

4. **4th: the historical PB floor**
   - Current PB of 3.36x vs. a historical low of 2.7x
   - A repeat of a 2022-level bear market implies only about 20% further downside
   - **Core protection**: valuation is no longer in bubble territory

## 11. Conclusion: Is Tencent a "Good Enough Name" or a "Perfect Name"?

Tencent is not a Graham-style "cigar butt" — hard assets are only 22% of market cap, so it must be supported by the moat of its core business.

Nor is Tencent a "buy with eyes closed" price — the SOTP discount is only about 22% at the median, far short of the 52% deep undervaluation seen in 2022.

**But Tencent is one of the rare blue chips where "the cushion is thick enough and the upside elasticity is large enough"**:
- Mathematically expected worst-case decline: -18%
- Credible worst case within 5 years: -47% (vs. Alibaba's -80% and Meta's -76%)
- A 10-year holding period can absorb a -48% paper loss
- Upside is clearly greater than defensive blue chips

> **Duan Yongping-style closing thought**: the most important thing in investing is to "think first about how bad it could get." What is the worst case for buying Tencent right now? The answer: **-18% is the likely floor, -47% is the credible floor within 5 years, -57% is the floor under an extreme joint event, and -78% is the theoretical physical floor.** Protected by these four layers of floor, and combined with an annual shareholder return of 2.9-5% and Non-IFRS growth of +18%, **this is a qualified name for "avoid losing first, then seek to win"** — not perfect, but good enough.
