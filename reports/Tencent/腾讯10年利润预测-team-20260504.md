## 1. One-sentence conclusion

**Tencent's 2036 Non-IFRS net profit is forecast at a neutral RMB 580.0 billion (vs. RMB 222.8 billion in 2025, a 10-year CAGR of 8.7%). Layering in buybacks and share cancellations that reduce share count by 16.6%, EPS CAGR rises to 12.0%. The current market cap of RMB 4 trillion is a 16% discount to neutral-case intrinsic value — a "reasonably priced but cheap" zone: it barely qualifies under Buffett's "wonderful company at a fair price" standard, but doesn't count as a big bargain under Li Lu's 30% margin-of-safety standard. The biggest variable is the return on AI capex; the biggest risk is regulation plus geopolitics.**

## 3. Deep dive on the three business segments across three scenarios

### 3.1 🎮 Gaming business (10-year profit CAGR 6.6%)

**Twin flywheels of evergreen franchises**:
- **Domestic "decade-class" lineup**: Honor of Kings (DAU 139 million / RMB 50 billion+ in gross billings), Peacekeeper Elite, Dungeon & Fighter Mobile, League of Legends: Wild Rift, Delta Force, Arena Breakout
- **Overseas "global No. 1" portfolio**: Riot Games 100% (League of Legends + Valorant + Teamfight Tactics + Arcane), Supercell 84%, Epic Games 31.5%, Krafton 13.5%, Funcom/Miniclip/Sharkmob

**Key drivers**:
- AI cost reduction (Hunyuan cuts art costs by 50%; development cycles shrink from 4-5 years to 2-3 years) → margin rises from 48% to 52%
- Overseas penetration (currently 32% vs. Sony/Nintendo's 70-80%) → still room to double
- IP extensions (Arcane Season 2, the Fortnite movie, the Honor of Kings animated series)

**Key counter-checks**:
- Probability of the gaming industry disappearing within 10 years is <5% (Nintendo: 45 years, Activision: 45 years, EA: 43 years)
- AGI is more likely to empower gaming than disrupt it (at its core, gaming is about socializing + a sense of achievement)
- It's highly likely Honor of Kings will still be thriving in another 10 years (cf. World of Warcraft's 21 years, LoL's 17 years)

### 3.2 💬 Advertising + WeChat ecosystem (10-year profit CAGR 9.4%)

**Five growth engines**:
1. **Channels (Video Accounts) advertising**: current monetization rate 1.8% vs. Douyin's 7.2% → potential 2036 revenue of RMB 200-250 billion
2. **WeChat Stores + e-commerce**: 2024 GMV +92%; if it reaches Douyin's scale within 5-10 years → RMB 3-4 trillion in GMV
3. **AIM+ / Miaosi AIGC**: 65% penetration; AI lifts eCPM by 30-50%
4. **WeChat Search + Yuanbao**: 1 billion searches per day; an option on becoming the AI entry point
5. **Overseas WeChat**: non-core; contributes <5% over 10 years

**Key calculation (ARPU ceiling)**:
- WeChat's 2025 ARPU is ~$15 vs. Meta's global $55-60
- Assuming it reaches 50% of Meta's level ($28/MAU) → 1.4 billion users × $28 = **RMB 280 billion rational ceiling**

**Duan Yongping's view**:
- **Betting on monetization efficiency**, not time spent — Channels' watch time is already 60% of Douyin's, but monetization is only 25% — this is the most certain source of alpha

### 3.3 ☁️ FBS + AI (10-year profit CAGR 9.2%)

**10-year fate of the sub-segments**:
| Sub-segment | 10-year judgment | Confidence |
|-------|---------|-------|
| WeChat Pay | Cash cow but losing upside (market share of 59.7% already exceeds Alipay's 36.2%; regulatory risk) | High |
| Tencent Cloud | Structurally falling behind (IDC market share 7.9%, ranked 5th; AI cloud 7%) | Medium-high |
| Hunyuan LLM | Comprehensively losing ground in the MaaS layer (2025 H1: Volcano Engine 49.2% / Alibaba 27% / Hunyuan outside the top five) | High |
| Yuanbao AI assistant | Likely to end up second-tier; "AI in All" is the real alpha | Medium |
| **Enterprise WeChat + Stores + Enterprise Services** | **Where the real alpha lies (private-domain + AI is something neither Alibaba nor ByteDance has)** | **High** |

**Core insight**: FBS's alpha isn't in the cloud — it's in "monetizing the WeChat ecosystem for B2B." Enterprise WeChat has 750 million DAU + 14 million enterprises + a RMB 500 billion private-domain market (CAGR 28.9%)

## 6. Reverse-Engineered DCF Valuation (Core)

### Three-scenario 2036 market cap (10-year-out PE of 15x, mature-stage valuation)

| Scenario | 2036 net profit | Core business market cap (15x) | Discounted to 2026 (10%) | vs. current RMB 4 trillion |
|------|----------|------------|--------------|----------|
| Bear | RMB 320.0B | RMB 4.8 trillion | RMB 1.85 trillion | -54% |
| **Neutral** | **RMB 580.0B** | **RMB 8.7 trillion** | **RMB 3.35 trillion** | **-16%** |
| Bull | RMB 880.0B | RMB 13.2 trillion | RMB 5.09 trillion | +27% |

**Adding in the investment portfolio (conservative 80% haircut)**:
- Neutral: RMB 8.7 trillion core business + RMB 1.45 trillion portfolio = **RMB 10.15 trillion** → discounted RMB 3.91 trillion
- Current RMB 4 trillion → **roughly flat to -2%**

### Expected-value method
**E(discounted intrinsic value) = 0.20×1.85 + 0.55×3.35 + 0.25×5.09 + investment portfolio ≈ RMB 4.03 trillion**

→ **Current market cap of RMB 4 trillion ≈ expected intrinsic value; margin of safety close to 0%**

→ But **anchored on the neutral scenario**: the current price is **16% undervalued** relative to neutral-case intrinsic value (core business) + **the investment portfolio thrown in for free**

Li Lu-style judgment: **"Reasonably priced and on the cheap side, but not a great bargain."**

## 9. Perspectives of the Four Masters

### Buffett's "See's Candies" framework
- See's, bought in 1972, has contributed $2 billion+ in dividends over 50 years
- Honor of Kings already has **all the hallmarks** of See's Candies: brand franchise + low reinvestment + pricing power
- → **Tencent's gaming business is very likely to be bigger in 10 years than it is today**

### Duan Yongping's view
- Has held Tencent for many years; the core logic is the **dual cash-flow engine of gaming + WeChat**
- An asymmetric business with "unlimited upside, floored downside"
- The current share-price range fits Duan Yongping's preference

### Li Lu's "long-term compounding"
- Tencent = a hybrid of **"China's Microsoft (a high-ROIC core business) + Berkshire Hathaway (a high-quality passive portfolio)"**
- WeChat ≈ Windows + Office (operating-system level); the investment portfolio ≈ Berkshire's holdings
- The gap: ROIC isn't as good as Microsoft's (FBS is a drag), and capital allocation isn't as good as Berkshire's (early-stage investments were too scattered)
- **Confidence: 75%**

### Munger's "invert, always invert"
- Where does Tencent Cloud stand once AGI arrives? AGI can only run "on top of WeChat" — it cannot "bypass WeChat." **WeChat is the ultimate moat**
- Antitrust impact on payments: 59.7% is already a de facto monopoly; if fee regulation is imposed, it could cut 20% off
- The "80/20 split" between Alipay and WeChat Pay: WeChat Pay's implied valuation of $80-100 billion is not reflected in Tencent's share price

## 11. Four core conclusions

### Conclusion 1: Tencent is "China's scarce dual-engine compounding machine"
The combination of core business (RMB 222.8B) + investment portfolio ($148.8B) + net cash (RMB 107.1B) is unique in China. The 10-year neutral forecast has Non-IFRS net profit rising from RMB 222.8B → **RMB 580.0B** (CAGR 8.7%), driven jointly by the core business and the portfolio. **Confidence: 70%**

### Conclusion 2: Buybacks contribute a "silent 3.3pct EPS acceleration"
Cumulative 16.6% share cancellation over 10 years lifts EPS CAGR from the total-profit CAGR of 8.7% to **12.0%**. This is what Li Lu calls "long-term shareholders passively benefiting" — retail holders passively receive a growing concentration of equity in the company. **Confidence: 80%**

### Conclusion 3: The current price is "reasonably cheap, but not a great bargain"
It trades at a 16% discount to neutral-case intrinsic value, but the weighted expectation is roughly flat. Not cheap under Li Lu's 30% margin-of-safety standard; barely qualifies under Buffett's "wonderful company at a fair price" standard. **Recommended action: build the position in tranches, don't add leverage.** **Confidence: 65%**

### Conclusion 4: The biggest variable is the return on AI capex; the biggest risk is regulation plus geopolitics
Over the next 10 years, Tencent's fate hinges not on whether gaming stays strong or advertising keeps growing, but on **whether the ~RMB 150 billion of 2026E AI capex turns out to be money well spent**. The bull case requires Hunyuan to become WeChat's AI-agent entry point; the bear case has AI turning into a pure cost. Geopolitical risk (Riot/Epic/Supercell's US assets) is a black swan. **Confidence: 60%**
