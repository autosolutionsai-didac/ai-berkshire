## II. Business Model Essence: Core Business Definition and Revenue Structure

### 2.1 The one-line definition

Tencent's core business is **"a super traffic-monetization platform built on the social relationship chain."** The social graph connecting 1.4 billion people is the foundation; gaming, advertising, payments, and content are the floors built on top of it. Once that foundation is in place, the cost of tearing it down far exceeds the cost of adding another floor.

> **Duan Yongping**: "What is a good business? It's one where you don't have to get everything right every single day — as long as you don't make a major mistake, time is on your side."

WeChat is exactly that kind of business — you don't need to invent a new WeChat every year; you just keep stacking new features onto the existing ecosystem (Mini Programs, Video Accounts, WeChat Stores), and users get locked in more tightly with each addition.

### 2.3 The secret of the profit structure

The revenue structure makes fintech look like the biggest piece at 30%, but **the profit structure tells a completely different story**:
- **Gaming**: gross margin of 60-70%, the absolute mainstay of profit
- **Advertising**: gross margin of 50-60%, extremely low marginal cost, and margins are still rising as AI drives efficiency gains
- **Fintech**: gross margin of only 30-35%, large in scale but thin in profit

**Gaming and advertising are the real moneymakers.** Fintech functions more as a way of "keeping the water within the family" — WeChat Pay locks users into spending within the ecosystem, contributing ecosystem stickiness rather than direct profit.

> **Duan Yongping**: "A business that doesn't make money isn't a good business." — But even though fintech's own margin is low, it creates the payment loop that underpins gaming and advertising, indirectly contributing to the growth of those higher-margin businesses. This is ecosystem logic.

## III. The WeChat Ecosystem Flywheel

### 3.1 How the flywheel turns

```
Social (1.4 billion MAU)
    ↓ Relationship chain accumulates
Payments (WeChat Pay penetration >90%)
    ↓ Transaction data accumulates
Mini Programs (DAU 800 million+)
    ↓ Service loop closes
Video Accounts (DAU 500 million+)
    ↓ Content retention + ad monetization
Advertising (AI-driven precision targeting)
    ↓ Merchant ROAS improves → merchants spend more
    ↓ More merchants join Mini Programs
WeChat Store (GMV growing rapidly)
    ↓ E-commerce loop closes
    → Back to social sharing, payments, Mini Programs... the flywheel accelerates
```

**Key inflection points (2024-2025)**:
1. **Video Accounts feed advertising went fully live**: ad revenue grew +60% YoY in 2024 and continued growing strongly in 2025
2. **WeChat Store consolidated and upgraded**: expanded from a "Video Accounts store" into e-commerce infrastructure for the entire WeChat ecosystem
3. **Taobao/Tmall integrated WeChat Pay**: a competitor voluntarily entering Tencent's ecosystem, in turn reinforcing WeChat Pay's usage frequency
4. **AI capability injected into the ad system**: improved ad-placement ROAS drove organic growth

### 3.2 Where is the flywheel's ceiling?

**The bullish narrative**: WeChat is the operating system for 1.4 billion people, and its ceiling is China's GDP.

**But the ceiling has to be acknowledged**:

| Ceiling dimension | Constraint | Severity |
|-----------|------|---------|
| User growth | MAU of 1.414 billion is nearing the upper limit across China and the overseas Chinese diaspora; growth has slowed to 2% | High |
| Time-spend competition | Total user time is being siphoned off by Douyin and Xiaohongshu; WeChat's share of total usage time has fallen from about 40% to about 30% | Medium-high |
| E-commerce GMV | WeChat Store GMV is roughly RMB 400-500 billion, only about 1/4 to 1/5 of Douyin's e-commerce GMV | Medium |
| Ad load rate | Moments' ad load rate is far below Douyin's feed; there's room to raise it, but user experience constrains how far | Medium-low |
| Regulation | Antitrust, data security, and minor-protection rules continue to cap the monetization ceiling | Medium |

**How would Duan Yongping see this?**

> **Duan Yongping**: "This 'ceiling' talk — people were saying QQ had peaked ten years ago too. A good company always finds a new growth point. The question isn't where the ceiling is; it's whether the company has the ability to open up new businesses as it approaches that ceiling."

WeChat's ceiling isn't about any single business line topping out — it's about **whether the whole ecosystem can keep stacking new monetization layers**. From Official Accounts to Mini Programs to Video Accounts to WeChat Store, Tencent has already proven, four times over, its ability to "build a new floor on the same plot of land."

## IV. Deep Dive on the Moat

### 4.1 Social network effects ★★★★★

**Core evidence**: WeChat's MAU stood at 1.414 billion (Q3 2025), covering nearly every smartphone user in China. This isn't a moat you can build by throwing money at it.

**Why is it irreplaceable?**
- **Extremely high migration cost for the relationship chain**: a person's WeChat friends, group chats, Official Account subscriptions, Mini Program favorites, payment habits, and chat history — the migration cost of these "digital assets" far exceeds the appeal of any single competing feature
- **Two-sided network effects**: merchants (Official Accounts, Mini Programs, WeChat Stores) depend on WeChat to reach users, and users depend on WeChat for services — a two-sided lock-in
- **Historical validation**: ByteDance's Duoshan, Alibaba's Laiwang, and NetEase's Yixin all failed at building a competing social app. Even a company with ByteDance's execution ceiling couldn't dent it

**Counter-check — what could disrupt WeChat?**

| Potential threat | Likelihood | How Duan Yongping might think about it |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| AI-native social apps (e.g., ChatGPT-style conversation replacing WeChat messaging) | Low | People's need to socialize with other people won't be replaced by AI; AI is a tool, not a relationship |
| Hardware-entry shift (AR glasses replacing phones) | Medium-low | Possible on a 10-year horizon, but WeChat would migrate to the new hardware |
| Regulatory breakup (antitrust forcibly splitting up the WeChat ecosystem) | Low | No sign of this currently; policy risk has eased substantially |
| Abandonment by the next generation of young users | Medium-low | QQ still has 530 million MAU; Tencent has the double insurance of both QQ and WeChat |

**Conclusion**: There is essentially no chance of WeChat's moat being disrupted within five years. On a 10-year horizon, the only thing worth watching is a shift in hardware form factor — but Tencent has ample resources and time to respond.

### 4.2 The dual-wheel barrier of game development and publishing ★★★★

**Development capability**:
- Both the TiMi Studio Group (Honor of Kings) and Lightspeed Studios (PUBG Mobile / Delta Force) are world-class
- In 2025, domestic gaming revenue reached RMB 164.2 billion, with 14 titles each posting annual gross billings above RMB 4 billion — an evergreen game portfolio
- Overseas revenue reached RMB 77.4 billion (+33%), passing the $10 billion milestone for the first time

**Publishing and investment barriers**:
- Controlling stake in Supercell (Brawl Stars, Clash of Clans) and Riot Games (League of Legends, VALORANT)
- Minority stakes in Epic Games (Unreal Engine) and Krafton (PUBG)
- A global publishing network covering more than 200 countries and regions

**Counter-check**:
- **Licensing-policy risk**: two separate suspensions of new game licenses, in 2018 and 2021, dealt heavy blows to the entire industry. Although policy has loosened for now, the risk of "one pen stroke" of policy change never disappears
- **Single-title dependence**: Honor of Kings and PUBG Mobile still account for the bulk of domestic gaming revenue; the new breakout hit Delta Force still needs to prove out its longevity
- **The miHoYo challenge**: Genshin Impact and the Honkai series prove that a non-Tencent studio can produce a global hit on pure product strength, eroding Tencent's channel advantage in head-to-head product competition

> **Duan Yongping**: "A good game is absolutely a good business, but an average game may not be. A good game company has a moat; most game companies don't. Tencent and NetEase are among those that do."

### 4.3 The scenario-based moat in payments/fintech ★★★★

**Sources of the moat**:
- WeChat Pay penetration exceeds 90%, covering both online and offline scenarios
- In 2024, Taobao/Tmall integrated WeChat Pay, further expanding its use cases (while WeChat has not reciprocally opened up to Alipay)
- Wealth-management products like LiCaiTong and consumer-lending products like Weilidai continue to lift ARPU

**Constraints**:
- **Regulatory ceiling**: fintech is subject to multiple layers of oversight from the central bank and banking/insurance regulators, which suppresses margins
- **Fee-rate pressure**: payment processing fees face downward pressure
- **Competition from Ant Group**: although WeChat Pay dominates the social scenario, Alipay still holds a strong position in commercial scenarios

**The low margin is a structural issue**: the fintech and business services segment brought in RMB 229.4 billion in revenue, but gross margin is only 30-35%, far below gaming and advertising. This segment's value to Tencent lies more in **ecosystem stickiness** than in **profit contribution**.

### 4.4 Cloud and enterprise services ★★★

**Current state**:
- Enterprise services revenue grew nearly 20% YoY in 2025
- The cloud business has achieved profitability at scale
- Demand for AI-related services is strong, driven by the Hunyuan large model

**Gap versus competitors**:
- **Alibaba Cloud**: the No. 1 public cloud provider in China by market share (roughly 30%), with a clear first-mover advantage
- **Huawei Cloud**: dominant in the government/enterprise and carrier markets, with self-controlled technology
- **Tencent Cloud**: ranks third by market share, with distinctive strengths in gaming cloud and audio/video cloud

**How would Duan Yongping see this?**

> Cloud is a business that requires heavy capital investment and where economies of scale matter a great deal. Tencent doesn't need to be No. 1 — it isn't, and it doesn't have to be. The question is whether it can earn a reasonable profit in the niches where it does have an edge (gaming cloud, audio/video, Enterprise WeChat). Chasing the top market-share spot is dangerous; chasing a reasonable return on investment is the right approach.

- In 2025, investment activity consisted mainly of 37 small-ticket deals, with no more large-scale expansion

> **Duan Yongping**: "Investing isn't Tencent's core business. Tencent's value lies in its own operations; investment gains are a bonus. Don't buy Tencent because the portfolio is worth a lot — look at whether the core business itself is worth it."

## V. Five and Ten Years Out — Will the Moat Still Be There?

### 5.1 Five-year outlook (2031)

| Moat | Status in 5 years | Confidence |
|--------|----------|--------|
| WeChat social | **Stronger** — MAU could reach 1.5 billion, and the ecosystem becomes more closed and more powerful | 95% |
| Gaming | **Flat to slightly stronger** — overseas revenue could exceed domestic, but competition intensifies | 80% |
| Payments | **Flat** — a stable duopoly persists once regulation settles | 85% |
| Advertising | **Stronger** — AI-driven ad efficiency keeps improving, and Video Accounts ad inventory keeps releasing | 85% |
| Cloud/enterprise services | **Uncertain** — depends on the payoff from AI investment; could catch up or could fall further behind | 50% |

### 5.2 Ten-year outlook (2036)

**The biggest variable: will AI change how people interact with the internet?**

If AI agents become the mainstream mode of interaction (users completing tasks through an AI assistant rather than an app), WeChat's value as a gateway could be eroded. But Tencent has two lines of defense:
1. **WeChat itself can become an AI gateway**: Tencent's Yuanbao is already embedded in the WeChat ecosystem
2. **The social relationship chain is unaffected by AI**: people's need to communicate with each other won't be replaced by AI

**Duan Yongping's framework for thinking about this**:

> "Will WeChat still be around in 10 years? I think most likely yes. Because socializing is a basic human need, and WeChat solves the problem of connecting people to people. Unless some entirely new hardware form factor makes the phone disappear, a social network at WeChat's scale won't be displaced."

**But there's something to watch out for**:
- WeChat could go from being a "super app" to being "infrastructure" — still used by everyone, but with limited room to grow, and its valuation multiple could compress
- A fate similar to Facebook's: the social moat remains deep, but the growth narrative shifts from social to AI/metaverse

### 7.2 The essence of Video Accounts: primarily defense, with offense secondary

**The market consensus**: Video Accounts is Tencent's biggest growth engine, positioned to rival Douyin's trillion-yuan monetization potential.

**The non-consensus view** (Duan Yongping's perspective):

Video Accounts is more of a **defensive product** than an **offensive product**:
1. **Defense**: if WeChat had no short-video feature, user time would keep flowing to Douyin, and WeChat's status as a "super app" would be undermined
2. **Limited offense**: Video Accounts' recommendation algorithm, content ecosystem, and e-commerce infrastructure all lag Douyin's by at least 2-3 years
3. **A differentiated path**: Video Accounts' strength lies in "relationship-chain-based social recommendation," which makes it a **private-domain tool** rather than a **public-domain traffic pool**

> **Duan Yongping**: "Don't judge your own business by someone else's yardstick. Video Accounts doesn't need to become the next Douyin — it just needs to make the WeChat ecosystem more complete."

### 7.3 Sizing the monetization potential

| Scenario | Current state | 3-year potential |
|------|---------|---------|
| Feed advertising | Ad load rate far below Douyin's | Could rise 50-100% (but constrained by user experience) |
| Livestream e-commerce | GMV growing fast but off a small base | RMB 300-500 billion GMV (vs. Douyin's RMB 2 trillion+) |
| Brand advertising | Just getting started | High-ticket brands favor social settings |
| Local services | Working in tandem with WeChat Mini Programs | Some diversion of traffic away from Meituan |

**Conclusion**: Video Accounts' ad revenue could grow from roughly RMB 50-60 billion today to RMB 100-150 billion within three years — a reasonably high-confidence growth engine. But don't value it as if it were "the next Douyin."

## VIII. The Ultimate Assessment Against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

### 8.1 Duan Yongping's three criteria

#### Criterion one: Differentiation ★★★★★

> **Duan Yongping**: "A product without differentiation is not a good product. Differentiation means you can do things others cannot."

Tencent's differentiation is expressed through:
- **A social relationship chain of 1.4 billion people**: one of the largest non-financial digital networks in the world, impossible to replicate
- **Game development plus a global publishing network**: a combination of creative capability and commercialization capability
- **The closed loop of the WeChat ecosystem**: social → payments → services → transactions → content, a feat no second company has achieved

Differentiation rating: **full marks**.

#### Criterion two: Pricing power ★★★★

> **Duan Yongping**: "Only a company with pricing power is a good company. If you raise prices and users still don't leave, that's pricing power."

- **Gaming**: ★★★★★ — extremely strong pricing power on skins; limited-edition Honor of Kings skins priced above RMB 1,000 still sell out
- **Advertising**: ★★★★ — after AI lifted ROAS, ad prices keep rising and advertisers keep paying
- **Payments**: ★★★ — fee rates are constrained by regulation, limiting pricing power
- **Membership subscriptions**: ★★★★ — video/music membership price hikes have kept churn manageable

Pricing power overall is stronger than at most internet companies, but the fintech segment drags down the average.

#### Criterion three: Sustainable competitive advantage ★★★★★

> **Duan Yongping**: "What is a moat? It's being able to see that 10 years from now the company is still making money, and others still can't break in."

- Social network effects: **sustainable for 10 years**
- Game IP and studio portfolio: **sustainable for 10 years** (but requires continued hit output)
- WeChat ecosystem lock-in effect: **sustainable for 10 years**
- Brand trust: **sustainable for 10 years**

## X. Conclusion

### 10.1 The ultimate judgment from Duan Yongping's perspective

Tencent is the Chinese internet company that best fits Duan Yongping's "good business" standard — **extremely strong differentiation, clear pricing power, and a competitive advantage sustainable for more than 10 years**. WeChat's social relationship chain of 1.4 billion MAU is a moat that is nearly impossible to cross; the gaming business is a high-margin money printer; and the advertising business is accelerating, benefiting from both AI-driven efficiency gains and the incremental contribution of Video Accounts.

**The core advantage in one sentence**: Tencent doesn't need to invent something new every year — it just needs to keep building new floors on the plot of land called WeChat, and time is on its side.

### 10.2 Variables that require continued tracking

1. **The AI competitive landscape**: is the gap between Yuanbao and Doubao narrowing? How does Hunyuan 3.0 (launching April 2026) perform?
