## IV. Core findings

### 1. The "low-PE trap" is widespread across the memory industry

The memory industry is currently at the top of an AI-driven super-cycle, and almost every company is displaying extreme earnings elasticity. A low PE (especially Forward PE) in this industry **does not signal undervaluation** — it more often reflects the market's reasonable pricing of the view that "peak earnings are unsustainable."

### 2. DRAM > NAND > HDD (ranked by business quality)

- **DRAM/HBM**: an oligopoly of three, high differentiation in HBM, the strongest pricing power
- **NAND**: five or six competitors plus state-willed competitor YMTC, weak differentiation
- **HDD**: a duopoly that, surprisingly, is displaying "good business" characteristics in the AI era (sold-out capacity, strong pricing power)

### 3. All three low-valuation companies are "cyclical stocks"

The low valuations of Samsung, Nanya Technology, and Winbond Electronics are **not genuine pockets of value** — they reflect the market's rational pricing of memory's cyclicality. Value investors should wait for a cyclical pullback and buy at a valuation based on "normalized earnings," rather than being drawn in by a low Forward PE at the top of the cycle.

### 4. If one had to pick just one

Samsung Electronics is the name most worth continuing to track:
- HBM4 passing NVIDIA's top rating validated its ability to catch up
- Diversification provides downside protection
- Korea governance reform is heading in the right direction
- **But it's worth waiting for a 20-30% pullback before building a position**
