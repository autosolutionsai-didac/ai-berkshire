## 1. One-line conclusion

Yongxin Co., Ltd. is a packaging manufacturer that is **financially sound but ordinary as a business**. A long-run ROE above 15%, a dividend yield of roughly 5%, and financial health are the highlights, but a shallow moat, weak pricing power, and 2025's "revenue up, profit down" result (net profit -6.33%) expose its weak position in the middle of the manufacturing value chain. At the current PE of ~16x, **the margin of safety is insufficient** — the neutral scenario offers only 10% upside, while the conservative scenario carries 37% downside. In addition, the effectiveness of the 2025 new management team (second-generation succession) remains to be proven.

> "Good financials cannot mask a bad business. If a company can be run well without needing good management, that's a good business." — Duan Yongping / Buffett

## 4. Summary by Dimension

### 4.1 Business Model Analysis (Duan Yongping's lens) ★★☆☆☆

- **Five-dimension moat test:** Brand ★ / Switching costs ★★★ / Network effects ★ / Scale effects ★★ / Technology ★★★ — **overall moat is weak**
- **Pricing power test:** In 2025, revenue rose while profit fell (revenue +5.61%, net profit -6.33%) — a clear signal that pricing power is absent and costs cannot be passed through to customers
- **Duan Yongping's "good business" three criteria:** Differentiation ★★ / Pricing power ★★ / Sustainable competitive advantage ★★ — **all fall short**
- **Conclusion: not a good business. Financially sound, but the underlying business is ordinary — a case of "buying an ordinary company at an ordinary price" risk**

### 4.2 Financials & Valuation Analysis (Buffett's lens) ★★★☆☆

- **Financial health:** The balance sheet is still healthy, but the debt-to-asset ratio crossed above 40% (41.68%) for the first time, and the trend is negative
- **Cash flow deterioration:** Operating cash flow / net profit fell from 98% to 78%; free cash flow was cut in half to RMB 165 million
- **ROE analysis:** Still above 15% but declining, and the improvement has relied on rising leverage (equity multiplier 1.39 → 1.71)
- **Valuation:** PE of ~16x sits at roughly the 30th percentile of its historical range, but risk-adjusted expected return is **negative**
- **Three scenarios:** Optimistic (PE 20x) → RMB 19.2 / +68% (15% probability); Neutral (PE 15x) → RMB 12.5 / +10% (50%); Conservative (PE 10x) → RMB 7.2 / -37% (35%)
- **Conclusion: financially sound but with a deteriorating trend; the current valuation offers insufficient margin of safety**

### 4.3 Industry & Competition Analysis (Munger's lens) ★★☆☆☆

- **Industry attractiveness:** Mature stage, 5-8% growth, CR10 of only 15-20% — displays all the hallmarks of a "permanently mediocre industry"
- **Value chain position:** Sits at the low point of the smile curve (gross margin 22-25%), with both upstream and downstream holding stronger positions
- **Industry trend:** Environmental regulation is driving consolidation, but slowly
- **Munger-style judgment:** "In an industry with low barriers to entry and high barriers to exit, competition tends to devolve into price wars" — the packaging industry fits this pattern
- **Conclusion: the industry itself lacks attractiveness**

### 4.4 Risk Management Assessment (Li Lu's lens) ★★★☆☆

- **Core highlight:** Cumulative payout ratio of 77.77%, dividend yield of ~5% (versus an industry average of 1.48%), zero share pledges
- **Core concern:** New management in 2025 (second-generation succession) — chairwoman Jiang Lei has no background in the packaging industry
- **Environmental risk reassessment:** Printed and laminated composite packaging is not on the core plastic-ban catalog; the market may be overly worried
- **Li Lu's lens:** The high dividend lets investors "sleep at night," but the new management and industry transition make it hard to "sleep soundly"
- **Conclusion: risk is manageable but must be tracked closely; awaiting the verification period for new management**

## 5. Investment Thesis (Bull vs Bear)

### 🟢 Bull case

1. **High-dividend defensive value:** Dividend yield of about 5% versus an industry average of just 1.48%, providing "bond-like" allocation value
2. **Financial health:** Debt-to-asset ratio of 41% (still manageable), ample cash, no share pledges
3. **Consistently strong ROE:** Above 15% for five consecutive years, higher than most peers
4. **Shareholder-return culture:** Cumulative payout ratio of 77.77%, 20+ consecutive years of dividends
5. **Environmental policy favors the leader:** Small and mid-size capacity is being cleared out; Yongxin, as a compliant leader, stands to benefit
6. **Valuation at historical lows:** PE of 16x sits at the 30th percentile of the past five years
7. **Understandable business:** Simple manufacturing, avoiding black-swan risk

### 🔴 Bear case

1. **Lack of pricing power:** 2025's "revenue up, profit down" is a clear signal of weakening pricing power
2. **Ordinary business model at its core:** Shallow moat, insufficient differentiation — not a "good business" by Duan Yongping's definition
3. **Mediocre industry outlook:** Growth in the mature industry is slowing, and the competitive landscape won't improve in the near term
4. **New management risk:** Second-generation succession plus a simultaneous change of both top executives; the chairwoman has no industry experience
5. **2025 data deterioration:** Net profit -6.33%, net margin 13.37% → 11.87%, free cash flow cut in half
6. **Insufficient margin of safety:** Only 10% upside in the neutral scenario versus 37% downside in the conservative scenario — an unattractive risk/reward ratio
7. **Long-term threat from environmental substitution:** Biodegradable/recyclable materials could disrupt traditional plastic packaging

## 8. Summary

Yongxin Co., Ltd. can be summed up as a manufacturing enterprise that is **"financially presentable but operationally mediocre."** Taking the four masters' perspectives together:

**On the ledger:** A 5% dividend yield, zero pledges, and 20 consecutive years of dividends hold some appeal for investors seeking stable cash flow. The financials are unlikely to run into serious trouble in the near term.

**But the nature of the business sets a ceiling:** Yongxin sits in a weak midstream position in the value chain, lacks pricing power, and does not have a wide moat. 2025's "revenue up, profit down" is an alarm bell — revenue is still growing, but profit is already being squeezed. This does not meet Duan Yongping's standard for a "good business," nor Buffett's requirement for a "wide moat."

**Management uncertainty:** In 2025 both the chair and general manager changed simultaneously, with second-generation succession — this is a major variable that must be tracked continuously. Jiang Lei's background at consumer-goods multinationals may bring fresh thinking, but it will take time to prove out.

**Overall:** Yongxin Co., Ltd. is suited to a very limited allocation (≤2%) as a **"high-dividend, bond-like"** holding, not as a growth-oriented core position. The current price of RMB 11.4 (PE 16x) offers an acceptable but not fully sufficient margin of safety. A more attractive entry range would be RMB 9-10 (PE 12.5-14x). The ideal core holding is a company that combines both financial soundness and a deep moat — Yongxin satisfies only the former, not the latter.

- Information rating: Grade B — financial data is ample, but management information and in-depth broker research are limited
- Confidence in the management assessment is relatively low: Jiang Lei's background relies on unofficial sources
- The environmental policy risk assessment involves judgment calls; actual enforcement intensity is uncertain
- This report does not constitute investment advice; investors should exercise independent judgment
