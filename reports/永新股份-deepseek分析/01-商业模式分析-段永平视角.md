## 1. Business Model Essence

### Core Business Definition

Yongxin Co.'s core business is manufacturing and selling **color-printed composite packaging products** and **vacuum aluminized film products**, a midstream segment of the plastic packaging manufacturing industry.

> "Buying stocks is buying companies; buying companies is buying businesses." — Duan Yongping

### Revenue Structure Breakdown (data source: 2025 annual report, published April 23, 2026)

| Product category | Revenue share (estimated) | Gross margin | Growth characteristics |
|---------|----------------|-------|---------|
| Color-printed composite packaging materials | ~65-70% | 20-22% | Core business, stable but slowing growth |
| Vacuum aluminized film | ~15-20% | 25-28% | Higher technical barriers, large profit contribution |
| Other (ink, auxiliary materials, etc.) | ~10-15% | 15-20% | Supporting ancillary business |
| **Total** | **100%** | **23.0%** | |

**Core finding:** The company is a typical B2B manufacturing enterprise, providing packaging solutions for downstream food, personal-care, and pharmaceutical brand owners. Revenue in 2025 was 3.723 billion yuan, up 5.61% year over year, but this was **volume without price** — revenue growth was driven mainly by volume, while gross margin fell from 23.78% to 23.00%.

### Business Model Panorama

```
Upstream (raw materials) → Yongxin Co. (manufacturing) → Downstream (brand owners)
Plastic pellets/ink/aluminum foil    Color printing + aluminized film    Food/personal care/pharma
   Weak bargaining power             Midstream processing            Strong bargaining power
```

## 2. Flywheel Effect Assessment

### Flywheel Mechanism Test

B2B packaging manufacturing lacks the network effects of internet/platform companies. Testing whether other flywheels exist:

| Flywheel link | Present? | Description |
|---------|---------|------|
| Scale → lower cost → customers → more scale | ⚠️ Partial | Some scale effect exists, but the industry is fragmented and the scale advantage is not pronounced |
| Technology → quality → customer recognition → more revenue → technology | ⚠️ Partial | Some technical accumulation exists, but innovation is slow and barriers are not high |
| Brand → premium → profit → R&D → technology | ❌ None | Brand premium is limited in the B2B space |
| Network effects | ❌ None | Traditional manufacturing has no network effects |

**Flywheel effect rating: ★★☆☆☆**

**Conclusion:** Lacks a strong flywheel effect. As a manufacturing enterprise, it forms a modest positive cycle mainly through economies of scale and technical accumulation, but the effect is far weaker than that of platform companies.

## 3. Moat Analysis (Five Dimensions Examined Individually)

### 3.1 Brand Moat ★☆☆☆☆

| Test item | Assessment |
|-------|------|
| Do end consumers know the brand? | ❌ Not a consumer brand — B2B model, end consumers are almost unaware |
| Do downstream customers purchase based on brand recognition? | ⚠️ Partially — some large customers have supplier certification systems, but it is not brand-driven |
| Does the brand confer pricing power? | ❌ No |

### 3.2 Switching Cost Moat ★★★☆☆

| Test item | Assessment |
|-------|------|
| How high is the cost of switching suppliers? | Moderate — requires re-certification, testing, and trial production, a cycle of about 3-6 months |
| How strong is customer stickiness? | Fairly good — large brand owners tend to prefer stable suppliers |
| Is there any data lock-in? | ❌ None — this is traditional manufacturing, not software/SaaS |

**Conclusion:** Switching cost is Yongxin Co.'s most important source of moat, but its strength is limited — valuable for large customers, but switching costs are low for small customers.

### 3.3 Network Effect Moat ★☆☆☆☆

**Conclusion:** Packaging manufacturing has no network effects (product value does not increase as the user base grows).

### 3.4 Scale Effect Moat ★★☆☆☆

| Test item | Assessment |
|-------|------|
| Is there a clear unit cost advantage? | ⚠️ Some advantage exists, but it lags larger-scale players such as Zijiang Enterprise (whose revenue is 3x that of Yongxin) |
| What does an industry CR10 of only 15-20% imply? | The industry is extremely fragmented; scale effects are not pronounced |
| Impact of capacity utilization on profitability? | Significant — fixed costs make up a high proportion of the cost base |

### 3.5 Technology/Patent Moat ★★★☆☆

| Test item | Assessment |
|-------|------|
| Vacuum aluminized film technical barrier | Relatively high — requires long-term process accumulation and heavy equipment investment |
| Color-print composite packaging technical barrier | Moderate — technology is mature, differentiation is limited |
| R&D spending ratio | ~3% (estimated), an industry-standard level |
| Number of patents | Some patent reserves exist, but not a core barrier |

**Moat composite rating: ★★☆☆☆ (weak moat)**

Of the five dimensions, only "switching cost" and "technology" provide moderate protection, and neither is particularly wide.

## 4. Customer/User Value Analysis

### Unique Value Created for Customers

| Value dimension | Assessment |
|---------|------|
| Product quality | Stable, already certified under major brand owners' supplier systems |
| Delivery responsiveness | Advantage in the East China region, relatively fast response |
| Customization capability | Can customize color printing and specifications to customer needs |
| Cost advantage | Limited — as a mid-sized enterprise, no clear cost advantage |
| One-stop service | Combined supply of color printing + aluminized film, adds convenience |

### Industry Pain Point Solved

Yongxin Co.'s core value proposition is addressing the packaging outsourcing needs of food/personal-care/pharmaceutical brand owners — these brands do not build their own packaging lines, but instead seek stable, high-quality packaging suppliers.

### Pricing Power Test (Key Question)

> Duan Yongping asks: "If Yongxin Co. raised prices 10%, would customers leave?"

- **Standard color-print packaging**: Most likely to churn — competition is intense and customers can easily switch suppliers
- **High-end aluminized film**: Partial churn — there is a technical threshold, but it is not irreplaceable
- **Conclusion: weak pricing power ★★☆☆☆**

### Duan Yongping-Style Conclusion

**This is not a "good business" by Duan Yongping's definition:**
- Lacks differentiation: commoditized product competition
- No pricing power: cannot pass on costs
- Narrow, shallow moat: easy for competitors to enter

**Investment recommendation**: Does not meet Duan Yongping's "good business" standard. Unless the price is extremely low (providing a margin of safety), it is not worth investing in.

> "A good business model is a good business model. A good business model has a strong moat, a very wide moat." — Duan Yongping

Yongxin Co.'s moat is neither strong nor wide.
