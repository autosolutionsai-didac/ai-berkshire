### 1.2 Industry Growth Drivers and Suppressing Factors

**Positive drivers:**
- Consumption upgrade: rising quality standards in food, household chemicals, and pharmaceuticals are driving packaging demand
- E-commerce: continued growth in express-delivery/logistics packaging demand
- Environmental substitution: plastics are partly replacing traditional materials

**Suppressing factors:**
- Environmental pressure: plastic-restriction policy is suppressing traditional plastic packaging
- Overcapacity: overcapacity in low-end packaging is driving intense price competition
- Consumption downgrade trend: cost-cutting pressure on packaging in some segments

**Growth score: ★★★☆☆** — the industry has entered a mature phase, where structural opportunities outweigh overall-volume opportunities

**Strengths:**

2. Relatively high technical barriers in vacuum aluminized film, with a large contribution to profit

**Weaknesses:**

## IV. Industry Trends and Disruption Risk

### 4.1 Technology Change Trends
| Trend | Impact on Yongxin |
|------|------------|
| Green packaging (biodegradable, recyclable) | Requires a technology transition, creating cost pressure |
| Smart packaging (RFID, etc.) | New opportunities, but new capabilities are required |
| Functionalization (high barrier, freshness preservation) | Favors companies with technical accumulation |

### 4.2 Policy Environment Impact
- **Positive:** rising environmental standards eliminate outdated capacity, benefiting compliant leaders
- **Negative:** plastic-restriction policy suppresses traditional plastic packaging

### 4.3 New Entrant Threats
- Low-end market: low barriers, intense competition
- High-end market: requires customer certification plus technical accumulation, higher barriers

**Munger's warning:** "When an industry has low barriers to entry and high barriers to exit, competition tends to devolve into price wars."

## V. Value Chain Distribution

### Value Chain Analysis

```
Upstream raw materials (plastic pellets/ink/aluminum foil)
      ↓ Weak bargaining power (commodity pricing)
Midstream manufacturing (Yongxin Co. and other packaging companies)
  Gross margin 22-25%, net margin 11-13%
      ↓ Weak bargaining power (customers hold the upper hand)
Downstream brand owners (food/household chemicals/pharma)
  Gross margin 40-60%, high brand premium
```

**Core finding:** Packaging manufacturing sits at the bottom of the smile curve — both upstream and downstream are strong, squeezing midstream profit.

## VI. Munger-Style Inversion

**1. "If Yongxin raised prices 10%, would customers leave?"**
- Ordinary products: yes, competition is intense
- High-end products: some would, since the technical threshold is not irreplaceable
- **Conclusion: weak pricing power**

**2. "Will the packaging industry's competitive landscape improve in five years?"**
- Possibly a slight improvement (environmental policy accelerating the elimination of small companies)
- But no fundamental change (large companies' expansion will also intensify competition)
- **Conclusion: no fundamental improvement**

**3. "What technology could disrupt the packaging industry?"**
- Biodegradable materials, smart packaging, circular packaging models
- Large-scale substitution is still 5-10 years away
- **Conclusion: disruption risk exists but is not imminent**
