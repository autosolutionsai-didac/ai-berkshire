# Big Industry, Small Company

> "Understanding Yongxin Co." series - Part 03 - Industry and Competition
> Reading time: about 10 minutes

## A hundred-billion-yuan market, but no winner-takes-all

Yongxin is the segment leader in flexible packaging, and that fact alone is worth pausing on: a company that has been profitable for 20 consecutive years, with ROE consistently above 15%, had a market cap of only 7.2 billion yuan in 2025, and an industry market share of just 2-3%. If the leader looks like this, you can imagine what the rest of the industry looks like.

China's overall packaging industry is roughly **RMB 1.2-1.3 trillion** in size (2024 figures), of which plastic packaging accounts for about **RMB 400-450 billion**, and flexible packaging (printed composite packaging + film, etc.) accounts for about **RMB 80-100 billion**. Yongxin's 2025 revenue was **RMB 3.723 billion**, giving it a share of roughly **2-3%** (estimate) of the printed composite packaging market.

*Source: aggregated industry research reports; figures vary by methodology, midpoint used*

This raises a question: **in a hundred-billion-yuan-plus market where the leader holds only 2-3% share, does that indicate large room for growth, or does it indicate that the industry is structurally incapable of consolidating?**

The evidence leans toward the latter.

---

## Why concentration is so low

Flexible packaging's CR5 is only about **6%**, and CR10 is about **15-20%** (estimate). This level of concentration is extremely low even by manufacturing-industry standards.

*Source: aggregated industry research reports*

There are three reasons:

### 1. Low barriers to entry

Setting up a low-to-mid-tier packaging plant requires equipment investment of only a few million to a few tens of millions of yuan. This has led to thousands of small and mid-sized packaging firms scattered across the country, with fierce regional competition.

### 2. Transport-radius constraints

Packaging materials are bulky, lightweight, and low unit-price — so freight cost as a share of total cost is high. A packaging plant in Huangshan shipping product to Guangdong could see freight eat up most of its profit. This naturally produces a regionally fragmented competitive landscape.

Yongxin's production base is mainly in East China (Huangshan, Anhui), serving the Yangtze River Delta region. This also explains why the company's "nationalization" has progressed slowly.

### 3. Low product differentiation

A bag of potato chips made by Factory A versus Factory B looks the same to the consumer. Product homogeneity means competition happens along a single dimension — mainly price and service responsiveness, rather than brand or technology barriers.

**Counterargument:** Precisely because concentration is low, there is large room for industry consolidation. Environmental policy (VOC controls, tighter emissions standards) is accelerating the elimination of small players. Leader Yongxin stands to benefit from this process, gradually gaining market share. By one estimate, every 1% of small capacity eliminated is equivalent to adding half a year's worth of revenue for Yongxin.

## Who are the competitors

Placing Yongxin within the competitive landscape:

| Company | Revenue scale | Main products | Competitive relationship with Yongxin |
|------|---------|---------|---------------|
| **Amcor (global)** | ~RMB 97.2 billion (2024) | Full-category flexible packaging | Indirect competition, different price tiers |
| **Zijiang Enterprise Group** | ~RMB 10.6 billion | Plastic bottles, integrated packaging | Partial overlap |
| **Yutong Technology** | ~RMB 15.0 billion | Paper packaging, 3C packaging | Different track |
| **ORG Packaging** | ~RMB 15.0 billion | Metal packaging (aluminum cans) | Different material, but ORG is Yongxin's second-largest shareholder |
| **Hishine Advanced Materials** | ~RMB 2.0-2.5 billion | Pharmaceutical high-barrier packaging | Technology competition |
| **Thousands of local small firms** | <RMB 1.0 billion | Various low-to-mid-tier packaging | Price competition |

*Source: Eastmoney, respective company annual reports*

A few points worth noting:

**Amcor is the global leader**, with 2024 revenue of about **RMB 97.2 billion**, roughly **26 times** Yongxin's size. It also operates in China, but mainly serves multinational brand owners, with limited overlap with Yongxin's customer base. Amcor's existence shows that scaling flexible packaging is possible — but it requires going global.

**ORG Packaging is Yongxin's second-largest shareholder** (holding **22.20%**). ORG makes metal packaging (aluminum cans) while Yongxin makes plastic flexible packaging — different materials, with room for synergy (offering the same customer a combined "metal + plastic" packaging solution). But this synergy has not yet shown up in the financial data.

**The real competition comes from local small firms.** Thousands of small plants with revenue under RMB 1 billion compete for orders on price. This is the biggest competitive pressure Yongxin faces — not being crushed by giants, but being nibbled away by ants.

## Who captures the most profit in the value chain

Mapping out how profit is distributed along the flexible packaging value chain clarifies Yongxin's position:

```
Upstream: plastic resin / ink / aluminum foil (commodities)
  -> Prices set by international crude oil and petrochemical capacity
  -> Yongxin has no pricing power over upstream

Midstream: Yongxin Co. (packaging manufacturing)
  -> Gross margin 23%, net margin 12%
  -> Squeezed from both sides

Downstream: brand owners (food / household chemicals / pharma)
  -> Gross margin 40-60%
  -> High brand premium, strong pricing power
```

This is the classic **"low point of the smile curve"** — profit is captured by both upstream and downstream, with the midstream manufacturing link getting the smallest slice.

Some concrete numbers to feel this: Yongxin's 2025 gross margin was **23%**, net margin **11.87%**. Its customers — for example Yili (gross margin about 32%) and Unilever (gross margin about 42%) — earn margins 2-4 times Yongxin's.

**This is why Yongxin "looks profitable but isn't really a great business":** what it earns is hard-won money, not brand money, not platform money, not monopoly money.

## Drivers and ceilings of industry growth

### Positive drivers

1. **Stable consumer-goods demand**: people need to eat, do laundry, take medicine — packaging demand correlates strongly with consumption frequency, and cyclicality is weak
2. **Consumption upgrading drives packaging upgrading**: moving from simple plastic bags to high-barrier, fresh-keeping, aesthetically refined composite packaging raises unit prices
3. **Takeout / e-commerce**: new consumption scenarios create new packaging demand
4. **Tighter environmental standards**: compliant companies benefit as small capacity is cleared out

### Negative pressures

1. **Plastic-restriction policy**: the long-term direction is reduced use of single-use plastics, which pressures traditional plastic packaging
2. **Overcapacity**: severe overcapacity in low-end packaging, frequent price wars
3. **Consumption downgrade / cost-cutting**: some brand owners are cutting packaging costs
4. **Slowing industry growth**: China's plastic-packaging growth rate has fallen from double digits to **5-8%**

**Overall assessment: the industry has entered a mature phase, where structural opportunities outweigh total-volume opportunities.** Incremental growth mainly comes from: premiumization (functional films), environmental substitution (compliant leaders gaining share), and exports (emerging markets).

## Plastic-restriction policy: overworried?

One market concern about Yongxin is the plastic-restriction policy. But breaking it down carefully:

| Dimension | Actual situation |
|------|---------|
| Core plastic-ban catalog | Mainly targets single-use plastic shopping bags, straws, etc. — **Yongxin's core food/pharma composite packaging business is not in the core catalog** |
| China's plastic film output | **17.856 million tonnes** in 2024, still growing, no significant decline |
| Environmental investment | Yongxin's 2026 VOC-control investment is RMB 46.47 million, which can generate economic returns through solvent recovery |
| Impact on the leader | Tighter environmental standards accelerate the exit of small firms, **benefiting the compliant leader** |

*Source: industry statistics, Yongxin Co. annual report*

**Conclusion: the direct impact of plastic-restriction policy on Yongxin is limited.** The market may be oversimplifying "plastic restriction = bad news for all plastic packaging." In reality, food-grade composite packaging is barely affected, and rising environmental thresholds may actually benefit the leader.

**But a genuine long-term risk exists:** if biodegradable-material technology breaks through and costs fall enough to replace traditional plastic composite packaging, Yongxin would face pressure to switch technology routes. That window is roughly **5-10 years** out — not an immediate threat.

## Disruption risk: what could change the rules of the game

| Trend | Time window | Impact on Yongxin |
|------|---------|------------|
| Biodegradable / recyclable materials | 5-10 years | Requires technology-transition investment; carries elimination risk |
| Smart packaging (RFID, etc.) | 3-5 years | New growth opportunity, but requires new capabilities |
| Functional packaging (high barrier, freshness preservation) | Happening now | **Favorable** to Yongxin's accumulated technology |
| Circular packaging models | 10+ years | Could disrupt the single-use packaging industry |

Of these, **functional packaging is the most certain near-term positive.** Yongxin's biaxially oriented multifunctional film project currently under development is aimed precisely at this direction.

**Counterargument:** Technological change isn't necessarily bad news for the industry leader. Yongxin has accumulated R&D and financial reserves — if environmental substitution really does arrive, small firms will fall first. The key is whether management has forward-looking positioning.

## Going abroad: room for an incremental growth story

In 2025, Yongxin's export revenue was **RMB 560 million**, up **24.04%** year over year, with its share rising from 12.81% to **15.05%**.

Export growth far outpaced domestic sales (+3.09%), making it the brightest incremental source over the past two years. But a few points call for a cool head:

1. The absolute scale of exports is still small (only RMB 560 million)
2. Part of the growth is driven by exchange-rate factors
3. Overseas market expansion requires longer customer-certification cycles
4. Geopolitical risks such as trade friction cannot be ignored

**Going abroad is an incremental story worth tracking, but it's too early to conclude it can change Yongxin's growth curve.**

**One-line summary: flexible packaging is not an industry born with a good hand.** It's big enough and stable enough, but not concentrated enough and not profitable enough. Within this industry Yongxin is a top student, but a top student's ceiling is set by the industry.
