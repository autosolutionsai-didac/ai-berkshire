# "Understanding Yongxin Co." Series Introduction

> Four articles that take apart a packaging company that "quietly makes money": what's good about it, what's not, and whether it's worth buying.
>
> The series takes about 40 minutes to read; reading in order is recommended.

---

## Series Positioning

Yongxin Co. is not a company that draws much buzz. It doesn't command attention the way Kweichow Moutai does, nor is it as controversial as Pinduoduo. But if you screen A-shares for "20 consecutive years of dividends + a 5% dividend yield + ROE consistently above 15%," it will certainly be on your list.

There is an interesting contradiction here:

- The dividend camp says: a cumulative payout ratio of **77.77%**, a dividend yield of **4.7%**, 20 consecutive years of dividends — this is one of the best "bond-like" picks in the A-share market
- The growth camp says: the flexible packaging industry's CR5 is only **6%**, gross margin is **23%**, and in 2025 revenue grew while profit fell — this is a mediocre business

**Both camps are right. The question is: if a mediocre business is cheap enough and generous enough with its payouts, is it still worth buying?**

What this series sets out to do is first lay out clearly "what it is" — business model, financial quality, industry landscape, risk, and valuation — and then let you judge for yourself.

## Series Writing Principles

1. **Lay out the data first, then the logic, then the conclusion** — no presumption of bullish or bearish
2. **Every core judgment is paired with a counterargument** — letting readers weigh it themselves
3. **No fabricated data** — every figure is sourced, and estimates are explicitly marked "estimate"
4. **No "probability-weighted expected return"** — scenario weights are purely subjective and risk giving readers a false sense of precision
5. **Not investment advice** — every chapter ends with a disclaimer
