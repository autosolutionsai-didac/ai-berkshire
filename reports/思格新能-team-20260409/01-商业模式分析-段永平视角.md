# 01 Business Model Analysis — Duan Yongping's Perspective

> "Do the right thing, and do the thing right." — Duan Yongping

---

## I. What is the core business?

Sigenergy's core business is **the integrated distributed solar-plus-storage solution** — bundling solar power generation, energy storage, EV charging, and energy management into a single "plug-and-play" product, sold to residential and commercial/industrial customers overseas (mainly Europe and Australia).

**Business model breakdown:**

| Layer | Content | Revenue share |
|------|------|---------|
| Hardware layer | SigenStor 5-in-1 solar-storage-charging unit | ~93% (2025) |
| Software layer | SigenOS AI energy management system | Bundled into hardware |
| Service layer | Installation, O&M, cloud services | Minimal (currently) |

**The SigenStor "5-in-1" product integrates:**
1. Solar inverter
2. Energy storage power conversion system (PCS)
3. Battery storage (stackable, 5-100kWh)
4. DC fast-charging module (EV charging)
5. AI energy management system (SigenOS)

**The core value of this design: it turns what used to require 5 devices, 5 brands, and 5 separate installations into 1 device, 1 installation, and 1 app.** It's analogous to how Apple bundled an MP3 player, a phone, and a camera into the iPhone.

---

## II. Assessing the business against Duan Yongping's "good business" standard

### 2.1 Is user demand real? ★★★★★

Real and non-discretionary. The global energy transition is an irreversible trend, and distributed solar-plus-storage is the direction of travel:
- European electricity prices are high (0.3-0.5 EUR/kWh), and storage systems pay back in just 3-5 years
- Australia has abundant sunlight and generous government subsidies
- The "5-in-1" unit is 20-30% cheaper than buying components separately, and cuts installation time by 50%+
- Users are genuinely voting with their wallets: in the first three quarters of 2024, Sigenergy held 28.6% of global shipments of stackable solar-plus-storage units

### 2.2 Is it a "simple" business? ★★★☆☆

**Not simple enough.** Duan Yongping's favorite businesses are the kind "even a fool could run." Sigenergy's business carries considerable complexity:
- High technical barriers (power electronics + electrochemistry + software + AI + EV charging), requiring sustained R&D investment
- Complex supply chain management (battery cells, IGBTs, PCBs, and other core components)
- High cost of building and maintaining overseas distribution channels (85 countries, 172 distributors)
- Installation and after-sales service require a localized service network

**Duan Yongping might say:** "This business is far more complicated than OPPO/vivo — it's more like what Huawei does. Then again, Xu Yingtong came out of Huawei in the first place."

### 2.3 Is there differentiation? ★★★★☆

**Yes, and fairly strong differentiation:**
- **Product level:** The world's first stackable "5-in-1" design; most competitors are still separate-component systems
- **Experience level:** One-stop purchasing, installation, and app management saves hassle for both installers and users
- **Brand level:** Established a "premium solar-plus-storage" positioning in the European, American, and Australian markets — similar to Apple's positioning in consumer electronics

**But the differentiation isn't durable:** 5-in-1 integration isn't an insurmountable technical barrier. Huawei, Tesla, Enphase, and others are all capable of similar integration. The differentiation stems more from **first-mover advantage plus channel network plus brand perception** than from a technological moat.

### 2.4 How is pricing power? ★★★★☆

**Currently very strong pricing power:**
- Gross margin of 50.1% in 2025, far above peers' 20-30%
- SigenStor sells for roughly RMB 2.2/Wh, above the industry average
- Distributors are willing to push this brand (high profit, easy installation, low after-sales burden)

**Duan Yongping would caution:** "High gross margins invite competition. A 50% gross margin isn't sustainable in manufacturing — it will eventually get pulled down. The key question is how long it can be maintained."

### 2.5 Is there a channel moat? ★★★★☆

Sigenergy's channel network has been built at a striking pace:
- Coverage of 85 countries and 172 distributors within 2 years
- Ranked No. 1 in Australia's residential storage shipments (ahead of Tesla Powerwall)
- Signed a 1GWh MOU with Lawrence & Hanson (a leading Australian distributor)
- Rapid penetration in European markets (UK, Sweden, Netherlands)

**The Huawei DNA shows through most clearly in channel building** — Xu Yingtong once took Huawei's solar inverter business to No. 1 globally, and channel-building was his core competency.

## III. The biggest hidden danger in this "good business": single-product dependence

> "If 90% of a company's revenue comes from one product, it is fundamentally a product, not a company." — Duan Yongping

**This is Sigenergy's biggest structural risk:**

| Year | SigenStor revenue share |
|------|:----------------:|
| 2023 | 96.4% |
| First three quarters of 2024 | 90.5% |
| 2025 | ~93% |

Single-product dependence means:
1. **Anything that affects SigenStor affects the entire company** (technology shifts, competitive substitution, tariff barriers)
2. **There is no "second curve"** — if SigenStor's growth slows, the company stalls
3. **Supply chain concentration is high** — the supply security of core components like battery cells is the company's lifeline

**Reference comparison:** Apple's iPhone revenue share is around 50%, and that is already considered a high level of dependence. Sigenergy's 93% is an extreme level.

---

## IV. Duan Yongping's overall verdict

### Rating: ★★★★ 4.0/5

**Strengths:**
- Strong product, real user demand, clear differentiation
- First-rate channel-building capability (Huawei DNA)
- Strong pricing power, gross margin far above peers
- Formidable execution by the team (RMB 9 billion in revenue in 3 years from zero)

**Weaknesses:**
- Not a "simple" business — high technical and supply-chain complexity
- Single-product dependence above 90% is a fatal vulnerability
- A 50% gross margin will inevitably invite intense competition
- Only 3 years old; operating resilience is untested

**Duan Yongping's likely conclusion:** "The product really is good, and the team can really execute. But the business model isn't simple enough, the single-product dependence is too heavy, and a 50% gross margin can't be sustained long-term in Chinese manufacturing. I'll watch closely, but I won't take a large position at IPO — let the bullet fly for a while."
