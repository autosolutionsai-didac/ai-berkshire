## A Set of Facts to Reconsider Wuliangye

What does the most classic value trap in China's A-share market look like? PE 11x, dividend yield 5.6%, cash on hand 127.4 billion yuan—the numbers look "extremely cheap" no matter how you look at them. But two consecutive board chairmen have fallen from power, 30.3 billion yuan in overstated revenue has been exposed, and financial report credibility has zeroed out. It's cheap, but nobody dares to buy. That's Wuliangye today.

On April 30, 2026, on the final day of the annual report disclosure deadline, Wuliangye released a "Prior Period Accounting Error Correction Announcement" after hours at 22:00. The adjustment results:

- 2025 revenue plummeted from the expected **~89.1 billion** to **40.5 billion**, a year-on-year **-54.6%**
- Net profit attributable to parent company dropped from the expected **~31.8 billion** to **8.95 billion**, a year-on-year **-71.9%**
- Cumulative revenue reductions of **30.3 billion** and net profit reduction of **16.9 billion** for the first three quarters

The missing 30.3 billion was not due to operating losses, but rather a shift in accounting methods—in the past "revenue was recognized when shipped to distributors," now it's "revenue is recognized only after terminal sales." 

**In one sentence: Wuliangye acknowledged that over the past several years, a large portion of its reported revenue consisted of goods sitting in distributor warehouses that were never actually sold to consumers.**

The Shenzhen Stock Exchange issued an inquiry that very night. Two months prior (February 2026), Wuliangye's board chairman Zeng Congqin was placed under investigation. Further back (January 2025), former board chairman Li Shuguang was also investigated.

**Two successive board chairmen falling from power + 30.3 billion yuan in inflated revenue—this is one of the most serious corporate governance crises in A-share baijiu sector history.**

Yet on the other hand:

- 2026 Q1 revenue **22.8 billion** (+33.7%), net profit **8.06 billion** (+82.6%), showing underlying profitability has not collapsed
- Major shareholders announced share buybacks of **3-5 billion**, repurchases of **8-10 billion**, and full-year dividends of **20 billion**
- As of mid-May 2026, stock price approximately **89 yuan**, total market cap approximately **346 billion yuan**, PE (calculated on 2024 net profit of 31.9 billion) approximately **11x**

**A company with annual profits in the 30-billion-yuan range, valued at 11x, dividend yield 5.6%, cash on hand 127.4 billion—yet two board chairmen have successively faced trouble, and financial report trust has collapsed.**

Cheap? Or is it cheap for a reason? That's what this series aims to answer.

## What Kind of Business Is Baijiu, Really?

Before deconstructing Wuliangye, let's first understand the essence of the baijiu business.

### Why Baijiu Is Called "One of the Best Business Models"

| Characteristic | Baijiu | General Manufacturing |
|-----------------|--------|----------------------|
| Gross Margin | **77-92%** | 15-30% |
| Capital Expenditure/Revenue | **~3%** | 20-30% |
| Inventory Depreciation Risk | **More valuable over time** | Depreciates over time |
| Accounts Receivable | **Nearly zero** (payment before shipment) | Extensive credit sales |
| Advance Payment Model | **Distributors pay before taking delivery** | Shipment before payment |
| Product Lifecycle | **Unchanged for centuries** | 2-5 year iterations |

Baijiu is a "low-input, high-output" money-printing machine—no need to continuously spend on developing new products, no need to build more factories, aging cellars become more valuable, and brand heritage commands premium pricing.

**Wuliangye's Financial Metrics** (2024):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Gross Margin | **77.05%** |
| Net Margin | **37.22%** |
| ROE | **23.35%** |
| Free Cash Flow | **~31.3 billion** (est.) |
| Capital Expenditure/Net Profit | **~6%** |
| Interest-Bearing Debt | **Nearly zero** |
| Cash and Cash Equivalents | **127.4 billion** |

*Data Source: Wuliangye 2024 Annual Report*

**Requires almost no additional investment to maintain competitiveness, extremely high profit quality, and zero-leverage operations.** These characteristics are world-class across any industry.

**Yet the baijiu industry also faces long-term structural challenges—China's declining population, changing drinking habits among young people, and restrictions on government-related consumption.** Among consumers aged 30 and under, baijiu accounts for only **8%** of total alcohol consumption (industry research data), and the 25-35 age group shows a **22%** year-on-year decline in baijiu drinking frequency (Union Credit research report). The core baijiu consumer demographic (ages 30-59) is shrinking by approximately **1%** annually.

There's nothing wrong with a good business. The question is: is Wuliangye the best in this good business?
