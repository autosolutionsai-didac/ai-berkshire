## What Money Is Wuliangye Making?

A company that spent twice the sales expenses of its competitor yet earned only 40% of its competitor's profit—what does this tell us? It shows the company is spending money to artificially prop up a brand that is losing appeal. This is the core contradiction of Wuliangye's business model.

Let's first examine the revenue structure and find the answer in the data.

### Revenue Breakdown (2024 Annual Report, pre-adjustment basis)

| Product Category | Revenue (¥100M) | Proportion | Gross Margin |
|---------|-----------|------|--------|
| **Wuliangye Products** | 679 | **76.1%** | **87.02%** |
| Series Wines (Wuliangye Spring/Wuliangye Pure, etc.) | 153 | 17.1% | 60.81% |
| Other Business | 60 | 6.8% | — |
| **Total** | **892** | 100% | **77.05%** |

*Data Source: Wuliangye 2024 Annual Report*

**76% of revenue comes from one product—eighth-generation Wuliangye.** This is Wuliangye's lifeline.

Looking more closely (estimates based on industry research, not official disclosure):

| Sub-product | Estimated Revenue (¥100M) | Notes |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Eighth-Gen Standard Wuliangye (52°) | **~540-560** | Core flagship product, approximately 60% of total revenue |
| Classic Wuliangye Series (10/20/30/50) | ~40-50 | Ultra-premium line |
| Wuliangye 1618 | ~30-40 | 2024 growth 20%+ |
| Series wines total | 153 | Lower-to-mid range, high volume low profit |

**A counter-intuitive data point**: Series wines' sales volume is **3 times** that of Wuliangye products (1.2 million tons vs 400,000 tons), yet revenue comprises only **17%**. Converting this, Wuliangye products' per-ton price is over **13 times** that of series wines.

The same grain fermented into wine—slap "Wuliangye" on the label—and value increases more than tenfold. This is the power of brand premium.

**But the issue is**: is this brand premium supported by genuine consumer demand, or maintained through pushing inventory onto distributors? The 30.3 billion yuan in inflated revenue incident suggests the answer may not be as optimistic.

## Pricing Power: The Most Critical and Controversial Issue

If there's only one metric to judge a baijiu company's brand power, it's **the relationship between factory price and market wholesale price**.

### Eighth-Generation Standard Wuliangye Price System (2025-2026)

```
Factory Price (Nominal) → Invoice Price (Actual) → Market Wholesale → Suggested Retail
    1,019 yuan              900 yuan               850 yuan              1,399 yuan
                        (reduced from 2026)      (fluctuation range)   (merely formality)
```

Key Price Points:

| Price Point | Amount (yuan/bottle) | Notes |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Factory Price (Nominal) | **1,019** | Unchanged since 2024 |
| Distributor Invoice Price | **900** | Reduced from 2026, includes 119-yuan subsidy |
| Distributor Actual Cost | ~800-850 | After rebates and discounts |
| Market Wholesale Price | **~850** | 2026 Q1 data |
| Suggested Retail Price | 1,399 | No channel sells at this price |

*Data Sources: Paileet News, 21st Century Business Herald, Channel Research*

### Core Issue: Price Inversion

**Inversion** = distributor cost price > market selling price. Distributors lose money on every bottle sold.

| Period | Distributor Cost | Wholesale Price | Per-Bottle Profit/Loss |
|------|----------|------|---------|
| H1 2025 | ~920 yuan | ~850 yuan | **-70 yuan** |
| H2 2025 | ~920 yuan | ~810-830 yuan | **-90 to -110 yuan** |
| 2026 (after invoice price reduction) | ~800-850 yuan | ~850 yuan | **0 to +50 yuan** |

Throughout 2025, distributors essentially lost money selling each bottle of standard Wuliangye. After the invoice price reduction in 2026, they barely break even or make minimal profit.

**What does this mean?**

If a brand's distributors consistently lose money, there are only two possibilities:
1. Distributors "piggyback"—earning money by selling Wuliangye series wines or other products, with standard Wuliangye serving as a traffic driver
2. Distributors are exiting—Wuliangye's distributor network shrunk by net **90 dealers** in 2025

Reality probably involves both.

### Comparison with Kweichow Moutai: Real Pricing Power vs. False Pricing Power

| Product | Factory Price | Wholesale Price | Premium/Inversion |
|------|--------|------|----------|
| Feitian Kweichow Moutai | 1,169 yuan (before March 2026) → 1,269 yuan | **~1,560 yuan** | **+33% premium** |
| Eighth-Gen Standard Wuliangye | 1,019→900 yuan | ~850 yuan | **-6% to -17% inversion** |

*Data Sources: Sichuan Observation (Moutai wholesale), Channel Research (Standard Wuliangye wholesale)*

The gap is crystal clear:

- **Kweichow Moutai**: Factory price 1,269 yuan (after March 2026 increase), wholesale price 1,560—distributors earn **~300 yuan** per bottle. Distributors scramble for quotas.
- **Wuliangye**: Factory price 1,019 (later reduced to 900), wholesale price 850—distributors **lose 50-100 yuan** per bottle. Distributors are dumping inventory.

**The essence of pricing power isn't what you set as the price, but whether the market is willing to pay it.** Kweichow Moutai sets 1,269 and the market pays 1,560. Wuliangye sets 1,019 and the market only pays 850.

Wuliangye lowered its invoice price to 900 yuan in early 2026—the first official price cut in nearly a decade. The previous time was during the 2014 baijiu industry deep correction period.

**Positive View**: The price cut is a strategic choice—narrowing the gap between factory price and wholesale price, protecting distributor margins, achieving "short-term concessions for long-term stability."

**Negative View**: If truly pricing power existed, why cut prices? The market is voting with its feet—Wuliangye currently lacks pricing power above 1,000 yuan.

## Brand Power: Spent Twice Kweichow Moutai's Money, Earned Less Than Half Its Profit

### Sales Expense Comparison

| Metric | Wuliangye | Kweichow Moutai | Gap |
|------|--------|------|------|
| 2024 Sales Expenses | **10.7 billion** | ~5.5 billion | **Wuliangye is 2x Kweichow Moutai** |
| Sales Expense Growth | **+37.15%** | Single digits | Wuliangye is accelerating spending |
| Sales Expense Ratio | ~12% | ~3.5% | **3.4x difference** |
| Net Profit | 31.9 billion | ~82.3 billion | Kweichow Moutai is **2.6x** Wuliangye |

*Data Source: Company 2024/2025 Annual Reports*

**Wuliangye spent 2x Kweichow Moutai's sales expenses yet earned only 40% of its profit.**

What does this tell us?

- **Kweichow Moutai is a "lie back and sell" business**—the brand's natural pull is extremely strong, requiring almost no marketing
- **Wuliangye is a "push and sell" business**—requires continuous massive investment to maintain sales momentum

More concerning is the trend: 2024 Wuliangye sales expenses exploded **37%** year-on-year—spending more and more money to maintain sales, indicating the brand's natural pull is declining.

### Brand Value Quantified

| Metric | Wuliangye | Kweichow Moutai | Gap |
|------|--------|------|------|
| Brand Value (2025 Tsinghua Ranking) | **306 billion** | **662.6 billion** | Kweichow Moutai is **2.2x** Wuliangye |
| Market Cap (2026.5) | ~346 billion | ~1,760 billion | Kweichow Moutai is **5x** Wuliangye |

*Data Source: Brand Finance 2025*

The brand value gap expanded from a historical 1.5x to 2.2x. The market cap gap expanded even more from 3x to 5x.

**Is the trend improving or worsening? The answer is worsening.** Over the past decade, Kweichow Moutai's brand CAGR is approximately **12%**, while Wuliangye's is only **2%**. The gap is widening at an exponential rate.

## Moat Inventory: What Wuliangye Has and What It Lacks

### What Wuliangye Possesses

| Moat Type | Strength | Description |
|-----------|------|------|
| Brand Awareness | ★★★★ | Top brand in thousand-yuan price range, "Aroma King" |
| Ancient Cellar Pits | ★★★★ | Ming Dynasty ancient cellar pits continuously used for **600+ years**, pit age enhances wine quality |
| Consumer Scenario Lock-in | ★★★ | "Safe choice" for business banquets and weddings |
| Scale Effect | ★★★★ | Annual sales **1.6 million tons**, far exceeds Kweichow Moutai's ~500,000 tons |
| Financial Safety Cushion | ★★★★★ | Cash **127.4 billion**, zero interest-bearing debt |

### What Wuliangye Lacks

| Dimension | Problem | Severity |
|------|------|---------|
| Pricing Power | Wholesale price long below factory price, first price cut in decade | ★★★★★ |
| Consumer Mind Monopoly | "Best choice after Moutai" but highly substitutable | ★★★★ |
| Management Integrity | Two successive board chairmen fallen + 30.3 billion inflated revenue | ★★★★★ |
| Product Scarcity | Aroma-style process lower barrier than sauce-style, production unconstrained | ★★★ |
| Financial Attributes | Moutai has investment/collectible properties, Wuliangye hardly any | ★★★ |

### Is the Moat Widening or Narrowing?

| Evidence of Narrowing | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Brand Value Gap Widening | Kweichow Moutai/Wuliangye ratio expanded from 1.5x to 2.2x |
| Pricing Power Lost | First price reduction in decade |
| Sales Expenses Skyrocketing | 2024 +37%, spending more to sell the same amount |
| Competition Intensifying | Luzhou Laojiao 1573 wholesale price once approached standard Wuliangye parity |
| Governance Collapse | Two successive board chairmen investigated |

| Evidence Still Present | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Thousand-Yuan Price Range Leader | No replacement in sight short-term |
| Ancient Cellar Pits Irreproducible | 600-year pit age is core barrier for aroma-style quality |
| Consumer Scenario Necessity | Business banquets still need a "safe choice" |
| Cash Reserves | 127.4 billion, could sustain years of zero revenue |

**Comprehensive Assessment: The moat is slowly narrowing—contracting from a "wide moat" to a "medium moat."** It hasn't collapsed, but the company can no longer rest easy as before.

**However, on the other hand**—a down-cycle industry period is precisely the best time to test the real strength of the moat. If Wuliangye completes channel purging and management renewal in this cycle, the moat could widen again. Wuliangye faced similar difficulties during the 2014 baijiu deep correction period and achieved recovery in 3 years.

## Distribution Reality: How Are Distributors Doing?

### Channel Inventory

| Metric | Data | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Inventory Balance (H1 2025) | **17.7 billion** | +5% from year-start |
| Distributor Actual Inventory | Estimated **2-3 months** | Higher than company's stated 1.5-month standard |
| Net Distributor Decrease (2025) | **-90** | Active cleanup + natural attrition |

*Data Source: Wuliangye Annual Report, Channel Research*

### Channel Conflict Issues

Wuliangye launched a "digital wine certificate" system to trace flows, yet channel conflicts remain severe:
- E-commerce platform discounted prices as low as **820-860 yuan**
- Some offline channels reported **640-750 yuan** extreme low prices
- Major supermarkets and e-commerce platforms act as "price disruptors"

### Channel Vicious Cycle

```
Wholesale Inversion → Distributor Losses → Rush to Liquidate → Channel Conflict Worsens → Wholesale Price Falls Further
```

**Positive View**: After the 2026 invoice price reduction, inversion has narrowed; post-Chinese New Year channel feedback shows inventory down over **30%**. The worst may be past.

**Negative View**: Channel reform essentially "pays off past debt"—the company over-pushed inventory to distributors in past years to chase revenue growth; now it pays a larger price to digest. Kweichow Moutai never needs to do this—supply can't meet demand.

## A Critical Question: What Exactly Are Consumers Buying?

Back to the fundamental question: why do consumers buy Wuliangye?

**Kweichow Moutai Consumer Mindset**:
- "China's best baijiu"—needs no second choice
- "Social hard currency"—gifting Moutai requires no explanation
- "Investment/collectible"—has financial attributes

**Wuliangye Consumer Mindset**:
- "Best choice after Kweichow Moutai"—has a precondition
- "Premium price/performance"—substitute when can't get/afford Moutai
- "Safe choice"—doesn't disappoint but never impresses

**The problem is in this modifier—"after Kweichow Moutai."**

This positioning is both Wuliangye's safe zone (when Moutai is too expensive, consumers naturally step down) and ceiling (always a "second choice"). More critically, at the "after Moutai" position, Wuliangye's differentiation advantage is being eroded by Luzhou Laojiao 1573—their wholesale price gap narrowed from a historic 200 yuan to near parity.

**If consumers start thinking "Wuliangye and Luzhou Laojiao are basically the same"—then Wuliangye's most important moat would be cracking.**
