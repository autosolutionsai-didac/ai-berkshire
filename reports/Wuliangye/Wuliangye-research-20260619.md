## I. The Nature of the Business -- Duan Yongping's "the right business"

### One-sentence definition

**Wuliangye is a business that sells high-gross-margin distilled liquor on the back of 650-year-old ancient fermentation pits and brand mindshare -- in essence "liquid gold": a product with near-zero marginal cost, that never expires, and that gains value the longer it sits.**

**Key characteristics**:

1. **Zero interest-bearing debt.** No bank loans or bonds whatsoever; all liabilities are operating in nature (contract liabilities, taxes payable, etc.). In 2024, financial expenses were **negative 2.8 billion yuan** (net interest income), with 127.4 billion yuan of cash sitting on the balance sheet
2. **Extremely high capital returns.** ROE has sustained 23-25%, ROIC 22-24%, with almost no need for capital expenditure (annual capex of only 2.0-3.0 billion yuan, 3% of revenue)
3. **Growth has clearly slowed.** Revenue growth fell from 15.5% in 2021 to 7.1% in 2024, and net profit growth fell from 17.2% to 5.4%

> **Duan Yongping-style question**: What makes this business good?
> It's good because **the product needs almost no R&D, never expires, carries an 87% gross margin, has zero interest-bearing debt, and generates free cash flow roughly equal to net profit.** This is a "money-printing machine" business model. But the problem is -- this printing press is slowing down, and management has just cycled through two chairmen who got arrested.

## II. Moat Assessment -- Buffett's "economic moat"

### Item-by-Item Verification

| Moat type | Rating | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Brand / pricing power** | ★★★☆☆ Medium | Puwu's ex-factory price rose from 609 yuan in 2014 to 1,019 yuan in 2024; but the 2025 wholesale price fell to 780-810 yuan, a severe inversion. **Can raise the price but can't hold the price** -- pricing power fails the test |
| **Switching costs** | ★★☆☆☆ Weak | Baijiu brand loyalty is limited; the psychological barrier for consumers switching from Wuliangye to Guojiao 1573 is low. Unlike Moutai, which has become the "default choice" for high-end occasions |
| **Network effects** | ★☆☆☆☆ None | Consumer goods carry no network effects |
| **Economies of scale** | ★★★☆☆ Medium | Being second in the industry brings procurement and distribution advantages, but baijiu production isn't scale-driven -- ancient-pit capacity is the bottleneck |
| **Production barriers (ancient fermentation pits)** | ★★★★☆ Fairly strong | 179 Ming-dynasty ancient pits (650+ years), 679 pits over a century old, and 3.3 million pits of various vintages. Each gram of ancient pit mud contains over a hundred billion microbes; new pits take decades to mature and cannot be replicated. But Luzhou Laojiao likewise holds Ming-dynasty pits |

### The Moat Gap with Moutai

| Dimension | Moutai | Wuliangye |
|------|------|--------|
| Category exclusivity | Leader in the sauce-aroma (jiangxiang) category, where category = brand | The strong-aroma (nongxiang) track has many competitors (Luzhou Laojiao / Jiannanchun / Yanghe) |
| Price performance | Feitian's wholesale price has long stayed above its guidance price (still 1,800+ yuan in 2025) | Puwu's wholesale price has long stayed below its ex-factory price -- a price inversion |
| Social-currency attribute | Extremely strong -- "gifting Moutai" is the default social choice | Present but weaker than Moutai; a "replaceable second choice" |
| Capacity scarcity | Moutai Town's unique climate + Chishui River water source, a geographic monopoly | Ancient pits are scarce, but Yibin has no geographic exclusivity |

### Moat Trend

**Past 5 years: Narrowing.** The price gap between Puwu and Feitian has kept widening (from 500 yuan to 1,000+ yuan), and the brand-strength gap is growing. Within the strong-aroma track, the premiumization pushes by Fenjiu and Luzhou Laojiao are eroding Wuliangye's share.

**Next 5 years: Likely to remain under pressure.** The industry's "one dominant leader, several strong followers" structure is further entrenching, leaving Wuliangye stuck in the awkward position of "unable to reach Moutai above, being chased from below."

### Moat-Width Verdict: **Narrow moat**

Wuliangye has real competitive barriers (ancient pits + brand heritage), but they don't constitute a wide moat. Core evidence: **the wholesale price staying persistently below the ex-factory price shows supply exceeds demand, and brand premium is being eroded.** A consumer-goods company that truly holds a wide moat should not experience price inversion.

> **Buffett-style question**: Will this moat still be there in 10 years? What could destroy it?
> The ancient pits won't disappear, and the brand won't go to zero -- but the moat may keep narrowing. The biggest threat isn't any single competitor, but the **structural contraction of high-end baijiu demand** (young people not drinking baijiu) combined with **Moutai's continued siphoning of high-end mindshare**. Ten years from now, Wuliangye will still be among the industry's top three -- but "top three" and "worth investing in" are two different things.

## III. Inversion and Risk Checklist -- Munger's "invert, always invert"

### Failure-Path Analysis

| Risk path | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Structural decline in high-end baijiu demand | Medium-high | High | Only 31% of millennials drink baijiu; industry production volume has declined for 8 straight years |
| Continued deterioration in governance | Medium | High | Two consecutive chairmen have fallen; the new chairman has no industry experience; incentive mechanisms are lacking |
| Channel inventory builds up again | Medium | Medium-high | 2025 already exposed 6-8 months of inventory; although it has since been drawn down to 1 month, the impulse to stuff channels is rooted in the state-owned-enterprise assessment system |
| Moutai "dimensionality-reduction" attack | Low-medium | High | If Moutai lowers its ex-factory price or significantly ramps volume, Wuliangye's pricing room would be squeezed out entirely |
| Escalation of anti-corruption/alcohol-restriction policy | Low | Medium | Government-consumption spending already accounts for under 5%, so the marginal impact is limited |
| Consumption-tax reform | Low-medium | Medium-high | If the baijiu consumption tax shifts from the production stage to the retail stage, it could squeeze channel profits |

### Historical Analogy

**A similar case: the 2013-2015 baijiu winter.** After the "Eight-Point Regulation," Wuliangye's share price fell from a 2012 high of 36 yuan to a 2014 low of 15 yuan (down 58%), and net profit fell from 9.94 billion yuan in 2012 to 6.97 billion yuan in 2014 (down 30%). It then took 5 years to recover and surpass its prior high.

**Key difference**: The 2013 shock was policy-driven (government consumption was cut), and afterward business and personal consumption filled the gap. The 2025 shock is structural (slowing demand growth + young people not drinking baijiu), so the recovery path is far less certain.

> **Munger-style question**: Why wouldn't smart people buy?
> 1. Growth has already slowed to single digits, leaving limited room for valuation recovery
> 2. State-owned-enterprise governance is a structural problem that can't be solved just by swapping people
> 3. The industry's "volume down, price up" logic may be reaching its end -- volume is down, and now price is starting to fall too
> 4. At the same price, Moutai's business quality is far superior to Wuliangye's
