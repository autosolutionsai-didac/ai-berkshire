# Qualcomm (QCOM) Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analytical dimension: financial quality and intrinsic value | Data as of May 2026

## I. Revenue and profit trends

### Overall revenue
| Fiscal year | Revenue ($ billion) | YoY growth |
|------|---------------|---------|
| FY2023 | 35.8 | -19.2% |
| FY2024 | 38.9 | +8.7% |
| FY2025 | 44.3 | +13.7% |
| FY2026 Q1 | 12.3 | +5% YoY |

The steep decline in FY2023 stemmed from a global phone-market destocking cycle; FY2024-FY2025 saw a strong recovery. The 3-year CAGR (FY2022-FY2025) is roughly 0%, reflecting the semiconductor industry's strong cyclicality. But looking at the most recent two years, growth momentum has recovered.

### Segment performance (FY2025 Q2)
| Segment | Quarterly revenue ($ billion) | YoY growth |
|------|------------------|---------|
| QCT handsets | 6.93 | +12% |
| QCT automotive | 0.96 | +59% |
| QCT IoT | 1.58 | +27% |
| QTL licensing | ~1.60 | +4% |

Automotive is the fastest-growing engine, rising from about $0.6 billion/quarter in FY2024 to $1.1 billion/quarter in FY2026 Q1, with management targeting $4.0 billion for the full year FY2026 and $8.0 billion by FY2029.

### Profitability
- **Net margin**: 12.5% (GAAP) in FY2025, significantly affected by stock-based compensation and intangible-asset amortization
- **Operating margin**: 27.9% in FY2025, up 2.1 percentage points year-over-year
- **QTL pre-tax margin**: over 63%, the true profit core
- **Non-GAAP EPS**: $3.50 in FY2026 Q1, beating the market's expectation of $3.40

## II. Capital efficiency and cash flow

### ROIC (return on invested capital)
The five-year average ROIC for FY2021-FY2025 is 29.0%, with a median of 26.8%. This places Qualcomm in the top tier of the semiconductor industry, reflecting the enormous boost that the patent-licensing business provides to overall capital efficiency. As of March 2026, TTM ROIC is about 23.6% — somewhat lower, but still excellent.

### Free cash flow
FY2025 free cash flow was $12.8 billion, with an extremely high cash-conversion rate. The company is putting substantial cash toward shareholder returns — in March 2026 it announced a new $20 billion buyback program and raised the quarterly dividend from $0.89 to $0.92. As of April 2026, $5.4 billion of buybacks had already been completed.

### Balance sheet
| Metric | FY2025 data |
|------|------------|
| Cash and equivalents | $5.5 billion |
| Long-term debt | $14.8 billion |
| Net debt | $9.3 billion |
| Debt/equity ratio | 0.70 |
| Current ratio | 2.82 |
| Altman Z-Score | 6.35 (safe zone) |

The balance sheet is healthy, the current ratio is ample, and the long-term debt level is manageable. Net debt of about $9.3 billion equals less than a year of free cash flow.

## III. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level (May 2026)
| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Share price | $201.49 |
| Market cap | $212.4 billion |
| TTM PE | ~19x |
| Dividend yield | 1.77% |
| FCF yield | ~6.0% |

### Valuation assessment

**What a 19x PE means**: for a company with ROIC near 30%, ample free cash flow, and a patent-licensing "money printer," a 19x PE is not expensive. But several discounting factors are worth noting:
1. **Cyclical discount**: semiconductors are a strongly cyclical industry; currently in an upcycle, the PE may look cheaper than it really is
2. **Apple customer-loss discount**: Apple will completely stop using Qualcomm basebands by 2027, putting about $2 billion in annual revenue at risk of disappearing
3. **Geopolitical risk discount**: about 60% of Qualcomm's revenue comes from the China market, and the US-China tech rivalry adds uncertainty

### Simplified DCF valuation framework
Assumptions:
- FY2026 FCF of about $13.0 billion, with a 6-8% CAGR over the next 5 years (automotive/IoT growth offsetting the Apple loss)
- Terminal growth rate of 3%, discount rate of 10%
- Intrinsic value range of roughly $180-$240/share

The current price of $201 sits in the lower-middle of that range, providing some margin of safety, but it's not extremely cheap.

## IV. Peer comparison

| Company | PE (TTM) | ROIC | Net margin | Revenue growth |
|------|---------|------|--------|---------|
| Qualcomm | ~19x | 23.6% | 12.5% | +5% |
| Broadcom | ~35x | 15.2% | 24.1% | +22% |
| MediaTek | ~18x | 18.0% | 14.2% | +15% |
| Texas Instruments | ~32x | 20.5% | 30.1% | +3% |

Qualcomm's valuation is on the low side relative to its peers, mainly reflecting the market's concerns about Apple customer attrition and slowing growth in the phone market.

## V. Core judgment

At a 19x PE, Qualcomm offers a free cash flow yield of about 6% and a dividend yield of nearly 2%. The company's capital efficiency (ROIC near 30%) is top-tier within the semiconductor industry, and the balance sheet is healthy.

The key variable is whether automotive and IoT growth can be sufficient to offset the loss of the Apple baseband business. If the automotive business reaches $8 billion by FY2029 as planned (from about $4.0 billion annualized currently), then the impact of the Apple customer loss can be absorbed.

The current valuation is "reasonable-to-cheap" — not an extreme bargain, but for investors willing to hold for 3-5 years who believe in AI at the edge and automotive smart-tech, the risk-reward is acceptable.
