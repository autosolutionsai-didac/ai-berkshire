# Three Tracks for the Post-Smartphone Era

> *Understanding Qualcomm* series · Part 03 · AI and Diversification
> Reading time: approximately 10 minutes

---

## Why must Qualcomm "diversify"?

Of Qualcomm's $44.3 billion in FY2025 revenue, handset chips contributed roughly **$25 billion** (including Apple baseband), close to **57%** of total revenue. Add in QTL patent licensing revenue (which also comes mainly from phone makers), and handset-related revenue may account for close to **70%**. In short: Qualcomm is a company highly dependent on smartphone revenue, but the smartphone market's door is closing.

This structure carries two fatal vulnerabilities:

1. **Global smartphone shipments have already peaked** — at roughly **1.2 billion units per year**, with extremely limited room to grow
2. **The largest customer, Apple, is decoupling** — by 2027, **$5.7-5.9 billion** a year in baseband chip purchase revenue will go to zero

Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon said something telling in a Fortune interview in May 2026: "2026 is the year AI agents go mainstream — the era of the smartphone as the primary device is ending."

A handset-chip company's CEO publicly declaring that "the smartphone era is ending" is not an offhand remark — it's laying the groundwork for the company's strategic transformation.

Qualcomm's diversification isn't "icing on the cake" — it's **existential**.

(Sources: Qualcomm FY2025 10-K, Fortune interview)

## Track one: on-device AI — "running a large model on your phone"

### What is on-device AI?

Today's AI applications (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) mostly run in the cloud — your query is sent to a remote server, which computes and returns the result. On-device AI runs AI models directly on end devices — phones, PCs, cars — without needing a network connection.

The advantages of on-device AI:

| Dimension | Cloud AI | On-device AI |
|------|--------|--------|
| Latency | Requires network transmission; some latency | Local compute, nearly instant |
| Privacy | Data uploaded to the cloud | Data stays on the device |
| Cost | Every inference consumes cloud compute | Chip is bought once, inference is "free" |
| Offline use | Not possible | Possible |

### Qualcomm's position in on-device AI

Qualcomm's chips integrate dedicated AI accelerators (NPUs, Neural Processing Units):

- **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5** (phones): Hexagon NPU AI performance up **37%**, supports running large language models on-device
- **Snapdragon X2 series** (PCs): NPU compute reaches **80 TOPS**, ahead of contemporaneous Intel and AMD competitors
- **Snapdragon Digital Chassis** (automotive): supports in-vehicle AI agents, already integrated with Google Gemini

Qualcomm's strategic positioning is: **complementary to Nvidia — Nvidia is the "power plant" for cloud AI training and inference, while Qualcomm is the "AI engine" on end devices.**

### How much real value does on-device AI have?

This is a question that needs sober evaluation.

**The bull case**: AI features are becoming a core selling point for phones and PCs. AI phones can enable real-time translation, smart cameras, and local voice assistants — features that require a powerful NPU. Counterpoint Research forecasts that AI PCs will account for **59%** of global PC shipments in 2026. If AI features shorten upgrade cycles and lift ASPs for phones/PCs, Qualcomm can sell more chips at higher prices.

**The bear case**: On-device AI's "killer application" has yet to appear. Most consumers don't strongly perceive AI features on their phones — "a smarter voice assistant" and "better camera algorithms" are unlikely to make people upgrade early. If on-device AI ultimately turns out to be a marketing gimmick rather than a genuine need, the premium on Qualcomm's NPUs will be hard to sustain.

**What the data says**: In Q2 FY2026, handset chip revenue fell **13%** year-over-year, mainly due to reduced Apple baseband volume and China market destocking — on-device AI's "pull effect" has not yet shown up in the financials. Management's line is that "the AI-phone upgrade wave won't really take off until 2027."

**Judgment**: The direction of on-device AI is almost certainly correct, but there is substantial uncertainty about the timing and magnitude of the payoff. In the near term (1-2 years), on-device AI's lift to Qualcomm's revenue is limited; in the medium term (3-5 years), if AI agents genuinely go mainstream, Qualcomm — as the leading end-device chipmaker — would be a core beneficiary.

(Sources: Qualcomm Q2 FY2026 earnings, Counterpoint Research)

## Track two: automotive chips — the fastest-growing engine

### The story of going from zero to $5 billion

This is the **best-executed track** in Qualcomm's diversification strategy.

| Period | Annualized automotive chip revenue | Key milestone |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| FY2022 | ~$1.0 billion | Early stage |
| FY2024 | ~$2.8 billion | Overshot the $2 billion target |
| FY2025 Q4 | Quarterly revenue crossed **$1.0 billion** | First quarter to break the billion mark |
| FY2026 Q2 | **$1.33 billion** (+38% year-over-year) | Annualized run rate exceeds $5 billion |
| FY2026 target | Annualized run rate at exit **>$6 billion** | Management guidance |
| FY2029 target | **$8 billion** | Long-term plan |

Going from $1 billion to over $5 billion in four years — this growth rate is very rare in the semiconductor industry.

(Sources: Qualcomm's historical earnings, Q2 FY2026 earnings call)

### Qualcomm's unique advantage in automotive chips

The automotive chip market has many competitors (Nvidia, Mobileye, Renesas, NXP, and others), but Qualcomm's differentiation lies in **full-stack integration**:

| Automotive compute need | Traditional approach | Qualcomm's approach |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Digital cockpit (display, navigation, infotainment) | Separate chip | One-stop with Snapdragon Digital Chassis |
| Connectivity (5G, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth) | Separate chip | Integrated into the platform |
| ADAS (driver assistance) | Separate chip (Mobileye, Nvidia) | Integrated via Snapdragon Ride |
| V2X connectivity | Separate chip | Integrated into the platform |

The traditional approach requires automakers to source different chips from 3-4 suppliers; Qualcomm provides a one-stop solution, reducing integration complexity and total cost. BMW's Neue Klasse electric vehicle platform has fully adopted Qualcomm's Snapdragon Ride autonomous-driving system.

### What does a $45 billion design-win pipeline mean?

A "design win" is a key metric in the automotive chip industry — it represents an automaker having already chosen your chip for a future vehicle model. Because cars take **3-7 years** from design to mass production, a design win equals locked-in revenue for the next 3-7 years.

A **$45 billion** design-win pipeline, of which **$15 billion** is ADAS-related, provides very high revenue visibility.

**Counter-argument**: A design win is not the same as actual revenue. Automakers may delay production, cut volumes, or in extreme cases cancel projects entirely. Automotive chip gross margins are also typically lower than handset chip margins, so revenue growth doesn't necessarily translate into proportional profit growth. In addition, Nvidia's Orin/Thor platforms remain strong competitors in the high-end ADAS market.

### What will drive growth from $5 billion to $8 billion?

Management's FY2029 target is $8 billion. The incremental growth from $5 billion to $8 billion mainly comes from:

1. **ADAS penetration**: ADAS revenue currently accounts for roughly 30% of the automotive business, with $15 billion of the pipeline in ADAS — this share will keep rising
2. **The China market**: China has the highest EV penetration rate globally, and Qualcomm has a strong presence in China's auto market (NIO, XPeng, Li Auto, and others all use Snapdragon cockpit chips)
3. **Platform upgrades**: as older vehicle models are refreshed, they upgrade to higher-spec chips, lifting ASPs

**Can it be delivered?** Looking at the execution record of the past four years, management has **overdelivered on every commitment** in the automotive business. The original FY2024 target was $2 billion; the actual result was $2.8 billion. This is the area where Cristiano Amon's credibility is strongest. But the FY2029 target of $8 billion requires maintaining 15-20% annual growth for three straight years — and given the auto industry's uncertainties (slowing EV penetration, geopolitics, etc.), applying a 20% haircut is a prudent stance.

(Sources: Qualcomm earnings, Seeking Alpha, S&P Global Automotive)

## Track three: PC processors — chewing the hardest bone

### The Snapdragon X series enters the Windows market

Qualcomm has entered the Windows PC market with the Snapdragon X Elite/X Plus series, directly challenging Intel's and AMD's traditional territory.

**Advantages**:
- Built on the ARM architecture, with power consumption and battery life far superior to x86 chips (over **20 hours** of battery life)
- NPU compute of **80 TOPS** (X2 series), ahead of contemporaneous Intel and AMD
- Certified under Microsoft's Copilot+ PC standard, tied into the Windows AI ecosystem
- Already more than **100** laptop designs in mass production or development

**Reality**:
- Market share of roughly **10%** (as of end-2025, per PCWorld data), still small
- x86 software compatibility issues aren't fully resolved (some professional software runs at reduced performance on ARM)
- Faces strong competition from Apple's M-series chips (more mature in ecosystem and performance)
- Intel's Lunar Lake and AMD are also catching up on power efficiency

**Judgment**: The PC market is the track with the **slowest progress and greatest resistance** in Qualcomm's diversification strategy. A 10% share shows the product has been validated by the market, but going from 10% to 20% requires solving the fundamental software-ecosystem problem. The payoff on this track may take more than 5 years to materialize.

**Counter-argument**: Qualcomm's real motivation for entering PCs may not be short-term market share, but rather securing a position at the entry point of the "AI PC" era. If on-device AI truly becomes a core PC feature, Qualcomm's NPU advantage could make it the platform of choice for AI PCs. Counterpoint forecasts that AI PCs will account for 59% of global shipments in 2026 — if this trend holds, it's a structural positive for Qualcomm.

(Sources: PCWorld, Counterpoint Research, WindowsCentral)

## CEO Cristiano Amon's diversification report card

After Amon took over as CEO in 2021, "shedding over-reliance on handsets" became the top strategic priority. Five years on, here's the report card:

| Strategic commitment | Actual progress | Grade |
|---------|---------|------|
| Automotive revenue to reach $2 billion by FY2024 | Actual $2.8 billion (40% overshoot) | A |
| Raise the share of non-handset revenue | Rose from ~30% to ~40% | B+ |
| Mass-produce the self-designed Oryon architecture | Achieved; total victory in the ARM litigation | A |
| Break into the PC market | Share ~10%, some progress but slow | B- |
| Automotive annualized revenue >$6 billion by FY2026 | Annualized run rate already exceeds $5 billion as of Q2 FY2026, on track | A- |

**Overall assessment**: Amon is a technically grounded CEO with a clear strategy and strong execution. The automotive business's overdelivery is the best endorsement. The Nuvia acquisition has proven to be an extremely high-return strategic decision.

**Deductions**: PC market progress is slower than expected; concentration risk in the China market (~60% of revenue) has not yet been effectively diversified.

**A detail worth noting**: In March 2026, Qualcomm announced a new **$20 billion** buyback program and raised its quarterly dividend from $0.89 to $0.92. This shows management's confidence in the company's cash-flow generation and its willingness to actively return capital to shareholders — a plus in the semiconductor industry, especially in contrast with companies that sit on large cash piles without distributing any.

(Sources: Qualcomm's historical earnings, Bloomberg)

## Overall assessment of the three tracks

| Track | Current scale | Growth certainty | Moat strength | Payoff timeline |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| On-device AI | Ramping, NPU-leading | Moderate (killer app not yet present) | Moderate (MediaTek also chasing) | 3-5 years |
| Automotive chips | Annualized run rate >$5 billion | **High** ($45 billion design-win pipeline) | **Strong** (auto-grade lock-in effect) | Already delivering |
| PC processors | Share ~10% | Low (high ecosystem barriers) | Moderate (NPU-leading but x86 still strong) | 5+ years |

**Core judgment**: Qualcomm's diversification strategy has the **right direction, excellent automotive execution, a clear AI positioning, and a PC track that requires patience**. Among the three tracks, automotive chips are the highest-certainty growth engine that is already delivering; on-device AI is the highest-potential but highest-uncertainty bet; PC is the longest-payback long-term investment.

Can the three tracks together fill the $5.7-5.9 billion annual revenue gap left by Apple's decoupling? Mathematically, the incremental growth from automotive chips alone (FY2026 ~$5 billion vs. the FY2029 target of $8 billion) would be enough to cover it. But this calculation implicitly assumes "automotive growth proceeds without surprises" — and the auto industry itself is a field full of variables.
