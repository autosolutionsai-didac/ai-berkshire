## Back to the Most Fundamental Question

Across four parts we've laid the whole household out on the table: Taotian's profits are rich but its moat is narrowing; Alibaba Cloud is accelerating but its margin is only a quarter of AWS's; the frontier businesses have moved from heavy losses to slim profits, which doesn't change the valuation.

Putting it all together, our neutral-case forecast for Alibaba Group's ten-years-out profit is approximately **350-460 billion yuan** — Taotian 240-280 billion, Alibaba Cloud 80-140 billion, frontier businesses 20-45 billion. Ten years from now, Alibaba will most likely still exist.

*Source: ai-berkshire 10-year profit projection report*

But "still existing" and "worth holding" are two different things. This finale asks only one question: **at the current price, is what you're paying for this company reasonable?** Answering it requires first assessing management's credibility, the true magnitude of the risks, and the logic of the valuation itself.

## I. Management: Who Is at the Helm?

### 1.1 Eddie Wu — the return of a technical founding veteran

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Background | One of the "18 Arhats" (founding team), employee number six, an engineer by training |
| Power | Simultaneously holds Group CEO + Taotian CEO + Alibaba Cloud CEO |
| Direction | Dual strategy of "user-first + AI-driven" — the direction is correct |
| Risk | One person running three trillion-yuan-scale businesses — an extremely wide span of control |
| Key decisions | Streamlining the structure into four major segments, the 380-billion-yuan AI investment, an all-out push on Taobao Flash Sale |

**Positive assessment:** Wu's technical background lets him understand the essence of AI strategy without being fooled by buzzwords. As a founding veteran, he carries enough authority within the organization to drive change. The FY2026 strategic direction (focus + AI + instant retail) is the clearest the company has articulated in the past three years.

**Negative assessment:** One person simultaneously serving as the top executive of three businesses has rarely succeeded in business history. His management style leans toward delegation — which may not be sufficient on battlefields like e-commerce and instant retail that demand fine-grained operational execution. Taotian's GMV growth has not visibly improved since he took over as its direct manager.

### 1.2 Joe Tsai — master of capital allocation

Joe Tsai is the figure in Alibaba's management team who gives investors the most reassurance.

**Positives:**
- FY2025 buybacks of $11.9 billion — one of the largest in Chinese ADR history
- Total buyback authorization increased to **$65 billion**, valid through March 2027
- Tsai personally increased his Alibaba stake by $150 million through his Blue Pool fund
- In a March 2026 speech at Stanford, he publicly admitted: "Alibaba's biggest mistake was forgetting who its real customer is," and "it was foolish to pitch investors on six separate business groups."

This kind of public willingness to admit error is exceedingly rare among Chinese entrepreneurs.

**Negatives:**
- The premise for that admission of error is that these mistakes have already cost "over 100 billion yuan"
- The acquisitions of Youku, Ele.me, Sun Art Retail, Intime, and other poorly integrated assets were all completed with Tsai's involvement
- Alibaba's capital allocation over the past five years has, on the whole, **destroyed value** (compared with Tencent's investment portfolio, which has continued to appreciate over the same period)
- Admitting error is not the same as creating value — it is only stopping the bleeding

### 1.3 Jack Ma — the subtle return of the soul of the company

- Re-emerged as active in 2025: attending a symposium for private-enterprise entrepreneurs, participating in an Alibaba Cloud launch event, appearing at Alibaba's campus
- The Ma family has increased its stake in the stock, and may have already overtaken SoftBank as Alibaba's largest single shareholder
- The market reads this as a signal of "normalization of the political relationship"

**But an investment should never depend on any individual's aura.** A good company should run well even after its founder has completely stepped away.

### 1.4 Duan Yongping's stance

Duan Yongping is the most valuable weathervane for judging what counts as a "good business." His actions:

| Time | Action |
|------|------|
| 2024 | Increased his Alibaba position |
| Q3 2025 | **Cut his position by more than 25%** |
| Q4 2025 | Continued reducing his position |

Duan's actions speak for themselves: **in his eyes, Alibaba is not a stock that is "obviously so cheap you must buy it."** What he said in 2023 — that he "can't see through" e-commerce — still holds true today.

## II. Governance Structure: The Double-Edged Sword of the Partnership System

### 2.1 The design of the system

Alibaba's Partnership (currently 36 people) has the right to nominate a majority of the board's members. Jack Ma and Joe Tsai are the only two "permanent partners."

**This means external shareholders have almost no say in governance.**

### 2.2 Advantages

- Ensures strategic stability for the founding team
- Avoids being forced into short-term moves by activist capital
- Enables management to execute long-term strategies like the "380-billion-yuan AI investment" — under a shareholder-vote system, such a plan might well be rejected

### 2.3 Risks

- When management decides to burn 86 billion yuan on instant retail or spend 380 billion yuan on AI, outside shareholders cannot stop it
- There is no sunset clause — the control structure never automatically expires
- Historically, a large number of low-return investments (Youku, Ele.me, Sun Art Retail, and others) were made under this exact governance structure
- If management makes a wrong call, shareholders have no corrective mechanism

**For a value investor, the partnership system is a risk factor that must be priced in.** If Alibaba's intrinsic value is X, the partnership structure warrants at least a 10-20% discount.

## III. Risk Checklist

### 3.1 Competitive risk (the largest known risk)

- Taotian's share could continue slipping from 37% down to 25-30%
- Douyin E-commerce's GMV could catch up to Taotian's as early as 2028-2029
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine is closing in fast on Alibaba Cloud
- The three-way instant-retail brawl (Alibaba vs. Meituan vs. JD) could be a war of attrition lasting 3-5 years

### 3.2 Capital expenditure risk

- 380 billion yuan of AI investment plus 86 billion yuan of instant-retail losses — burning cash on two fronts at once
- FY2026 free cash flow has already turned negative, at -46.6 billion yuan
- If AI investment returns fall short of expectations, the capacity for buybacks and dividends will be squeezed
- Tsai himself has warned that "there may be a bubble in AI data centers"

### 3.3 Geopolitical risk

- U.S. SEC scrutiny of Chinese ADRs over potential delisting
- The VIE structure continues to be criticized as carrying "fraud risk"
- Alibaba has completed a dual primary listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, reducing reliance on a single market
- But the risk of forced selling by U.S. institutional investors and a widening valuation discount remains

### 3.4 Regulatory risk

- The 18.2-billion-yuan antitrust fine of 2021 is in the past, and the current regulatory tone leans toward "supporting development"
- But a defining feature of Chinese regulation is "policy reversibility" — today's encouragement does not guarantee tomorrow will not bring a tightening
- The instant-retail subsidy war has already drawn regulatory attention (the Economic Daily publicly criticized it in March 2026)
- Ant Group's financial holding company license remains undecided

### 3.5 Macro risk

- China's CPI stayed at low levels through 2024-2025, with weak consumption
- If deflation persists for more than five years, e-commerce, local services, and cloud would all come under pressure simultaneously
- This is the **most systemic risk**, and it is nearly impossible to hedge

**Neutral SOTP valuation of ~$476.4 billion vs. current market cap of ~$318.0 billion — implying roughly 50% upside.**
**Conservative SOTP valuation of ~$247.5 billion vs. current market cap of ~$318.0 billion — the current price looks expensive on this basis.**

The limitations of the SOTP valuation are clear:
1. Taotian's 12x EBITA multiple assumes its profits are sustainable — if growth falls to 0%, the market might assign only 8-10x
2. Alibaba Cloud's 8x P/S multiple is benchmarked against global cloud vendors — but Alibaba Cloud's margin is only 9% (versus 36% for AWS), so the premium is questionable
3. Holding-company discounts in the Chinese market are typically 20-30% (versus 10-15% in developed markets)

## V. Margin of Safety Assessment

### 5.1 Evidence supporting "a margin of safety exists"

1. **Net cash of 389 billion yuan** (end of FY2025), about 17% of market cap — provides a hard floor
2. **P/E of 17x, 47% below its historical average** — if you believe Alibaba is not going away, room for valuation recovery exists
3. **FY2025 shareholder returns of $16.5 billion** — $11.9 billion in buybacks plus $4.6 billion in dividends, shrinking shares outstanding by 5-6% annualized
4. **Alibaba Cloud's accelerating growth** — external revenue up 40%, with AI revenue now exceeding 30% of the total
5. **Management admitting mistakes and refocusing** — divesting non-core assets, streamlining the organizational structure, and the founder increasing his stake

### 5.2 Evidence supporting "this is a value trap"

1. **Non-GAAP net profit plunged 62%** — whatever the cause, profit is disappearing
2. **Free cash flow turned negative, at -46.6 billion yuan** — burning cash simultaneously on 380 billion yuan of AI investment and 86 billion yuan of Flash Sale losses
3. **Taotian's share is still declining** — the core business's moat is narrowing
4. **ROE of only 13%** — on the low side for an internet company, dragged down by a large cash pile and low-efficiency investments
5. **Fighting three wars at once** — the e-commerce defense, the instant-retail offensive, and the AI investment push; history offers almost no precedent of winning all three simultaneously
6. **Duan Yongping is reducing his position** — the person who best understands "good businesses" is selling

### 5.3 Conclusion

**Alibaba more closely resembles a "transition-phase company with a margin of safety" than a classic "value trap."**

Reasoning:
- The hallmark of a classic value trap is "profit disappearing irreversibly" — Alibaba's profit decline is a deliberate management choice, not a sign of vanishing demand
- Net cash plus aggressive buybacks provide protection: "even if the judgment turns out wrong, the downside is limited"
- Alibaba Cloud's accelerating growth provides optionality: "if the judgment turns out right, the upside is enormous"

**But the margin of safety is not large enough.**

Using a 30% margin-of-safety standard (i.e., buy at a price no higher than 70% of intrinsic value):
- Neutral intrinsic value of approximately $153 × 70% = **$107**
- Current price of $134.58 → **not in "clearly cheap" territory**
- If it pulls back to **$100-110** (around HK$100 in Hong Kong), the margin of safety would rise to 30-40% — at which point it would be much more attractive to buy

## VI. A Framework for Thinking Under Different Risk Appetites

The following is not "trading advice" — it simply lays out what different types of investors, by risk appetite, should focus on when looking at Alibaba:

| Risk appetite | Core focus | Signal to wait for |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **Can tolerate 2-3 years of declining profit** | Long-term return on the AI investment | Whether Alibaba Cloud's revenue growth can sustain 30%+ |
| **Needs to see a profit inflection point** | When free cash flow returns to positive growth | Instant-retail losses narrowing + capex growth slowing |
| **Strictly requires a margin of safety** | Whether the current price is well below a conservative valuation | Alibaba Cloud's margin breaking above 15%, Taotian's share decline stopping |

### Signals to add to a position

1. Taotian's GMV share stops declining and turns up sequentially
2. Alibaba Cloud's AI revenue maintains 30%+ quarterly growth
3. Instant retail's EBITA losses narrow, with a profitability inflection point emerging
4. Free cash flow returns to positive growth
5. Ant Group's IPO makes substantive progress

### Signals to reduce a position

1. Taotian's CMR (customer management revenue) growth turns negative (pricing strategy failing)
2. Alibaba Cloud's share is further eaten away by Volcano Engine / Huawei Cloud
3. Revenue growth fails to visibly accelerate after the 380-billion-yuan AI investment
4. Buyback pace slows significantly
5. The regulatory environment for Chinese ADRs deteriorates

## VII. Final Judgment

Alibaba is a company currently in the middle of a strategic transition of **"breaking the old to build the new."**

**"The old":** Taotian's e-commerce moat is being continuously eroded by Pinduoduo and Douyin — its share has dropped 14 percentage points in four years, and this erosion may well be structural.

**"The new":** Alibaba Cloud is showing strong momentum — its AI cloud share of 35.8% is far in the lead, with revenue growth of 34%. But its margin is only 9%, and the return on the 380-billion-yuan investment is highly uncertain.

**"The cost":** FY2026 Non-GAAP net profit plunged 62%, free cash flow turned negative at -46.6 billion yuan, all while fighting the e-commerce defense, the instant-retail offensive, and the AI investment push at once.

**"The cushion":** P/E of 17x, 47% below its historical average; net cash of 389 billion yuan; FY2025 buybacks plus dividends of $16.5 billion.

Putting it all together:

> **Alibaba is not a "good business you can just sit back and collect money from" — it is a position that "bets on the success of a transformation."** You need to be willing to accept 2-3 years of declining profit in exchange for a re-rating if the transformation succeeds. If you're not willing to wait, or you don't believe Alibaba Cloud can replicate AWS's path to success, this is not the stock for you.

Ten years from now, Alibaba could plausibly look like any of several pictures:

| Scenario | Description |
|------|------|
| Bull | AI cloud becomes the core engine, with annual revenue exceeding $100 billion; e-commerce share stabilizes above 30%; market cap of $500 billion+ |
| Base case | E-commerce share declines slowly but profit stabilizes; cloud becomes the second-largest profit source; buybacks continue, market cap of $300-400 billion |
| Bear | E-commerce share falls below 25%; AI investment fails to generate sufficient returns; the company becomes a "Japanese Yahoo"-style relic |
| Extreme | Forced breakup / delisting / major regulatory crackdown |

*Source: Li Lu-perspective risk assessment report*

How likely each scenario is depends on your own judgment of Taotian's moat and Alibaba Cloud's execution — this isn't a math problem with a single correct answer.

Under the base-case scenario, holding Alibaba for ten years would produce an annualized return of about 8-12% (including buybacks and dividends). Not bad, but not outstanding either.

**If you're asking "is it worth holding," the answer depends on your opportunity cost** — whether you have an alternative with higher certainty and a return no lower than 10%. If you do, Alibaba may not be the best choice. If you don't, Alibaba's combination of "cushion plus AI optionality" is worth considering.
