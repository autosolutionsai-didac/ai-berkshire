# The Empire's Frontier — International Commerce, Instant Retail, and Other Businesses

> *Understanding Alibaba* series · Episode 04

## 400 Billion Yuan in Revenue, Near-Zero Profit Contribution

The first two pieces dissected Alibaba's two core assets: Taotao (Taobao/Tmall) is the profit engine, and Alibaba Cloud is the growth option.

This piece takes on the hardest-to-explain part of Alibaba — the businesses that are neither core nor negligible, together accounting for nearly 40% of revenue: the "frontier of the empire." International commerce (AIDC), Cainiao logistics, Digital Media & Entertainment, DingTalk, Freshippo... combined revenue of nearly 400 billion yuan, more than JD Logistics' entire annual revenue.

But this 400 billion yuan generates almost no profit. It's either losing money, barely breaking even, or has already been sold off at a loss.

The collective silence of the frontier businesses is part of the ten-year ledger of Alibaba's capital allocation. This piece takes them apart one by one, judging which still hold value and which should be cut loose.

---

## I. Alibaba International Digital Commerce (AIDC): From Cash Burn to Bleeding Stopped

### 1.1 What Is AIDC?

AIDC (Alibaba International Digital Commerce) comprises:
- **AliExpress**: cross-border e-commerce, focused on Europe, Latin America, and the Middle East
- **Lazada**: Southeast Asian e-commerce platform
- **Trendyol**: Turkish e-commerce platform (86% owned by Alibaba), Alibaba's most profitable overseas asset
- **Daraz**: South Asian market
- **Miravia**: Spanish market

### 1.2 Financial Profile

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Annual revenue | 132.3 billion yuan | **144.2 billion yuan** | +9% |
| Q4 quarterly revenue | — | 35.4 billion yuan | +6% |
| Q4 adjusted EBITA | — | Loss of 138 million yuan | **Sharply narrowed** |
| FY2025 full-year loss | ~15.1 billion yuan | — | — |

*Source: Alibaba FY2026 Q4 earnings report*

**Key signal: AIDC is moving from "heavy losses" toward "breakeven."** The FY2026 Q1 quarterly loss had already narrowed to 59 million yuan, nearly breaking even. The Q4 loss was just 138 million — if this trend holds, achieving breakeven in FY2027 is plausible.

### 1.3 Competitive Landscape: A Four-Way Battle

| Platform | 2025 Global GMV (estimate) | Core markets |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| Amazon third-party | ~$750 billion | Global |
| Shopee (Sea) | ~$130 billion | Southeast Asia + Brazil |
| **Alibaba AIDC** | **~$80-90 billion** | Global (fragmented) |
| Temu (Pinduoduo) | ~$70-80 billion | Europe/US + global |
| SHEIN | ~$50 billion | Europe/US + Middle East |
| TikTok Shop | ~$35-40 billion | Southeast Asia + Europe/US |

*Source: Marketplace Pulse 2026Q1, company earnings estimates*

**AIDC's problem is "regional fragmentation with no global network effect":**
- AliExpress has an edge in Europe and Latin America but faces intense price-war pressure from Temu
- Lazada is second in Southeast Asia (25-28% share), but far behind Shopee (45-50%), and TikTok Shop is catching up fast
- Trendyol is number one in Turkey and expanding into the Middle East — this is the most certain asset in the portfolio
- Competitive dynamics differ by region, preventing any unified global flywheel effect

### 1.4 The Impact of US-China Relations

- In 2025 the US eliminated the "de minimis" exemption, hitting Temu's, SHEIN's, and AliExpress's US businesses
- AIDC's main battleground has shifted from the US to "non-US markets" — Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and parts of Europe
- Cross-border compliance costs continue to rise (EU DSA, various countries' data-localization requirements)

### 1.5 Assessment

| Dimension | Neutral forecast |
|------|---------|
| FY2028 breakeven | Likely achieved (current trend is already close) |
| Revenue in 10 years | 300-530 billion yuan (15% CAGR) |
| Operating margin in 10 years | 4-6% (referencing Shopee's steady-state margin) |
| Operating profit in 10 years | **12-22 billion yuan** |
| Strategic positioning | Can contribute profit, but **unlikely to take the baton from Taotao** |

**AIDC will not become the next Taotao — it is more like a portfolio of regionalized e-commerce assets.** Trendyol is the most valuable piece. AliExpress's Choice model is improving unit economics. Lazada will most likely be marginalized but won't disappear.

## II. Cainiao Logistics: Fulfillment Backend, or Independent Asset?

### 2.1 Cainiao's Shifting Position

Cainiao was once Alibaba's most promising spin-off IPO candidate — it nearly went public independently in 2023. But the IPO was called off in 2024, and Cainiao was reintegrated into the group, folded into the AIDC structure.

**Downgrading from "independent IPO candidate" to "AIDC's fulfillment department" is a significant signal.**

### 2.2 Financial Profile

| Metric | FY2025 |
|------|--------|
| Annual revenue | 101.3 billion yuan (+2%) |
| EBITA | 300 million yuan (-78%) |

*Source: Alibaba FY2025 annual report*

### 2.3 Competitive Position

Cainiao's strength lies in **cross-border logistics** — a logistics network spanning more than 200 countries and regions globally, with broad overseas-warehouse coverage. This is the core fulfillment capability underpinning AIDC (AliExpress, Lazada).

But in the domestic logistics market, Cainiao is far from the leader:
- JD Logistics: integrated in-house warehousing and delivery, best user experience
- The Tongda network (ZTO, YTO, Yunda, etc.): lowest prices, largest scale
- J&T Express: the fastest-growing disruptor
- SF Express: the premium-courier leader

### 2.4 Assessment

**Logistics is fundamentally not a good business** — gross margins of 5-10%, capital-intensive, no pricing power. Duan Yongping rarely invests in logistics companies.

Cainiao's value lies in being the **necessary infrastructure** that keeps AIDC's cross-border e-commerce running. Without Cainiao's logistics network, AliExpress's and Lazada's fulfillment capabilities would deteriorate sharply.

**Cainiao should be viewed as AIDC's "cost center," not an independent profit center.** Annual profit ten years out may fall in the range of 5-13 billion yuan — not a large amount.

## III. Digital Media & Entertainment (renamed "Whale Entertainment"): The Biggest Historical Burden

### 3.1 Alibaba's Entertainment Track Record

Alibaba's investments in the entertainment space amount to a textbook case of failed cash-burning:

| Acquisition/investment | Time | Amount invested | Outcome |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Youku Tudou | 2016 | ~27 billion yuan | Long-term losses, long-video ranked third/fourth |
| Xiami Music | 2013 | — | Shut down in 2021 |
| Alibaba Pictures | 2014 | ~17 billion yuan | Long-term losses, later marginally profitable |
| Damai | 2017 | — | Ticketing market has moats; one of the few bright spots |

Joe Tsai admitted in a March 2026 speech at Stanford that Alibaba paid "over 100 billion yuan" for strategic misjudgments — Digital Media & Entertainment was the largest single piece of that.

### 3.2 Current Status

- Officially renamed "Whale Entertainment" in 2025
- FY2025 full-year scale was about 22.3 billion yuan, just 2% of the group total
- FY2025 full-year loss was still 550 million yuan, but Q4 turned profitable (Youku turned around)
- Damai holds a near-monopoly in the live-event ticketing market — the most valuable asset within the entertainment segment

### 3.3 Assessment

**Digital Media & Entertainment is one of the biggest investment mistakes in Alibaba's history.** The nature of the content business — hit-dependent on luck, with no economies of scale — is entirely different from the platform-plus-traffic model Alibaba excels at.

Joe Tsai has acknowledged the mistake, but the 100-billion-yuan cost has already been paid.

The most likely outcome for entertainment in ten years is **further shrinkage, a spin-off, or a merger into other businesses.** It will not become a profit source for Alibaba — at best it becomes "no longer a money-loser."

## IV. DingTalk: The Underrated Potential Stock

DingTalk may be the most underrated asset in Alibaba's "other" segment:

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Users | 700 million+ |
| ARR | >$200 million (H1 FY2025) |
| Domestic collaborative-office share | Number one |
| Synergy with Alibaba Cloud | Cross-selling to enterprise customers |

DingTalk's network effect is real — once a company deploys DingTalk as its internal collaboration tool, employee switching costs are extremely high. This resembles the lock-in effect Alibaba Cloud enjoys with its enterprise customers.

**But DingTalk's problem is that it's too small** — $200 million in ARR is less than 0.5% of Alibaba's trillion-yuan revenue base. Even growing at 30% for five years, that's only 6-7 billion yuan in revenue.

Ten years from now, DingTalk could become "a pretty good SaaS business," but its impact on Alibaba's overall valuation will be negligible.

## V. Exited Businesses: The Price of Admitting Mistakes

Alibaba completed two major divestitures in 2024-2025:

| Asset | Sale date | Sale price | Loss |
|------|---------|--------|------|
| Intime Retail | Dec 2024 | 7.4 billion yuan | ~9.3 billion yuan |
| Sun Art Retail (RT-Mart) | Jan 2025 | HK$13.1 billion | ~13.0 billion yuan |
| **Total loss** | | | **~22.5 billion yuan** |

This marks **the first time in Alibaba's history that it has admitted "non-core business" at scale and proactively exited.**

Viewed positively: admitting a mistake takes courage, and cutting losses beats continuing to burn cash.
Viewed negatively: a 22.5-billion-yuan loss is real money, proof that Alibaba's past "buy-buy-buy" capital-allocation strategy generated poor returns.

## VI. Overall Assessment of the Frontier Businesses: Assets or Burdens?

| Business | Asset or burden | Neutral profit forecast in 10 years | Keyword |
|------|-------------|------------------|--------|
| AIDC (international commerce) | **Asset** (bleeding is stopping, growth potential) | 12-22 billion yuan | Regionalized, unlikely to dominate |
| Cainiao | **Neutral** (AIDC's infrastructure, not independently valuable) | 5-13 billion yuan | Cost center |
| Digital Media & Entertainment | **Burden** (shrinking, historical losses over 100 billion) | 0-1 billion yuan | Should be wound down soon |
| DingTalk | **Potential stock** (but too small in scale) | 2-5 billion yuan | A good business, but immaterial |
| Instant retail | **Strategic investment** (short-term black hole, long-term defensive asset) | 0-5 billion yuan | See Episode 02 |
| Freshippo | **Pending divestiture** (retail is not a good business) | Marginal profit or loss | Awaiting disposal |

**Combined neutral profit forecast for the frontier businesses in ten years: roughly 20-45 billion yuan.**

Set against Alibaba Group's total profit pool of 400-500 billion yuan, that's no more than about 10%.

**Conclusion: the frontier businesses as a whole are shifting from "a heavily loss-making burden" to "a marginally profitable auxiliary" — the trend is right, but they will not become a pillar of Alibaba's valuation.** Alibaba's valuation story, in the end, still comes back to its two core assets: Taotao and Alibaba Cloud.

## VII. Duan Yongping's "Focus" Test

Duan Yongping values "focus" above almost everything else. Good businesses are usually focused ones.

Alibaba's 2026 business map: e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, internationalization, logistics, entertainment, SaaS, local services, AI large models...

Even after consolidating into four major segments, the company still spans at least seven different industries. Joe Tsai himself has admitted that pitching investors on six business groups back in the day was "foolish."

The good news: management is subtracting — selling Intime, selling Sun Art, shrinking Digital Media & Entertainment, folding Cainiao into AIDC, folding Ele.me into Taotao.

The bad news: the subtraction isn't happening fast enough. Freshippo hasn't been dealt with yet, and Digital Media & Entertainment hasn't been wound down. And while subtracting in some areas, the company is adding aggressively in two others — instant retail and AI — with combined annual cash burn exceeding 200 billion yuan.

**"Adding and subtracting at the same time" — this is Alibaba's greatest strategic tension right now.**
