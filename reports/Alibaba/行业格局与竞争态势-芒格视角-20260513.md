# Alibaba Industry Landscape and Competitive Dynamics: An In-Depth Study

> Researcher: Industry Research Analyst (Munger's Perspective)
> Date: 2026-05-13
> Information richness: Grade A
> Core question: Is Alibaba's competitive position actually improving, or continuing to deteriorate?

## I. Panorama of China's E-Commerce Industry: Scale and Landscape

### 1.1 Market size

In 2024, China's online retail sales reached 15.5 trillion yuan, up 7.2% year-over-year, keeping China the world's largest online retail market for 12 consecutive years. Of this, online retail sales of physical goods came to 13.08 trillion yuan, up 6.5%.

**Key judgment:** The growth rate of China's overall e-commerce market has shifted from the high-speed expansion of 2015-2020 (double-digit growth) to a low-to-mid-single-digit pace (6-8%). This means the industry has entered an era of stock-market competition, in which share battles are effectively a zero-sum game.

### 1.2 GMV and market share by platform (2024)

| Platform | 2024 GMV (RMB trillion) | Market share | YoY growth |
|------|-------------------|----------|----------|
| Taotian Group | ~8.0 | ~37.3% | ~6% |
| Pinduoduo | ~5.2 | ~21.0% | ~20%+ |
| JD.com | ~4.5 | ~17.4% | ~13% |
| Douyin E-commerce | ~3.5 | ~13%+ | ~30% |
| Kuaishou E-commerce | ~1.39 | ~5%+ | ~17% |

**Note:** Market share figures vary depending on the measurement basis. On a Tmall-only, B2C basis the share is 32.7%, but Taotian as a whole (including C2C) is approximately 37.3% on a full-scope basis. The figures above use the full-scope basis.

## II. Evolution of the Competitive Landscape: Share Migration, 2021-2025

### 2.1 How much has Alibaba lost?

| Year | Alibaba share | Pinduoduo share | Douyin + Kuaishou share | JD.com share |
|------|----------|------------|---------------|----------|
| 2021 | 51.3% | 15.9% | 9.2% | ~20% |
| 2022 | ~46% | ~18% | ~14% | ~18% |
| 2023 | 39.2% | ~20% | ~19.3% | ~17% |
| 2024 | 37.3% | ~21% | ~21.4% | ~17.4% |
| 2026E | ~36.2% | ~22% | ~22.7% | ~16% |

**Over four years, Alibaba's share fell from 51.3% to 37.3%, a loss of roughly 14 percentage points.**

### 2.2 Who took it?

The direction of share flow is very clear:

- **Douyin + Kuaishou:** Rose from 9.2% to 21.4%, an increase of roughly 12 percentage points — the biggest "predator" by far. Livestream and short-video commerce has consumed Alibaba's lost share at almost a one-to-one ratio.
- **Pinduoduo:** Rose from 15.9% to 21%, an increase of roughly 5 percentage points — eating mainly into Alibaba's lower-tier-city and price-sensitive users.
- **JD.com:** Share has been roughly flat, even slightly down — JD is not the winner of the share war, but it also hasn't hemorrhaged share the way Alibaba has.

### 2.3 Key inflection points

- **2024:** Douyin E-commerce broke into the top three for the first time, while JD fell out of the top three by GMV for the first time (on some measurement bases).
- **Extrapolating from current growth rates:** Douyin E-commerce's GMV could catch up to Taotian's as early as 2028-2029 — a striking timeline.

## III. Analysis of Key Competitors

### 3.1 Pinduoduo: King of Low Prices, Plus the Dual Engine of Temu's Overseas Expansion

**Domestic e-commerce:**
- 2024 GMV of approximately 5.2 trillion yuan, holding steady in second place
- Its core weapon is "ultimate low price" — not simple price wars, but structural low pricing achieved through a C2M (consumer-to-manufacturer) model
- The "hundred-billion subsidy" program has been upgraded from a marketing tool into a routine, normalized operating mechanism
- User base exceeds 900 million, with extremely high penetration

**Temu's overseas expansion:**
- As of 2025, Temu's global monthly active users reached 530 million, with cumulative downloads surpassing 1.2 billion
- Its business covers 86+ countries and regions, and it has captured 6.7% of the U.S. e-commerce market
- 2025 revenue is projected to contribute more than 50% of Pinduoduo Group's total
- It has pioneered a dual "fully managed + semi-managed" model that balances scale with merchant autonomy
- In Poland, it has already overtaken the local incumbent Allegro to take the top spot

**Threat level to Alibaba: Extremely high**
- Domestically: It continues to suppress Taotian's position in consumers' "low-price mindset" — "check Pinduoduo for price first" has become a habitual step in the purchase-decision path
- Overseas: Temu is in direct competition with AliExpress, and is growing far faster than AliExpress
- The question Munger would ask: "Is Pinduoduo's low-price advantage structural or cyclical?" — The answer is structural (the C2M model plus lower operating costs), which makes it very hard for Alibaba to counter simply by spending more money

### 3.2 Douyin E-Commerce: The Biggest Disruptor

**Scale and growth:**
- 2024 GMV of approximately 3.5 trillion yuan, up 30% year-over-year
- 2025 target of 4.2-4.3 trillion yuan, with growth still holding at 25-30%
- At a pace of closing roughly a 900-billion-yuan gap each year, it could catch up to Taotian within 3-4 years

**Where the disruption lies:**
1. **A structural advantage in traffic-acquisition costs:** Douyin has 800 million daily active users, giving it what is effectively "free customer acquisition"; Taobao has 300-400 million daily active users and must continuously buy traffic to acquire customers. This more-than-10x cost gap is not a simple competitive edge — it is a crushing advantage at the level of the business model itself.
2. **Dual-engine drive from content commerce to shelf commerce:** In 2024, the share of GMV coming from "shelf" scenarios (as opposed to content-driven impulse buys) rose to 40% (from 30% in 2023), showing that Douyin is no longer just a platform for "impulse consumption" — it is encroaching on Taobao's core territory of active, search-driven shopping.
3. **Shift of brand launch venues:** More and more brands are treating Douyin as their primary venue for new-product launches and brand announcements, while traditional e-commerce platforms are increasingly reduced to "distribution shelves."
4. **Rise of store-run livestreaming:** In 2024, the share of GMV from store-run livestreams (as opposed to influencer-run) exceeded 30%, and the number of merchants doing it grew 113% in a single year — meaning Douyin E-commerce is shifting away from dependence on top influencers toward a healthier, merchant-operated ecosystem.

**Threat level to Alibaba: Extremely high (the highest)**
- This is a fundamental challenge to Taotian's business model — as consumer attention and time shift to short-video platforms, the starting point of a shopping decision changes from "open Taobao and search" to "get inspired while scrolling Douyin"
- As Munger would put it: "When consumers' shopping entry point undergoes an irreversible migration, the value of the old entry point is inevitably diluted."

### 3.3 JD.com: A Differentiated Moat Built on Self-Operated Retail Plus Logistics

**Core figures:**
- Full-year 2024 revenue of 1,158.8 billion yuan; 2024 GMV of approximately 4.5 trillion yuan
- Q1 2025 revenue of 301.1 billion yuan, up 15.8% year-over-year, the fastest growth rate in nearly three years
- JD Logistics posted 2024 revenue of 182.8 billion yuan, with a profit margin of 4.3% — its best level since going public

**Differentiated competitive strengths:**
- **Self-operated retail plus self-built logistics:** 1,600+ warehouses plus 2,000+ cloud warehouses, covering every county-level district in the country, with fulfillment times in core cities improved to four hours
- **Instant retail (JD Now / "Miaosong"):** Covering 2,300+ counties and cities nationwide, with more than 500,000 partner stores, delivering as fast as 9 minutes
- **Food delivery:** Average daily orders set to exceed 20 million, with more than a million partner merchant storefronts onboarded, competing on differentiation via "quality-focused delivery plus rider-welfare protections"
- **3C category moat:** Its consumer mindshare in the 3C (computers, communications, consumer electronics) category remains solid

**Threat level to Alibaba: Moderate**
- The competition between JD and Alibaba is more about differentiation than direct overlap — JD's user base (which prioritizes quality and service) differs from the users being fought over by Pinduoduo and Douyin
- However, JD's expansion in instant retail directly encroaches on Alibaba's Ele.me / flash-delivery businesses

### 3.4 Kuaishou: Gatekeeper of the Lower-Tier Market

**Core figures:**
- Full-year 2024 e-commerce GMV of 1.39 trillion yuan, up 17.3% year-over-year
- Penetration in lower-tier markets of 67% (far exceeding Douyin's 52% and Taobao's 48%)
- Full-year 2024 revenue of 126.9 billion yuan

**Competitive characteristics:**
- Holds a unique trust-based, relationship-driven form of commerce in third-, fourth-, and fifth-tier cities — the so-called "old pal" (laotie) economy
- Growth has clearly slowed (Q2 2025 GMV growth fell to 15%), and it faces a growth bottleneck
- Its scale is only about one-sixth of Taotian's, so its direct threat to Alibaba is limited

**Threat level to Alibaba: Low-to-moderate**
- Kuaishou competes primarily with Pinduoduo in lower-tier markets, and its direct impact on Alibaba is smaller than Pinduoduo's or Douyin's
- But Kuaishou's presence further fragments e-commerce traffic, making it harder for Alibaba to rebuild market dominance

## IV. Cloud Computing: Alibaba Cloud's Moat

### 4.1 Market share (2025)

| Vendor | IaaS market share (Q2 2025) | AI cloud market share (H1 2025) |
|------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Alibaba Cloud | 26.8% (1st) | 35.8% (1st) |
| Huawei Cloud | 12.9% (2nd) | 13.1% (3rd) |
| China Telecom Cloud (Tianyi Cloud) | 12.3% (3rd) | — |
| Volcano Engine (ByteDance) | — | 14.8% (2nd) |
| China Mobile Cloud | 9.4% (4th) | — |
| Tencent Cloud | 7.9% (5th) | 7.0% (4th) |
| Baidu Cloud | — | 6.1% (5th) |

### 4.2 Competitive landscape analysis

**Alibaba Cloud's strengths:**
- IaaS market share of 26.8%, growing for five consecutive quarters, with a solid lead
- AI cloud market share of 35.8%, far ahead of second-place Volcano Engine at 14.8% — an enormous lead
- In Q1 2025, Alibaba Cloud held a 33% share of China's cloud infrastructure market

**Analysis of key competitors:**
- **Huawei Cloud:** Ranks first in the hybrid cloud market (ahead of Alibaba), with a distinct advantage among government and enterprise clients, but lags Alibaba in public cloud IaaS
- **Telecom-operator clouds (China Telecom / China Mobile / China Unicom):** Combined share now exceeds 30%, and they are rising quickly on the strength of government-enterprise relationships and infrastructure advantages — a force that cannot be ignored
- **Volcano Engine (ByteDance):** Has surged in the AI cloud market (14.8%), and ranks first in large-model API market share at 46.4% (driven by Doubao) — making it Alibaba Cloud's biggest challenger in the AI era
- **Tencent Cloud:** Share has declined, with only 7% in the AI cloud market — its competitiveness is weakening

**Munger's view:** Cloud computing is one of the few Alibaba businesses whose "moat is deepening." The wave of AI large models has driven an explosion in compute demand, and Alibaba Cloud, as China's largest public cloud platform, is capturing this windfall. But Volcano Engine, riding ByteDance's ecosystem (the Doubao large model plus the Douyin application scenario), is catching up fast — Alibaba Cloud's lead is not unassailable.

## V. AI Large-Model Competition: Where Does Qwen Stand?

### 5.1 Competitive landscape

**Consumer users (February 2025 monthly active users):**
- DeepSeek: 180 million
- Doubao (ByteDance): 101 million
- Tencent Yuanbao: 26.36 million
- Kimi: 24.51 million
- Qwen App: not in the top five

**Enterprise-level API call market (June 2025):**
- Doubao: 46.4%
- Alibaba Cloud (Qwen series): 27% (ranks first by average daily call volume, but second by market share)
- Baidu: 17%

**Open-source ecosystem:**
- Qwen has surpassed 600 million global downloads, with 300+ open-source models and more than 170,000 derivative models — the largest open-source model ecosystem in the world
- ModelScope community: 16 million+ users, 81,800 models
- Bailian platform: serves more than 980,000 enterprises and developers

### 5.2 Key judgment

Qwen's position in AI large models shows a "strong on the enterprise side, weak on the consumer side" pattern:
- **Enterprise / open source:** First in the world in open-source ecosystem, with strong enterprise-level API call volume — this forms an excellent synergy with Alibaba Cloud: "models drive traffic, compute drives revenue"
- **Consumer:** Far behind DeepSeek and Doubao in consumer-facing AI applications, with insufficient consumer mindshare
- **Technical level:** Qwen2.5-Max ranks 7th on the LMArena leaderboard, surpassing DeepSeek-V3, placing it in the first tier technically

**Munger's view:** Alibaba's strategic choice in AI large models is correct — take the open-source route and use models to drive cloud-computing revenue. This resembles what Munger might call a "fishing" strategy: the free model is the bait, the paid compute is the hook. But the absence of consumer-facing AI applications is a lingering concern — if users are all using DeepSeek and Doubao, the inference compute demand behind those applications may flow to ByteDance's Volcano Engine rather than Alibaba Cloud.

## VI. International E-Commerce: AliExpress / Lazada / Trendyol vs. Temu / Shein / TikTok Shop

### 6.1 Competitive landscape

**Comparison of the "Four Little Dragons of Overseas Expansion" (2024-2025):**

| Platform | Monthly active users | Markets covered | Core model | Growth |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|------|
| Temu | 530 million | 86+ countries | Fully managed + semi-managed | Extremely high |
| AliExpress | 422 million | 200+ countries | Fully managed + self-operated | Moderate |
| Shein | 170 million | 150+ countries | Owned brand + platform | Slowing |
| TikTok Shop | Fast-growing | Expanding | Content commerce | High |

### 6.2 Analysis of Alibaba's international e-commerce

- FY2025 international retail revenue grew 19% year-over-year, approaching breakeven
- AliExpress was overtaken by Temu in unique visitors at the end of 2023 (422 million vs. 467 million)
- In Southeast Asia, Lazada faces a two-front squeeze from Shopee and TikTok Shop
- Chinese e-commerce platforms together now hold roughly a 50% share of online retail in Southeast Asia's core markets

### 6.3 Key judgment

The picture for Alibaba's international e-commerce is mixed:
- **Concerns:** With more extreme low prices and a faster pace of expansion, Temu has overtaken AliExpress in multiple markets. Shein's growth has slowed (H1 2024 revenue growth fell from 40% to 23%), but it retains brand strength. TikTok Shop is rising quickly in Southeast Asia, hitting Lazada directly.
- **Bright spots:** Alibaba's international business overall grew revenue 19%, approaching breakeven — meaning the deepest loss-making phase may already be behind it. AliExpress's user base in mature markets (such as Europe) remains sizable.

## VII. Industry Trends: Structural Variables

### 7.1 AI's impact on e-commerce

- AI large models have already penetrated deep into the e-commerce value chain: 78% of livestream e-commerce operations use generative AI to optimize operations
- AI is reshaping the shopping entry point — from "search-driven shopping" to "AI-recommendation-driven shopping" — a long-term threat to Taobao's core "search" advantage
- In 2024, China's branded e-commerce service industry was worth 446.85 billion yuan, with AI technology upgrading from an auxiliary tool to a core driver
- Alibaba has a first-mover advantage in AI e-commerce applications (Qwen plus e-commerce scenario data), but Douyin's recommendation algorithm is equally powerful

### 7.2 Regulatory environment changes

- The antitrust "stick" has largely landed already, and policy pressure on Alibaba is easing
- The State Council's "Regulations on the Administration of Network Data Security" has taken effect, strengthening data-security governance
- ESG metrics have been incorporated into platform assessment frameworks — large platforms (Alibaba, JD) have an advantage in compliance
- Regulatory scrutiny of cross-border e-commerce is tightening (especially U.S. attention on Temu/Shein), which may indirectly benefit Alibaba's compliance-oriented positioning

### 7.3 Consumption trends: "K-shaped" divergence

- The consumption market shows a pronounced "K-shaped" divergence: basic consumption chases extreme value-for-money, while emotional consumption is willing to pay a premium
- Nearly 60% of young people are willing to pay for "emotional value"
- "Spend less to get more done" has become a widely shared consensus — this favors Pinduoduo and Douyin, and is unfavorable to Tmall (where brand premiums are being compressed)
- The livestream e-commerce user base is nearly 600 million, with penetration jumping from 4.9% in 2019 to 37.8% in 2024

## VIII. Munger's Ultimate Judgment: Is the Moat Being Repaired, or Still Being Eroded?

### 8.1 First, a more fundamental question: What exactly is Alibaba's moat?

When Munger analyzes a moat, he typically looks at it along four dimensions:

1. **Brand mindshare:** Taobao/Tmall's brand mindshare is being diluted. The default consumer behavior of "shop on Taobao first" has been broken — now it's "cheap stuff on Pinduoduo, browsing on Douyin, 3C on JD, and maybe brands on Tmall."
2. **Network effects:** Alibaba's most powerful moat — more buyers attract more sellers, and more sellers attract more buyers — is being weakened. Merchants are starting to operate across multiple platforms, and consumers are starting to comparison-shop across multiple platforms. But the 88VIP membership program (already more than 5.6 million members) is helping rebuild user stickiness.
3. **Economies of scale:** Alibaba's scale advantage remains (8 trillion yuan of GMV), but marginal returns are diminishing. Pinduoduo, by contrast, has achieved higher profit margins with a lower operating-cost structure.
4. **Switching costs:** Switching costs in e-commerce have always been inherently low — a user can download a new app in 30 seconds. But Alibaba is raising switching costs through 88VIP, Taobao Flash Sale, and the Alipay ecosystem.

### 8.2 Segment-by-segment judgment

| Business | Moat trend | Rationale |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Domestic e-commerce (Taotian) | Erosion slowing, partial repair | Rate of share decline has slowed (from -4% per year to -1% per year); 88VIP user growth is in double digits; take rate is rising; but the absolute level of share is still declining |
| Cloud computing (Alibaba Cloud) | Clearly repairing / deepening | AI cloud market share of 35.8%, far in the lead; IaaS has grown for five consecutive quarters; the AI large-model wave is driving compute demand into a positive feedback loop |
| AI large models (Qwen) | Under construction, high potential | First in the world in open-source ecosystem, but consumer-facing applications lag DeepSeek/Doubao; the enterprise-cloud synergy is the right path |
| International e-commerce | Stabilizing | Revenue up 19%, approaching breakeven, but facing intense competition from Temu/TikTok Shop |
| Instant retail (flash delivery) | Building a new moat | Peak daily orders of 120 million, share has caught up to Meituan (45.2% vs. 45.0%), but the investment is enormous (50 billion yuan in subsidies) |

### 8.3 Ultimate conclusion

**Alibaba's competitive position is going through a "divergent repair" — neither a full-scale recovery nor continued across-the-board deterioration, but rather:**

**Areas of improvement:**
- Cloud/AI: This is Alibaba's most solid moat, and it is deepening further in the AI era
- Instant retail: Taobao Flash Sale has nearly caught up to Meituan, opening a new growth avenue
- Monetization rate: Take rate keeps rising, so the same GMV generates more revenue
- 88VIP membership system: Lock-in of high-value users is strengthening

**Areas of continued deterioration:**
- The absolute level of e-commerce market share is still declining (though the pace of decline is slowing)
- Alibaba is at a disadvantage in the battle over the "shopping entry point" for content/livestream commerce — Douyin is reshaping consumers' shopping paths
- Pinduoduo has firmly captured the low-price mindshare, which will be hard to win back
- Consumer AI applications (the Qwen App) lack user mindshare

**Munger-style summary:**

> "Alibaba is no longer that unshakeable giant that once monopolized Chinese e-commerce. The quasi-monopoly position represented by its old 51% market share is gone for good. But nor is it a company in the process of collapsing — it is evolving from an 'e-commerce platform company' into a composite of 'AI + cloud computing + e-commerce + instant retail.' The question is: can the value created by the new businesses (cloud, AI, flash delivery) make up for the e-commerce share it lost and the tens of billions in subsidies it has poured out during this evolution?"

> "There are three signals I would watch closely: (1) whether Taotian's market-share decline has truly stopped — not just slowed, but stopped; (2) whether Alibaba Cloud's AI revenue growth rate can stay ahead — Volcano Engine is closing in fast; and (3) whether the 50-billion-yuan investment in Taobao Flash Sale is building a new moat, or simply filling a bottomless pit."

### 8.4 Market consensus vs. my challenge

| Market consensus | My challenge |
|----------|----------|
| "Alibaba's e-commerce share decline has already bottomed out" | A slowing rate of decline is not the same as a bottom. Douyin E-commerce is still expanding at 30% growth and could catch up to Taotian's GMV by 2028; Alibaba's e-commerce share may continue to slowly bleed away |
| "Alibaba Cloud is a certain growth engine" | Broadly agree, but watch out for the rise of Volcano Engine. Doubao's large-model API market share has already reached 46.4% — if inference-compute demand migrates along with it, Alibaba Cloud's lead could be eaten away |
| "Taobao Flash Sale is the second growth curve" | Flash Sale has indeed caught up to Meituan in share, but the sustainability of the 50-billion-yuan subsidy is questionable. Meituan lost money on food delivery for a decade before building its moat — does Alibaba have that same patience and resources? |
| "AI will reshape Alibaba's competitiveness" | AI's benefit to Alibaba is mostly on the enterprise side (cloud computing and enterprise services); the improvement on the consumer side (consumer experience) is limited. What is truly reshaping the shopping experience may be Douyin's recommendation algorithm, not Alibaba's AI |
