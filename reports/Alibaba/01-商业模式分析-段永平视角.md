## I. The Essence of the Business Model: Defining the Core Business

### What business is Alibaba actually in?

**One-sentence definition:** Alibaba's core business is "the distribution and monetization of commercial traffic" — connecting merchants with consumers, and making money from traffic-based advertising and transaction commissions.

Two layers need to be distinguished here:

- **Layer one (a transition that has already occurred):** the shift from an "e-commerce platform" to a group with dual core businesses of "consumer + cloud computing." In fiscal year 2026 the structure was consolidated into four major segments — China e-Commerce Group, International Digital Commerce Group, Cloud Intelligence Group, and All Others — with the core strategy now clearly defined as "e-commerce + cloud + AI."
- **Layer two (a narrative still awaiting validation):** management hopes Alibaba can become an "AI-driven technology group," a story similar to what AWS represents for Amazon. But Alibaba Cloud + AI revenue currently accounts for only about 12% of group revenue — still some distance from the "tech group" narrative holding up.

**What would Duan Yongping say?** He would certainly ask: what is Alibaba's true core focus? When a company tries to simultaneously run e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, internationalization, digital media and entertainment, and logistics ... that alone is a warning sign. Good businesses are usually focused.

2. **Alibaba Cloud is the "growth engine" but still small in scale**: AI is driving 40% growth, but annual revenue is only around RMB 130 billion, still far from being able to "carry the group's valuation."
3. **International e-commerce and instant retail are "cash-burning businesses"**: high growth but heavy losses, with an uncertain path to profitability.
4. **Divesting non-core assets is the right direction**: Intime for RMB 7.4 billion and RT-Mart for RMB 13.1 billion, sharpening focus on the core business.

## II. The Platform Flywheel Effect: Is Taobao and Tmall's Flywheel Still Spinning?

### The Classic Flywheel Model

The original flywheel logic for Taobao and Tmall was: **more merchants → more products → lower prices → more consumers → more merchants**.

### Evidence the Flywheel Has Been Disrupted

**The data:**

- Taobao and Tmall's GMV share: about 51% in 2020 → about 31% in 2025. Market share fell 20 percentage points over five years.
- Douyin e-commerce GMV: near zero in 2020 → about RMB 4.3 trillion in 2025, a 24% share, growing at over 30%.
- Pinduoduo GMV: about RMB 5.2 trillion, a 19% share.
- At the current pace, the GMV gap between Douyin e-commerce and Taobao/Tmall is narrowing by about RMB 900 billion a year, and is expected to close by 2028-2029.

**The root causes of the flywheel breaking down:**

1. **Traffic gateways have been diverted**: Douyin has 800 million DAU versus Taobao's 300-400 million DAU. Consumer attention has shifted from "search-driven shopping" to "content-driven discovery." Taobao's flywheel relies on consumers actively searching for what they need, but Douyin has created a new path to discovering demand through short video and livestreaming — effectively building an entirely new wheel outside Taobao's flywheel.
2. **Mid- and low-end merchants are leaving**: Pinduoduo is more friendly to no-name/generic-brand merchants (zero-cost onboarding, deposits as low as RMB 500, commission-free policies); Douyin saved merchants RMB 32 billion in costs in 2025. Taobao, by contrast, added a new 0.6% basic software service fee in September 2024, adding to merchants' burden.
3. **Loss of price competitiveness**: the average price of Pinduoduo's top-10 bestsellers is only 30%-40% of Taobao's. The "low price" mindshare is now firmly held by Pinduoduo.

**But the flywheel hasn't stopped completely either:**

- Taobao and Tmall's CMR (customer management revenue) grew +12% in FY2025 Q4, and monetization rates are rising.
- Adoption of the "full-site promotion" advertising tool keeps rising, suggesting merchants are still willing to spend on advertising at Taobao and Tmall.
- Taobao still has an edge in quality- and brand-oriented e-commerce, and Tmall's brand merchants face relatively high switching costs.
- Taobao Flash Sale's push into instant retail reached a 45.2% share in Q4 2025 — close to Meituan — opening up a new growth pole.

**A key judgment from the Duan Yongping lens:**

> "If a company's moat needs subsidies to maintain it, it isn't a real moat."

Taobao and Tmall's flywheel isn't dead, but it has shifted from "self-accelerating" to "externally sustained." Concretely: the rise in monetization comes more from charging merchants higher fees (the 0.6% technology service fee) than from the scale effects of organic GMV growth. This is "extractive" growth rather than "flywheel" growth, and it is not sustainable over the long run.

## III. Moat-by-Moat Verification

### 3.1 Brand Moat: ★★★☆ (3/5, and weakening)

- **Taobao and Tmall's brand awareness is extremely high**, but what the brand means is changing: from "Taobao has everything" to "not cheap enough, not fast enough."
- Tmall still offers brand-endorsement value for branded merchants, but Pinduoduo's "RMB 10 billion subsidy" program has already moved into the genuine-brand-goods space.
- The Alibaba Cloud brand is strong in the government/enterprise market, but on the consumer AI application side, Qwen's app (30 million MAU) trails far behind ByteDance's Doubao (197 million MAU).

**Counter-evidence:** the brand did not stop GMV share from falling from 51% to 31%. If the brand were truly a moat, this scale of share loss should not have happened.

### 3.2 Switching Costs: ★★★☆ (3/5, with large variation by tier)

**High-switching-cost groups:**
- Tmall flagship-store brand merchants: they have already built out store-operations systems, membership programs, and CRM systems, so migration costs are high.
- Alibaba Cloud enterprise customers: the cost of migrating data and rebuilding systems is enormous — this is Alibaba Cloud's most solid moat.

**Low-switching-cost groups:**
- Small and mid-sized merchants: they can run stores on Taobao, Pinduoduo, and Douyin simultaneously, so the real switching cost is close to zero.
- Consumers: with Taobao, Pinduoduo, and Douyin all installed on the same phone, price comparison takes only seconds.

**Key insight:** switching costs are inherently low for e-commerce platforms. This is the core reason Duan Yongping said he "doesn't know where the moat is" — consumers have no loyalty to any platform; they go wherever it's cheapest.

### 3.3 Network Effects: ★★★☆ (3/5, already diluted)

- The two-sided network effect still exists: merchants come because consumers are there, and consumers come because products are there.
- But this network effect has been heavily diluted by Pinduoduo and Douyin — both merchants and consumers are now "multi-homing."
- The key metric for a network effect is "exclusivity." When every merchant is on every platform, the network effect no longer functions as a moat.
- Taobao and Tmall's network effect remains relatively strong in specific categories (such as apparel and beauty brands), but it has no advantage left in standardized-goods categories (consumer electronics, daily necessities).

### 3.4 Scale Effects: ★★★★ (4/5, this is the most solid moat)

- Alibaba's technology infrastructure (Taobao and Tmall's transaction systems, payment systems, logistics network) has been built up over many years, and its scale effects are significant.
- Cainiao's network covers more than 200 countries and regions worldwide — something hard for a new entrant to replicate.
- Alibaba Cloud is China's largest public cloud, and scale advantages translate into cost advantages.
- But scale effects also mean "it's hard for an elephant to turn around" — organizational inertia and slow decision-making have been the most-criticized problems at Alibaba over the past few years.

### 3.5 Technology Barriers: ★★★☆ (3/5, AI is a plus but not decisive)

- Qwen's open-source models have been downloaded more than 600 million times globally, with 170,000+ derivative models — the world's leading open-source model family.
- Alibaba Cloud's AI cloud market share is 35.8%, exceeding the combined share of the #2 through #4 players.
- But at the consumer AI application layer (Qwen app's 30 million MAU vs. Doubao's 197 million MAU), the gap is enormous.
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine accounts for 46.4% of public-cloud large-model API calls, posing a direct threat to Alibaba Cloud.

### Overall Moat Assessment: Has It Widened or Narrowed Over the Past Three Years?

**Conclusion: overall it is narrowing, but with structural divergence.**

| Dimension | Trend | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| E-commerce moat | Narrowing markedly | Share fell from 51% to 31% — this isn't "narrowing," it's "being breached" |
| Cloud computing moat | Widening | Leading share amid the AI wave; scale and technology advantages are expanding |
| Instant retail | Being built from scratch | Taobao Flash Sale went from zero to a 45% share, but it's subsidy-driven; the moat is not yet established |
| International e-commerce | Uncertain | First-mover advantage exists but faces intense competition from Temu/Shein |

> Duan Yongping's original words from 2023: "I can't see through e-commerce; I don't know where the moat really is, and I don't know who will still be doing well in 10 years."

This judgment still holds today. Alibaba's core contradiction is: **the moat around its most profitable business (Taobao and Tmall) is narrowing, while the business whose moat is widening (Alibaba Cloud) has not yet become the main profit engine.**

## IV. User/Customer Value Analysis

### 4.1 Value to Merchants

| Platform | Core value proposition | Merchant cost | Suitable merchant type |
|------|------------|---------|-------------|
| Taobao/Tmall | Full category coverage, brand endorsement, data tools | Relatively high (0.6% service fee + ad spend) | Branded merchants, mid-to-large merchants |
| Pinduoduo | Low barrier to entry, high traffic, extreme value-for-money | Relatively low (many commission-free policies) | No-name/generic-brand merchants, industrial-cluster factories |
| Douyin e-commerce | Content traffic dividend, viral-hit logic | Moderate (but customer-acquisition cost 10x lower) | Merchants with content capability |
| JD.com | Quality assurance, logistics experience | High (self-operated model) | Branded merchants, consumer electronics |

**The core value Taobao and Tmall offer merchants is being squeezed:**
- The full-site promotion tool has improved advertising efficiency, but at its core it means "getting merchants to spend more money buying traffic."
- Operating on multiple platforms at once has become the norm for merchants; Taobao and Tmall are no longer a "must-have" but just "one option among several."
- The new 0.6% technology service fee added in 2024 boosted monetization in the short term, but over the long run it could accelerate merchant attrition.

### 4.2 Value to Consumers

| Platform | Core value proposition | Weakness |
|------|------------|------|
| Taobao/Tmall | Widest category selection, genuine branded goods, mature shopping experience | Not cheap enough, delivery not fast enough |
| Pinduoduo | Extreme low prices, social-sharing growth loops | Uneven quality, weaker after-sales service |
| Douyin e-commerce | Content-driven discovery, immersive shopping | High return rates, unplanned spending |
| JD.com | Fastest logistics, quality assurance | Limited categories, higher prices |

**Taobao's consumer value proposition is caught in a "sandwiched" bind:**
- Want cheap? Pinduoduo does it better.
- Want speed? JD.com and Meituan Flash Sale do it better.
- Want fun/entertainment? Douyin does it better.
- Want quality? Tmall has an edge, but JD.com is closing in too.

Taobao Flash Sale is a meaningful breakthrough — its move into instant retail (a 45.2% share in Q4 2025, close to Meituan) gives consumers new value in the form of "delivery within 30 minutes." But this share was bought with subsidies, and the profit model has not yet been proven.

### 4.3 Where Is the Differentiation?

**Differentiation Taobao and Tmall still hold:**
1. The broadest product pool (still #1 in the industry by SKU count)
2. The trust endorsement of Tmall flagship brand stores
3. The most mature merchant operating tools and data systems
4. Cross-platform benefits from the 88VIP membership program

**But this differentiation is shrinking, not growing.** Pinduoduo's RMB 10 billion subsidy program moving into genuine branded goods, Douyin e-commerce expanding into every category, JD.com opening up to third-party sellers — competitors are all eroding Taobao and Tmall's differentiated ground.

## V. Business Portfolio and Synergies: Real Synergy or Fake Synergy?

### Real Synergies Already Achieved

1. **Taobao Flash Sale + Ele.me = instant retail**: this is the most successful case of synergy. After resources were connected, daily order volume surpassed 40 million, with a 2026 goal of taking the number-one market position outright. Consumers can order both food delivery and flash-sale purchases within the Taobao app, and the traffic reuse is genuine.
2. **Alibaba Cloud + Taobao/Tmall data = an AI training advantage**: the massive data generated by the e-commerce business provides Qwen's training with a unique data resource.
3. **Cainiao + International e-commerce (AIDC)**: Cainiao's cross-border logistics network directly supports the fulfillment capability of AliExpress and Lazada.

### Fake or Weak Synergies

1. **Synergy between Digital Media/Entertainment and e-commerce**: the synergy between Youku, Damai, and Orca Entertainment with the e-commerce business has always been "synergy on paper" — talked about for years with no substantive results visible. Digital Media/Entertainment's value to Alibaba looks more like "strategic defense" than "synergy-driven value creation."
2. **The relationship between Amap/DingTalk/Quark and the core businesses**: these businesses are grouped under the "All Others" segment, with limited synergy with e-commerce and cloud. DingTalk has some synergy with Alibaba Cloud (cross-selling to enterprise customers), but its standalone value is limited.
3. **Alibaba Health, Freshippo (Hema), and others**: these businesses look more like "diversified investments" than a "synergy portfolio."

### A Judgment from the Duan Yongping Lens

> "When a company says it has synergies, you need to be especially careful. Real synergy doesn't need to be talked about; fake synergy is what gets talked about the most."

**Alibaba's synergy score: ★★★ (3/5) — a mix of real and fake, but the proportion of "real synergy" is rising.**

The 2023 "1+6+N" split-up period was when synergy was weakest — independent operation of each business fragmented internal resources. The 2025-2026 consolidation into four segments re-emphasized synergy, and the integration of Taobao Flash Sale with Ele.me is a positive signal. But synergy among the "four segments" (for example, between China e-commerce and Alibaba Cloud) still remains at the level of "technical support," far from the kind of "profit flywheel" that AWS represents for Amazon.

## VI. Assessment Against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

### 6.1 Differentiation (★★★, 3/5)

- Taobao and Tmall have differentiation in branded e-commerce, but it is being eroded.
- Alibaba Cloud has differentiation in China's public cloud market (#1 share plus AI leadership) — this is the strongest differentiation point.
- Qwen has differentiation in the open-source large-model space (the world's #1 open-source model family), but its consumer application is behind.
- Overall, Alibaba's differentiation is "broad and shallow" rather than "narrow and deep."

### 6.2 Pricing Power (★★☆, 2.5/5)

**Where there is pricing power:**
- Tmall's ad pricing for branded merchants: because branded merchants need Tmall's brand endorsement and traffic, there is some pricing power in the short term.
- Alibaba Cloud with government/enterprise customers: data migration costs are high, creating some lock-in effect.

**Where there is no pricing power:**
- With small and mid-sized merchants: amid multi-platform competition, merchants vote with their feet, and Taobao's added 0.6% technology service fee has already stirred merchant discontent.
- With consumers: consumers have zero loyalty to any platform and go wherever it's cheapest.
- Instant retail: share is being grabbed via subsidies, with no pricing power whatsoever.

**Duan Yongping values pricing power most of all, and this is precisely where Alibaba is weakest.** An e-commerce platform is fundamentally an intermediary, and an intermediary's pricing power depends on the alternatives available to both sides — when both merchants and consumers have many alternatives, the intermediary's pricing power is weak.

### 6.3 Sustainable Competitive Advantage (★★★, 3/5)

- Alibaba Cloud + AI's competitive advantage is relatively sustainable: scale advantage + accumulated technology + customer lock-in.
- The e-commerce business's competitive advantage is not sustainable: the share decline over the past five years has already proven this.
- Instant retail's competitive advantage does not yet exist: share bought with subsidies is not a competitive advantage.

### Overall Rating on Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard: ★★★ (3/5)

**Alibaba is not a "good business" by Duan Yongping's definition.** The reasons:

1. Not focused enough: the business is too spread out; even after consolidating into four segments, it still spans e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, internationalization, logistics, media and entertainment, and more.
2. Weak pricing power: the nature of being an e-commerce intermediary inherently limits pricing power.
3. The moat is narrowing in the core business: the share of the most profitable segment, Taobao and Tmall, keeps declining.
4. Requires ongoing large capital outlays: AI infrastructure, instant-retail subsidies, and international expansion all require continued cash burn.

This also explains Duan Yongping's actual trading: he added to his Alibaba position in 2024 → then gradually reduced it in 2025 (cutting more than 25% in Q3, and continuing to reduce in Q4). He showed his view through his actions.

## VII. Deep Dive: Alibaba Cloud + AI Strategy

### 7.1 Qwen's Competitiveness

**Strengths:**
- Leading open-source ecosystem: 600 million downloads worldwide, 170,000 derivative models, a 12% global share on OpenRouter (#4).
- The Qwen3 series comprises 8 open-source models, covering the full range of sizes and modalities.
- Has deployment advantages in the B2B/enterprise market (leveraging Alibaba Cloud's customer base).

**Weaknesses:**
- Consumer-app MAU of 30 million versus ByteDance Doubao's 197 million — a 6.5x gap.
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine holds a 46.4% share of large-model API calls and is encroaching from the application layer into the cloud layer.
- DeepSeek's rise has diverted attention within the open-source ecosystem.

**Key risk:** ByteDance could replicate the "Douyin disrupts Taobao" playbook — entering from the application layer and then reverse-encroaching on Alibaba Cloud's underlying infrastructure market. Volcano Engine already poses a threat in the AI cloud space.

### 7.2 Alibaba Cloud's Growth Outlook

**Favorable data:**
- FY2026 Q4 external revenue +40%, the fastest growth in 9 quarters.
- AI product revenue at an annualized RMB 35.8 billion, with 11 consecutive quarters of triple-digit growth.
- AI products surpassed 30% of cloud external revenue for the first time.
- Management has committed that "cloud and AI investment over the next three years will exceed the total of the past decade."

**Risks to watch:**
- Much of Alibaba Cloud's accelerating growth comes from a surge in AI inference demand; whether this growth is sustainable depends on the overall development of the AI industry.
- Both ByteDance's Volcano Engine and Huawei Cloud are catching up faster.
- Competition in AI cloud could evolve into a "price war" (cutting prices for scale), compressing margins.
- End-of-2025 daily token-consumption data: Doubao processes 630 trillion tokens/day, and the scale advantage is tilting toward ByteDance.

### 7.3 AI's Impact on Alibaba Overall

**Positive effects:**
- Cloud Intelligence is becoming a new growth pole, with potential to become the group's second-largest profit source within 3-5 years.
- AI is empowering Taobao and Tmall (smart recommendations, smart customer service) to improve operating efficiency.
- Qwen's open-source strategy can build a developer ecosystem, indirectly driving Alibaba Cloud subscriptions.

**Neutral/negative effects:**
- AI infrastructure investment is enormous and suppresses profit in the near term (FY2026 full-year net profit of RMB 105.9 billion versus FY2025's comparable net profit of roughly RMB 110 billion+ — no significant profit growth).
- AI's benefit to e-commerce is not yet clearly visible — has AI helped Taobao win back share taken by Pinduoduo/Douyin? There is no evidence of that so far.

## VIII. Counter-Check: Is the Moat Really Being Repaired?

The mainstream market narrative is "Alibaba has bottomed and is rebounding." Let's check this narrative point by point.

### Narrative 1: "Taobao and Tmall have stabilized"

**Supporting evidence:**
- FY2025 Q4 CMR +12%; FY2026 Q4 comparable CMR +8%.
- Monetization rates keep rising.
- Taobao Flash Sale has opened a new front.

**Counter-evidence:**
- CMR growth mainly comes from price increases (the 0.6% service fee plus higher full-site promotion pricing), not organic GMV growth.
- GMV share is still declining (31% and the trend has not reversed).
- FY2026 full-year revenue grew only +3% YoY (+11% on a comparable basis), and traditional e-commerce growth remains weak once instant retail is stripped out.
- Price-increase-driven revenue growth is essentially "passing costs on to merchants," which will accelerate merchant attrition over the long run.

**Judgment: Taobao and Tmall's "revenue stabilizing" and the "moat being repaired" are two different things.** Revenue can be propped up in the short term through price increases, but moat repair requires GMV share to stop declining and start recovering — and that has not happened.

### Narrative 2: "Alibaba Cloud + AI is the second growth curve"

**Supporting evidence:**
- External revenue +40%; AI product revenue annualized at RMB 35.8 billion.
- #1 in China's AI cloud market share, exceeding the combined share of #2 through #4.

**Counter-evidence:**
- Cloud Intelligence's full-year revenue is about RMB 130 billion, only 12% of the group total. To "carry the valuation," it would need to reach at least 20-25% of the total — at 20% growth, that would take another 3-4 years.
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine advantage at the AI application layer could transmit down to the cloud layer.
- Massive capital expenditure is suppressing near-term profit.

**Judgment: the direction is right, but "the second curve becoming the main engine" will take time — it is not yet a fact that has happened.**

### Narrative 3: "Management's aggressive buybacks show the stock is undervalued"

**Supporting evidence:**
- FY2025 buybacks of $11.9 billion, a 6.5% shareholder-return yield (the highest among large-cap Chinese ADRs).
- The buyback program has $19.1 billion of authorization remaining, valid through March 2027.
- Proceeds from selling non-core assets such as Intime and RT-Mart are being redirected to focus resources.

**Counter-evidence:**
- Buybacks alone don't prove the stock is cheap — they only show that management believes it's cheap.
- Alibaba is simultaneously making massive investments in AI infrastructure ("exceeding the past decade over the next three years"), and its capital needs are enormous.
- Buybacks are running in parallel with large capital expenditures, so it's important to watch whether free cash flow is sufficient to support both.

**Judgment: the buyback intensity is indeed large, but it's necessary to see whether free cash flow can support both buybacks and AI investment at the same time.** If buybacks are cut in the future because AI investment is too large, the market may re-rate the stock.

### Narrative 4: "Organizational restructuring has unlocked efficiency"

**Supporting evidence:**
- Consolidating from "1+6+N" to four major segments has eliminated internal fragmentation.
- The integration of Taobao Flash Sale and Ele.me is an effective case of synergy.

**Counter-evidence:**
- Over the past three years, Alibaba has gone through frequent organizational restructurings (splitting up → re-consolidating), and organizational instability is itself a cost.
- Businesses such as Digital Media/Entertainment, Cainiao, and Amap have been grouped into "All Others," which has become a catch-all basket for "we don't know what to do with this yet, so park it here."

**Judgment: the direction of the restructuring is correct, but the frequency of the changes itself reflects wavering in management's strategic direction.**

## IX. Core Conclusions

### The Essence of Alibaba's Business Model

Alibaba is a **large conglomerate in the process of transforming from an "e-commerce empire" into a dual-engine company of "AI cloud + consumer."** The direction of the transformation is correct, but the transformation is far from complete.

### Assessment Through the Lens of Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

| Assessment dimension | Rating | Notes |
|---------|------|------|
| Business model clarity | ★★★ | Businesses are spread out, but the "e-commerce + cloud + AI" focus direction is now clear |
| Differentiation | ★★★ | Broad but shallow — every area has competitors closing the gap |
| Pricing power | ★★☆ | An e-commerce intermediary inherently lacks pricing power |
| Moat trend | ★★☆ | Core e-commerce is narrowing, cloud computing is widening; overall it's narrowing |
| Flywheel effect | ★★★ | The flywheel is still spinning but has slowed, sustained by external force |
| User/customer value | ★★★ | Full category selection is an advantage, but the "sandwiched" bind is clear |
| Business synergy | ★★★ | A mix of real and fake synergy, improving over time |

**Duan Yongping's judgment is most likely: "I can't see through it."** In fact, this is precisely why he gradually reduced his position in 2025. Alibaba's problem is not that the business is bad, but that the predictability of its moat is low — you don't know whether Taobao and Tmall can still hold 30% share in five years, you don't know whether Alibaba Cloud can stay ahead in the AI era, and you don't know whether instant retail will be profitable once the subsidy spending stops. For a value investor pursuing certainty, having too many "don't know"s is a fundamental problem.

### The Three Key Variables Most Worth Tracking Going Forward

1. **Whether Taobao and Tmall's GMV share can stop declining**: if it keeps falling below 25%, its status as the "cash cow" could be at risk.
2. **Whether Alibaba Cloud's AI revenue can surpass RMB 50 billion in FY2027**: this is the key validation point for whether the "second curve" can carry the group's valuation.
3. **Whether instant retail can reach a profitability inflection point in the second half of 2026**: if a business that keeps burning cash shows no hope of profitability, it will become a long-term drag.
