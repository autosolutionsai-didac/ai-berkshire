## 0. Core Judgment in One Sentence

> **Alibaba's FY2036 profit center of gravity will no longer be determined by the e-commerce "take rate" alone, but jointly by three things: (1) whether Taotian can defend a 28-32% share of China's total retail pie and hold its take rate steady; (2) whether Alibaba Cloud can push its operating margin to 18-22% under a dual "China No.1 + Global Top 4" positioning; and (3) whether AIDC can turn from a "loss-making overseas bet" into a "second profit curve growing 30%+." The probability of getting all three right is about 25-30%, the probability of botching all three is about 15-20%, and the most likely outcome falls in between — a transformation story "stronger than IBM, weaker than Microsoft."**

## 1. China E-Commerce: Taotian's "War of Attrition," from Share Defense to Redistribution in the Agent Era

### 1.1 Current Landscape (Late 2025 - 2026Q1)

Mainstream figures (QuestMobile 2025 annual report, LatePost 2026-02 industry review, China Merchants Securities 2026-01 internet annual strategy):

| Platform | 2025 GMV estimate | Share of China's physical-goods e-commerce |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Taotian (Taobao + Tmall) | ~RMB 8.5-9 trillion | 38-42% |
| Pinduoduo (main platform) | ~RMB 5.2-5.6 trillion | 23-25% |
| Douyin E-Commerce | ~RMB 4.0-4.3 trillion | 18-20% |
| JD.com | ~RMB 3.6-3.8 trillion | 15-17% |
| Xiaohongshu (RED) E-Commerce | ~RMB 450-600 billion | 2-3% |
| Kuaishou E-Commerce | ~RMB 1.4-1.5 trillion | 6-7% |

**Note**: Figures vary widely by methodology (whether GMV includes returns/refunds, whether it includes services), and the numbers above are the median of "GMV including refunds / physical goods" figures. Cross-checking two sources: QuestMobile puts Taotian at 39% and Pinduoduo at 24%; China Merchants Securities puts Taotian at 41% and Pinduoduo at 23% — broadly consistent.

### 1.2 Three Key Variables Over the Next 10 Years

**Variable A: Will AI agents reshape "people - goods - venue"?**

Supporting evidence:
- OpenAI Operator (launched 2025-01) plus Anthropic Computer Use (2024-10) can already replace humans on desktop for the full chain of "compare prices → add to cart → place order"
- Alibaba's "AI Wanxiang" plus the "Qwen App" (2025 flagship products) are building a consumer-facing agent entry point
- In 2026-04 Pinduoduo began testing an "AI product-selection assistant," already in a 5% user rollout

Counter-evidence:
- Agents reshape "decisions," but 40%+ of consumption decisions involve "emotion / impulse / social" components that are hard for agents to replace (the logic behind Xiaohongshu and Douyin livestreaming)
- The real winners of the agent era may be **the agent platforms that control the entry point** (OpenAI / the Qwen App / Doubao), rather than existing e-commerce platforms
- 90% of Chinese mobile internet time is spent inside individual apps (QuestMobile 2025), so cross-app agent workflows will long be constrained by the "walled gardens" of WeChat / Douyin / Taobao

**Judgment**: Over the next 10 years, agents will take over 30-40% of "commodity/standardized-goods" purchase decisions, but the remaining 60% — "non-standard + content-driven" — will still be the platforms' battleground. If Taotian can **use Qwen to turn "Taobao Wenwen" into China's largest agent shopping entry point**, that is an opportunity; if it fails, traffic will be siphoned off by Doubao / the Qwen App.

**Variable B: Will Pinduoduo's and Douyin's offensives lose steam?**

Pinduoduo side (both sides of the argument):
- For (continued encroachment): Pinduoduo's main-platform GMV growth in 2025 is still about 18-22% (LatePost 2026-02), above the industry's 6-8%; repeat-purchase stickiness in lower-tier markets and the "10 Billion Subsidies" program has already taken hold
- Against (signs of slowing growth): Pinduoduo's average orders per user already exceeded 70 per year in 2024, nearing a ceiling; Temu is dragging down group profitability (Temu lost over RMB 5 billion in a single quarter in 2025 Q4, per 36Kr 2026-03)
- **10-year base-case forecast**: Pinduoduo's share rises from 24% to 26-29%, Taotian's falls from 40% to 33-36%, but **the pace of decline slows** (no longer 2pct per year, but 0.5-1pct per year)

Douyin E-Commerce side:
- For: dual engines of livestreaming plus shelf e-commerce, 2025 GMV growth still 25%+
- Against: take rate has already risen above 6%, merchant ROI is deteriorating; shelf e-commerce mindshare is weak (users still default to Taobao for search)
- **10-year base-case forecast**: Douyin E-Commerce's share rises from 19% to 22-25%, with the gains coming mainly from "content-driven new categories" (interest-based goods, local services, instant retail) rather than Taotian's core standardized-goods categories

**Variable C: Will instant retail redefine "e-commerce"?**

2025-2026 is the year of the "flash-sale wars":
- Taobao Flash Sale relaunched in 2025, in coordination with Ele.me (integrated into Taobao's main entry point in 2026-03)
- Meituan Instant Retail's 2025 GMV was about RMB 450 billion (LatePost 2026-02)
- Douyin's local instant-delivery GMV in 2025 was about RMB 150 billion

**Ten years from now, "instant retail" will likely grow from ~RMB 1 trillion in 2025 to RMB 6-8 trillion** (10-12% of total retail sales, comparable to today's food-delivery penetration rate), redefining Chinese retail. For Taotian this is a double-edged sword:

- Upside: it fills the "30-minute delivery" gap and wins back the electronics/daily-goods use cases that JD.com and Meituan took away
- Downside: the subsidy war burns cash for a long time (based on Meituan's history, per-order losses of RMB 2-3, requiring 5+ years to break even)

### 1.3 Reverse Flow of Foreign Brands: Negligible

SHEIN/Temu represent "Chinese brands going global"; the reverse flow — "foreign brands entering Chinese e-commerce" — remains a minor variable over a 10-year horizon (domestic Chinese brands already hold 70%+ mindshare). Unless US-China relations improve and cross-border logistics undergo a revolution, this will not affect the overall landscape.

### 1.4 Taotian's FY2036 Share Forecast

| Scenario | Taotian share | Taotian GMV growth (CAGR) | Take rate |
|------|---------|---------------------|-----------|
| Bull | 33-35% | 4-5% | 4.0%+ |
| Base | 28-32% | 2-3% | 3.5-3.8% |
| Bear | 22-26% | 0-1% | Below 3.0% |

**Under the base case, Taotian's FY2036 operating profit is about RMB 240-280 billion (vs. roughly RMB 170-180 billion for FY2025E), a CAGR of 4-5%** — essentially a story of "moderate defense plus a slight uptick in take rate."

## 2. Global Cloud Computing: Alibaba Cloud's "China No.1 + Global Top 4" Window

### 2.1 Current Landscape (2025)

Source: Synergy Research 2025 Q4, Canalys 2025 annual report, IDC's 2026-03 China cloud market report.

**Global cloud infrastructure services market share (2025)**:
- AWS: 30-31%
- Azure: 22-23%
- Google Cloud: 12-13%
- Alibaba Cloud: 4-5% (global No. 4)
- Others (IBM, Oracle, Salesforce, Tencent, Huawei): combined about 28-30%

**China public cloud IaaS+PaaS market share (2025)**:
- Alibaba Cloud: 33-36%
- Huawei Cloud: 18-21%
- Tencent Cloud: 14-16%
- ByteDance Volcano Engine: 8-12% (rising fast, up from just 5-6% in 2024)
- China Telecom Tianyi Cloud: 10-12%
- AWS China / Microsoft Azure China: combined about 5-7%

### 2.2 Key Variables Over 10 Years

**Variable A: Total industry size**

- Global cloud was about USD 330 billion in 2025 (Synergy); the consensus range for 2035 is USD 1.2-1.6 trillion (Gartner 2026-01, Goldman 2026-02), a CAGR of 13-17%
- China's cloud market was about RMB 600 billion in 2025 (IDC); the base-case forecast for 2035 is RMB 2.5-3 trillion (CAGR 15%)
- AI inference workloads will account for 40-50% of total compute spending (vs. under 20% in 2025), the single largest source of incremental growth

**Variable B: Alibaba Cloud's hand-to-hand combat with ByteDance's Volcano Engine in China**

ByteDance's Volcano Engine is the biggest threat:
- Advantage: Doubao's model usage has already surpassed Qwen's (per QuestMobile 2025-12 data, consumer DAU was 78 million for Doubao vs. 45 million for Qwen), creating a "model popularity → customer lock-in" flywheel
- Disadvantage: Volcano Engine lacks a government/enterprise customer base, and the state-owned-enterprise cloud / financial cloud segments are locked up by Alibaba and Huawei

**10-year base-case forecast**:
- Alibaba Cloud's China share: 33-36% → 28-32% (a modest decline, but still No. 1)
- Huawei Cloud: 18-21% → 22-26% (capturing the state-owned-enterprise cloud dividend)
- ByteDance Volcano Engine: 8-12% → 18-22% (capturing AI-native customers)
- This is a "three-way rivalry plus state-owned cloud" landscape

**Variable C: The real scope for overseas expansion**

Positive:
- In Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand), Alibaba Cloud is already No. 2-3; its 2025 Southeast Asia revenue was about RMB 8-10 billion (Canalys), growing 35%+
- In the Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE), Alibaba Cloud won several large government-cloud contracts in 2024-2025
- Europe has strict compliance requirements (GDPR); AWS/Azure still dominate, leaving limited room for Alibaba Cloud

Negative:
- The US market is essentially closed off (entity-list risk plus data-security concerns)
- Intensifying US-China decoupling → multinational companies (even European and Southeast Asian customers) are "de-risking away from Chinese cloud"

**10-year base-case forecast**: Alibaba Cloud's overseas revenue share rises from ~12% in 2025 to 25-30% in 2035, driven mainly by Asia-Pacific, the Middle East, and parts of Africa.

### 2.3 Can Alibaba Cloud's Margins Close the Gap with AWS?

- AWS's 2024 operating margin was 36-38% (Amazon financial reports)
- Azure's operating margin is estimated at 35%+ (Microsoft does not disclose it separately)
- Alibaba Cloud's FY2025 adjusted EBITA margin was about 8-9% (Alibaba financial reports)

Sources of the gap: (1) Scale — AWS's revenue is 6-7 times Alibaba Cloud's; (2) Software/PaaS mix — high-margin PaaS makes up 50%+ of AWS's business versus only 25-30% for Alibaba Cloud; (3) Pricing power — AWS commands a monopoly premium in the enterprise market.

**10-year base-case forecast**: Alibaba Cloud's operating margin rises from 8-9% to 18-22% (accompanying a 3-4x increase in scale plus high-margin AI inference), but remains **significantly below AWS (35%+)**. This is "narrowing the gap," not "closing it."

### 2.4 Alibaba Cloud's FY2036 Financial Forecast

| Scenario | Revenue | Operating margin | Operating profit |
|------|------|-----|---------|
| Bull | RMB 550-650 billion | 22-25% | RMB 130-160 billion |
| Base | RMB 400-480 billion | 18-21% | RMB 80-100 billion |
| Bear | RMB 280-340 billion | 12-14% | RMB 35-45 billion |

**Under the base case, Alibaba Cloud becomes one of Alibaba Group's largest profit engines by FY2036** (approaching Taotian in scale).

## 3. AI Applications and the Agent Economy: Alibaba's "Narrative vs. Execution" Race

### 3.1 Current Landscape (Early 2026)

**Global Tier-1 foundation models**: OpenAI (GPT-5), Anthropic (Claude 4.x), Google (Gemini 2.x)
**Global Tier 2**: Meta (Llama 4), xAI (Grok 4), DeepSeek (V4/R2), Alibaba (Qwen 3.x)

QuestMobile 2025-12 China AI app MAU:
- Doubao: 180 million
- DeepSeek: 120 million
- Qwen App: 80 million
- Kimi: 60 million
- Ernie (Baidu): 45 million

### 3.2 Key Variables Over 10 Years

**Variable A: Can Qwen hold a global Top 3-5 position?**

Positive:
- Qwen's open-source ecosystem is the world's No. 1 (top download count on Hugging Face in 2026-Q1, surpassing Llama)
- Alibaba's capex has surged in 2025-2026 (the market expects a cumulative RMB 300-380 billion spent on AI infrastructure over the next three years)
- Qwen has flagship products across all three tracks of Chinese-language, multimodal, and agent capabilities

Negative:
- In overseas markets (North America/Europe), Qwen's commercialization is close to zero — open source does not equal revenue
- DeepSeek built a Top-3 model with 1/10th the compute, proving that "stacking compute" is not necessarily a moat
- ByteDance's Doubao plus Volcano Ark form a closed "model + cloud" loop that is fully on par with Alibaba's

**Judgment**: The probability of Qwen holding a global Top 5 spot is about 65%, and of reaching Top 3 about 25-30%. **The key is not the model itself, but whether Qwen becomes the "default AI supplier for Chinese companies going global"** — on this front the Alibaba Cloud + Qwen combination has a clear advantage.

**Variable B: What the agent economy will actually look like**

Ten years from now, the most likely shape is:
- On the B2B side: agents embedded in enterprise SaaS / ERP / customer service, with per-agent annual fees of USD 500-5,000, for a global market of USD 500 billion (Gartner 2026-02 estimate)
- On the consumer side: agents will mainly be split between "super-app-embedded" entry points (Taobao, WeChat, Douyin) and 1-2 standalone agent apps (the GPT App, the Qwen App)
- Alibaba's position: on B2B it has the Alibaba Cloud + DingTalk + Qwen Enterprise Edition combination; on consumer it has the Taobao + Amap + Qwen App combination. **In theory this positioning is excellent**, but Alibaba's execution history shows its product capability has been inconsistent

**Variable C: How much substance is behind the "token economy" narrative**

In 2025, ATH (Alibaba's cloud conference) put forward the narrative that "tokens are like water and electricity." The two sides:

Supporting evidence:
- AI inference token calls grew 50-100x in 2024-2025 (disclosed in Alibaba Cloud's financial reports)
- Alibaba Cloud's per-token price fell over 90% in two years, but call volume grew over 100x, so **total revenue still rose 5-10x** — the volume-price scissors worked
- Domestically, Alibaba has a full stack of "model + compute + applications," similar to a combined AWS + OpenAI

Counter-evidence:
- The "token economy" is fundamentally a "selling water" business, with inherently lower gross margins than the application layer
- Open-source models like DeepSeek plus inference optimization are driving token prices down over the long term; whether the volume-price scissors can hold for 10 years is uncertain
- Alibaba's track record at the "application layer" (consumer agent apps) has historically been weak, and it risks repeating the pattern of "DingTalk / Taobao Live launching late"

### 3.3 AI's 10-Year Incremental Profit Contribution to Alibaba

Base-case breakdown:
- Alibaba Cloud's AI-related revenue contribution: by FY2036, about 50%+ of Alibaba Cloud's total revenue, i.e., RMB 200-250 billion
- Qwen's B2B API business: RMB 30-50 billion
- DingTalk plus Qwen Enterprise Edition: RMB 10-20 billion
- Taobao's AI-driven transformation (lifting take rate by +0.2-0.4pct): contributing RMB 20-40 billion in GMV-linked profit

**Base-case forecast for AI's incremental profit contribution in FY2036: RMB 50-80 billion** (15-20% of the group's total profit of RMB 350-450 billion).

## 5. Local Services: A Long-Term Loss-Making Black Hole — Can It Become a "Low-Margin, High-Traffic" Business?

### 5.1 Current Landscape (2026Q1)

Source: LatePost 2026-02, QuestMobile 2025-12, various company financial reports.

**Food delivery**:
- Meituan: 65-68% share, peak daily order volume over 90 million
- Ele.me: 28-32% share, daily order volume of 35-40 million
- Douyin food delivery: 3-5% share, daily order volume of 3-5 million

**In-store/local services**:
- Meituan in-store: 55-60% share, 2025 revenue of about RMB 110 billion
- Douyin local services: 25-30% share, 2025 GMV of about RMB 700 billion (including unredeemed vouchers)
- Amap plus Koubei (Alibaba): 8-12% share

**Instant retail**:
- Meituan Flash Purchase: daily orders over 15 million
- Taobao Flash Sale (relaunched in 2025 in coordination with Ele.me): daily orders of 8-10 million (2026-03 data)
- Douyin instant retail: daily orders of 2-3 million

### 5.2 Key Variables Over 10 Years

**Variable A: How real is the threat from Taobao Flash Sale?**

Supporting evidence:
- Taobao's main app has over 400 million daily active users, giving Flash Sale enormous room for traffic referral
- Alibaba's "Taobao + Ele.me + Amap" three-way coordination unified its merchant back-end system in 2026-04

Counter-evidence:
- Meituan's decade-long delivery network and merchant relationships cannot be shaken in 1-2 years
- Per-order costs in instant retail are 3-5 times those of traditional long-distance e-commerce, meaning cash burn with no end in sight
- With Douyin, Flash Sale, and Meituan all burning cash, whoever backs off first effectively exits

**Judgment**: Over 10 years, Taobao Flash Sale can capture 25-30% of the instant-retail market (vs. 50% for Meituan and 15-20% for Douyin), but **the point of profitability is likely to come after 2030**.

**Variable B: The impact of AI automation on per-order costs**

- Delivery: drones / autonomous delivery vehicles reach limited commercial deployment after 2030, potentially cutting per-order delivery cost from RMB 6-7 to RMB 3-4
- Dispatching: AI dispatch optimization has already cut Meituan's per-order cost by 15-20% (2024-2025), with another 10-15% of room over the next five years
- In-store redemption: AI agents doing automatic price comparison and automatic voucher redemption will compress in-store margins

### 5.3 Alibaba Local Services FY2036 Forecast

| Scenario | Revenue | Operating margin | Operating profit |
|------|------|-----|---------|
| Bull | RMB 220-260 billion | 5-8% | RMB 11-20 billion |
| Base | RMB 150-180 billion | 0-3% | RMB 0-5 billion |
| Bear | RMB 80-110 billion | -8% to -3% | RMB -9 billion to -3 billion |

**Judgment**: In the best case, local services becomes a **"low-margin, high-traffic" business** (similar to what JD Logistics is to JD.com); in the worst case, it remains a persistent black hole. **It is unlikely to become a significant profit source, but its strategic value lies in "defending Taotian"** — preventing Meituan from using local-services traffic to counterattack into long-distance e-commerce.

## 6. Inverting: Scripts Under Which Alibaba's FY2036 Profit Comes In Significantly Below Optimistic Expectations

### Script 1: Pinduoduo and Douyin Continue Eroding Take Rate
- Probability: 35-40%
- Profit impact: Taotian's FY2036 operating profit comes in RMB 60-100 billion below the base case
- Trigger: Taotian's take rate falls below 3.5%, or its share drops below 25%
- Counter-evidence: Taotian has already stabilized its take rate in 2024-2025, and the trend is easing

### Script 2: Alibaba Cloud Gets Squeezed Between ByteDance Volcano Engine and Huawei Cloud
- Probability: 25-30%
- Profit impact: Alibaba Cloud's FY2036 operating margin gets stuck at 12-14%, with profit coming in RMB 40-60 billion below the base case
- Trigger: Volcano Engine's share exceeds 25% and it takes away Alibaba Cloud's core internet-customer base
- Counter-evidence: Alibaba Cloud's government/enterprise customer base is stable, and the Qwen ecosystem is feeding back into it

### Script 3: Geopolitical Shocks to Overseas Business
- Probability: 30-35%
- Profit impact: AIDC revenue -30%, AliExpress exits multiple markets, FY2036 profit comes in RMB 10-30 billion below the base case
- Trigger: a sharp deterioration in US-China relations, plus the EU following with sanctions, plus Southeast Asian countries following suit on data localization
- Counter-evidence: Alibaba's main overseas battlegrounds are non-US markets, giving it lower geopolitical risk exposure than TikTok/Huawei

### Script 4: Prolonged Macro Deflation in China Plus Weak Household Consumption
- Probability: 30-35% (not to be taken lightly — China's CPI has stayed persistently low through 2024-2025)
- Profit impact: e-commerce, local services, and cloud all come under pressure simultaneously, with FY2036 profit coming in RMB 80-150 billion below the base case
- This is the **most systemic risk**, and is almost impossible to hedge
- Counter-evidence: China's GDP base is large, and structural upgrading, the silver economy, and going global still offer structural opportunities

### Script 5: A Resurgence of Antitrust Regulation / Platform Economy Governance
- Probability: 15-20% (the current regulatory cycle is relatively lenient, but the political cycle could reverse in 5-10 years)
- Profit impact: take rate is forcibly cut, forced exclusivity ("pick one of two") is banned, data sharing is mandated, with FY2036 profit coming in RMB 40-80 billion below the base case
- Counter-evidence: regulation has shifted toward "promoting growth" from 2023-2026, and Alibaba has essentially completed its compliance overhaul

### Script 6: AI Capex Devours Profit While Commercialization Lags
- Probability: 30-35%
- Profit impact: free cash flow drops sharply over the next 3-5 years, with cumulative capex exceeding RMB 400 billion before FY2030, but AI revenue growth lags depreciation growth, leaving FY2036 profit RMB 30-50 billion below the base case
- Trigger: Alibaba Cloud's AI revenue CAGR falls below 30% while depreciation CAGR exceeds 40%
- Counter-evidence: in 2025, Alibaba's AI-related cloud revenue already accounted for 25-30% of Alibaba Cloud's total, growing at a triple-digit rate

**These script probabilities are not independent** — if Script 1 and Script 4 occur together (e-commerce erosion plus macro deflation), FY2036 profit could come in RMB 150-250 billion below the base case.

## 7. Historical Analogies and Lessons

### Analogy 1: Microsoft 2014-2024 (Successful Transformation)
- Key moves: Satya Nadella took over as CEO, went all-in on cloud, abandoned "Mobile First" for "Cloud First," and acquired LinkedIn/GitHub/Activision
- Result: market cap grew from USD 300 billion to USD 3 trillion, with the cloud business becoming the main engine
- Similarities to Alibaba: a CEO change (Eddie Wu in 2023), an all-in bet on AI plus cloud, and the divestment of non-core assets (Sun Art Retail, Intime)
- Differences from Alibaba: (1) Microsoft had Office's cash flow to fund a decade of transformation, whereas Taotian's cash flow is being squeezed by e-commerce competition; (2) Microsoft held a near-monopoly position in the enterprise market, whereas Alibaba Cloud's China market share is only 33%

**Lesson for Alibaba**: the key to a successful transformation is "stable enough cash flow from the legacy business plus a second growth curve built within five years." 2026-2030 is Alibaba's critical window.

### Analogy 2: IBM 2010-2024 (Failed Transformation)
- Key moves: bet on Watson AI, acquired Red Hat, divested infrastructure businesses
- Result: revenue stagnated for a decade, market cap chronically underperformed the broader market, and cloud market share fell from 7% to 4%
- Reasons for failure: (1) the legacy business (mainframes/services) declined too fast to be caught by anything new; (2) the new business (Watson) suffered from poor execution; (3) the organizational culture never refreshed

**Lesson for Alibaba**: beware of "picking the right track (AI / cloud) but failing to execute." Alibaba has a track record of this — "leading in underlying technology but weak in product execution" (Fliggy, Xiami, and Taobao Live all launched later than Kuaishou and Douyin).

### Analogy 3: Tencent 2018-2024 (Re-rating After the Darkest Hong Kong Stock Period)
- Darkest point: in 2021-2022, regulation, antitrust action, gaming-license freezes, and a Hong Kong stock liquidity crisis combined to drag market cap down from HKD 7.5 trillion to HKD 2 trillion
- Path to re-rating: gaming stabilized, Video Accounts (Channels) took off, WeChat Pay/advertising recovered, overseas gaming (Sea / Supercell) gained traction, and buybacks accelerated
- Result: market cap returned to the HKD 4-5 trillion range in 2024-2026
- Extremely similar to Alibaba: both are Hong Kong-listed, both went through a regulatory cycle, and both relied on buybacks plus a stabilizing core business to repair valuation

**Lesson for Alibaba**: the Tencent case shows that "even a Chinese super-platform company facing its deepest headwinds will eventually see its valuation repaired, as long as its core cash flow doesn't collapse." **This is the closest of the three analogies to Alibaba's situation** — but Tencent's "recovery" was powered by Video Accounts, an unexpected second curve; Alibaba's equivalent is "Alibaba Cloud + Qwen," and whether that can carry the re-rating banner is the core bet.

### Which Analogy Is Most Instructive?

**Tencent > Microsoft > IBM.** The Tencent analogy is the closest fit because it shares the same Chinese-regulatory and Hong Kong-listing environment. ## 10. Closing: A Munger-Lens Summary

**Inverting**: if Alibaba is going to "lose" over the next 10 years, it won't be to a particular competitor — it will be to itself:
- Losing to "organizational aging plus slow decision-making" (the IBM disease)
- Losing to "picking the right track but failing to execute on product" (Alibaba's historical ailment)
- Losing to "systemic macro and geopolitical headwinds" (uncontrollable)

**Cross-application of multiple mental models**:
- Economics: both the e-commerce duopoly and the cloud three-way rivalry have already taken shape; structural incremental growth now depends mainly on AI tokens and overseas expansion
- Psychology: 60% of consumption decisions are still emotion-driven, so pure agent substitution has a ceiling
- Political science: US-China relations plus the regulatory cycle are "tail risks with a not-insignificant probability"
- Probability theory: stacking the tail risks of 10 independent variables → the probability of Alibaba being down 30%+ over 10 years is about 15-20%

**Final judgment**: Alibaba will most likely follow a "modest, upgraded Microsoft" path — it won't become a 10x story like Microsoft, but it won't become IBM either. **A FY2036 profit center of RMB 350-400 billion and a market-cap center of HKD 4-5 trillion** is a reasonably grounded, base-case expectation. This expectation leaves current buyers with a "modestly positive expected value," but falls well short of "significantly undervalued."
