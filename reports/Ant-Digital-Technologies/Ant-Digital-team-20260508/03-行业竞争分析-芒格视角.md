## IV. Munger-Style Inversion: Under What Circumstances Would Ant Digital Technologies Fail?

> "Tell me where I'm going to die, so I don't go there."

### Scenario 1: Parent-company risk transmission (probability: medium)
- Regulatory uncertainty at Ant Group could continue to weigh on brand trust and financing capacity
- Alipay's market share has fallen from 82.8% to 20.7%; losing this core traffic source means the overall appeal of the Ant ecosystem is declining

### Scenario 2: Huawei's "full-stack sweep" (probability: medium-high)
- Huawei has full-stack capability spanning chips (Kunpeng/Ascend), OS (openEuler), databases (GaussDB), AI (Pangu), and cloud
- Against the backdrop of domestic substitution, government and enterprise clients naturally lean toward "integrated domestic solutions"; Huawei's bundling capability is Ant Digital Technologies' biggest structural threat
- The openGauss ecosystem already holds 30%+ of the centralized-database market; if it pushes into distributed databases, it would directly hit OceanBase

### Scenario 3: Large models reshaping the database market (probability: medium)
- As AI becomes deeply embedded in databases, the competitive axis may shift from "stability/performance" to "intelligence"
- Alibaba Cloud, Huawei Cloud, and ByteDance — which have the strongest large-model capabilities — could rewrite the competitive landscape as a result

### Scenario 4: Blockchain and privacy computing as "false demand" (probability: low-medium)
- The BaaS market is only RMB 1.89 billion — the absolute scale is too small
- Large models could replace privacy computing in some use cases (e.g., synthetic data replacing federated learning)

### Scenario 5: Insufficient commercialization capability (probability: medium)
- Only reached break-even in 2024; absolute scale is still small
- Not a listed company; financing channels are limited

### Ranking of the Biggest Competitive Threats
1. **Squeeze from Huawei's full-stack ecosystem** — a structural threat
2. **Tencent's TDSQL maintaining its lead in financial distributed databases** — its 21.32% share exceeds OceanBase's 17.1%
3. **The large-model wave rewriting the competitive axis** — Ant Digital Technologies has no advantage in compute or foundation-model capability
4. **Regulatory-risk transmission from the parent company** — a risk unique to its "Ant lineage"

## VI. Position in the Value Chain

Ant Digital Technologies is positioned at the **midstream platform/product layer** — it does not make heavy asset investments in upstream chips/compute, nor does it engage in downstream, labor-intensive integration services; it focuses on midstream, high-margin software products and platforms.

Key weakness: **no control over upstream compute/chips**. If Huawei binds GaussDB to its Ascend chips to create an integrated upstream-downstream advantage, Ant Digital Technologies could face the risk of being "a tree without roots."

**Overall score: ★★★☆☆**

**Munger's summary**: The industry Ant Digital Technologies operates in is, in itself, a good business (high margins, high switching costs, favorable policy tailwinds), but it is not an "easy" business. On the main battlefield, it faces full-stack giants like Huawei, Tencent, and Alibaba, and as an "independent player" it is at a resource disadvantage. Blockchain and privacy computing may be leading, but the two markets combined are less than RMB 3 billion — not enough to support a large valuation.
