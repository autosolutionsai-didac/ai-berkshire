## 0. Executive Summary

Ant Digital Technologies is currently a **limited liability company** with registered capital of RMB 2.107 billion (completed via a capital injection from RMB 100 million in May 2024). It has not yet completed a joint-stock conversion, and **there is no true "per-share" concept** — only "market value per yuan of registered capital." The underlying asset of the employee equity incentive instruments (SER/RSU) is **Ant Group**, not Ant Digital Technologies; after the March 2025 "one-split-into-four" mapping, Ant Digital Technologies accounts for only an implied **1.4%** of that equity.

This report breaks "per-share value" down into three layers:
- **Layer A (current basis)**: market value per yuan of registered capital → **RMB 21.4 to 35.6**
- **Layer B (implied employee-incentive basis)**: accounting fair value of the "Digital Technologies portion" per SER → **RMB 1.51/SER** (approximately RMB 1.51-1.69 after a market-based correction)
- **Layer C (post-IPO forecast)**: IPO offering price range based on comparable companies → **HKD 6 to 30 per share** (depending on the post-listing share count and valuation combination)

The gaps between the three layers are mainly driven by **accounting conservatism + related-party transaction discounts + liquidity discounts + IPO multiple expansion**, with a multiplier relationship of roughly **B → A about 14-24x, A → C about 0.5-3x** (depending on the post-listing share structure design).

## 1. Layering the Concept: Which "Per Share" Are We Talking About?

Much of the public discussion treats "how much is Ant Digital Technologies worth per share" as a single question — **this is incorrect**. At the current stage, Ant Digital Technologies' "per share" must be split into at least three layers:

### 1.1 Comparison table of the three layers

| Layer | Underlying asset | Unit | Marketized? | Does it currently exist? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer A / Current capital contribution** | Ant Digital Technologies itself | Market valuation per "yuan" of registered capital | No (no external financing anchor) | ✅ Exists |
| **Layer B / Implied employee incentive** | Ant Group equity (including the 1.4% Digital Technologies mapping) | The Digital Technologies portion within each SER (RMB 108.21 post-split) | Semi-marketized (based on parent company fair value) | ✅ Exists |
| **Layer C / Post-IPO stock** | Ant Digital Technologies' listed shares | Per share (proposed HKD denomination) | Yes (public market) | ❌ Does not yet exist |

### 1.2 Why these three layers must be kept separate

1. **Different legal entities**: Layer A is the capital contribution interest in "Ant Chain (Shanghai) Digital Technology Co., Ltd."; Layer B's underlying asset is equity in "Ant Technology Group Co., Ltd."; Layer C is the future post-restructuring IPO entity (most likely a newly established Hong Kong-listed holding company).
2. **Different liquidity**: Layer A has zero liquidity (transferring equity in a limited liability company requires shareholder approval); Layer B is an internal corporate mapping, with departing employees subject to mandatory cash buybacks; Layer C, if listed, would trade freely on the Hong Kong secondary market.
3. **Different sources of value**: Layer A reflects "what Ant Digital Technologies is worth as a company today"; Layer B reflects "how Ant Group's internal accounting values Digital Technologies"; Layer C reflects "what multiple the future listed market is willing to pay."
4. **Different holders**: Layer A shareholders are only Ant Group and its holding platforms; Layer B holders are current/former employees; Layer C will eventually be public market investors.

> **Core reminder**: Layer B (the Digital Technologies portion within an SER) is not the same as "holding Ant Digital Technologies stock." This is the root cause of the dispute between some employees and the company during the March 2025 "one-split-into-four" event — the SER's underlying asset has always been Ant Group; Digital Technologies is merely an accounting mapping.

### 2.1 Known facts

- Existing 4/17 research valuation range: **RMB 45-75 billion** (median RMB 55 billion)
- As of 2026-04-28, **there is no external independent financing round pricing** (the capital injection was an internal capital-structure adjustment)

| Valuation assumption | Valuation (RMB) | Value per yuan of registered capital |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | RMB 30 billion | **RMB 14.2** |
| 4/17 lower bound | RMB 45 billion | **RMB 21.4** |

### 2.3 Important caveats

Once it undergoes a joint-stock conversion and completes an IPO, the share structure will be redesigned by splitting each yuan of registered capital into N shares.
- The actual source of funds for the capital injection to RMB 2.107 billion has not been publicly disclosed; industry speculation suggests it may include subscriptions by employee shareholding platforms into Ant Digital Technologies.

## 3. Layer B: The Value of the "Digital Technologies Portion" Implied in Employee Incentive SERs

### 3.1 Accounting fair value from the one-split-into-four event

**Core data** (source: Sina Finance, original article 2025-03-11, cross-verified across multiple sources 🟢):

| Item | Pre-split | Post-split (Ant Group portion only) | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| SER pre-tax fair value | RMB 115.85/unit | **RMB 108.21/unit** | -6.6% |
| RSU fair value | RMB 20.95/unit | **RMB 19.58/unit** | -6.5% |

**Mapping ratios**: Ant Group **93.4%** / Ant International **4.8%** / Ant Digital Technologies **1.4%** / OceanBase **0.4%**

### 3.2 Implied "Digital Technologies portion per SER"

**Accounting basis** (based on the one-split-into-four fair value):

```
Digital Technologies portion per SER = (115.85 − 108.21) × (1.4% ÷ 6.6%) ≈ RMB 1.62/SER
```
Or equivalently, back-calculated using the mapping ratio:
```
Digital Technologies portion per SER ≈ 108.21 × (1.4% ÷ 93.4%) ≈ RMB 1.62/SER
```

> Rounded, approximately **RMB 1.5-1.7/SER** is the accounting-basis "Digital Technologies value implied behind each SER."

### 3.3 Back-solving with a market-based valuation (a key correction)

Limitations of the accounting fair value: **(1)** no external financing anchor; **(2)** related-party transactions not stripped out; **(3)** internal allocation may be conservative.

Using Ant Group's 2025 Hurun valuation of RMB 635 billion (cross-verified against the RMB 571 billion one-split-into-four fair-value figure):

| Ant Digital Technologies valuation assumption | Share of group | Digital Technologies portion per SER |
|---|---|---|
| RMB 8 billion (implied employee-incentive basis) | 1.40% | RMB 1.51/SER |
| RMB 30 billion (conservative, marketized) | 5.25% | RMB 5.69/SER |
| RMB 55 billion (neutral, 4/17 median) | 9.63% | RMB 10.42/SER |
| RMB 75 billion (4/17 upper bound) | 13.13% | RMB 14.21/SER |
| RMB 150 billion (optimistic) | 26.27% | RMB 28.42/SER |

**Note**: Share of group = Ant Digital Technologies valuation / (group's RMB 571 billion + (Ant Digital Technologies valuation − RMB 8 billion) × 1). Simplified calculation: Ant Digital Technologies valuation ÷ RMB 571 billion × 108.21.

> **Key finding**: Under the current accounting mapping, **the "Digital Technologies portion" per SER is only RMB 1.51-1.62**; but back-solving using a neutral marketized valuation (RMB 55 billion) implies approximately **RMB 10.4/SER** — the accounting basis **understates the market-based basis by roughly 6-7x**. This is the core commercial logic behind departing employees' protests over "mandatory buybacks."

### 3.4 But be careful: Layer B is not "Digital Technologies stock"

Even after back-solving to RMB 10/SER on a market basis, what employees actually hold remains **Ant Group** SER cash-flow rights — unless Ant Digital Technologies goes public independently and executes a "distribution" mechanism (currently, **no such right is granted to active employees under the agreements**). The 1.4% mapping is used only for internal buyback pricing.

- Accounting-basis Digital Technologies portion per SER ≈ RMB 1.51

## 7. Liquidity Discount and Time-Value Discussion

### 7.1 Layers of liquidity discount

The discount from unlisted to listed equity typically comes in three stages:

| Discount type | Typical magnitude | Applicability to Ant Digital Technologies |
|---|---|---|
| **Private primary market to secondary market** | 30-50% | Chinese private equity funds have discounted 40-50% over the past two years (TR Capital data, 2024-2025) |
| **Pre-IPO to IPO pricing** | 10-25% | IPOs typically leave cornerstone investors 15-20% of book-value upside |
| **Hong Kong small-cap liquidity** | 10-30% | Daily trading volume among Hong Kong stocks in 2025-2026 has diverged sharply; small-cap liquidity is poor |

> **Overall judgment**: The current Layer A "neutral RMB 26.1 per yuan of registered capital," absent an external financing anchor, **still requires an additional 30-40% discount** to reflect a true "tradable price." In other words, the true Layer A valuation likely falls between RMB 18-21 per yuan of registered capital.

### 7.2 Time-value discount

The time dimension of employee equity incentives (referencing Ant Group's new rules effective April 2025):

- **Cliff period**: 1 year (no sale permitted before first vesting)
- **Vesting period**: 4 years (vesting 1/16 per quarter)
- **Earliest IPO timing**: second half of 2027 (per the CEO's public statement) → roughly 18 months from now

**Discount rate assumption of 10%** (referencing Ant Group's cost of equity capital):

```
RMB 1 today vs. RMB 1 at end-2027 → discount factor ≈ 0.85
RMB 1 today vs. RMB 1 at end-2030 (full liquidity) → discount factor ≈ 0.62
```

> **Key finding**: Even assuming a successful listing, **today's "book value" is worth at least a 15% discount to the post-listing tradable price**. Layering on the probability of IPO failure (a conservative assumption of 30-40%), the true "risk-adjusted" value must be discounted further still.

### 7.3 Hong Kong small-cap liquidity discount case studies

- **OneConnect** (OCFT): IPO offering price of USD 10 in December 2019; as of April 2026 only USD 7.89, a 21% discount; Hong Kong ticker 6638 trades extremely thinly
- **Hong Kong 18C specialist-tech listings**: New small-cap tech listings in 2025-2026 have commonly broken issue on day one or traded very thinly

> **Lesson**: Even if Layer C successfully lists, **the share price will most likely trade around the offering price for the first 2-3 years**; realizing genuine tradable value may not happen until 3+ years after listing.

## 9. Synthesis and Conclusions

### 9.1 Anchoring the three layers of per-share value

> **Layer A (current capital contribution)**: market value per yuan of registered capital **RMB 21-36** (based on the 4/17 valuation range of RMB 45-75 billion), neutral RMB 26.1 per yuan of registered capital
> **Layer B (implied employee incentive)**: accounting-basis Digital Technologies portion per SER **RMB 1.51-1.62**; market-corrected basis **RMB 5.7-14.2** (conservative-to-upper-bound), neutral RMB 10.4
> **Layer C (IPO forecast)**: HKD **8-30 per share** (neutral scenario, depending on share structure design), neutral ~HKD 18-26/share

### 9.2 Multiplier relationship between current "book value" and "IPO expectation"

| Path | Starting point | Ending point | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting-basis SER Digital Technologies portion → neutral market-based Digital Technologies valuation | RMB 1.51 | RMB 10.4 | **6.9x** |
| Neutral market-based Digital Technologies valuation → post-listing tradable IPO value | RMB 10.4 | RMB 13-15 | **1.3-1.5x** |
| **Total multiplier (accounting → tradable)** | RMB 1.51 | RMB 13-15 | **approximately 8.6-9.9x** |

> **Core conclusion**: If the neutral scenario materializes and employees are able to share in the proceeds of Digital Technologies' standalone listing, **the multiplier between today's book value and future tradable value is roughly 8-10x**. But the preconditions for realizing this are strict (successful IPO + a participation mechanism). If the IPO fails (an extreme scenario), the book value = 0.

### 9.3 A decision framework for readers

1. **Judging the valuation range**: Do you believe the 4/17 "RMB 45-75 billion" median, or the "RMB 8 billion employee-incentive back-calculated basis"? The former implies market-based logic; the latter implies accounting conservatism.
2. **Judging IPO probability**: The base-rate probability is about 50-60% (referencing Ant International going first in 2026, with Digital Technologies at the earliest in the second half of 2027).
3. **Judging share structure design**: A compact structure (1.1 billion shares) versus a large structure (3.3 billion shares) implies roughly a 3x difference in offering price.
4. **Judging liquidity**: Small-cap Hong Kong listings typically have poor liquidity for the first 2-3 years post-listing; there is still a 30-50% discount between "book value" and "realizable cash."

> **The simplest possible answer**: If someone asks today, "How much is the Digital Technologies portion behind one SER of Ant Digital Technologies worth?" — **the accounting answer is RMB 1.6, the market-based answer is RMB 10, the optimistic post-IPO answer is RMB 15, and the failed-IPO answer is close to the accounting buyback price**. The gap is 6-10x.
