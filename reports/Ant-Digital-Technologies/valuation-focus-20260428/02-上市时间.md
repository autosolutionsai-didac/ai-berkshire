### Key causal chains

**Chain 1 (mainstream path)**:
> Ant Group financial holding license → Ant International Hong Kong filing/listing → Ant Digital Technologies files 12-18 months later

**Chain 2 (alternative path)**:
> Ant Group overall A+H IPO → Ant Digital Technologies disclosed as a "technology services" business segment → Ant Digital Technologies **never lists independently**

**Chain 3 (low-probability aggressive path)**:
> Hong Kong stablecoin second batch + Ant Digital Technologies makes the list → RWA narrative re-rated → Ant Digital Technologies files in 2027 H2, ahead of OceanBase

Historically, HKEX **has never approved a standalone IPO for a subsidiary spun off from an unlisted parent**, so **Chain 1's "parent or Ant International goes first" is an almost insurmountable hard constraint**. This is the single most important bottleneck for Ant Digital Technologies' IPO timing.

Source: [HKEX New Listing Rules, Practice Note 15 on spin-offs](https://en-rules.hkex.com.hk/rulebook/practice-note-15) (industry consensus at the practice level: an unlisted parent's spin-off will not be approved)

## II. Three possible paths and their probabilities (vs. 4/17)

### 2.1 Path probability distribution table

| # | Path | 4/17 probability | 4/28 revision | Change vs. 4/17 | Reason for revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Independent Hong Kong IPO** (Scenarios 1+2+3) | 52% | 50% | ↓ 2pp | Ant-affiliated entities absent from the 19-name large Hong Kong IPO list ([Sina Finance, 2026.4.19](https://finance.sina.com.cn/wm/2026-04-19/doc-inhvackq0245889.shtml)), indicating no filing action within 2026 |
| 2 | **Parent's overall A+H IPO, with Ant Digital Technologies as a segment** | 20% | 22% | ↑ 2pp | Parent's "3-year no change in controlling shareholder" threshold was met as of 2026.1, raising the feasibility of an overall IPO |
| 3 | **Independent STAR Market (A-share) IPO** | <3% | <3% | Unchanged | JD Digits' withdrawal remains a cautionary lesson; A-share regulation of fintech classification remains tightened |
| 4 | **Merged with OceanBase into a combined Hong Kong IPO** | 10% | 10% | Unchanged | No new signal |
| 5 | **No listing / bought back and absorbed** | 15% | 15% | Unchanged | No new signal |

> The five probabilities sum to 100% (mutually exclusive and exhaustive). "Independent Hong Kong IPO" remains the single most likely path, but **Ant Digital Technologies' IPO has only a 50% probability of occurring within 5 years**; the other 50% consists of being folded into a segment, merged, or not listing at all.

### 2.2 Arguments for and against each path

#### Path 1: Independent Hong Kong IPO (baseline 50%)

**Supporting arguments**:
- Hong Kong equity liquidity recovered in 2025-2026, with IPO fundraising leading the world ([Wall Street CN, 2026](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3762424))
- HKEX Chapter 18C is open to unprofitable specialist technology companies, and Ant Digital Technologies' ABC business line fits ([HKEX Chapter 18C in practice](https://www.eet-china.com/mp/a408910.html))
- With the STAR Market effectively closed to fintech, Hong Kong is the only realistic path

**Counter-arguments**:
- Of eight prior spin-off-type fintech subsidiary listings, the **full-success rate is 0%** (see report 07 from 4/17)
- HKEX's practical constraint that "an unlisted parent's spin-off will not be approved" remains unresolved
- Related-party transactions at 30-40% exceed the market convention threshold (30%)

#### Path 2: Parent's overall A+H IPO, with Ant Digital Technologies as a segment (revised up to 22%)

**Supporting arguments**:
- Ant Group completed its change of controlling shareholder in January 2023 and, as of January 2026, satisfies the A-share main-board "3-year no change in controlling shareholder" threshold ([Eastmoney, 2026.3.18](https://caifuhao.eastmoney.com/news/20260318170135977473130))
- Han Xinyi, as a CFO-turned-CEO, favors "an overall group IPO to maximize valuation"
- The HK$2.8 billion acquisition of Bright Smart Securities rounded out the group's Hong Kong financial licenses ([TMTPost, 2026.3](https://www.tmtpost.com/7554195.html)), which the market reads as the "final piece of the puzzle" before a parent-company Hong Kong listing

**Counter-arguments**:
- The financial holding company license has still not been issued; the People's Bank of China's 2020 requirement has remained unresolved for 6 years
- Ant Group's official position is "there is currently no listing plan" (reaffirmed multiple times)
- An anchor valuation for an overall IPO is hard to find — there is a 73% gap between the RMB 2.1 trillion valuation from 2020 and the RMB 567.1 billion valuation used in the 2025 buyback

#### Path 3: Independent STAR Market (A-share) IPO (<3%)

**Supporting arguments**:
- Ant Digital Technologies' business classification as "to-B technology" is in theory better suited to the STAR Market than fintech is

**Counter-arguments**:
- JD Digits' 2021 withdrawal set an industry precedent; A-shares have zero tolerance for "parent spin-off + financial affiliation" ([21jingji.com, 2021.4.2](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20210402/herald/40e9a5ef220059a6580f457a10b3d8b0.html))
- A-share IPO pace overall is tight in 2026, and screening standards for new entrants are stricter

#### Path 4: Combined with OceanBase (10%)

**Supporting arguments**:
- OceanBase's CEO stated IPO is "progressing naturally" (2025.11), and it belongs to the same technology to-B segment as Ant Digital Technologies
- A merger could lower the compliance difficulty around related-party transactions

**Counter-arguments**:
- OceanBase's database-business margin structure differs greatly from Ant Digital Technologies' AI/RWA business, making a consistent valuation anchor difficult
- OceanBase has already operated independently since 2024 and completed an independent capital raise with 35 shareholders, which instead points toward an independent path

#### Path 5: No listing / bought back and absorbed (15%)

**Supporting arguments**:
- Ant Group's 2025 buyback of employee equity (at a RMB 567.1 billion valuation, [stcn.com](http://stcn.com/article/detail/928279.html)) sets a precedent for "internal absorption"
- There is tail risk that regulatory delay causes the window to be missed

**Counter-arguments**:
- As one of Ant Group's three major subsidiaries, the group has an incentive to push it toward external financing
- Employee shareholders' demand for an exit cannot be suppressed indefinitely

## VI. The investor's-eye view of "waiting cost": an IRR estimate

### 6.1 Estimation framework

**Key assumptions** (🔴 estimate):

**Baseline scenario (2029 listing)**, weighted by post-listing outcome distribution:
