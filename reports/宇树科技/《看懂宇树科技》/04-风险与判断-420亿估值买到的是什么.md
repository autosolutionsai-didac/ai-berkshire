## Let's do the math first

A 42-billion-yuan valuation, 1.7 billion yuan in revenue, PS of 25x — these three numbers holding true simultaneously tells us the market is prepaying for a future that hasn't happened yet. The question is: how confident should we be in that future? Is the price being paid for it reasonable?

Before diving into the risk analysis, let's lay the numbers out clearly.

### The valuation's evolution

| Time | Event | Valuation |
|------|------|------|
| June 2025 | Series C financing (700 million yuan) | **12 billion yuan** |
| March 2026 | IPO filing | **42 billion yuan** |

**In 10 months, the valuation rose 3.5x.** The Series C investors (Tencent, Alibaba, Ant Group, China Mobile, and others) are sitting on paper gains of nearly 3x.

### Valuation multiples

| Metric | Calculation |
|------|------|
| 2025 revenue | **1.708 billion yuan** |
| 2025 net profit excluding non-recurring items | **600 million yuan** |
| IPO valuation | **42 billion yuan** |
| **PS (price-to-sales)** | **approx. 25x** |
| **PE (price-to-earnings)** | **approx. 70x** |

(Source: prospectus)

For comparison:

| Company | PS | PE | Industry |
|------|----|----|------|
| Unitree Robotics (IPO) | **approx. 25x** | **approx. 70x** | Robotics |
| DJI (private-market valuation) | approx. 15-20x | — | Drones |

| Tesla | approx. 10x | approx. 80x | New-energy vehicles + AI |

(Source: public market data, estimates)

What does a PS of 25x mean? **The market is prepaying a premium for Unitree's expected high growth over the next 3-5 years.** If Unitree can sustain 100%+ annual growth — revenue reaching 3.5 billion yuan in 2026 and 7 billion yuan in 2027 — then based on 2027 revenue, PS would fall to around 6x, entering a reasonable range.

**But if growth falls short of expectations, the 42-billion-yuan valuation becomes a "castle in the air."**

## Five core risks

### Risk one: the AI-brain shortfall (risk level: extremely high)

This is the core contradiction already analyzed in detail in Part 02 — here we'll just add the key numbers:

| Dimension | Unitree Robotics | Benchmark |
|------|---------|------|
| R&D spend | **90.2 million yuan** (7.73% of revenue) | Tesla's AI R&D: billions of dollars |
| R&D headcount | A portion of a 480-person team | Tesla's AI team: over 10,000 |
| AI portion of IPO proceeds | **2.022 billion yuan** | OpenAI's single funding round: $6.6 billion |

Of the 4.2 billion yuan raised in the IPO, 48% is allocated to AI R&D — the right direction, but the amount is two orders of magnitude behind the industry giants.

**Worst case**: if AI capability never catches up, within 3-5 years Unitree gets downgraded from a "finished-product brand" to a "hardware contract manufacturer" — building bodies for companies that actually own the AI brain. In that scenario, gross margin falls from 60% to 20-30%, and the valuation shrinks dramatically.

**Best case**: Unitree takes the "hardware platform + open-source AI ecosystem" path, similar to Android — it doesn't need to build the best AI itself, only the best hardware platform, letting AI companies build applications on top of it. If this path works, Unitree can still sustain a high gross margin.

### Risk two: the Tesla impact (risk level: high)

Tesla's Optimus Gen 3 mass-production version had its design finalized in Q1 2026, with large-scale production lines coming online in August 2026 and official external sales beginning in 2027.

**Target cost once in mass production: $20,000-25,000** (approx. 140,000-180,000 yuan).

That price is much cheaper than Unitree's high-end H1 ($90,000), but much more expensive than the R1 (29,900 yuan). In the near term, Tesla's and Unitree's price bands don't fully overlap, but Tesla's AI advantage might make customers willing to pay more.

**Time-window analysis**:

| Scenario | Unitree's time window | Probability |
|------|---------------|------|
| Tesla mass-produces on schedule (2027) | **Under 2 years** | Low-to-moderate |
| Tesla delayed 1-2 years (2028-2029) | **3-4 years** | Moderate-to-high |
| Tesla delayed 3+ years (2030+) | **5+ years** | Low |

Musk's product timelines have historically run well behind schedule — Cybertruck was delayed 2 years, Roadster 2 by more than 5 years. But even if Optimus is delayed, the competitive model it represents — "AI large models plus manufacturing scale" — will eventually arrive.

**Counter-argument (bullish on Unitree)**: Tesla may not take robotics seriously — its core business is selling cars and autonomous driving, and Optimus could remain a long-term side project. And China's market has policy barriers, so Tesla's robots may not sell well in China regardless.

### Risk three: pace of commercialization (risk level: moderate-to-high)

**73.6%** of humanoid robot revenue comes from research and education — a customer mix that indicates Unitree's humanoid robots haven't yet found a "killer application."

| Use case | Current status | Challenge |
|------|------|------|
| Research/education | Primary (73.6%) | Limited ceiling |
| Factory manufacturing | Pilot stage (CRRC Zhuzhou and others) | Requires autonomous AI decision-making |
| Logistics/material handling | Pilot stage | Competing with purpose-built robots |
| Consumer services | R1 just launched | Use case unclear |

Unitree has already deployed its industrial-grade embodied large model UnifoLM-X1-0 in its own factory to complete joint-motor assembly — but this is still just an "in-house pilot," far from large-scale commercialization.

The shipment target for 2026 is **10,000-20,000 units** (Wang Xingxing's public statement, per public reporting). If the upper bound of 20,000 units is achieved, revenue could reach the **3-4 billion yuan** level (depending on product mix and changes in average selling price).

**Counter-argument**: research/education customers won't grow indefinitely — university budgets are limited. If commercial-consumer and industrial applications fail to show a clear breakthrough in 2026-2027, growth will fall off a cliff — and at that point, the 42-billion-yuan valuation would have no support.

**Supporting argument**: quadruped robots took 3 years to go from "69% research" to "42% commercial-consumer" — humanoid robots are walking the same path; it just takes patience.

### Risk four: valuation overextension (risk level: high)

The 42-billion-yuan valuation already bakes in a large amount of future expectation. Let's do a simple reverse calculation:

**For the 42-billion-yuan valuation to look "reasonable" three years from now (2029)** —

- Assume 2029 PE falls back to 30x (a reasonable level for a growth-stage company)
- Then 2029 net profit needs to reach **1.4 billion yuan** (42 billion / 30 = 1.4 billion)
- 2025 net profit was 600 million yuan → that requires a **2.3x** increase over 4 years, an annualized growth rate of about **24%**

A 24% annualized profit growth rate isn't overly demanding for a company in an industry-wide boom — but only if the industry truly booms, commercialization truly takes hold, and the AI shortfall gets closed.

**Another calculation**: using PS valuation —

- 2025 PS is 25x; for it to fall to 10x by 2028 (a reasonable level for tech stocks)
- 2028 revenue needs to reach **4.2 billion yuan**
- 2025 revenue was 1.7 billion yuan → that requires a **2.5x** increase over 3 years, an annualized growth rate of about **35%**

Given that 2025's growth rate was 335%, a 35% annualized rate implies growth will slow substantially — that's normal; the key is not slowing down too fast.

**Core question**: the 42-billion-yuan valuation assumes "everything goes right" — the industry booms, commercialization advances, and AI closes the gap. If any single assumption fails to hold, the valuation faces a substantial risk of correction.

### Risk five: the IPO on-site inspection (risk level: moderate)

On April 1, 2026, Unitree Robotics was selected for an IPO on-site inspection. This is a routine procedure, but it will delay the listing timeline.

| Impact | Estimate |
|------|------|
| Inspection duration | 1-2 months |
| Impact on the review process | Delay of **3-9 months** |
| Estimated earliest listing date | **Late 2026 to early 2027** |
| Risk of withdrawal | Low (all 13 companies selected for inspection in 2026 are proceeding normally) |

(Source: 21jingji.com, Cailian Press)

Industry insiders say on-site inspections have shifted from the earlier "high-pressure screening" approach to "routine disclosure verification" — being selected doesn't indicate a material problem.

**But the delay itself carries an indirect impact**: if the listing is pushed to 2027, Tesla's Optimus may already be in mass production by then — and market expectations for Unitree's valuation could shift as a result.

## Management: a one-man company

Wang Xingxing holds **26.97%** of the equity, but controls nearly **70% of voting rights**. This isn't unusual on the STAR Market, but it means Unitree's governance is highly dependent on a single person.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Technical ability | Strong — chose the electric-drive path correctly from day one |
| Cost discipline | Strong — a 60% gross margin isn't an accident |
| Strategic judgment | Yet to be proven — the shift from quadruped to humanoid is sound; whether AI catches up remains to be seen |
| Management experience | Yet to be proven — a 480-person team; has never managed an organization of thousands |
| Capital-markets experience | None — first-time IPO |

Early investors describe Wang Xingxing as someone whose "eyes light up when talking about technology" — a classic trait of a technical founder. The advantage of a technically-driven CEO is strong product execution; the drawback can be a lack of experience in organizational management and capital operations.

**A company valued at 42 billion yuan, with a 36-year-old CEO who has never taken a company public and a team of only 480 people** — this is both the upside and the risk.

| Scenario | Profile |
|------|------|
| Best case | "The DJI of robotics" — AI gap closed, a globally leading finished-product brand |

It will most likely become the leader of the global low-to-mid-end humanoid robotics market — similar to BYD's position in new-energy vehicles, or DJI's position in consumer drones. That's a good business, but its valuation ceiling is limited.

## How should investors think about this company?

This series doesn't offer buy or sell recommendations. But it can help you clarify a framework for thinking about it.

### If you're bullish on Unitree, you need to believe:

1. The humanoid robotics industry genuinely booms within 3-5 years (from 15,000 units to hundreds of thousands)
2. Unitree's cost advantage can convert into a durable scale moat
3. The AI shortfall can be closed through IPO proceeds plus external partnerships — or the "hardware platform" path is valuable enough on its own
4. Tesla's Optimus won't pose a fatal threat within 2-3 years
5. The 42-billion-yuan valuation will be absorbed by growth within 2-3 years

### If you're bearish on Unitree, you need to believe:

1. Humanoid robots are a false need — purpose-built robots (robotic arms, etc.) are sufficient for industrial use cases
2. The AI brain is the ultimate moat, and hardware companies will eventually be reduced to contract manufacturing
3. Tesla's Optimus will mass-produce on schedule or close to it, delivering a generational blow
4. 73% of revenue from research/education shows commercialization is far from opened up, and growth will fall off a cliff
5. A PS of 25x bakes in too much optimism, leaving substantial downside

### One key mental framework

**Don't ask "is Unitree Robotics a good company" — ask "is Unitree Robotics good at a 42-billion-yuan valuation."**

Unitree is an excellent hardware company — that's not in dispute. The question is: has this price already fully priced in "excellent" — or even overextended past it?

An analogy: Tesla in 2021 was also an excellent company, but its $1.2 trillion market cap meant the market had already prepaid the growth expectations for 2025 — investors who bought at that level saw limited returns even as the company kept growing.

**Unitree Robotics at a 42-billion-yuan valuation could face a similar situation: the company keeps growing, but the valuation doesn't necessarily keep rising.**

## A note to readers

If you've finished this series and still feel "uncertain" — congratulations, that means you've understood it correctly.

Unitree Robotics is a **highly uncertain** company. It could be one of the most successful Chinese tech companies of the next decade, or it could end up a bit player in the AI wave.

**Uncertainty itself isn't a bad thing — it just means you need to demand adequate risk compensation for that uncertainty.** If the price you pay already fully reflects optimistic expectations, then even if the company performs well, the investment return may still disappoint.
