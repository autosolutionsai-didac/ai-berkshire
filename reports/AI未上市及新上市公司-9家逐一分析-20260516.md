# Company-by-Company Analysis of 9 AI-Track Companies

> Analysis date: May 16, 2026 | Data sources: public financial reports, prospectuses, media coverage

---

## I. Autonomous Driving Track

---

### 1. Pony.ai

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Beijing/Guangzhou, founded December 2016; co-founders James Peng (former chief architect of Baidu's autonomous driving unit) and Lou Tiancheng |
| Business | Three business lines: L4 Robotaxi, Robotruck, and technology licensing. The seventh-generation Robotaxi is already in commercial operation, and Guangzhou has achieved per-vehicle operating profitability |
| Financing/valuation | Dual-listed on Nasdaq (PONY) and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (02026). Market cap of approximately HK$49 billion (about US$4.7 billion). Cash reserves of 10.593 billion yuan |
| Competitive positioning | The fastest-commercializing domestic L4 autonomous driving company, and the first in the world to achieve city-wide per-vehicle profitability for Robotaxi. Fleet target of 3,000+ vehicles by end-2026, deployed across 20+ cities globally |
| Financials | Full-year 2025 revenue of 629 million yuan (+20%). Robotruck revenue of 284 million yuan, technology licensing revenue of 229 million yuan. Q4 marked the first quarterly profit, with net income of 528 million yuan (including investment gains) |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4 stars) — the fastest commercialization progress in the autonomous driving track, with per-vehicle profitability already validated; but overall profitability still depends on scaling up, and the valuation is on the high side and needs time to digest |

Pony.ai is the most solidly commercialized of the three autonomous driving companies. The core highlight is that the seventh-generation vehicle has already achieved per-vehicle profitability; if the fleet-scale target of 3,000 vehicles by end-2026 is met, it would represent a qualitative shift. The risks lie in the uncertain pace of Robotaxi policy progress, and competitive pressure from the L2+ mass-production market (Momenta's home turf). A market cap of US$4.7 billion against revenue of 629 million yuan implies a P/S of roughly 50x — not a cheap valuation, but the ceiling for this track is extremely high. Cash reserves of over 10.5 billion yuan mean the company is not short on ammunition. Inclusion in the MSCI China Index is a liquidity positive.

---

### 2. WeRide

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Guangzhou, founded April 2017; founder Tony Han (former chief scientist of Baidu's autonomous driving unit) |
| Business | Robotaxi, autonomous minibuses, driverless street sweepers, and autonomous freight — the broadest product matrix. Global fleet of 2,113 vehicles, including 1,125 Robotaxis |
| Financing/valuation | Dual-listed on Nasdaq (WRD) and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Hong Kong-listed market cap of approximately HK$22.2 billion. Cash reserves of 7.1 billion yuan |
| Competitive positioning | The autonomous driving company with the fullest product lineup, covering four scenarios — taxi, minibus, street sweeping, and freight. But this "broad but not deep" approach also carries the greatest risk |
| Financials | Full-year 2025 revenue of 685 million yuan (+89.6%). Product revenue of 360 million yuan (+310%), minibus revenue of 231 million yuan. Net loss of 1.7 billion yuan (narrowed 34.2%), gross margin of 30.2% |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐ (3 stars) — impressive revenue growth but losses remain large, the product line is overly dispersed, and per-vehicle breakeven is expected in 2026 with company-wide breakeven not until 2028 — a long wait |

WeRide's strategy is "multi-scenario coverage," in contrast to Pony.ai's focus on Robotaxi. The 89.6% revenue growth rate looks impressive, but the absolute figure is only 685 million yuan, while the net loss still stands at 1.7 billion yuan. The 30.2% gross margin is far below Pony.ai's, indicating that hardware revenue makes up a larger share of the product mix while software licensing accounts for less. The stock price has fallen from a high of US$12.55 to around US$8, reflecting market skepticism about the pace of commercialization. Huatai Securities projects company-wide breakeven only by 2028, meaning the company will still need to "burn cash" for the next two years. Cash reserves of 7.1 billion yuan are sufficient but not abundant. Compared with Pony.ai, WeRide carries greater uncertainty.

### 3. Momenta

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Beijing/Suzhou, founded 2016; founder Cao Xudong (bachelor's and master's from Tsinghua, former Microsoft Research Asia) |
| Business | A "two-legged" strategy: L2+ mass-production intelligent driving solutions (core business) plus L4 fully driverless technology. Cumulatively deployed in nearly 700,000 vehicles, with 170+ vehicle models secured, in deep partnerships with Volkswagen, Toyota, Honda, Mercedes-Benz, General Motors, and others |
| Financing/valuation | Not yet listed; has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO, with an expected valuation of over 100 billion yuan (about US$14 billion). Cumulative financing of US$1.25 billion. Strategic partnership established with Uber |
| Competitive positioning | The absolute leader in L2+ mass-production intelligent driving solutions, far ahead of peers in the number of automaker partnerships and secured vehicle models. Its "flywheel" model — using mass-production data to feed back into L4 development — is its core moat |
| Financials | Not disclosed. According to reports, cumulative losses from 2021-2023 exceeded 17.5 billion yuan (including non-recurring items such as equity incentives). The mass-production business is believed to already generate sizable revenue, but specific figures await prospectus disclosure |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4 stars) — the strongest player in the mass-production intelligent driving track; 700,000 vehicles deployed forms a data-flywheel moat, but the IPO valuation of over 100 billion yuan is on the expensive side — worth watching for post-listing pricing |

Momenta's biggest difference from the previous two companies is that it is not a pure L4 company, but rather follows a strategy of "L2+ mass production first, L4 as a long-term buildout." This means it has a real, scalable revenue source (selling intelligent driving solutions to automakers), rather than relying purely on the policy-sensitive Robotaxi scenario. The 700,000-vehicle deployment scale and 170+ secured vehicle models form a dual moat of data and customers. The Uber partnership opens up overseas potential. The core risks are that giants like Huawei and DJI are also competing for the mass-production intelligent driving market, and that the 100-billion-yuan-plus valuation implies extremely high growth expectations. It is advisable to wait for the IPO prospectus disclosure before making a judgment.

## II. Humanoid Robotics Track

---

### 4. UBTECH

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Shenzhen, founded 2012; founder Zhou Jian |
| Business | Full-size humanoid robots (Walker series) as the core, alongside educational robots, logistics robots, and others. Walker S has already been deployed in factory settings |
| Financing/valuation | Listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (09880), market cap of approximately HK$51.4 billion (about HK$102 per share). Historical high of about HK$280, a drawdown of over 60% |
| Competitive positioning | The "first humanoid robotics stock," ranking first globally in full-size humanoid robot sales and revenue. Having transitioned from educational robots, however, it still needs to rebuild brand recognition |
| Financials | 2025 total revenue of 2.001 billion yuan (+53.3%), net loss of 790 million yuan (narrowed 32%), gross margin of 37.7%. Humanoid robot revenue of 820 million yuan (+2,204%), unit sales of 1,079 |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐ (3 stars) — humanoid robot shipment volume has already validated demand, but a scale of 1,079 units is still small, and the high unit price (about 760,000 yuan per unit) limits the pace of volume growth; losses are narrowing but the company is not yet profitable |

UBTECH's biggest highlight is "1,079 units" — the first real shipment figure of any scale in the global humanoid robotics field. Humanoid robot revenue of 820 million yuan accounts for 41% of total revenue, having already surpassed educational robots as the largest business line. But viewed soberly: 1,079 units at a unit price of 760,000 yuan suggests customers are primarily large industrial enterprises running pilot projects, rather than scaled procurement. A market cap of HK$51.4 billion against revenue of 2 billion yuan implies a P/S of roughly 20x — given the imaginative upside of the humanoid robotics track, the valuation is not outlandish, but nor is it cheap. The key variable is whether shipment volume can leap from the thousands to the tens of thousands in 2026-2027. Revenue for 2026-2027 is projected (estimate) at 2.884 billion / 3.792 billion yuan, respectively.

### 5. Galbot

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Beijing, founded May 2023; founder Wang He (assistant professor at Peking University, Stanford PhD, born 1992) |
| Business | Embodied-intelligence general-purpose robots, with core products Galbot G1 (dual-arm plus single-leg wheeled chassis) and heavy-payload robot Galbot S1. Already deployed on CATL production lines |
| Financing/valuation | Latest funding round of 2.5 billion yuan (March 2026), led by the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund Phase III. Cumulative financing of about US$800 million, valuation of over US$3 billion |
| Competitive positioning | The most highly valued embodied-intelligence startup in China. Co-building a smart pharmacy with Meituan, and has signed a 1,000-unit deployment plan with Baida Precision — pursuing a "practicality first, incremental" path |
| Financials | Not disclosed. Founded less than three years ago, revenue scale is presumed to be extremely small, and the company is in a cash-burning investment phase |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐ (3 stars) — strong academic background, rapid financing pace, and a pragmatic deployment approach, but the US$3 billion valuation belongs to a startup with almost no revenue — a pure conviction bet |

Galbot is the "new star" of the humanoid robotics track. Founded in 2023, its valuation had already surpassed US$3 billion by 2026 — a striking pace. Its core differentiation lies in not pursuing "full humanoid" bipedal walking, but instead adopting a practical design of a wheeled chassis plus dual arms, prioritizing the ability to actually "get work done." This strategy has enabled faster deployment in industrial settings such as CATL. Its shareholder relationship with Meituan brings exclusive resources in retail scenarios. The risk is obvious: founded less than three years ago, commercial revenue is close to zero, and the US$3 billion valuation rests entirely on faith in the future. The endorsement of the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund Phase III is a plus, but it does not change the underlying nature of "high valuation plus zero revenue." Primary-market investors' risk tolerance can support this, but if a Hong Kong IPO is planned, the secondary market may be far more demanding.

## III. AI Video/Content Track

---

### 6. PixVerse

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Beijing, founded April 2023; founder Wang Changhu (former head of visual technology at ByteDance, former Microsoft Research Asia) |
| Business | AI video generation platform PixVerse, supporting text-to-video and image-to-video. In early 2026 released PixVerse R1, described as the world's first general-purpose real-time world model |
| Financing/valuation | Completed a US$300 million Series C round in March 2026 (the largest single round in Asia's AI video sector), valuation of 6-8 billion yuan (about US$1 billion) |
| Competitive positioning | First-tier globally in video generation, ranking second worldwide in both image-to-video and text-to-video. Global user base has surpassed 100 million, with MAU exceeding 16 million, and partnerships with tens of thousands of enterprises |
| Financials | Specific revenue not disclosed. Membership subscriptions plus enterprise API are the main monetization channels. In a phase of rapid growth but most likely still unprofitable |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4 stars) — a leading global AI video generation product; surpassing 100 million users validates product-market fit, and a valuation around US$1 billion is reasonable, but it faces strong rivals such as Kling AI |

PixVerse is one of the "best value for money" investment targets among these nine companies. Reasons: 1) a global user base surpassing 100 million and MAU of 16 million confirm that the product has validated market demand; 2) a valuation of about US$1 billion, far below Kling's US$20 billion, yet its product capability ranks globally second only to Kling; 3) founder Wang Changhu brings complete experience from ByteDance's visual technology team, and the team's execution has already been demonstrated through its financing pace and product iteration speed. The core risk is that competition in the AI video generation track is extremely intense — Kling AI, Runway, Pika, and others are all iterating rapidly, and the technical moat may not be as deep as it appears. The choice of commercialization path (to-C subscriptions vs. to-B API) will also determine the ceiling.

### 7. Kling AI (under Kuaishou / planned spin-off)

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Developed in-house by Kuaishou, launched June 2024; driven directly by Kuaishou founder Cheng Yixiao |
| Business | AI video generation foundation model Kling, supporting text-to-video, image-to-video, digital humans, and more. Already the largest video-generation AI application in China by commercial monetization scale |
| Financing/valuation | Kuaishou plans to spin off Kling AI for independent financing, with a pre-IPO round valuation of about US$20 billion, raising US$2 billion; Tencent and others are expected to participate. An independent IPO is planned for next year |
| Competitive positioning | The absolute leader in domestic AI video generation, with ARR already reaching US$500 million, most of it from overseas revenue. Technical capability is world-class, and Kuaishou's data and computing power form its core support |
| Financials | ARR of US$500 million (doubled from before the Spring Festival), Q1 2026 revenue of US$75 million. Q4 2025 single-quarter revenue of 340 million yuan. Paying users grew 350% quarter-over-quarter |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4 stars) — an ARR of US$500 million is real strength, but the US$20 billion valuation implies a P/S of 40x, while Kuaishou's own market cap is only US$29 billion — the spin-off pricing is contentious |

Kling AI is the best-commercialized AI application among these nine companies. ARR of US$500 million, a high share of overseas revenue, and explosive growth in paying users — these metrics rank at the very top among global AI applications. The core controversy lies in valuation: a US$20 billion valuation versus Kuaishou's own market cap of US$29 billion means a single subsidiary business accounts for seven-tenths of the parent company — can the market accept that? From a P/S perspective, US$20 billion / US$500 million = 40x, which isn't outlandish for a high-growth AI application (Midjourney's implied valuation is even higher), but this assumes ARR growth can be sustained. Another risk: after the spin-off, will Kling lose the boost from Kuaishou's data and traffic, and is its independent operating capability sufficient? Worth watching the terms of the spin-off financing and future standalone financial data.

## IV. Other AI Tracks

---

### 8. Horizon Robotics

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Beijing, founded July 2015; founder Yu Kai (former director of Baidu's Institute of Deep Learning, PhD in computer science from the University of Munich) |
| Business | Automotive-grade intelligent driving chips plus software platform. The Journey series chips are the core product, with the Journey 6 series set for full-scale volume production in 2026. Cumulative shipments of several million units |
| Financing/valuation | Listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (09660), market cap of approximately HK$122.5 billion. Up 132% year-to-date, one of the best-performing AI chip stocks on the Hong Kong market in 2026 |
| Competitive positioning | The absolute leader in domestically produced automotive-grade intelligent driving chips, the only local chipmaker to achieve original-equipment mass production. Deeply tied to Volkswagen, BYD, Li Auto, and others; the Journey 6 is positioned to rival Nvidia's Orin |
| Financials | 2025 revenue of 3.76 billion yuan (+57.7%), gross margin of 64.5%. R&D expenses of 5.15 billion yuan, still operating at a loss. 2026 revenue is projected (estimate) to grow 50%+, with a return to profitability expected (estimate) in 2027 |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5 stars) — the highest-certainty stock among these nine companies. Domestic substitution for intelligent driving chips is a certain trend; the 64.5% gross margin proves product strength, and profitability by 2027 is in sight |

Horizon Robotics is the company I am most bullish on among these nine. Reasons: 1) the highest track certainty — China's automotive intelligence trend is a sure thing, and domestic chip substitution is driven by both policy and market forces; 2) the best business model — a chip-plus-software-platform model, with a gross margin of 64.5% far exceeding the roughly 30% of autonomous driving operating companies; 3) the strongest growth visibility — the Journey 6 series has already secured large numbers of design wins, with 2026 shipment volume projected (estimate) to exceed 10 million units; 4) the clearest path to profitability — a turn to profit expected (estimate) in 2027. A market cap of HK$122.5 billion against revenue of 3.76 billion yuan implies a P/S of roughly 25x — given a chip company's scalability and 50%+ growth rate, the valuation is reasonable. The core risks are the potential disruption from Nvidia's next-generation chips and competition from Huawei's Ascend line.

### 9. SenseTime

| Dimension | Content |
|------|------|
| Basic information | Shanghai/Beijing/Hong Kong, founded October 2014; founder Tang Xiao'ou (passed away December 2023), CEO Xu Li |
| Business | Transitioning from traditional computer-vision AI to a generative AI platform. Generative AI (large model training/inference/fine-tuning) is the core business, with vision AI as a secondary line. Operates a large-scale GPU compute cluster |
| Financing/valuation | Listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (00020), market cap of approximately HK$70 billion (share price of about HK$2.1). Raised HK$3.2 billion through a share placement in April 2026 |
| Competitive positioning | A dual-engine strategy of "AI compute plus large models," with generative AI revenue exceeding 72% of the total. However, its positioning is somewhat blurred — engaged simultaneously in compute leasing, model services, and industry applications, lacking focus |
| Financials | 2025 total revenue of 5.015 billion yuan (+32.9%), a historical high. Generative AI revenue of 3.63 billion yuan (+51%). Adjusted net loss narrowed 58.6%, with EBITDA turning positive for the first time in the second half at 380 million yuan |
| Investment verdict | ⭐⭐⭐ (3 stars) — the transition direction is correct and generative AI is growing quickly, but the "burden" of cumulative losses exceeding 52.5 billion yuan is too heavy, frequent share placements are diluting shareholders, and the cost to trust remains extremely high |

SenseTime is the most controversial company among these nine. On the optimistic side: revenue of 5 billion yuan hit a record high, generative AI's share reached 72%, and EBITDA turned positive for the first time — the transition is genuinely happening. On the pessimistic side: cumulative losses exceed 52.5 billion yuan, frequent share placements are diluting shareholders (the latest being HK$3.2 billion), and team stability is in question following the founder's passing. The stock has fallen from a historical high of nearly HK$10 to HK$2.1, with the market voting with its feet. Goldman Sachs forecasts (estimate) a return to profitability in 2026, but after SenseTime's many past "false alarms," the market is no longer inclined to believe it. A market cap of HK$70 billion against revenue of 5 billion yuan implies a P/S of roughly 11x, which looks inexpensive, but given ongoing dilution and profitability uncertainty, this valuation is not actually cheap. Unless two to three consecutive quarters of genuine profitability materialize, a large position is not advisable.
