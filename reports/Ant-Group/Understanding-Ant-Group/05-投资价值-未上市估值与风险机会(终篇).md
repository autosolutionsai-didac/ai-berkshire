Put together, Ant's valuation question is essentially a multiple-choice problem: **which profit figure do you believe?**

If you believe the RMB 38.3 billion from 2024 — the current P/E of 18-20x isn't expensive.
If you believe the RMB 15.3 billion from 2025 — the current P/E of 46-49x is absurdly expensive.
If you believe "normalized profit" sits between RMB 35-42 billion — the current valuation is reasonable-to-slightly-high.

## What is "normalized profit," really?

The 60% profit collapse in 2025 has one clear cause: **R&D spending jumped from RMB 23.5 billion to RMB 28.98 billion, an increase of about RMB 5.5 billion**. Ant Group's official statement was that "record-high R&D investment caused full-year profit to decline year-over-year."

Reversing out this factor:

| Basis | Profit | Logic |
|------|------|------|
| 2025 actual net profit | ~RMB 15.3 billion | Includes RMB 28.98 billion of R&D spending |
| If R&D spending had stayed at the 2024 level (RMB 23.5 billion) | ~RMB 20.8 billion (est.) | Adding back the extra RMB 5.5 billion spent |
| If all "strategic acceleration" spending is excluded | ~RMB 25-28 billion (est.) | Closer to "steady-state earning power" |
| 2024 recurring profit (excluding investment gains) | ~RMB 18-20 billion | The most conservative baseline |

**Neutral judgment**: Ant's "normalized recurring profit" sits in the **RMB 20-28 billion** range. The RMB 38.3 billion in 2024 contains a large amount of investment gains and fair-value changes that aren't sustainable; the RMB 15.3 billion in 2025 contains a large amount of strategic spending that isn't the norm either.

If we take **RMB 25 billion** as the "normalized profit" baseline, the current valuation of RMB 700-750 billion implies a **28-30x normalized P/E** — for a company growing at 7-8%, that's **on the expensive side**.

## What will Ant be worth in 5 years? Three scenarios

### Scenario one: optimistic (probability 20%)

**Assumptions**: Ant International successfully IPOs in Hong Kong in 2027, Alipay+ becomes the Asia-Pacific standard, AI monetization kicks in, and regulation stays loose.

| Year | Net profit | Driver |
|------|--------|------|
| 2026 | RMB 43 billion | AI spending converges + internationalization accelerates |
| 2028 | RMB 58 billion | AI monetization + rising international margins |
| 2031 | **RMB 78 billion** | 5-year CAGR ~15% |

**2031 valuation**: RMB 78 billion × 24x P/E = **RMB 1.87 trillion** (~$260 billion). vs. current valuation, +150%.

### Scenario two: neutral (probability 50%)

**Assumptions**: Domestic payments stay flat-to-slightly-down, credit is stable, international revenue grows at a 20% CAGR with slowly rising margins, AI stops losing money but monetization is limited, and regulation stays steady.

| Year | Net profit | Driver |
|------|--------|------|
| 2026 | RMB 40 billion | AI spending continues to weigh on profit |
| 2028 | RMB 46 billion | International steadily contributes |
| 2031 | **RMB 55 billion** | 5-year CAGR ~7.5% |

**2031 valuation**: RMB 55 billion × 20x P/E = **RMB 1.10 trillion** (~$150 billion). vs. current valuation, +47%.

### Scenario three: pessimistic (probability 25%) + black swan (probability 5%)

**Pessimistic assumptions**: Digital yuan erodes 10-15% of the core payments business, the consumer-lending rate cap falls to 20%, AI investment yields no return, and overseas expansion hits geopolitical barriers.

| Year | Pessimistic net profit | Black-swan net profit |
|------|---------|-----------|
| 2026 | RMB 35 billion | RMB 30 billion |
| 2028 | RMB 34 billion | RMB 20 billion |
| 2031 | **RMB 35 billion** | **RMB 15 billion** |

**Pessimistic 2031 valuation**: RMB 35 billion × 10x = **RMB 350 billion** (vs. current, -53%)
**Black-swan 2031 valuation**: RMB 15 billion × 6x = **RMB 90 billion** (vs. current, -88%)

### Probability-weighted outcome

| Metric | Calculation |
|------|------|
| **Probability-weighted 2031 valuation** | 0.20 × 1,870 + 0.50 × 1,100 + 0.25 × 350 + 0.05 × 90 = **~RMB 1.03 trillion** |
| **vs. current valuation** | +37% (over 5 years) |
| **Implied annualized IRR** | **~6.5%** |

**A 6.5% annualized expected return** — beats government bonds but falls short of the CSI 300's long-term average (~8%), and comes with a 30% probability of downside.

| **China Merchants Bank** | ~RMB 1.5 trillion | ~RMB 150 billion | 10x | Comprehensive financial comp (China's version) |

Ant's business mix sits somewhere between PayPal and Visa — it has payment-network characteristics, but also heavier businesses like credit and wealth management. A reasonable P/E range is **16-20x** (neutral), which, applied to "normalized profit" of RMB 25 billion, would put a reasonable valuation at roughly **RMB 400-500 billion**.

**The current RMB 700-750 billion valuation already bakes in an "Ant International growth premium" and an "IPO expectation premium."** If either of those expectations fails to materialize, there's substantial downside room in the valuation.

## Three identities, three perspectives

### Identity one: Alibaba shareholder

You **already hold a 33% option on Ant for free**.

Alibaba owns 33% of Ant; at the neutral 2031 valuation of RMB 1.1 trillion × 33% = **RMB 363 billion (~$50 billion)**, that's roughly 15-20% of Alibaba's current market cap.

**Conclusion**: There's no need to make a separate bet on Ant. Holding Alibaba already comes with Ant's upside built in for free. And Alibaba itself has buybacks and dividends, with good liquidity — much better than holding Ant directly (unlisted, no liquidity).

### Identity two: Ant employee (SER/RSU holder)

SER (share-based incentive certificates) currently trade at **RMB 108.21** per unit (after a 1-for-4 split in March 2025).

| Scenario | 2031 valuation | Implied SER price | vs. current | Annualized |
|------|----------|-------------|--------|------|
| Optimistic | RMB 1.87 trillion | ~RMB 300 | +177% | 10.8% |
| Neutral | RMB 1.10 trillion | ~RMB 187 | +73% | 5.7% |
| Pessimistic | RMB 350 billion | ~RMB 60 | -44% | -11% |

**Conclusion**: Under the neutral scenario, SER rises 73% over 5 years (5.7% annualized) — **far below the "doubling" hope some hold**. And there are things to watch out for:

- Ant is an unlisted company, so SER liquidity is extremely poor
- Departing employees are subject to a forced cash buyback, which may strip them of future gains from subsidiaries
- The most recent official action was a **markdown** (SER cut from RMB 115.85 to RMB 108.21 in March 2025)
- The dividend resolution passed at the April 2026 shareholders' meeting is good news, but it doesn't equal "an upward revision of option value"

**Recommendation**: Don't treat SER as "savings" — it's fundamentally a risk asset with very poor liquidity. If SER makes up more than 30% of your total assets, it's worth diversifying when the opportunity arises.

### Identity three: prospective investor (via private secondary markets)

Platforms like UpMarket offer private secondary-market trading in Ant, at a valuation of roughly **$103 billion** (~RMB 740 billion).

**The problems**:
1. Extremely poor liquidity — easy to buy, hard to sell
2. Severe information asymmetry — Ant doesn't disclose full financial statements
3. High trading thresholds — usually requires accredited-investor qualification
4. Unclear exit path — no fixed timetable for a full group listing

**If you require a 10% annualized return + a 20% margin of safety**:
2031 neutral valuation RMB 1.1 trillion / (1.10^5) / 0.80 = **~RMB 550 billion**

That is, the current valuation would need to be **below RMB 550 billion** to meet a safe standard. The current RMB 700-750 billion **does not** meet it.

## Final judgment

**Ant Group is a giant transforming from "a Chinese payments company" into an "Asia-Pacific fintech + AI platform."** Its fundamentals (900 million MAU + duopoly position + international growth + AI first-mover advantage) are better than most people think.

**But now is not a good entry point.** Three reasons:

1. **The valuation isn't cheap** — RMB 700-750 billion against a normalized profit of RMB 25 billion is a 28-30x P/E, implying an annualized return of just 6.5%, which isn't enough to compensate for the uncertainty
2. **Information is extremely opaque** — as an unlisted company, investors sit at a severe information disadvantage
3. **The exit path is unclear** — the financial holding license hasn't been approved, the group's listing has no timetable, and SER liquidity is poor

**If you already hold Ant indirectly through Alibaba — no additional action is needed.** Ant is Alibaba's "free option."

**If you're an Ant employee — manage your expectations.** The neutral scenario has SER rising 73% over 5 years (5.7% annualized); don't bet on "doubling."

**If you're a prospective investor — wait for a better price.** Consider getting in once Ant's valuation falls below **RMB 500-550 billion** (a normalized P/E of 20-22x), or after the financial holding license is approved and Ant International successfully IPOs.

As the old saying goes: **A good company isn't the same as a good investment — price and timing matter just as much.**

## The one-sentence core conclusion of this series

> **Ant Group is a fintech giant that has been through a "regulatory rebirth." Its fundamentals are better than the market perceives, but at the current RMB 700-750 billion valuation, the implied return (~6.5% annualized) isn't enough to compensate for the risks of information opacity, an unclear exit, and disruption to the AI gateway. Worth tracking long-term, but not worth a heavy bet right now.**
