## Ant is fighting three wars at once

In the first three parts, we broke down "what Ant is" — its business structure, profit sources, and the arc of its regulatory overhaul. Starting with this part, we turn to a harder question: **where is Ant headed, and can it actually get there?**

A single company is fighting on three fronts at once, and the outcome of each will fundamentally reshape its value. Let's lay out the battlefield first:

| Front | Opponent | Ant's posture | What decides the outcome |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| Domestic payments | WeChat Pay | **Defense** | Can it hold 30%+ share |
| Overseas expansion | Visa / PayPal / Stripe / local wallets | **Offense** | Can it take the #1 spot in Asia-Pacific |
| AI pivot | ByteDance / Alibaba Cloud / OpenAI+Stripe | **Land grab** | Can it keep the "user gateway" from being bypassed by AI agents |

The outcome of each front will fundamentally reshape Ant's value. Let's take them one at a time.

---

## Front One: Alipay vs. WeChat Pay — permanent runner-up?

### A war that has already run for 10 years

| Year | Alipay share | WeChat Pay share | Key turning point |
|------|-----------|------------|---------|
| 2013 | >90% | Near 0 | WeChat Pay launches |
| 2014 | ~80% | ~10% | WeChat red envelopes — the Lunar New Year campaign that changed the game |
| 2017 | ~54% | ~40% | WeChat mini-program ecosystem takes shape |
| 2020 | ~55% | ~39% | Ant's IPO is halted |
| 2024 | 45.7% (by value) | 41.8% | WeChat Pay's offline penetration accelerates |
| 2025 Q1 | **36.2% (by transaction count)** | **59.7%** | WeChat Pay tops 60% of transaction count for the first time |

Where does Alipay lose? In one sentence: **WeChat has social; Alipay doesn't.**

WeChat has **1.47 billion** monthly active users, the highest-penetration app in China. Users open WeChat every day to chat, browse Moments, and use mini-programs — paying is just "something you do along the way." Alipay, by contrast, requires you to "open it specifically" — and in the mobile-internet era, that extra step is high friction.

Even more damaging is the offline scenario: WeChat Pay's penetration in dining and retail reaches **63.1%**, with daily usage frequency **1.5 times** that of Alipay. Walk into a small shop for a bottle of water or a bowl of noodles, and the owner says "scan WeChat" — once that muscle memory forms, it is extremely hard to change.

(Data sources: QuestMobile 2025 Q1; [Zhihu/Sina Finance](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1927658499732673892))

### Alipay's counterattack: tap-to-pay + becoming a content platform

Alipay is not sitting still. There are two main counterattack directions:

**Direction one: tap-to-pay (NFC payment).** Tapping a phone against a merchant's terminal completes payment 0.5-1 seconds faster than scanning a QR code. In January 2026, daily transaction volume passed **100 million**, with **200 million** users, covering more than **2,260 everyday scenarios**.

The logic of tap-to-pay is: **build an edge at the experience level** — in the QR-code era, WeChat won on "gateway frequency"; in the tap-to-pay era, Alipay wants to win on "payment speed." But how long this edge lasts depends on whether WeChat follows with NFC. If WeChat also adopts tap-to-pay, its gateway-frequency advantage will once again overwhelm Alipay's speed advantage.

**Direction two: becoming a content and lifestyle-services platform.** The Alipay app has added more lifestyle services (food delivery, ride-hailing, local services) in an attempt to increase how often users open it. But compared with WeChat's and Douyin's content ecosystems, Alipay inherently lacks the "scroll for fun" quality.

### Endgame judgment: duopoly coexistence, but the gap may keep widening

| Scenario | Probability | Alipay share by 2030 | Logic |
|------|------|-----------------|------|
| Optimistic | 25% | 35-37% | Tap-to-pay goes mainstream + content strategy succeeds in driving traffic |
| Base case | 50% | 32-34% | Status quo persists, share drifts down slowly |
| Pessimistic | 25% | 29-31% | WeChat adopts NFC + Douyin Pay rises + digital yuan siphons off volume |

**Core conclusion**: Alipay is very likely to remain the "permanent runner-up" in payments. But "runner-up" doesn't mean "worthless" — the duopoly's combined share (CR2) exceeds **94%**, and new entrants can barely make a dent. Alipay's 30%+ share still represents a traffic gateway of **900 million monthly active users**, the foundation of every other Ant business.

**The real danger isn't WeChat Pay — it's that AI agents may make the very act of "opening an app to pay" redundant.** That's the subject of Front Three.

## Front Two: overseas expansion — Ant International's dream of becoming "Asia-Pacific's Visa"

### Why might Ant International be the single most important piece on Ant's board?

The answer is simple: **the domestic ceiling has been reached, but there is still 10x the room overseas.**

China's mobile-payment penetration has already exceeded **85%**, and third-party payment transaction volume was around **RMB 560 trillion** in 2024 — with limited room for further growth. Meanwhile the global cross-border payments market is projected to reach **$25 trillion** by 2030, and digital-payment penetration in many emerging markets remains below 30%.

Ant International's strategic position is not "bring Alipay overseas" — it's **building the digital-payments "railway network" of the Asia-Pacific region**.

### Regional attack-and-defense posture

| Region | Opportunity rating | Ant's competitiveness | Key positioning |
|------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Southeast Asia** | S-tier (core stronghold) | Very strong | GCash (Philippines), Dana (Indonesia), TrueMoney (Thailand) |
| **Northeast Asia** | A-tier | Strong | Kakao Pay (South Korea) partnership, Japan travel payments |
| **Middle East** | A- tier | Moderate | Saudi central bank already partnering with Alipay+ on cross-border QR (launching 2026) |
| **Latin America** | B-tier | Weak | Mercado Pago's dominance is very strong |
| **Europe** | B-tier | Weak | Mainly serves cross-border scenarios |
| **North America** | C-tier | Very weak | Geopolitical barriers |

Ant's playbook in Southeast Asia is worth noting — rather than pushing Alipay directly, it **invests in or acquires local wallets and then strings them together with the Alipay+ protocol**. It's much like how Visa doesn't run retail banks; it only runs the clearing network between banks.

### Ant International vs. global giants

| Metric | Ant International | PayPal | Stripe | Visa |
|------|---------|--------|--------|------|
| 2025 revenue | ~$3.7 billion | ~$32 billion | ~$26 billion | ~$37 billion |
| Growth rate | **25-30%** | 8% | 25% | 10% |
| Net margin | ~8% (estimate) | 13% | ~15% (estimate) | **55%** |
| Asia-Pacific share | Top 3 | Moderate | Weak | Strong |
| Model | Network + wallet + B2B | Wallet + acquiring | Acquiring + infrastructure | Pure network (toll collector) |

**Ant International's current net margin is only ~8% — far below Visa's 55%.** That means it is still in the "laying track" phase, earning hard-won money. Whether it can eventually achieve a Visa-style high-margin "toll collector" model depends on whether the Alipay+ network can become the "standard" for the Asia-Pacific region.

### IPO and valuation

Ant International plans to list separately in Hong Kong and has already engaged CICC and Morgan Stanley. But as of May 2026, **it still has not filed**.

Valuation estimates vary widely:

| Source | Valuation range |
|------|---------|
| Bloomberg Intelligence | $8-24 billion |
| Some analysts | RMB 200 billion ($27.8 billion) |
| Conservative estimate (15x P/E) | $11.5-14.5 billion |
| Optimistic estimate (35x P/E) | $27-34 billion |

**Bear case**: Geopolitical risk is Ant International's biggest uncertainty. If US-China relations deteriorate, a TikTok-style ban risk could emerge. Moreover, local digital wallets in various countries (Indonesia's GoPay, Vietnam's MoMo, Brazil's Pix, etc.) hold a natural home-turf advantage, and Ant, as "Chinese capital," may run into policy barriers in sensitive markets.

(Data sources: [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-05-05/exclusive-ant-group-in-talks-for-international-unit-ipo-in-hong-kong-102316506.html); [Caproasia](https://www.caproasia.com/2025/05/20/alibaba-fintech-ant-group-plans-hong-kong-ipo-for-ant-international-with-reports-of-valuation-between-8-billion-to-24-billion-ant-group-had-removed-controlling-shareholders-in-2024-including-jack-m/))

## Front Three: the AI pivot — Ant's biggest opportunity and its biggest threat are the same thing

### Is AI a "friend" or an "enemy" to Alipay?

The answer: **a friend in the short term, potentially an enemy in the long term.**

Start with the "friend" side — Ant's progress in AI has exceeded expectations:

| Product | Data | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| Alipay AI Pay | Users past **100 million**, **120 million** transactions in a single week | The first AI-native payment product globally to pass 100 million on both metrics |
| Ant AFu | Users past **100 million**, 30 million monthly active | An AI health assistant, with 52% of new users from tier-3-and-below cities |
| Bailing (Ling) large-model family | **18 models** fully open-sourced | Includes Ring-1T, the world's first open-source trillion-parameter reasoning model |
| ACT Protocol | China's first agent commerce trust protocol | The Qwen app already connects Taobao + Alipay |
| Linggguang (LingGuang) | Downloads passed 1 million within 4 days of launch | Positioned as "the Alipay of the AGI era" |

Ant's speed of response in AI is genuinely striking — the figure of 120 million AI Pay transactions per week suggests it is actively leading the shift rather than passively defending against it.

(Data sources: [Science and Technology Daily, 2026-02-24](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-02/24/content_476005.html); [Xinhua](http://www.news.cn/tech/20251106/992549d2c775465cb8908eb8bff545c7/c.html))

### But the "enemy" side of AI deserves more caution

**Core threat: AI agents could bypass Alipay entirely.**

Picture the scenario: three years from now, you tell your phone's AI assistant, "book me a flight to Shanghai for tomorrow." The AI agent automatically searches flights, compares prices, places the order, and pays — with no need for you to ever open Alipay. The AI agent calls your bank card directly to complete payment, and Alipay is demoted from "user gateway" to "backstage pipe."

This is not science fiction. It is already happening globally:

| Protocol/product | Initiator | Core function |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| ACP (Agentic Commerce Protocol) | OpenAI + Stripe | Purchase and payment directly inside ChatGPT |
| Trusted Agent Protocol | Visa + 10+ partners | A secure framework for AI-agent transactions |
| Agent Pay | Mastercard | Agent-based payments with a verifiable intent layer |
| AP2 | Google | Agent Payments Protocol |
| **ACT Protocol** | **Alipay + Qwen + Taobao** | **China's first agent commerce trust protocol** |

Stripe released ACP in late 2025, and ChatGPT Instant Checkout has already launched — users complete purchases directly inside ChatGPT, with no need to open any payment app at all.

**Ant's defense**: the ACT Protocol + AI Pay + tap-to-pay + Ant AFu. The core idea is to **proactively become the "financial operating system" of the AI-agent era**, rather than passively waiting to be bypassed.

### AI threat assessment

| Threat dimension | Severity | Time window |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Gateway migration (app → agent) | **High** | 3-5 years |
| AI agents bypassing payments | Medium-high | 2-4 years |
| Disintermediation of robo-advisory | Medium | 3-5 years |
| Democratization of risk-control capability | Medium-low | 5+ years |

### Three possible endgames for Ant in AI

| Endgame | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| **Best case** | 25% | Ant becomes "the financial operating system of the agent era," the ACT Protocol becomes the standard, and every AI agent completes financial operations through Alipay |
| **Base case** | 50% | Ant retains its dominant position in the domestic payments market, but margins are squeezed by AI competition; AI Pay and AFu serve as effective defenses |
| **Worst case** | 25% | Eroded by AI-native competitors; Alipay is reduced to a "backend pipe," with fees compressed and stickiness lost |

**Key judgment**: what decides the outcome is not AI technology itself (Ant has already proven it can keep pace), but **the speed at which user interaction paradigms shift**. If most users are still "opening the app to scan/tap" three years from now, Ant is safe. If within five years "just talking to AI" can accomplish everything, Alipay's gateway position is in danger.

## The fourth front that can't go unmentioned: the digital yuan

Digital yuan (e-CNY) 2.0 officially launched on January 1, 2026, with cumulative transactions exceeding **RMB 7 trillion** (as of end-2024; 2026 figures will be higher), more than **180 million** personal wallets, pilots covering 17 provinces and 26 regions, and cross-border reach now extended to 50 countries.

### Will the digital yuan "replace" Alipay?

**No, but it will eat into profit margins.** The core distinction:

| Dimension | Digital yuan | Alipay |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Nature | **Money** itself (legal tender, M0) | A **wallet** + services platform |
| Issuer | The central bank | A commercial company |
| What it can do | Payments | Payments + wealth management + credit + insurance + lifestyle services |

The digital yuan solves only "payment," whereas Alipay provides a full financial-services ecosystem. To use an analogy: **the digital yuan is "new-style cash," while Alipay is "wallet + bank + department store"** — they don't compete on the same plane.

Moreover, Ant has already embraced it proactively: MYbank has become a digital-yuan operating institution, and the Alipay app allows users to open a digital-yuan sub-wallet. Ant has shifted from "competitor" to "distribution channel."

**But the long-term worry is this**: the digital yuan is accelerating the "utility-ization" of payments, further compressing the room for Alipay to charge fees. If users can eventually pay with the digital yuan at "zero fees," Alipay's "toll" will become even harder to collect.

**Bear case**: rollout of the digital yuan faces insufficient user incentives, a large experience gap, and privacy concerns. The central bank has repeatedly stressed that "this is not a replacement relationship." Among the 180 million personal wallets, the active proportion is very low. The actual near-term threat is limited.

## Overall assessment: odds on the three fronts

| Front | Ant's odds | Key variable |
|------|---------|---------|
| Domestic payments vs. WeChat | Hold 30%+ share (70% probability) | Tap-to-pay penetration, whether WeChat adopts NFC |
| Overseas expansion | Top 1-2 in Asia-Pacific (55% probability) | Geopolitics, IPO progress, density of the Alipay+ network |
| AI pivot | Hold the gateway (50% probability) | Speed of user-interaction-paradigm shift, whether the ACT Protocol becomes the standard |
| Digital yuan | Coexistence (80% probability) | Central bank's push, zero-fee policy |

**Of the three fronts, overseas expansion has the highest certainty (already RMB 27 billion in revenue as a base), while AI carries the greatest uncertainty (the attack-defense balance could flip within 2-3 years).**

Ant's fate ultimately hinges on one question: **can it build a new castle in the "new world" (AI + overseas) before the "old world" (the payments app) collapses?**
