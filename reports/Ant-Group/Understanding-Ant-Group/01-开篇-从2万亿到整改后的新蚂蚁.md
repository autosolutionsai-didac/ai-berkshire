## A set of numbers that will make you rethink Ant

In 2020, Ant Group came within 11 days of what would have been the largest IPO in history. At an offer price of RMB 68.8 per share, the deal implied a valuation of **RMB 2.1 trillion (roughly US$315 billion)**; dual listing hearings on both the STAR Market and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange had already been cleared, and the planned fundraising size exceeded Saudi Aramco's — global financial markets were holding their breath.

Then everything stopped cold. On October 24, Jack Ma delivered his now-famous 21-minute speech at the Bund Finance Summit in Shanghai, criticizing the Basel Accords as an "old people's club" and calling banks' mindset a "pawnshop mentality." Two weeks later, on November 3, 2020, the Shanghai Stock Exchange and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange simultaneously announced the suspension of the listing. That money was never raised.

After the IPO was halted, Ant entered three years of regulatory rectification.

Five years on, here are the key figures side by side:

| Metric | 2020 (pre-IPO) | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|------|-------------------|---------|---------|------|
| Valuation | **RMB 2.1 trillion** | RMB 635 billion (Hurun) | RMB 700-750 billion (est.) | **-65%** |
| Net profit | RMB 31.2 billion | **RMB 38.3 billion** (+61%) | **~RMB 15.3 billion** (-60%) | Sharp swings |
| R&D spending | ~RMB 10 billion (est.) | RMB 23.5 billion | **RMB 28.98 billion (record high)** | +190% |
| Alipay MAU | ~700 million | 890 million | ~900 million+ | +30% |
| Alipay market share | ~55% | 45.7% (by value) | **36.2% (by transaction count, Q1)** | Continuing to decline |
| Ant International revenue | Almost zero | **RMB 27 billion ($3 billion)** | ~RMB 33 billion ($3.7 billion, est.) | From nothing to something |
| Jack Ma's voting power | **53.46%** | **6.2%** | 6.2% | Change of controlling party |

These numbers reveal a counterintuitive fact: **Ant's valuation has shrunk 65%, yet its user base is still growing, its international business has gone from zero to RMB 27 billion in revenue, and its R&D spending has nearly tripled.**

The 60% plunge in 2025 profit has left the market even more puzzled — Ant is pouring **RMB 28.98 billion** into AI and new businesses. Is this "cutting off an arm to survive," or a bottomless pit?

(Data sources: Ant Group's 2020 IPO prospectus; Alibaba's FY2025-2026 financial reports, profit inferred via the equity method; Hurun Global Unicorn List 2025; [Beijing Business Today, 2026-05-13](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2026/0513/593232.shtml))

## What kind of company is Ant, really? Three layers

Ask most people to define Ant Group in one sentence, and they'll say "Alipay's parent company." That's not wrong, but it's seriously incomplete.

A more accurate definition is:

> **Ant Group = a super-app traffic gateway with 900 million monthly active users (Alipay) + a data-driven fintech monetization machine (credit + wealth management + insurance) + a rapidly growing international financial infrastructure business (Ant International) + an AI bet worth RMB 29 billion whose outcome is still unclear.**

Breaking it down layer by layer:

### Layer one: the traffic gateway — Alipay

Alipay is Ant's "front door." **890 million monthly active users** open it every day to scan codes, transfer money, pay bills, and shop. This traffic isn't Ant's profit center — payment fees have already been cut to a range of **0.2%-0.4%**, with cumulative fee reductions over the past three years exceeding **RMB 21 billion**.

Alipay's real value lies in channeling 900 million users into Ant's financial and service ecosystem. Think of it this way: Alipay is the "mall entrance" that lets you in for free, while Ant makes its money from the "stores inside the mall."

**But this front door is losing foot traffic to WeChat Pay.** In Q1 2025, WeChat Pay's share by transaction count reached **59.7%**, while Alipay's fell to **36.2%**. WeChat Pay's monthly active users surpassed **1.47 billion** — 1.6x Alipay's. In offline, low-value, high-frequency scenarios (dining, retail), WeChat Pay's penetration rate is **63.1%**, and its average daily usage frequency is **1.5x** that of Alipay.

Counterargument: the decline in share is overstated. Measured by transaction value (rather than count), Alipay still holds the edge in large-ticket payment scenarios, with average monthly payment value per user 2.3x that of WeChat Pay. The "Tap to Pay" NFC feature has already surpassed **200 million** users, with daily transaction volume topping **100 million** in January 2026. Alipay remains irreplaceable in "low-frequency, high-value" transactions and financial services.

### Layer two: the monetization machine — digital finance

This is where Ant actually makes its money.

| Segment | 2024 revenue | What it does | Profit contribution |
|------|-----------|--------|---------|
| Ant Consumer Finance (Huabei/Jiebei) | **RMB 15.2 billion** | Consumer credit (~RMB 2.1 trillion in loans outstanding, including joint loans) | Net profit RMB 3.05 billion |
| MYbank | **RMB 21.3 billion** | Small and micro-enterprise lending | Net profit RMB 3.17 billion |
| Ant Fund | **RMB 16.3 billion** | Fund distribution (RMB 1.57 trillion in non-money-market assets under distribution, #1 in the industry) | Net profit ~RMB 1 billion |
| Ant Insurance | ~RMB 7 billion (est.) | Internet insurance brokerage (#1 in the industry) | Net profit ~RMB 240 million |

**Credit technology is Ant's biggest profit engine** — Ant Consumer Finance and MYbank together posted combined net profit of over **RMB 6.2 billion** in 2024, more than 16% of the group's profit. But this figure significantly understates the real picture: Ant's own on-balance-sheet loans account for only about 15% of total loans outstanding, with 98% of the funding coming from partner banks, on which Ant collects a technology service fee of roughly **1%**. In other words, Ant doesn't bear the credit risk, yet still pockets a steady technology-fee cut.

Counterargument: this "asset-light" model is now being squeezed by regulation. New rules issued by eight government departments in April 2026 are reshaping how payments and credit are bundled, and Huabei's usage frequency could fall by **30-40%**. The interest-rate cap, under discussion for a reduction from 24% to 20%, is the biggest variable for the industry. Ant Consumer Finance's non-performing loan ratio has crept up for three straight years (1.69% → 1.74% → 1.78%).

(Data sources: Ant Consumer Finance/MYbank 2024 annual reports; Ant Fund 2025 H1 report; [Securities Times](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/3765475.html))

### Layer three: the growth engine — internationalization + AI

Ant International's 2024 revenue was **RMB 27 billion ($3 billion)**, about 20% of the group's revenue, growing to roughly **$3.7 billion** in 2025. Alipay+ now covers more than **100 countries and regions** and **90 million+ merchants**, connecting 40 international payment partners and 1.8 billion user accounts.

On the AI front, Ant's 2025 R&D spending hit a record **RMB 28.98 billion**. The AI health assistant "Ant Afu" surpassed **100 million** users during the 2026 Spring Festival period, while Alipay's "AI Fu" payment feature also crossed **100 million** users at the same time, with weekly transaction volume exceeding **120 million** — making it the world's first AI-native payment product to surpass 100 million in both users and transaction volume.

Counterargument: Ant International plans a Hong Kong IPO, but as of May 2026 it still had not filed with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Valuation estimates diverge sharply — Bloomberg Intelligence puts the range at **$8-24 billion**. AI spending is the main reason 2025 profit plunged 60%, and when this "cash-burning" will pay off remains unknown.

(Data sources: [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3310945/ant-groups-global-unit-pulls-us3-billion-revenue-ahead-spin); [Science and Technology Daily, 2026-02-24](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-02/24/content_476005.html))

## How is Ant different from Tencent and Pinduoduo?

If you've read "Understanding Tencent" and "Understanding Pinduoduo," you'll know that Ant, Tencent, and Pinduoduo are **three completely different species**:

| Dimension | Tencent | Pinduoduo | Ant Group |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| Core asset | WeChat + gaming | Algorithmic advertising machine | Alipay + fintech |
| Business model | Social monopoly → traffic monetization | Extreme efficiency → advertising + commissions | Payment gateway → financial service fees |
| Moat strength | Extremely strong (WeChat is irreplaceable) | Moderate (efficiency advantage) | Fairly strong (data network effects) but being eroded |
| Profit quality | Highly certain | Extremely efficient | Strong rebound potential but volatile |
| Listing status | Listed in Hong Kong | Listed in the US | **Unlisted** |
| Biggest risk | Regulatory tail risk | Temu politics + opaque management | **Financial holding license + AI-driven disruption of its gateway** |
| Suited to which investors | Steady, conservative investors | Arbitrage-minded investors | **Investors who need extreme patience and tolerance for limited information** |

The single biggest difference between Ant and these two is: **opacity**.

Tencent publishes detailed quarterly reports, and Pinduoduo is at least a listed company — but Ant, a giant valued at RMB 700 billion, gives investors only a single line item of "equity-method investment income" buried in Alibaba's financial reports, scattered subsidiary annual reports, and various media estimates.

**If you want to invest in a company you can't see inside, the first thing to figure out isn't "what is it worth" — it's "what does it actually look like."** That's exactly what the next four articles set out to do.

## A small preview: what does the 60% drop in 2025 profit really mean?

Here's a hook to leave you with. Ant's 2025 net profit fell from RMB 38.3 billion to roughly **RMB 15.3 billion** (inferred via the equity method from Alibaba's FY2026 annual report), a decline of **60%**. Over the same period, R&D spending jumped from RMB 23.5 billion to **RMB 28.98 billion**.

What does this combination of numbers mean? There are two completely different readings:

**The bull case**: Ant is proactively "sacrificing short-term profit for long-term room to grow" — pouring money into AI and internationalization, much like Amazon's two decades of losses that incubated AWS. Ant Afu crossing 100 million users and AI Fu crossing 100 million users are both signs that the investment is starting to pay off.

**The bear case**: Halved profit isn't "strategic investment" — it's "the core business deteriorating." Payment share keeps bleeding away, credit is being suppressed by regulation, and AI spending shows no return in sight. Of the RMB 28.98 billion in R&D spending, how much is "effective investment" and how much is "defensive cash-burning"?

Which is closer to the truth? In the next article, we'll take Ant's six business lines apart one by one.
