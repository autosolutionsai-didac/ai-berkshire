## Start with the big picture

Most people carry a deep-rooted misconception about Ant: **that Alipay is a company that makes money from payment fees.** On the surface this judgment seems reasonable, but it is completely wrong. Ant's real profit engine has never been payments — payments are the front counter; credit tech is the kitchen. Once you look at the table below, the structure becomes clear at a glance: **payments are Ant's thinnest-margin segment, credit tech is the true profit engine, wealth tech is the most certain growth pole, and internationalization is the biggest source of imagination over the next five years.**

| Business | 2024 Revenue | 2024 Net Profit (est.) | Growth Rate | Industry Position | Profit Role |
|------|-----------|---------------|------|---------|---------|
| **Payments (domestic)** | ~RMB 50 billion (est.) | Thin margin | 2-3% | #2 (36.2%) | Traffic gateway |
| **Credit tech** | RMB 36.5 billion (consumer finance RMB 15.2B + MYbank RMB 21.3B) | **RMB 6.2 billion** | 6-10% | #1 in consumer finance scale | **Profit engine** |
| **Wealth tech** | RMB 16.3 billion (Ant Fund) | ~RMB 1 billion | 18-20% | #1 industry-wide | Stable growth pole |
| **Insurance tech** | ~RMB 7 billion (Ant Insurance, est.) | ~RMB 240 million | 15-20% | #1 among internet insurance intermediaries | Incubation stage |
| **Internationalization** | RMB 27 billion ($3 billion) | ~RMB 2.2 billion | **25-30%** | Top 3 in Asia-Pacific | **Growth engine** |
| **AI & innovation** | RMB 2.5-4 billion (est.) | Loss-making | 40%+ | #1 in financial AI | Strategic bet |

**Total estimated revenue is roughly RMB 135-150 billion** (figures vary across sources depending on scope, with an error margin of ±15%). Of the **RMB 38.3 billion** in 2024 net profit, roughly RMB 20 billion came from non-recurring items such as investment gains and fair-value changes — meaning recurring profit was only about **RMB 18-20 billion**.

(Sources: annual reports of Ant Consumer Finance/MYbank/Ant Fund; equity-method estimates derived from Alibaba's financial filings; Ant Group ESG report; [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3310945/ant-groups-global-unit-pulls-us3-billion-revenue-ahead-spin))

## I. Payments: from king to a permanent runner-up

### Core judgment

**Alipay has settled from "former king" into "permanent runner-up" in domestic payments. The payments business itself is accelerating its shift toward "infrastructure-ification," and its profit margin keeps narrowing. Alipay's value to Ant lies not in making money, but in driving traffic.**

### Share shifts: a table that's uncomfortable reading for Alipay

| Year | Alipay Share | WeChat Pay Share | CR2 |
|------|-----------|------------|-----|
| 2015 | ~70%+ | ~20% | ~90% |
| 2020 | ~55% | ~39% | ~94% |
| 2024 (by value) | 45.7% | 41.8% | 87.5% |
| Q1 2025 (by transaction count) | **36.2%** | **59.7%** | **95.9%** |

Some clarification is needed: the gap between the "by value" and "by transaction count" figures is huge. WeChat Pay leads in high-frequency, small-ticket scenarios (scan-to-pay for breakfast), while Alipay still leads in large-ticket scenarios (online shopping, transfers). **On a per-user monthly payment-value basis, Alipay is 2.3 times WeChat Pay.** So "36.2% vs. 59.7%" doesn't equal "Alipay has lost" — but the trend is indeed worsening.

(Sources: [Zhihu / Sina Finance Toutiao](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/7879848900/1d5acf3c401902qb4u); QuestMobile Q1 2025)

### "Tap to Pay" — stopping the bleeding, or a comeback?

Since 2024, Alipay has been pushing "Tap to Pay" NFC payments: tap your phone against a merchant terminal to complete a transaction, faster than scanning a QR code. As of January 2026, "Tap to Pay" daily transaction volume has surpassed **100 million**, reaching **200 million** users, and covering over **2,260** everyday-life scenarios.

**The bull case**: Tap to Pay is Alipay's weapon for counterattacking offline, and its NFC experience is superior to QR scanning — it could win back some ground in offline scenarios.

**The bear case**: WeChat Pay can also build NFC support — as soon as WeChat follows suit, the social-entry-point advantage won't change. And 200 million users against Alipay's 900 million MAU is a penetration rate of just 22%, still far from enough to shift the competitive landscape.

### Payment take rates: increasingly like a public utility

The payment take rate has continuously declined from 0.6% to a range of **0.2-0.4%**. Over the past three years, Alipay's fee reductions have handed back more than **RMB 21 billion** to merchants and users. Regulators have explicitly pushed for "fee reduction and profit sharing," and payments are turning into infrastructure — the same category as electricity or tap water.

**Conclusion**: Payments revenue growth is expected to run at **2-3% a year** over the next five years, with razor-thin margins. Its value to Ant isn't "making money" — it's maintaining the "gateway status" that comes with 900 million MAU. Once that gateway gets bypassed by AI agents, Ant's entire ecosystem would be shaken. We'll cover that question in Part 04.

## II. Credit tech: Ant's most profitable business, and the one most constrained by regulation

### Core judgment

**Credit tech is Ant's true profit engine, but its proprietary lending scale has already hit the capital ceiling, and growth depends entirely on the "joint lending" model. Regulators are tightening how joint lending gets bundled with payments.**

### The two core subsidiaries

| Metric | Ant Consumer Finance (Huabei/Jiebei) | MYbank |
|------|---------------------|---------|
| Positioning | Consumer credit | Micro/small business lending |
| 2024 total assets | RMB 313.7 billion | ~RMB 470 billion |
| 2024 revenue | RMB 15.2 billion | RMB 21.3 billion |
| 2024 net profit | **RMB 3.05 billion** | **RMB 3.17 billion** |
| NPL ratio | 1.78% (rising for three straight years) | 2.30% |
| Users served | **400 million+** | 68 million |

**The key question: Ant Consumer Finance serves 400 million users with an outstanding loan balance of roughly RMB 2.1 trillion — yet only earns RMB 3 billion itself?**

The answer lies in the "joint lending model." Roughly **85%** of Ant's outstanding loan balance is funded by partner banks; Ant only contributes traffic and its risk-control model, collecting roughly a **1% technology service fee**. This means:

- The bulk of the profit goes to the partner banks
- But Ant also doesn't bear the credit risk (bad debt sits with the banks)
- What Ant earns is a "rain-or-shine" asset-light service fee

This model is clever — but it's now running into two hard constraints:

**Constraint one: the capital ceiling.** Ant Consumer Finance's registered capital is RMB 18.5 billion, with a leverage cap of 10x, putting its proprietary lending ceiling at roughly **RMB 380-530 billion**. Its proprietary assets already reached RMB 313.7 billion at the end of 2024, near the cap. Further growth requires a capital injection.

**Constraint two: tightening regulation.** New rules issued jointly by eight regulatory bodies in April 2026 adjusted the way payments and credit are bundled. Huabei's embedding within the payment flow is now restricted, and usage frequency could fall **30-40%**. The biggest swing factor is whether the interest-rate cap gets cut from a discussed 24% down to 20% — if that happens, Ant Consumer Finance's profit could be cut in half.

**The counterargument**: Ant Consumer Finance's technology-service-fee model may actually come out healthier after the rectification — low leverage, asset-light, with bad-debt risk transferred elsewhere. In 2025, Ant Consumer Finance's revenue grew to **RMB 21.6 billion**, and net profit of **RMB 3.1 billion** was essentially flat, which is evidence the model is resilient.

(Sources: Ant Consumer Finance's 2024-2025 annual reports; [Securities Times](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/3765475.html); MYbank's 2024 annual report)

### Comparison with WeBank

| Metric | Ant Consumer Finance | WeBank (Weilidai) |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| 2024 net profit | RMB 3.05 billion | **RMB 10.9 billion** |
| 2024 revenue | RMB 15.2 billion | RMB 38.1 billion |
| NPL ratio | 1.78% | 1.44% |

**Ant Consumer Finance has six times WeBank's user count, yet its net profit is only 28% of WeBank's.** The core reason is the difference in models: WeBank has a higher proportion of proprietary lending and a wider interest spread. But looked at another way, Ant doesn't bear credit risk, which may make it more resilient during an economic downturn.

## III. Wealth tech: Ant's most certain growth engine

### Core judgment

**Ant Fund is the undisputed dominant player in internet wealth management. In H1 2025, revenue was RMB 9.3 billion and net profit was RMB 430 million (up 361% year-over-year). Non-money-market AUM stands at RMB 1.57 trillion, more than 50% ahead of second-place China Merchants Bank.** This is the most certain growth pole across all of Ant's businesses.

### Competitive landscape: a runaway lead

| Platform | Non-money-market AUM (H1 2025) | Core user base |
|------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Ant Fund** | **RMB 1.57 trillion (#1)** | Predominantly Gen Z (58%) |
| China Merchants Bank | RMB 1.04 trillion (#2) | High-net-worth |
| Tiantian Fund | RMB 637.4 billion (#3) | Active traders |
| Tencent LiCaiTong | Undisclosed (#4-5) | Age 35+, high-net-worth |

Ant Fund's advantage comes from three "flywheels":
1. **User scale** — within Alipay's 900 million MAU, Yu'ebao alone converted hundreds of millions of wealth-management users
2. **Full category coverage** — from Yu'ebao (money-market funds) to equity funds, #1 across every category
3. **Gen Z stickiness** — 58% of users are young people, and once their wealth-management habits form, they're hard to shift away from

**Five-year forecast**: Under a neutral scenario, Ant Fund's revenue is projected to grow from RMB 20.2 billion (2025) to **RMB 38-42 billion** (2030), with non-money-market AUM growing from RMB 1.6 trillion to **RMB 3.5-4.0 trillion**.

**Main risk**: The third phase of the fund fee-reduction reform (effective January 1, 2026) cuts industry-wide distribution fees by **34%**. Yu'ebao's 7-day annualized yield has already fallen below **1%**, reverting from a "wealth-management tool" back to a "spare-change management tool," and its scale will shrink modestly.

**The counterargument**: Wealth tech's profit elasticity is enormous — profit can double in a bull market and turn to losses in a bear market. The 361% net-profit growth in H1 2025 was mainly driven by the A-share bull market, and it's not sustainable. On top of that, the fee-reduction reform is continuously squeezing commission income.

(Sources: [China Fund News](https://www.chnfund.com/article/AR260026e9-4fa6-ffec-006a-3a1be7d1fae5); Ant Fund's H1 2025 report)

## IV. Insurance tech: #1 in scale, profit model still unproven

### Core judgment

Ant Insurance ranks #1 among the top 100 insurance intermediaries with **RMB 6.7 billion** in revenue and more than **600 million** users, but its net margin is only **3.6%**. Internet insurance penetration is under **10%** (versus over 40% for fund distribution), leaving huge room for growth, but the new "same-filing, same-practice" rule is compressing commission rates.

Its flagship product "Haoyibao" — with 70 million users — is China's largest million-yuan critical-illness medical insurance product. The 2025 version has been upgraded to cover international and VIP departments as well as private hospitals.

**Five-year forecast**: Under a neutral scenario, Ant Insurance's revenue is projected to grow from ~RMB 7 billion to **RMB 14-17 billion** by 2030, with net profit rising from RMB 240 million to **RMB 1.2-1.8 billion**.

**Key variable**: The digestion period for the "same-filing, same-practice" rule is expected to run 2024-2026, after which it should actually benefit compliant leading platforms (like Ant Insurance), as smaller players get weeded out.

## V. Internationalization: the biggest growth engine, with an endgame of becoming "the Visa of Asia-Pacific"

### Core judgment

**Ant International is Ant Group's most certain growth engine over the next five years, and its biggest source of imagination. Its endgame is not "the global PayPal" — it's "the Visa of Asia-Pacific," i.e., the underlying interoperability infrastructure for digital payments across the Asia-Pacific region.**

### Business matrix

| Sub-business | What it does | Scale |
|--------|--------|------|
| **Alipay+** | Cross-border payment network | Covers **100+ countries, 90 million+ merchants**, 1.8 billion user accounts |
| **WorldFirst** | Cross-border e-commerce B2B collections and FX | 38,000+ merchants |
| **Antom** | Merchant-side acquiring/payment technology services | Serves merchants globally |
| **Local wallet portfolio** | GCash (Philippines), Dana (Indonesia), TrueMoney (Thailand), etc. | Southeast Asia penetration |

### Revenue growth trajectory

Ant International's 2024 revenue was **RMB 27 billion ($3 billion)**, with net profit of roughly **RMB 2.2 billion**. In 2025, revenue grew 20-25% to about **$3.7 billion**. The Saudi central bank has already partnered with Alipay+ on cross-border QR-code payments (launching in 2026).

**Global competitor comparison**:

| Dimension | Ant International | PayPal | Stripe | Visa |
|------|---------|--------|--------|------|
| Revenue | ~$3.7 billion | ~$31 billion | ~$25 billion+ | ~$36 billion |
| Growth rate | **30-40%** | 8-10% | 25-30% | 10-12% |
| Asia-Pacific competitiveness | **Very strong** | Medium | Weak-to-medium | Strong |
| Europe/US competitiveness | Weak | Very strong | Very strong | Very strong |

Ant International's advantage is concentrated in Asia-Pacific and emerging markets — which happen to be the fastest-growing regions for global digital payments. But in Europe and the US, Ant has almost no presence, and geopolitical constraints make a breakthrough there difficult.

### IPO progress

Starting in May 2025, multiple media outlets reported that Ant Group plans to list Ant International (registered in Singapore) separately in Hong Kong, and has already engaged CICC and Morgan Stanley. **As of May 2026, no filing has been made with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange**, and there is no confirmed timeline. Valuation estimates diverge widely: Bloomberg Intelligence gives a range of **$8-24 billion**.

**The counterargument**: Ant International's growth is impressive, but geopolitical risk cannot be ignored. Risks similar to the TikTok ban, competition from local digital wallets in each country, and fragmented cross-border regulation could all hinder expansion. And Ant International's 2024 net margin was only **~8%**, far below Visa's 55% — right now it's earning money the hard way, not yet running a "toll-collector" business.

(Sources: [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-05-05/exclusive-ant-group-in-talks-for-international-unit-ipo-in-hong-kong-102316506.html); [Caproasia](https://www.caproasia.com/2025/05/20/alibaba-fintech-ant-group-plans-hong-kong-ipo-for-ant-international-with-reports-of-valuation-between-8-billion-to-24-billion-ant-group-had-removed-controlling-shareholders-in-2024-including-jack-m/))

## VI. AI and innovation: where is the RMB 29 billion in R&D going?

### Core judgment

**Ant's overall AI scale is not in the first tier (it lags well behind ByteDance and Alibaba Cloud), but it leads in three vertical tracks: financial AI, health AI, and agent-based payments. The 60% profit collapse in 2025 was mainly driven by AI investment.**

### Seven AI sub-businesses

| Sub-business | Current status | Key data |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Ant AFU (AI health assistant)** | Users surpassed **100 million**, monthly active users 30 million | 52% of new users during the 2026 Spring Festival came from tier-three cities and below |
| **Alipay AI Pay** | Users surpassed **100 million**, **120 million** transactions in a single week | The world's first AI-native payment product to cross 100 million on both metrics |
| **Bailing model family** | **18 models**, all fully open-sourced | Includes Ring-1T, the world's first open-source trillion-parameter reasoning model |
| **Lingguang (general-purpose AI assistant)** | Downloads surpassed one million within 4 days of launch | Positioned as "the Alipay of the AGI era" |
| **Financial AI agents** | 100+ scenarios, serving hundreds of financial institutions | Risk-control effectiveness up 10%, advisory efficiency up 100% |
| **OceanBase** | 4,000+ customers, #1 distributed database domestically | Serves 100+ global fintech customers |
| **ACT protocol** | China's first agent-based commerce trust protocol | Qwen App already connects with Taobao + Alipay |

### The soul-searching question of AI investment vs. output

Ant's 2025 R&D spending was **RMB 28.98 billion**, more than **15%** of revenue. Compared with peers:

| Company | 2025 R&D spending (est.) | As % of revenue |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| ByteDance | ~RMB 160 billion | ~15% |
| Alibaba | ~RMB 60 billion | ~7% |
| Tencent | ~RMB 70 billion | ~10% |
| **Ant Group** | **RMB 28.98 billion** | **>15%** |

Ant's R&D spending is smaller in absolute terms than ByteDance's, but its **share of revenue** is the highest among the major internet giants. The question is: what will this RMB 29 billion buy back?

**Optimistic scenario**: The AI health assistant (Ant AFU) finds a monetization loop across "medical + pharmacy + insurance," and AI Pay becomes the payment standard of the agent era. AI-related revenue surpasses RMB 20 billion by 2031.

**Pessimistic scenario**: AI investment keeps losing money, user growth plateaus, and monetization remains far off. It drags down net profit by RMB 3-5 billion every year.

**Neutral forecast**: By 2028, AI starts monetizing across three scenarios — risk control, intelligent investment advisory, and health management. By 2031, AI-related revenue reaches roughly **RMB 20 billion**, contributing **RMB 3-5 billion** to net profit.

(Sources: [Beijing Business Today, 2026-05-13](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2026/0513/593232.shtml); [Science and Technology Daily, 2026-02-24](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-02/24/content_476005.html); Ant Group ESG report)

## Summary of the six businesses: what will Ant look like in 5 years?

Putting all the businesses together, Ant's business structure will change fundamentally five years from now:

```
2024 (current)                     2030 (5-year baseline forecast)
┌──────────────────────┐          ┌──────────────────────┐
│ Credit tech   ~40%   │          │ International ~30%   │ ← Biggest growth
│ Payments      ~25-28%│    →     │ Credit tech    ~25%  │
│ International ~20%   │          │ Wealth         ~17%  │ ← Certain growth
│ Wealth        ~12-15%│          │ Payments       ~12%  │ ← Declining share
│ Insurance/AI   ~5%   │          │ Insurance+AI  ~16%   │ ← Emerging force
└──────────────────────┘          └──────────────────────┘
```

**In one sentence**: Five years from now, Ant will no longer be "a Chinese payments company" but rather "an Asia-Pacific fintech infrastructure + AI health platform" company. Internationalization and AI are the two engines driving the transformation, wealth tech provides steady profit growth, and payments and credit provide the base cash-flow layer.

But whether this transformation can succeed depends on one precondition: **whether the regulator allows it**. In the next part, we'll cover the full story of Ant's rectification.
