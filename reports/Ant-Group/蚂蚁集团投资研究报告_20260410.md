## 4. Summary by dimension

### 4.1 Business model & moat (Duan Yongping's perspective)

- **Core business definition**: a digital fintech platform that uses payments as the entry point, data as fuel, and technology-as-a-service to financial institutions as the profit model. It earns three kinds of income: traffic distribution fees, technology service fees, and credit spreads
- **Credit technology is the true profit engine** (roughly 40% of revenue); payments is merely the entry point and data-collection layer
- **Moat ranking**: scale effects (4.5/5) > network effects (4/5) = brand (4/5) = technology barriers (4/5) > switching costs (3.5/5)
- **Insufficient pricing power (3/5)** — payment fee rates keep declining, wealth-management commissions are being cut, and credit rates are regulated; under Duan Yongping's framework this is the biggest deduction
- **The flywheel effect is intact but not monopolistic** — WeChat Pay has taken nearly 60% of the market, diluting the network effect
- **The "new three pillars" strategy**: payments (the base business) + finance (the profit center) + health/AI (the second curve; Ant AQ has 30 million monthly active users)

### 4.2 Financials & valuation (Buffett's perspective)

- **A V-shaped net profit rebound**: 73 billion (2021 peak) → 23.8 billion (2023 trough) → 38.3 billion (2024), still 47% below the peak
- **A healthier model after rectification**: low leverage, asset-light, revenue mainly from technology service fees, with bad-debt risk transferred to partner banks
- **Ample cash reserves**: in 2023, the company deployed 43.1 billion yuan of its own funds for buybacks, demonstrating financial strength
- **Ant International is a growth highlight**: annual revenue of $3 billion, up from under 1% of the total to roughly 20%, and already profitable
- **Valuation is reasonable but not a "bargain"**: the buyback price of 567.1 billion implies a PE of roughly 14.8x; the Hurun valuation of 635 billion implies a PE of roughly 16.6x
- **Key financial risks**: 37.5% of pre-tax profit must be shared with Alibaba, credit scale is hard-capped by capital requirements, and financial transparency is poor

### 4.3 Industry & competition (Munger's perspective)

- **The payments duopoly is extremely stable**: Ant at ~36% and WeChat at ~60%, CR2 > 90%; new entrants can barely make a dent
- **Ranking of the main competitive threats**:
  - WeChat Pay: extremely high (an extremely deep social moat, leading offline share)
  - ByteDance/Douyin Pay: high (the biggest new threat over the medium term of 3-5 years; its traffic advantage cannot be ignored)
  - Digitalization by banks such as China Merchants Bank: moderate and rising steadily
  - Digital yuan (e-CNY): low in the short term but uncertain in the long term
  - JD Technology / international players: moderate
- **The domestic market has entered a mature phase**, with growth rates down to single digits; the real growth engine lies in internationalization and AI
- **Regulatory rectification has actually created a first-mover compliance advantage** — smaller players have been weeded out

### 4.4 Risk & management (Li Lu's perspective)

- **Regulatory risk (medium-high)**: the harshest period has passed, but the financial holding company license is still under application — a key precondition for listing
- **Management (B+)**: Jing Xiandong performed creditably during the rectification period but is not an exceptional entrepreneur; new CEO Han Xinyi (a former CFO) has been in the role for only a year and remains untested
- **Jack Ma's influence**: he has nominally stepped down as the actual controller, but "implicit influence" persists — he appeared at Ant's 20th anniversary celebration in December 2024 and returned to the Alibaba campus in 2025
- **Governance concerns**: the "no actual controller" status, Alibaba's 37.5% profit share, and an opaque listing timetable
- **Ant Consumer Finance's NPL ratio has crept up for three consecutive years** (1.69% → 1.74% → 1.78%); watch closely during an economic downturn
- **Listing prospects**: a 60-70% probability of an Ant International Hong Kong listing (2026-2027), and a 30-40% probability of a group-wide listing (after 2027)

## 5. Investment thesis (Bull vs Bear)

### Bull case

1. **The duopoly structure is solid**: payments CR2 > 90%, almost impossible to disrupt — this is the core moat
2. **Ant International is growing rapidly**: annual revenue of $3 billion, covering 50+ countries, with a planned independent Hong Kong listing that could value it at $30-40 billion
3. **Profit has rebounded sharply**: 2024 net profit of 38.3 billion, up 61%; Ant Consumer Finance's net profit is up 19x — earnings power has been proven
4. **Valuation is at a historic low**: the current 635 billion valuation is only 30% of the 2020 IPO valuation, leaving substantial room for recovery
5. **AI positioning has scenario advantages**: the Bailing large model + Ant AQ + financial agents give it data barriers in vertical domains
6. **Rectification dividend**: first-mover compliance advantage, with smaller competitors cleared out — the industry structure has actually improved
7. **"Tap to Pay" NFC payments**: users have surpassed 100 million, counterattacking WeChat Pay's offline stronghold

### Bear case

1. **The financial holding company license remains unresolved**: the biggest obstacle to listing, with a completely opaque timetable
2. **Payments share keeps sliding**: down from ~50% to 36.2%, while WeChat Pay's 59.7% is a commanding lead
3. **Lack of pricing power**: payment fee rates are falling, credit rates are regulated, and wealth-management commissions are being cut — prices cannot be raised
4. **Alibaba's 37.5% profit share**: structural profit erosion, tantamount to a "permanent tax"
5. **A ceiling on credit scale**: Ant Consumer Finance's capital of 18.5 billion yuan caps the loans it can carry at roughly 380-530 billion, limiting growth
6. **The long-term threat of the digital yuan**: if fully rolled out, it could reshape the underlying payments architecture
7. **Unlisted plus opaque disclosure**: investors are at a severe informational disadvantage, with financial figures relying on estimates

## 8. Summary

Ant Group is a fintech giant that has been through a "regulatory rebirth." Its core moat — data network effects from a billion-scale user base and duopoly status in payments — remains solid, and 2024 net profit of 38.3 billion demonstrates the resilience of its earnings power. **Core recommendation: Ant is a quality name worth tracking for the long term, but it is not actionable at the current juncture. The best strategy is to wait for an Ant International Hong Kong listing or a group-wide listing, then enter the public market at a reasonable valuation of PE 12-15x. As Li Lu has said: "Better to miss an opportunity than to take a risk when information is insufficient."**

## Appendix A: A deep dive into the financial holding company license

### A.1 What is a financial holding company license?

The financial holding company license is an access license approved by the People's Bank of China under the Trial Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Financial Holding Companies (effective November 2020). The core requirement: **any non-financial enterprise that holds a controlling stake in, or exercises actual control over, two or more categories of financial institutions, and meets certain scale thresholds, must apply to establish a financial holding company.**

The financial holding company itself does not conduct financial business; it merely serves as a "shell" holding the various financial licenses, with the aims of:
- **Isolating risk**: preventing risk contagion across different financial businesses
- **Unifying supervision**: bringing financial businesses scattered across different regulatory regimes into a single field of view
- **Preventing regulatory arbitrage**: preventing a company from evading financial regulation by presenting itself as a technology company

Key thresholds: paid-in registered capital of no less than 5 billion yuan, and total assets of controlled financial institutions of at least 100 billion yuan.

### A.2 Why must Ant apply?

Ant Group substantively controls **at least five categories of financial business**, fully triggering the application requirement:

| Area | Entity | License type |
|------|------|---------|
| Payments | Alipay | Third-party payment |
| Consumer credit | Ant Consumer Finance (Huabei/Jiebei) | Consumer finance |
| Fund distribution | Ant Fund / Tianhong Fund | Fund distribution / public fund management |
| Insurance | Ant Insurance / Guotai Property & Casualty | Insurance agency / insurance |
| Banking | MYbank (30% stake) | Private bank |
| Credit reporting | Qiantang Credit | Personal credit reporting |

**The consequences of not applying**: unlawful operation (in violation of State Council administrative regulations), forced business breakup, and an IPO that could never be approved. The lack of a financial holding company license was one of the core reasons the 2020 IPO was halted.

### A.3 Application progress timeline

| Time | Event |
|------|------|
| November 2020 | The IPO is halted; regulators demand rectification |
| April 2021 | Four regulatory bodies summon Ant for a talk, explicitly requiring it to apply for a financial holding company license |
| June 2021 | Ant Consumer Finance is approved and begins operating (registered capital of 8 billion, later raised to 18.5 billion) |
| January 2023 | Jack Ma steps down as actual controller, moving to a "no actual controller" structure |
| July 2023 | A fine of 7.123 billion is imposed, rectification is officially declared "essentially complete," and Ant submits its financial holding company application |
| 2024-2025 | The financial holding company license remains in the central bank's approval process, with no official confirmation of approval |

### A.4 Comparison with already-approved cases

| Dimension | Approved (CITIC, China Merchants Group) | Ant Group |
|------|----------------------|---------|
| Nature of shareholders | State-owned / central enterprise | Private enterprise |
| History | Traditional financial conglomerate, operating as separate entities for many years | Internet platform expanding horizontally |
| Political sensitivity | Low | High (the Jack Ma Bund speech incident) |
| Approval difficulty | Relatively smooth | More cautious |

**If Ant is approved, it would be the largest case of a privately owned financial holding company**, carrying major demonstrative significance.

### A.5 Without a financial holding company license, how is Ant still operating?

**A financial holding company license is not the same as an operating license for each business** — these are two different levels. Each of Ant's subsidiaries has its own "individual license," and each operates legally under license:

| Business | Licensed entity | License type | Issuing authority |
|------|---------|---------|--------|
| Payments | Alipay (China) Network Technology Co., Ltd. | Third-party payment license | The People's Bank of China |
| Consumer credit | Ant Consumer Finance Co., Ltd. | Consumer finance license | The former CBIRC |
| Fund distribution | Ant Fund Sales Co., Ltd. | Fund distribution license | The China Securities Regulatory Commission |
| Banking | Zhejiang MYbank | Private bank license | The former CBIRC |
| Credit reporting | Qiantang Credit | Personal credit reporting license | The People's Bank of China |
| Insurance agency | Ant Insurance Agency | Insurance agency license | The former CBIRC |

The financial holding company license governs the "shell layer on top" — when a non-financial enterprise controls multiple financial institutions at once, a financial holding company must be established at the top level to unify management, isolate risk, and be subject to unified supervision. To put it another way: **every child has an ID card (an individual license), but the parents need a "household registration booklet" (the financial holding company license) to prove they are capable of managing the whole family properly.**

Reasons Ant can keep operating without the financial holding company license:
1. **Transition-period arrangements**: after the 2020 Measures were issued, existing enterprises were given a transition period to apply
2. **Regulatory tolerance**: Ant has already submitted its application and rectification is "essentially complete," and regulators have not demanded a halt to operations
3. **Each subsidiary is independently compliant**: each operates in compliance within its own regulatory framework

**What is truly blocked is not operations, but the listing** — "having applied but not yet been approved" is a major compliance flaw, and an IPO prospectus cannot give a clear account of this regulatory risk. So Ant's current state is: each business runs normally, but the group has not yet obtained its "legal ID card" at the top level, and so it cannot go public.

### A.6 The impact of approval vs. non-approval

| Scenario | Impact |
|------|------|
| **Approved** | Compliance risk removed → the listing precondition is met → room for valuation recovery opens up (though constrained by financial holding company capital requirements, lowering the growth ceiling) |
| **Not approved / shelved long term** | No hope of listing → no exit for shareholders → talent loss → strategic retrenchment → valuation continues to decline |

## Appendix B: The digital yuan and Alipay — "replacement" is a false premise

### B.1 The essential distinction: money vs. wallet

| Dimension | Digital yuan (e-CNY) | Alipay |
|------|---------------------|--------|
| **Nature** | **Money itself** (legal tender, M0) | **A wallet plus service platform** (a third-party payment channel) |
| **Issuer** | The People's Bank of China | Ant Group (a commercial company) |
| **Legal status** | Legal tender, cannot be refused | A commercial service; merchants may choose not to accept it |
| **Credit basis** | Sovereign national credit | Corporate commercial credit |
| **Interest-bearing** | No interest | Products such as Yu'ebao can bear interest |
| **Anonymity** | Controllable anonymity (small-value transactions can be anonymous) | Real-name system; transaction data is held by the platform |

### B.2 A two-tier operating architecture

```
The central bank (issuing layer)
    ↓ Issuance / redemption
Operating institutions (distribution layer): ICBC / ABC / BOC / CCB / China Merchants Bank / MYbank / WeBank...
    ↓ Exchange / circulation
User layer: individuals / enterprises
```

Both MYbank (part of the Ant ecosystem) and WeBank (part of the Tencent ecosystem) have become operating institutions, and digital yuan sub-wallets can be opened from within the Alipay app.

### B.3 The logic behind the digital yuan potentially threatening Alipay

1. **Disintermediation**: the digital yuan enables point-to-point value transfer, in theory without needing a third-party payment channel
2. **Offline payments**: dual-offline payment is something Alipay/WeChat cannot achieve (subways, basements, disaster scenarios)
3. **Programmability**: smart contracts enable conditional payments (targeted use of fiscal subsidies, regulated escrow of advance funds)
4. **Breaking the monopoly**: cross-platform interoperability, not confined to a single wallet
5. **National motivation**: reclaiming sovereignty over payment data, lowering social payment costs, and advancing the internationalization of the renminbi

### B.4 Reasons the digital yuan is unlikely to fully replace Alipay

1. **User habit stickiness**: 20 years of "scan-to-pay" muscle memory across a billion users is extremely difficult to change
2. **Alipay has actively embraced it**: by integrating digital yuan sub-wallets, it has shifted from "competitor" to "distribution channel"
3. **Value-added services are irreplaceable**: the digital yuan only solves "payment," while Alipay offers a full ecosystem of wealth management, credit, insurance, and lifestyle services

| Service | Alipay | Digital yuan |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Payments | Yes | Yes |
| Wealth management (Yu'ebao/funds) | Yes | No |
| Credit (Huabei/Jiebei) | Yes | No |
| Insurance | Yes | No |
| Lifestyle services | Yes | Limited |
| Credit system | Yes | No |

4. **The central bank has no intention of replacing Alipay**: the two-tier architecture itself shows that the central bank does not want to face end users directly, and Mu Changchun (former director of the Digital Currency Research Institute) has repeatedly stressed that "this is not a replacement relationship"
5. **Promotion challenges**: insufficient user incentives, a large gap in feature experience, and privacy concerns

### B.5 Latest rollout data (2024-2026)

- Pilot scope: 26 regions across 17 provinces and municipalities
- Cumulative transaction value: over 7 trillion yuan
- Number of personal wallets: over 180 million
- Version 2.0 upgrade: launch of smart contracts, hardware wallets, and mBridge cross-border pilots

### B.6 The most likely endgame: coexistence rather than replacement

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Application layer (Alipay / WeChat / bank apps) │
│  Wealth management / credit / insurance / lifestyle services / payment entry point │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Payment clearing layer               │
│  Digital yuan | bank deposits | bank cards │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Currency issuance layer (The People's Bank of China) │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Conclusion**: the digital yuan settles in as the underlying monetary infrastructure, while Alipay continues to provide services at the layer above. Alipay's "payment channel fees" and "data monopoly" will be weakened, but its role as a "comprehensive financial services platform" will not disappear. The central bank reclaims monetary and data sovereignty, while Alipay retains its user relationships and service ecosystem.

## Appendix C: The impact of the AI paradigm shift on Alipay

### C.1 Three stages of the AI paradigm shift

| Stage | Period | Characteristics | Impact on Alipay |
|------|------|------|-------------|
| Back-office tool | 2020-2023 | Risk control / anti-fraud / customer service | Positive (cost reduction, efficiency gains) |
| Front-end assistant | 2024-2025 | Intelligent dialogue / investment advisory recommendations | Neutral (enhances the experience) |
| **Autonomous agents** | **2025-2026+** | **AI completes entire transaction chains on the user's behalf** | **Could potentially disrupt Alipay's role as the entry point** |

The core change is the emergence of **agentic commerce**: in the super-app era, users walk into a platform's ecosystem; in the agent era, users dispatch an AI agent to execute tasks across platforms.

### C.2 The global race for agent payment protocols

| Protocol | Initiator | Core function |
|------|--------|----------|
| ACP | OpenAI + Stripe | Direct shopping payments within ChatGPT |
| Trusted Agent Protocol | Visa + 10+ partners | A secure framework for AI agent transactions |
| Agent Pay | Mastercard | Agent payments with a verifiable intent layer |
| AP2 | Google | An agent payment protocol |
| **ACT Protocol** | **Alipay + Qwen + Taobao** | **China's first agentic commerce trust protocol** |

### C.3 Four dimensions of the AI threat to Alipay

**1. Migration of the entry point (the most fundamental threat)**

> "In the super-app era, users open the door and walk into the ecosystem. In the agent era, users send an agent in on their behalf." — Karen Webster, PYMNTS

If users get used to "buying things, managing money, or borrowing money just by talking to an AI," the frequency with which they open the Alipay app will inevitably decline. OpenAI has already launched Instant Checkout within ChatGPT, completing checkout directly with Shopify merchants.

**2. AI agents bypassing Alipay**: a user tells an AI "buy me a coffee," and the AI calls a bank card directly to complete the payment, without going through Alipay at all.

**3. Disintermediation of smart investment advisory**: over one-third of consumers already use AI for investment guidance. The channel value of Ant Fortune as a "shelf" for wealth-management products may shrink.

**4. Democratization of risk-control capability**: large AI models lower the technical barrier to risk control, and smaller companies may be able to partially replicate Ant-level risk-control capability.

### C.4 Four dimensions in which AI strengthens Alipay

**1. Ant is actively embracing agentic commerce**
- It has released the ACT Protocol (China's first agentic commerce trust protocol)
- During the Spring Festival, the number of users of Alipay's AI payment feature **surpassed 100 million**, with weekly AI payment transactions **surpassing 120 million** (the first to reach this scale globally)
- The Qwen app has already connected Taobao and Alipay via the ACT Protocol

**2. A strong reserve of AI technology**
- 18 large models have already been released (language / reasoning / multimodal), all open source
- Ant AQ (the AI health assistant) has surpassed 100 million total users, with 30 million monthly active users
- Linggguang (a general-purpose AI assistant) surpassed one million downloads within four days of launch and has been positioned as "the Alipay of the AGI era"

**3. Irreplaceable scenario data**
- Payment, credit, wealth management, and insurance data from a billion users is a unique asset for training financial AI
- OpenAI can build general intelligence, but assessing "whether this user has the ability to repay" requires Ant-level data

**4. AI-driven cost reduction and efficiency gains**
- Risk-control agents: modeling performance improved by 10%
- Wealth-management agents: advisor efficiency improved by 100%
- Training on domestic AI chips has lowered computing costs by roughly 20%

### C.5 Assessment of threats and opportunities

| Dimension | Severity | Time window |
|------|---------|---------|
| Entry-point migration (app → agent) | **High** | 3-5 years |
| AI agents bypassing payments | Medium-high | 2-4 years |
| Disintermediation of smart advisory | Medium | 3-5 years |
| Democratization of risk-control capability | Medium-low | 5 years+ |

| Opportunity | Potential | Status |
|------|------|------|
| First-mover advantage in AI payments / the ACT Protocol | **High** | Already deployed (120 million transactions per week) |
| Accumulated large-model technology | High | 18 models already released |
| The new AI health track | High | Users have surpassed 100 million |
| Financial data barriers | High | A long-term advantage |

### C.6 Conclusion

**Short term (1-2 years): opportunity outweighs threat.** Ant's speed of response has been remarkable; the data of 120 million AI payment transactions per week shows it is actively leading the transition rather than defending passively.

**Medium term (3-5 years): a critical transition period.** If AI agents become the mainstream mode of interaction, Alipay could be demoted from "entry point" to "pipe." Alipay must complete its identity transformation from "app" to "protocol/infrastructure" before that happens.

**Long term (5 years+): three possible endgames**
1. **Best case**: Alipay becomes the "financial operating system of the agent era," the ACT Protocol becomes the standard, and all AI agents complete financial operations through Alipay
2. **Base case**: Alipay retains its dominant position in China's payments market, but profit margins are compressed by AI-driven competition
3. **Worst case**: Alipay is eroded by AI-native competitors and reduced to a "back-end pipe"

> **What will ultimately determine the outcome is not the AI technology itself (Ant has already proven it can keep pace), but the speed at which the user-interaction paradigm shifts.**

### Statement on the limitations of AI-assisted research

1. **Information richness: Grade B**: Ant is an unlisted company, and core financial metrics such as revenue, cash flow, and ROE are all estimates; actual figures may differ significantly
2. **Limited sources for market-share data**: payments market-share figures from different sources use inconsistent methodologies (transaction count vs. transaction value); the figures of 36.2% and 59.7% require cross-validation
3. **AI knowledge cutoff limitations**: some agents' analysis is based on a knowledge base current only to before May 2025 and may miss important developments from the second half of 2025 through early 2026
4. **Low confidence in valuation estimates**: Ant's revenue scope is unclear (different sources report figures ranging from 120 billion to 200 billion), leading to substantial error in valuation-multiple calculations
5. **Regulatory risk is difficult to quantify**: the unpredictability of changes in Chinese regulatory policy is a core risk that models cannot accurately capture
