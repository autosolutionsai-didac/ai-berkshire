## I. Business Model Analysis — Duan Yongping's Perspective

> *"A good business model is about doing the right things, and doing things right."* — Duan Yongping

### 1.1 What Is the Company?

Gpixel is a Chinese high-performance CMOS image sensor (CIS) design company, incubated in 2012 by the Changchun Institute of Optics, Fine Mechanics and Physics under the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Its founder, Dr. Wang Xinyang, holds degrees from Zhejiang University and TU Delft (Netherlands). The company operates a **fabless model** (no wafer fab of its own), focused on two major non-consumer tracks:

- **Industrial imaging (~66% of revenue)**: machine vision, automated inspection, intelligent transportation
- **Scientific imaging (~29% of revenue)**: microscopy, spectrometers, astronomical observation
- Emerging areas (~5%): professional imaging (8K cinema-grade CIS, in partnership with DJI), medical imaging

According to Frost & Sullivan, in 2024, by revenue, Gpixel ranked third globally and first in China in both industrial imaging and scientific imaging CIS. Its global market share was 15.2% in industrial imaging and 16.3% in scientific imaging.

### 1.2 How Would Duan Yongping See It?

**Is it understandable?** Yes. Fabless chip design is a "brain business" — its core barrier lies in talent and IP, it is asset-light and high-margin, consistent with the business model Duan Yongping favors.

**Where is the moat?**

| Moat element | Assessment |
|-----------|------|
| Technical barriers | ★★★★ Nine major product families covering area-scan/line-scan/TDI sensors; successfully positioned during the CCD-to-CMOS substitution window |
| Customer stickiness | ★★★★ Industrial CIS is embedded in customers' product designs, making switching costly (re-certification takes 6-12 months) |
| Economies of scale | ★★★ Limited under the fabless model, but design reuse and accumulated IP have value |
| Brand/channel | ★★★ Good reputation within industrial/scientific circles, but limited brand premium in a B2B context |

**The ceiling problem**: The global industrial + scientific CIS market is only about USD 3.0-4.0 billion (roughly 15% of the ~USD 26 billion total CIS market) — a "small but beautiful" niche. The company's 2025 revenue was RMB 857 million (~USD 120 million), with a global share already above 15%; further doubling would require opening up new tracks (professional imaging, medical).

**Duan Yongping's rating: ★★★★** — A good business in a good niche, with a real moat, but the market ceiling is a genuine constraint.

**Key findings:**

### 2.2 Valuation Verification

**Precise calculation (RMB basis):**

```

Net asset value per share: approx. RMB 5.0

PE = 35.07 / 0.67 = 52.3x
PB = 35.07 / 5.0 = 7.01x
ROE = 0.67 / 5.0 = 13.4%
Earnings yield = 1/52.3 = 1.91%
```

**IPO market cap:** HKD 39.88 × ~437 million shares = ~HKD 17.4 billion (~RMB 15.3 billion)

## III. Industry and Competition Analysis — Munger's Perspective

> *"Invert, always invert."* — Munger

### 3.1 CIS Industry Landscape

The global CIS market was approximately USD 26.0 billion in 2025, projected to reach USD 40.0 billion by 2030 (CAGR ~9%).

**By application:**
- Smartphone CIS (~65%): a three-way split among Sony, Samsung, and OmniVision
- Automotive CIS (~15%): led by onsemi, with Sony catching up
- **Industrial/scientific CIS (~10-15%): Gpixel's main battlefield**
- Security CIS (~5%): SmartSens, GalaxyCore

**Industrial CIS competitive landscape:**

| Rank | Company | Headquarters | Share | Characteristics |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| 1 | Sony | Japan | ~25% | Full-category coverage, overwhelming technology lead |
| 2 | onsemi | USA | ~20% | Dual-engine of automotive + industrial |
| **3** | **Gpixel** | **China** | **~15%** | **Specialized in industrial/scientific, domestic substitution** |
| 4 | Teledyne e2v | UK | ~10% | High-end aerospace/defense |

### 3.2 Munger's "Invert"

**Why it might win:**
- Domestic substitution is a major trend: strong demand for domestic CIS replacement in industrial automation (Hikvision/Dahua/Keyence China)
- CCD-to-CMOS technology-transition dividend: the company is positioned exactly at the substitution window
- The fabless model is flexible: not hostage to capacity cycles, travels light

**Why it might lose (Munger-style inversion checklist):**
- **Sony's "dimensionality-reduction" attack**: once Sony chooses to prioritize the industrial CIS market, its accumulated technology and brand advantage could easily seize share
- **Ceiling effect**: industrial CIS is a small pond of ~USD 3.0 billion; Gpixel already holds 15%, so doubling again gets sharply harder
- **Downstream cyclicality**: industrial-automation capex is highly sensitive to the macro economy — near-zero revenue growth in 2022-2023 is the evidence
- **Consumer-grade players crossing over**: consumer CIS giants such as Will Semiconductor (OmniVision) have the technology and customer resources to penetrate the industrial space

### 3.3 The Real Progress of Domestic Substitution

Domestic substitution is real, but one must clearly recognize:
- High-end industrial CIS (large-area-array, high-frame-rate, low-noise) is still dominated by Sony/onsemi
- Gpixel's advantage lies mainly in the mid-range market, winning on value for money
- The true peak of domestic substitution may come in the next 3-5 years, but competitors are also flooding in

**Munger's rating: ★★★★** — A leader in its niche, with a solid domestic-substitution thesis. But beware the "big fish in a small pond" trap — the pond itself is not big enough.

## IV. Risk and Management Assessment — Li Lu's Perspective

> *"The most important thing in investing is honesty — honesty with yourself, honesty with the facts."* — Li Lu

### 4.1 Management Assessment

**Founders: Wang Xinyang (Chairman/CEO) & Zhang Yanxia (COO/Board Secretary)**
- Classmates in Applied Electronics at Zhejiang University, both hold doctorates from TU Delft, Netherlands
- Wang Xinyang previously worked at Cypress and CMOSIS (a well-known European CIS design company), giving him a solid technical background
- As a married couple, they jointly control 49.53% of the equity, giving them firm control
- Returned to China to start the company in 2012, and has spent 14 years focused solely on CIS — consistent with Duan Yongping's philosophy of "staying within one's proper scope"

**Positives:** technical founders, long-termism, domain focus
**Negatives:** the couple hold multiple concurrent roles (Wang is Chairman + CEO + General Manager; Zhang is Director + Deputy General Manager + COO + Board Secretary), raising governance concerns

### 4.2 Core Risk Checklist

| Risk | Severity | Description |
|------|--------|------|
| **Overseas supply-chain dependence** | ★★★★★ | Wafer foundry depends on Israel's Tower Semiconductor + Korea's DB HiTek (47.5% combined); packaging depends on Japan's KFT. All top-five suppliers are overseas, accounting for 63.7% of the total. A geopolitical conflict could cause supply cutoffs |
| **Related-party transactions** | ★★★★ | Related-party transaction figures differ hugely between the H-share and A-share prospectuses (a 145% discrepancy in 2023!); the profit margin on sales to related parties at the Changchun Institute of Optics is higher than on sales to external customers, raising suspicion of improper benefit transfer |
| **STAR Market withdrawal** | ★★★ | Voluntarily withdrew its STAR Market (Shanghai) IPO application in January 2025 after regulators questioned the reasonableness of its multi-billion-yuan valuation, then pivoted to a Hong Kong listing — a clear intent to sidestep the A-share market's stricter review |
| **Market ceiling** | ★★★ | Global industrial + scientific CIS market is only ~USD 3.0 billion; the company already holds 15%, leaving limited room for growth |
| **Gross margin volatility** | ★★★ | 76% → 59% → 67% — the sharp swings indicate an unstable product mix and pricing strategy |
| **Special shareholder clauses** | ★★ | Four shareholders — Zhuhai Qixin, Hillhouse Yurun, Huashun Guangzhou, and Xiamen Yuanfeng — retain special information-rights reinstatement clauses |

### 4.3 A Deeper Look at Related-Party Transactions

This is the risk most worth watching closely. Related-party transaction figures diverge between the Hong Kong and A-share prospectuses:
- 2022: A-share disclosure RMB 147 million vs. H-share RMB 123 million, a gap of RMB 24.6 million (-17%)
- 2023: A-share disclosure RMB 116 million vs. H-share RMB 47.07 million, a gap of RMB 68.46 million (**-59%**)

The same company, the same underlying data, showing such a large discrepancy across two prospectuses — investors have good reason to question the reliability of the disclosure.

### 4.4 Quantifying Supply-Chain Risk

```
Share of overseas suppliers: 63.7% (2024)
Israel's Tower Semiconductor + Korea's DB HiTek = 47.5% (wafer foundry)
Japan's KFT = 6.6% (packaging)
In-house packaging progress: Changguang Yuanxin was established in 2020, but as of 2024 the company is still "mostly outsourcing"
```

Against the backdrop of US-China tech decoupling and geopolitical conflict, a Chinese chip company whose core supply chain is 100% dependent on overseas suppliers is not facing an ordinary risk — this is an existential risk.

**Li Lu's rating: ★★★** — Management's technical competence passes muster, but related-party transactions and supply-chain risk are weaknesses that cannot be ignored.

## V. Bull vs. Bear Showdown

### Bull Case (Optimistic Scenario)

1. **Domestic substitution accelerates**: geopolitical tension actually pushes downstream customers to proactively switch to domestic CIS, and Gpixel — as China's sole leading industrial CIS player — benefits the most
2. **New tracks open up**: if professional imaging (8K CIS in partnership with DJI) and medical imaging scale up, the company could break through its ceiling
3. **Margins continue improving**: economies of scale plus a rising share of high-end products could stabilize gross margin above 65%
4. **IPO proceeds fund expansion**: capital used for R&D and capacity building, accelerating domestic supply-chain substitution
5. **3-year target price: HKD 60-65 (+50-60%)**

### Bear Case (Pessimistic Scenario)

1. **Supply-chain rupture**: Israeli/Korean foundries hit by geopolitical shocks, capacity disruption directly affects deliveries
2. **Sony/onsemi counterattack**: the giants ramp up investment in industrial CIS, a price war compresses margins
3. **Growth stalls**: the 27% growth rate in 2025 proves unsustainable, reverting to single digits, and the 52x P/E faces a Davis double-kill (falling earnings and falling multiple together)
4. **Related-party-transaction blowup**: the disclosure inconsistencies get further investigated, damaging market trust
5. **3-year target price: HKD 15-20 (-50% or more)**

## VI. IPO Pricing Assessment and Subscription Recommendation

### Pricing Assessment

| Metric | Value | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| IPO P/E (2025E) | ~52x | On the high side; semiconductor sector average is 30-40x |
| IPO P/B | ~7.0x | Corresponds to an ROE of only 13.4% — mediocre value |
| Cornerstone investors | 24 investors, USD 166 million (50% of the offering) | A star-studded lineup (Greenwoods, Green Court, E Fund, China AMC, etc.), but there will be heavy lock-up-expiry selling pressure once the 6-month cornerstone lockup ends |
| Oversubscription | ~38x on the public offer tranche | Strong market enthusiasm, but the break-below-offer-price rate for Hong Kong IPOs is not low |
| Market cap | ~HKD 17.4 billion | A substantial step-up from the RMB 10 billion valuation ahead of its withdrawn STAR Market application |

### Subscription Recommendation

**Conclusion: Participate cautiously with a small position; do not go in heavy.**

**Reasons:**
1. **The 52x P/E has already borrowed against 1-2 years of growth expectations.** Even if profit grows 30% in 2026, the P/E would still be around 40x — not obviously cheap
2. **24 cornerstone investors lock up 50% of the shares**, leaving a small free float at listing, which could create a short-term premium — but the 6-month lock-up expiry is a potential source of selling pressure
3. **Supply-chain risk is a "sword of Damocles"** — a geopolitical trigger event could halve the stock price in an instant
4. **The contradictory related-party-transaction figures** have not been adequately explained, undermining disclosure transparency

**If you participate:**
- Subscribe with a small amount; sell immediately upon allotment (to capture the first-day premium)
- Not recommended as a long-term holding
- If the price pulls back below HKD 30 after listing (~40x P/E), building a position for observation could be considered

**If you wait:**
- Watch the price action for 3-6 months after listing, waiting for the cornerstone lock-up expiry and the first Hong Kong-format earnings report to verify growth sustainability
- Ideal buy range: HKD 25-30 (35-40x P/E), offering a reasonable margin of safety

## VII. Team Lead's Final Verdict

Gpixel is a **good company** — it has achieved the No. 1 position in China and No. 3 globally in the industrial/scientific CIS niche, its technical founding team is credible, its fabless model is asset-light and high-margin, and its domestic-substitution thesis is solid.

But **a good company does not equal a good investment**:
- The 52x P/E pricing has already priced in the next two years' worth of growth expectations
- 100% dependence on overseas suppliers is a fatal weak point
- The contradictory related-party-transaction figures expose governance shortcomings
- The track's ceiling limits long-term upside

> *"Be fearful when others are greedy."* — Buffett

A 38x oversubscription shows the market is greedy. As a research team, our recommendation is: **stay mostly on the sidelines and wait for a better price.**
