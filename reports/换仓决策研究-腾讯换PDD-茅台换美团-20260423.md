## 4. Common Logical Problems with Rotation

### 4.1 Five Psychological Traps

| # | Trap | Investor Psychology |
|---|------|------------|
| 1 | **Anchoring effect** | PDD's 10x PE looks cheap (ignoring its zero return) |
| 2 | **Sunk-cost avoidance** | Wanting to swap out of a stock that hasn't moved in years (ignoring compounding) |
| 3 | **Narrative appeal** | The sexy Temu/KeeTa story (ignoring execution uncertainty) |
| 4 | **Opportunity-cost fallacy** | "I wish I'd swapped earlier" (ignoring that focus is the source of returns) |
| 5 | **FOMO** | Chasing a rising new stock, wanting to sell a flat old one (confusing price with value) |

### 4.2 The Math of Swapping vs. Adding

**Swapping requires being "right twice"**:

| Single-decision accuracy | Probability of being right both times |
|-----------|---------------|
| 70% | **49%** |
| 80% | **64%** |
| 90% | **81%** |

**Even a master with 80% accuracy has only a 64% success rate on a swap — barely better than a coin flip**.

This is the essence of Duan Yongping's saying that **"a stop-doing list matters more than a to-do list"**.

### 4.3 The Hidden Cost of Taxes and Dividends

| Market | Capital gains tax | Hidden loss |
|------|-----------|---------|
| US-listed ADR | 20% | Direct -10% hit to position |
| Hong Kong shares | 0% | **But loses 3-4% in annual dividends** (30-40% cumulative over 10 years) |
| A-shares | 0% | Same as above |

**Even where Hong Kong/A-shares are tax-free, giving up 10 years of dividends means a 30-40% loss of guaranteed return**. For the new company to compound at 15% over 10 years versus Moutai's 12% annualized, **the gap is often just 3%, while the risk is three times higher**.

### 4.4 Buffett's "Cutting the Flowers, Watering the Weeds"
> "To hold on to inferior businesses while selling superior ones is a 'cut the flowers and water the weeds' strategy."

- Tencent/Moutai = **flowers** (proven over 20+ years)
- PDD/Meituan = **weeds not yet proven**

## 10. Final Conclusion (In One Paragraph)

**"Don't swap" is the most rational answer**. Across all four dimensions — valuation, shareholder return, master endorsement, and risk match — both proposed swaps fail to beat holding the original positions:
- **Tencent's core business at 11-13x PE + a 3% shareholder return + a free-thrown-in investment portfolio = "a cheap good company"**
