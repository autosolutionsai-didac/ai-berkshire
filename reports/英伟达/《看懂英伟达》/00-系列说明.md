# *Understanding NVIDIA* Series Overview

> 5 main articles, peeling NVIDIA apart layer by layer to reveal its true structure.
>
> Total reading time for the series: about 60 minutes; reading in order is recommended.

---

## Series positioning

NVIDIA doesn't lack attention — it's the world's largest company by market cap, with hundreds of analysis pieces written about it every day.

But the vast majority of that analysis has two problems:

1. **The bulls look only at growth**: AI compute demand is exploding, the CUDA moat is bottomlessly deep, Jensen Huang is a genius — so it will "keep rising forever"
2. **The bears look only at the bubble**: it's a rerun of Cisco in 2000, customers' in-house chips will disrupt it, gross margins aren't sustainable — so it will "eventually collapse"

**Both sides are right, but neither is complete.** NVIDIA is a company where three things are simultaneously true: "growth is assured, its near-monopoly is loosening, and its valuation is expensive."

What this series aims to do is **look at all three things together** — first laying out clearly "what it is," then letting you judge for yourself "whether it's worth buying."

## The series' writing principles

This series follows these hard rules:

1. **Lay out the data first, then the reasoning, and only then the conclusion** — no bullish or bearish stance is preset
2. **Every core judgment carries a counterargument** — so readers can weigh it themselves
3. **Unreliable data would rather be dropped**: many key data points for NVIDIA (inference market share, the ratio of in-house chip substitution, etc.) are not officially confirmed, so this series uses only the most credible sources
4. **No "probability-weighted expected return" calculations**: assigning weights to scenarios is purely subjective and would give readers a false sense of precision
5. **This does not constitute investment advice** — a disclaimer appears at the end of every article

## Comparison with *Understanding Pinduoduo* and *Understanding Tencent*

| Dimension | Tencent | Pinduoduo | NVIDIA |
|------|------|--------|--------|
| Asset quality | WeChat is irreplaceable | Algorithm-driven advertising efficiency is extreme | Near-monopoly in AI compute |
| Valuation | Reasonable, on the cheap side | Extremely cheap | **On the expensive side** |
| Management | Highly transparent | Low transparency | Extremely capable + no succession plan |
| Core risk | Regulatory tail risk | Temu political risk | **Cyclicality + customers' in-house chips + valuation** |
| Certainty | High | Medium | **High near-term, low long-term** |

What makes NVIDIA distinctive is: **it has the highest quality of the three, but also the highest price.** A good company and a good investment are two different things.
