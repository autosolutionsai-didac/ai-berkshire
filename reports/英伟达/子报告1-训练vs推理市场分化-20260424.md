## 1. Core Judgment

The biggest structural change in the AI accelerator market over the next 3-5 years is **the center of gravity shifting from training to inference**: by 2026, inference workloads already account for roughly two-thirds of AI compute, and the inference market has for the first time overtaken training in size (about $118 billion vs. roughly $70-80 billion for training). By 2030, the inference TAM could be 3-10x that of training. NVIDIA still holds 90%+ share on the training side, but **its inference-side share has already fallen from 90% in 2024 to roughly 65-75% in 2026**, and will continue to be eroded over the next three years by three forces: (a) hyperscalers' in-house ASICs (TPU/Trainium/Maia/MTIA/Titan), (b) AMD's MI350/MI400 lead in tokens-per-dollar, and (c) dedicated inference architectures such as Groq/Cerebras. Under a neutral scenario, NVIDIA's 2030 inference share is around 45-55%, but because the total market roughly doubles or triples, absolute revenue still grows — **it's the gross margin that gets squeezed from the 75% range toward the 60% range.**

## 3. The Special Requirements of Inference Hardware

### Training hardware requirements
- **Massive memory + HBM bandwidth**: B200's 192GB HBM3E, Rubin Ultra's 365TB/rack
- **Extremely high interconnect**: NVLink 5 at 1.8TB/s, large-scale scale-up via NVL72/NVL576
- **Stable FP16/FP8/BF16 computation**: long training runs cannot suffer numerical collapse
- **Cluster communication**: NCCL, 800G InfiniBand, flat topology
- **Reliability**: a single training run lasts 30-90 days, so hardware failure rates must be extremely low
- **Concentrated customer base**: roughly 30 global frontier labs plus 20 sovereign-AI programs

### Inference hardware requirements
- **Low latency**: interactive use cases need TTFT under 100ms
- **High throughput**: tokens/sec/$ and tokens/sec/W are the real KPIs
- **Multi-precision support**: INT8/INT4/FP4/FP8 (B200's FP4 is a key selling point)
- **Cost sensitivity**: customers calculate cost per token (B200: $0.02/M tokens vs. H100: $0.09/M)
- **Diverse deployment**: from a single card to 8 cards, small clusters, or edge devices
- **Server density**: token throughput per rack / tokens per watt
- **Extremely fragmented customer base**: thousands of AI applications, SaaS companies, and in-house enterprise deployments worldwide

**Key difference**: training buys "extreme performance + reliability," while inference buys "value-for-money + latency." This means the inference market is inherently more fragmented, more price-sensitive, and **far easier for ASICs and challengers to break into**.

## 4. The Inference Hardware Competitive Landscape (2026)

### A. NVIDIA (GPUs, market leader)
- H100/H200: still the leading inference workhorses globally in 2026 (the H200's 141GB HBM3E is especially well-suited to long-context inference)
- B200/GB200 NVL72: FP4 performance 2x that of H100, 1.4 ExaFLOPS FP4 per rack, inference cost 4-6x lower than H100
- TensorRT-LLM + Dynamo: the software stack is the moat
- **2026 estimated inference share: 65-75%** (including cloud external sales + sovereign AI)
- Rubin (2026 H2) / Rubin Ultra (2027 H2): NVL576 delivers 15 EF FP4 for inference, 14x GB300

### B. AMD (GPUs, the value-for-money challenger)
- MI300X (192GB HBM3): major customers include Microsoft Azure / Meta / Oracle
- MI325X (256GB HBM3E): 2025
- MI350/MI355X (2026 H2, 288GB HBM3E): claims 30% faster Llama 3.1 405B inference than B200 and 40% higher tokens-per-dollar
- MI400 (late 2026/2027, 432GB HBM4, 19.6TB/s): positioned as "built for inference"
- **2026 overall AI GPU share**: 12-15% (about 15% on the inference side)
- Weakness: the ROCm ecosystem remains weak, and long-tail customers are hard to reach

### C. Google TPU (ASIC, primarily for in-house use)
- TPU v5e (inference-optimized) / v6e Trillium (2024) / v7 Ironwood (2025)
- 75%+ of Gemini inference runs on TPU
- Limited external availability: Anthropic is the largest external customer

### D. AWS Inferentia / Trainium
- Trainium 3 (2026) plus continued maturation of the Neuron SDK
- Anthropic's Project Rainier: a cluster of hundreds of thousands of Trainium 2 chips
- In 2026, about 30% of Amazon's internal AI workloads run on in-house chips

### E. Microsoft Maia
- Maia 100 (2024) / Maia 200 (2026)
- Mainly serves Azure OpenAI Service / Copilot inference
- In 2026 it still handles a minority (< 30%) of Microsoft's AI workload, but is growing fastest

### F. Meta MTIA
- MTIA v2 (2025) is already deployed at scale in recommendation systems + Llama inference
- Three generations running in parallel in 2026

### G. Groq LPU (dedicated inference ASIC)
- 500MB of on-chip SRAM, 150 TB/s bandwidth (45x that of H100)
- 300 tokens/sec on Llama 2 70B, 10x H100
- Energy efficiency 35x that of H100 (150 tokens/W vs. 4.3 tokens/W)
- **Acquired by NVIDIA for $20 billion in December 2025**

### H. Cerebras WSE-3
- A full wafer-scale chip with 44GB of SRAM (880x H100); Llama 3.1 405B runs at over 1,000 tokens/sec
- **In April 2026, OpenAI placed a $20 billion order, and Cerebras has filed for an IPO (at a $35 billion valuation)**

### I. Huawei Ascend
- 910C's inference performance is about 60% of H100's; 2026 production target is 600,000 units
- 920 (launching 2026): fills the gap left by H20's exit
- 950PR: 1.56 PFLOP, targeting the higher end
- **Domestic Chinese market share: domestic vendors combined hold 41%** (Bernstein/IDC, 2026 Q1)

### J. Cambricon
- 2026 target of 500,000 units in the Siyuan series (its first year of annual profitability)
- Constraints: SMIC's 7nm yield is only ~20%, and HBM supply is restricted by Korean manufacturers

## 5. ASIC vs. GPU Trade-offs in Inference Scenarios

### ASIC advantages
- Performance-per-watt is 2-5x that of GPUs
- Cost per token is 30-50% lower
- Optimized for transformer inference dataflow
- Can be deeply coupled with a hyperscaler customer's specific workload

### ASIC disadvantages
- Poor flexibility: changes in model architecture (e.g., MoE, state-space models) require a redesign
- Design cycle of 18-24 months: cannot keep pace with model iteration
- Weak software ecosystem: compilers and kernel libraries must be built in-house
- High production costs: NRE per chip runs into hundreds of millions of dollars
- Only suitable for the very largest customers

### GPU advantages
- Versatility: handles training, inference, HPC, and graphics alike
- The CUDA ecosystem has 18 years of maturity
- Keeps pace with model innovation: Mamba, diffusion, and MoE all run on it
- Strong liquidity in the rental market

### GPU disadvantages
- Relatively worse value-for-money (vs. a well-optimized ASIC)
- Lower utilization (inference load fluctuates)
- High HBM cost

**Judgment**: hyperscalers will keep moving toward ASICs, but **GPUs remain the default choice for the long tail of the market**.

## 7. Inference Margins vs. Training Margins

### NVIDIA's training chips
- Gross margin 75%+
- ASP: H100 $30-40k, B200 $30-50k, a full GB200 NVL72 rack around $3 million
- Customers are highly willing to pay a premium

### NVIDIA's inference chips
- Estimated gross margin 60-70%
- Customers are highly price-sensitive
- AMD's MI355X directly undercuts on price
- ASICs pull down the market reference price

### Three-year trend
- NVIDIA's overall data-center gross margin may fall from **75% to 65-68%** (by 2028)
- **Absolute revenue doubles** (data center revenue from ~$190B in 2026 to ~$350B in 2028)
- NVIDIA's response: maintain premium pricing through systemized sales (full-stack NVL72/NVL576/Spectrum-X), selling racks rather than chips

## 8. Three Scenario Forecasts (2030)

### Optimistic (NVIDIA inference share 55-60%)
- The CUDA + TensorRT-LLM + Dynamo ecosystem moat holds
- AMD's ROCm share plateaus at 12-15%
- NVIDIA data-center revenue in 2030: $500B+, gross margin 70%+

### Neutral (NVIDIA inference share 40-50%)
- AMD's MI400/MI500 captures 20-25% of the inference market
- The top 5 hyperscalers cover 50-60% of internal inference with in-house chips
- The China market is essentially lost
- NVIDIA data-center revenue in 2030: $350-400B, gross margin 65%

### Pessimistic (NVIDIA inference share 25-35%)
- Major customers' in-house chip programs all succeed, and OpenAI leaves NVIDIA
- AMD's MI500 overtakes on performance
- ASICs become commoditized
- NVIDIA data-center revenue in 2030: $250-300B, gross margin 55-60%

**ai-berkshire's baseline judgment**: neutral-to-optimistic. NVIDIA is likely to remain near-monopolistic in training over the next five years; on the inference side, although share is being eroded, the absolute market triples in size. **The real risk is a structural 10-15 percentage-point decline in gross margin.**

---

## 9. Key Investment Implications

1. **NVIDIA is not a "training chip stock" — it's an "AI systemized infrastructure stock"**: what you're really buying is the full-stack pricing power of NVL576 + Spectrum-X + CUDA + Dynamo + Groq
2. **The divergence in the inference market is irreversible**: NVIDIA's inference share will inevitably decline over the next five years, but total market size × share still yields rising absolute revenue
3. **AMD is the real number-two contender**: the MI400 is the pivotal product, and the 2027 earnings report is the validation window
4. **Broadcom is "the shovel-seller's shovel-seller"**: every major customer's ASIC program relies on Broadcom's design services
5. **China's AI chip market is a parallel universe**: Huawei plus Cambricon have already reached roughly parity with NVIDIA in the domestic market
6. **OpenAI's move toward Broadcom is the biggest crack in NVIDIA's long-term narrative**: 2027-2028 is the period to watch
7. **The shift of profit toward inference implies NVIDIA's P/E should compress**: from the training era's 35-40x toward the inference era's 25-30x
