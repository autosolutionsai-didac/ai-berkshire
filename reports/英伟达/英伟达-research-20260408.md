## 5. Investment thesis (Bull vs. Bear)

### 🟢 Bull case

2. **The CUDA ecosystem moat runs extremely deep** — 2 million+ developers, 20 years of accumulation, impossible to replicate in the short term
3. **Full-stack integration advantage** — GPU + NVLink + InfiniBand + CUDA + AI Enterprise, which competitors cannot break through at any single point
4. **Annual architecture iteration** (Blackwell → Rubin → Feynman) continues to widen the technology gap
5. **Financial metrics are near-perfect** — ROE of 124%, gross margin of 71%, FCF of $96.7 billion, net cash of $52.0 billion
6. **Forward PE of 21x**, given 65% growth, implies a PEG of just 0.89x — not unreasonable from a growth-valuation perspective
7. **Inference-market handoff** — as the shift moves from training to inference, total compute demand is actually increasing, and Rubin CPX is specifically optimized for inference

### 🔴 Bear case

1. **AI capex cyclicality** — $700 billion in annual capex is historically rare; once it pulls back, NVIDIA faces a double hit to revenue and valuation
2. **Customers are becoming competitors** — the top 4 customers account for 61% of revenue and are all developing in-house chips, creating high-confidence long-term erosion
3. **The CUDA moat is being eroded** — the Triton compiler, ROCm's progress, and the OpenAI-AMD 6GW agreement are all warning signs
4. **A 75% gross margin is not sustainable** — analysts expect it to fall back to 65-70% as intensifying competition compresses the premium
5. **Extreme revenue concentration** — data center revenue is 89.7% of the total, making it essentially a single-business company
6. **Succession risk** — a $4.3 trillion company with no succession plan, with the 63-year-old CEO as an irreplaceable core asset
7. **Geopolitical risk** — China export controls could expand further, and 100% reliance on TSMC as a foundry is a tail risk

## 8. Summary

NVIDIA is one of the most strategically valuable companies of the AI era, with a multi-layered moat built on the CUDA ecosystem, full-stack integration, annual iteration, and $15 billion+ in R&D investment. Its financial performance is textbook-quality — $215.9 billion in revenue, $120.0 billion in net income, 71% gross margin, 124% ROE, and nearly $100 billion in free cash flow. Jensen Huang is one of the most visionary CEOs in tech history — his 2006 bet on CUDA and the 2020 acquisition of Mellanox are decisions that have already made history.

However, at a $4.3 trillion market cap and 36x PE, the market has already priced in perfect growth. The most underrated risk is the cyclicality of AI capex — the sheer scale of nearly $700 billion in hyperscaler capex projected for 2026 has historically never sustained for more than 2-3 years. Customers' in-house chips (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) are slowly eroding NVIDIA's market share on the inference side, and while the CUDA moat runs deep, cracks have already appeared. From a Buffett-style value-investing perspective, the current share price lacks a sufficient margin of safety.

**Final judgment: A grade-A business at a grade-B- price. Balanced investors should wait for a better entry point (Forward PE of 15-18x, corresponding to $120-145) rather than chasing the stock at its current high.**
