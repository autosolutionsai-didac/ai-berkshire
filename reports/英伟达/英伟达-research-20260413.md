## II. Core Findings of This Research (Six Variables Already in Motion)

### Finding #1: The CUDA Moat Has Entered an "AI Coding-Tool Accelerated Erosion" Phase (Highest Priority · Not Priced by the Market)

**Evidence of a qualitative shift**:
- **The January 2026 Claude Code 30-minute CUDA→ROCm port event** (a public demo by johnnytshi): achieved near-native performance without relying on HIPIFY. Widely discussed by Techstrong, wccftech, and HardForum
- **The ROCm-vs-CUDA gap is narrowing**: an independent April 2026 ThunderCompute benchmark found the gap for compute-intensive workloads is now only **10-30%** (versus 2-3x in 2023)
- **The CASS academic translation model**: **95% accuracy** at the source-code level and 37.5% at the assembly level, significantly outperforming traditional HIPIFY
- **ROCm 7 / HIP 7** (released in H2 2025) explicitly states in its strategy that it will "align more closely with CUDA semantics" — AMD is officially paving the way for AI-tool-driven automatic translation
- **Triton 3.6.0** (January 20, 2026) + **TorchInductor going live in vLLM production**: PyTorch's backend abstraction is weakening the bundling chain of "developers writing CUDA directly"

**But the moat will not collapse overnight**:
- The **100,000-GPU-scale NCCL / collective-communication stack** remains deeply coupled to CUDA + NVLink + InfiniBand; academic papers (arXiv 2510.20171, Meta Mycroft SOSP25) show that 3-minute fault-detection recovery is a real barrier
- **Trust**: no CTO is willing to hand a billion-dollar training job to an unproven software stack
- No publicly disclosed frontier LLM training failure has ever been attributed to a "non-CUDA-stack defect"

**Assessment**: the CUDA moat has degraded from "four layers" (code lock-in + performance + ecosystem + trust) to "two layers" (**performance + trust**). The widest moat — code-migration cost — **is being filled in**: the impact on the training side is small (trust matters most there), while the **impact on the inference side is large** (code translation shows results most easily and inference has high fault tolerance).

**Moat score revision: baseline report 4.25/5 → 3.75/5**

### Finding #3: Training vs. Inference Are Diverging Into Two Different Battlefields

**Market-size shift**:
- Deloitte, 2026: **inference now accounts for two-thirds of total AI compute** (versus just one-third in 2023 and about one-half in 2025)
- Gartner: 55% of AI-IaaS spending goes to inference in 2026; ≥65% by 2029
- The inference-optimized chip market will exceed $50 billion in 2026

**NVDA no longer owns the price-performance advantage on the inference side**:
- Groq (LPU): Llama 3.1 70B at **$0.64 per million tokens**, 240+ tokens/s
- Cerebras: $0.60 per million tokens, 450 tokens/s per user
- DeepInfra: $0.03-0.05 per million tokens (the price floor on GPUs)
- NVDA's Blackwell Ultra / Rubin CPX **acknowledges the divergence** but **cannot capture it all** — the inference market is shifting from "GPU exclusivity" to "routing by use case"

**The one variable providing a floor**: **test-time compute scaling** (o1/o3/Claude thinking) is pulling per-query inference compute up by 10-100x. This is why NVDA's inference side still has growth, but it is not enough to hold share.

**Assessment**: **inference is not NVDA's extended territory — it is an open battlefield**. The baseline report's direction of "inference taking the baton from training" is correct, but the implicit assumption that "NVDA still dominates inference" needs to be weakened.

**Historically, hyperscaler CapEx growth of +50%+ year-over-year has never lasted more than 3 years.** 2024 was +40%, 2025 was +55%, 2026 is +70% — **2027 is, probabilistically, the most likely turning-point year**.

**Circular-deal risk (not adequately discussed in the baseline report)**:
- **NVDA invested up to $100 billion in OpenAI** (Sept 2025) → OpenAI committed to purchasing millions of NVDA GPUs
- **OpenAI committed $300 billion to Oracle over 5 years** (part of the Stargate project)
- Cross-investments and GPU purchase commitments between **NVDA and CoreWeave / xAI / Oracle**
- A 2026 Bloomberg feature mapped this ecosystem and labeled it "AI Circular Deals"

"NVDA is paying for its own future revenue" — this is the bears' sharpest argument, and **the data is missing**: NVDA has not separately disclosed what share of its FY26-27 incremental revenue is "self-financed."

**Demand-side counterargument (the bull case)**:
- Anthropic ARR: $5 billion in Aug 2025 → $9 billion at end-2025 → **$30 billion in March 2026** (YoY +1400%), with Claude Code alone contributing over $2.5 billion
- Google Gemini had 750 million MAU in Q4 2025
- Enterprise paid demand is **real**, and is not fully isomorphic to Cisco's "pure infrastructure bubble" of 2000

**Comparison with Cisco in 2000**:
- Cisco's P/E was >200 with shrinking margins / NVDA's P/E is ~36 with expanding margins
- **But NVDA's EV/Sales (24x) is already approaching Cisco's peak (31x)**
- Cisco fell 88% from its peak and **took 25 years to return to its previous high**

**Assessment**: the cyclicality of AI CapEx is **the single biggest risk** — the baseline report already flagged this, but **circular dealing** should be added as **its own separate risk category**.

### Finding #5: The Algorithmic-Efficiency Revolution — DeepSeek Has Proven "Instant Re-Rating" Is a Real Risk

**The DeepSeek event** (Jan 27, 2025):
- V3's training cost was about $5.6 million (versus an estimated >$100 million for GPT-4); R1's post-training cost was $294,000
- NVDA fell **-17% in a single day, wiping out $589 billion in market cap — the largest single-day market-cap loss in U.S. stock market history** (the previous record was Meta's $240 billion)
- NVDA was down -11% for the full month of January

**Counter-evidence for Jevons Paradox** (looking back 12 months later):
- Anthropic's ARR tripled in 5 months
- Hyperscaler 2026 CapEx is up +67%
- Jensen Huang's CES 2025 "three-layer scaling" narrative (pre-training, post-training, test-time) successfully rebuilt market confidence

**The real lesson**:
- The risk **is not in algorithmic efficiency itself** — efficiency gains stimulate more demand over the long run (Jevons Paradox)
- The risk **lies in the instant re-rating of narrative/expectations** — the market is extremely sensitive to any single event suggesting "GPU demand may be overestimated"
- The next trigger could be: Anthropic's Ironwood performance data, a breakthrough in Huawei's CANN ecosystem, or an AI coding agent cracking 100,000-GPU cluster tuning

**The baseline report should add "narrative risk" as an independent risk dimension.**

### Finding #6: The China Market — From "Already Priced In" to "Permanent TAM Loss"

**Short term (already happened)**:
- FY26 Q1 2025: **a $4.5 billion H20 write-down plus $2.5 billion of shippable inventory** (halted by the Trump administration)
- The earlier initial Trump-administration ban caused a $5.5 billion write-off
- A July 2025 policy reversal allowed H20 shipments to resume; the B30A (a cut-down Blackwell) **still requires approval**

**Long term (TAM loss)**:
- Huawei's Ascend 910C 2026 production volume is **600,000 units** (double 2025's)
- The **Ascend 950DT SuperCluster delivers 524 EFLOPS FP8** (shipping Q4 2026) — already approaching H100-class cluster performance
- **CANN went open source** (Dec 31, 2025): Huawei's answer to CUDA, backed by $2.1 billion per year in ecosystem investment over 5 years
- Chinese developers are extensively optimizing Qwen/Llama kernels for Ascend

**Assessment**: the baseline report's view that "the China impact, at roughly 3%, has already been absorbed" **underestimates future TAM** — China's AI compute market has a potential annual size of $20-50 billion, and **NVDA is being permanently excluded from it**.

### 🟡 Buffett's Perspective: Moat and Margin of Safety

**Moat**:
- CUDA ecosystem: **degraded from four layers to two** (performance + trust)
- Full-stack integration (GPU + NVLink + software stack): still strong in the near term
- Scale effects + pace of technology iteration: a clear positive feedback loop

**A Buffett-style question**:
> "Will this moat still be there in 10 years?"

Answer: **On the training side, most likely yes (still a major player in 2035); on the inference side, most likely not intact.**

**Margin of safety (revised DCF)**:
- Original baseline report: DCF of $150–160 vs. a share price of $178, a 10–19% premium

- **Revised DCF: $110–130**
- The current share price of $178 represents a **37-62%** premium to the revised DCF — **the margin of safety is seriously inadequate**

**Buffett's conclusion**: an A-grade business, but **buying at the current price violates the principle of "buying a dollar for fifty cents."**

### 🟢 Li Lu's Perspective: A Civilization-Level Paradigm, or a Flash in the Pan?

**AI is indeed a civilization-level paradigm** (on the same level as electricity and the internet). The question is not "will AI reshape the world," but "can NVDA become the primary beneficiary of this paradigm."

**A Li-Lu-style question**:
> "Looking back 20 years from now, will NVDA be this era's Standard Oil (a monopoly that endures), or its Cisco (mediocre once the infrastructure build-out is done)?"

Historical analogies:
- **IBM → Microsoft** (the PC era, a change of dynasty)
- **Cisco → AWS** (the internet era, the shovel-seller replaced by the cloud providers)
- **Intel → NVDA** (the GPU era, the previous champion disrupted)

**Key observation**:
- The internet's ultimate winners were not Cisco (which sold networking equipment), but Google, Amazon, and Facebook (which used the infrastructure to build applications)
- The ultimate winners of the AI era may be **the users of compute** (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google/xAI), **not the providers of compute**
- NVDA's position resembles "the Cisco of the AI era" — it has a chance, but history does not guarantee it as the winner

**Li Lu's conclusion**: this is a paradigm-level opportunity, but NVDA is "one of the highest-probability publicly listed players in this paradigm," not "a certain long-term winner." **Holding for 5 years may be reasonable; holding for 20 years requires a different logic.**
