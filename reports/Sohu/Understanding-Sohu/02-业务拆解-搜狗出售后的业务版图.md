## Sohu's Three Puzzle Pieces

Sohu's business today is much simpler — and much more fragile — than most people realize. Sogou has already been sold off, Changyou has already been taken private, and what's left can fit on a single table. This piece pulls apart the three remaining businesses one by one, to establish the real state of each.

Following the sale of Sogou (2021, sold to Tencent for $1.18 billion) and Changyou's privatization delisting (2020, Sohu bought out minority shareholders for $579 million), Sohu's current business map has been reduced to three pieces:

| Business segment | 2025 revenue | Share | One-line characterization |
|---------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Changyou gaming | **$506 million** | **86.6%** | Sohu's lifeline, riding on the Tian Long Ba Bu IP |
| Marketing services (portal + video advertising) | $60 million | 10.3% | A steadily shrinking legacy |
| Other | $18 million | 3.1% | Negligible |

Source: Sohu 2025 annual report (PR Newswire, February 9, 2026)

Let's take each apart in turn.

---

## I. Changyou Gaming: A 2007 IP Carrying an Entire Public Company

### The Fundamentals

Changyou was originally a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sohu, listed independently on NASDAQ in 2009, and taken private and delisted by Sohu in 2020 at $10.80/ADS (a roughly 68% premium). It is now Sohu's core revenue source.

Changyou has essentially one flagship product series: **Tian Long Ba Bu (TLBB)**.

| Metric | Data | Source |
|------|------|------|
| IP launch date | **2007** (PC game) | Public information |
| TLBB franchise revenue as share of Sohu total revenue | **~60%** | 2025 annual report, Form 20-F risk section |
| TLBB franchise revenue as share of gaming revenue | **~69%** | Same |
| 2025 gaming operating margin | **~47%** | Derived from Sohu financial reports |
| New July 2025 product | TLBB Return (New Tian Long Ba Bu: Return) | Public information |
| Q3 2025 gaming revenue | $162 million (+27% YoY) | Q3 earnings report |
| Q1 2026 gaming revenue | $125 million (+6% YoY) | Q1 2026 earnings report |

**Core assessment**: Changyou's gaming business is profitable, and not thinly so (47% operating margin). But 60% of all of Sohu's revenue is tied to a single 2007-vintage PC-game IP — and that IP has a "shelf life."

### How Much Longer Can TLBB Last?

Factors sustaining TLBB's ongoing monetization:
- **A loyal nostalgic user base**: paying users are concentrated among 30-45-year-old veteran players with high ARPU
- **TLBB Return launched in 2025**: the new version drove a wave of returning players, with Q3 gaming revenue up 27% YoY
- **The continuation of the "PC-to-mobile" life cycle**: similar to NetEase's Fantasy Westward Journey, a classic IP can be refreshed repeatedly

Factors threatening TLBB:
- **User aging**: mobile MAU fell **27%** YoY in 2025 (source: 2025 annual report), and the paying user base is shrinking
- **Fading new-launch dividend**: Q1 2026 gaming revenue grew only 3% quarter-over-quarter, and TLBB Return's initial surge is fading
- **No new IP pipeline**: Sohu/Changyou have not publicly disclosed any major new game pipeline
- **Industry competition**: against Tencent's roughly 200 billion yuan and NetEase's roughly 90 billion yuan and miHoYo's roughly 40 billion yuan in gaming revenue, Changyou's $500 million (roughly 3.6 billion yuan) doesn't even register as a rounding error in China's gaming market (source: estimated from each company's financial reports)

**Counterargument**: Fantasy Westward Journey (launched 2003) remains one of NetEase's top-three revenue sources today, proving that a classic MMO IP's life can exceed 20 years. As a similar type of IP, TLBB shouldn't hit a cliff within 3-5 years as long as it keeps getting new versions.

**Counter-counterargument**: Fantasy Westward Journey is backed by NetEase, a giant with roughly 90 billion yuan in annual gaming revenue continuously investing in R&D and operations. Changyou lacks resources at that scale — TLBB's ability to "keep going" depends on operational investment, and Sohu's operating team and capital allocation capability fall far short of NetEase's.

## II. Marketing Services (Portal + Video Advertising): A Vanishing Business

### The Full Picture in Numbers

| Year | Marketing services revenue | YoY |
|------|------------|------|
| 2023 | $73 million | — |
| 2024 | $73 million | Flat |
| 2025 | **$60 million** | **-18%** |
| Q1 2026 | $13 million | -8% (YoY) / -26% (QoQ) |

Source: Sohu quarterly and annual reports

**Five years ago, Sohu's advertising revenue was still in the hundreds of millions of dollars; now it's down to just $60 million. In China's 850-billion-yuan internet advertising market, Sohu's share is now less than six ten-thousandths of a percent.** (Source: industry research estimates)

### Why Can't Sohu Sell Its Advertising Inventory?

This isn't a problem unique to Sohu — it's the structural death of the entire portal-advertising model:

| Era | Traffic distribution method | Representative platforms | Sohu's position |
|------|-----------|---------|-----------|
| 2000-2010 | Editorial curation + active visits | Sina, Sohu, NetEase | **Lead role** |
| 2012-2018 | Algorithmic recommendation + feeds | Toutiao, Tencent News | Disrupted |
| 2018-2026 | Short video + social distribution | Douyin, WeChat Channels | **No longer at the table** |

China's top 5 internet advertising companies (2025 estimates):

| Rank | Company | Ad revenue (est.) | Market share |
|------|------|------------|-------|
| 1 | ByteDance | ~350 billion yuan | 41% |
| 2 | Alibaba | ~220 billion yuan | 26% |
| 3 | Tencent | ~140 billion yuan | 16% |
| 4 | Baidu | ~70 billion yuan | 8% |
| 5 | Kuaishou | ~50 billion yuan | 6% |

Source: each company's financial reports plus industry research estimates

**Sohu's ad revenue (roughly 400 million yuan) doesn't even crack the top 20. Advertisers are voting with their feet, and all traffic is flowing to algorithmic platforms and short-video platforms.**

### The Current State of Sohu Video

Sohu Video was once a pioneer in long-form video. From 2010-2014 it was a major buyer of shows like *House of Cards* and *Empresses in the Palace*. But it lost the 2015 content-rights war to BAT's capital firepower, and has been retrenching ever since.

Sohu Video in 2026:
- Monthly active users below **20 million** (compare: Tencent Video 450 million, iQIYI 400 million, Bilibili 340 million)
- Almost no new content purchases
- Core positioning is as the platform for Charles Zhang's "physics lectures + knowledge livestreams"
- **More like a "sign left hanging on the wall" than a real content platform**

**Counterargument**: Sohu Video is pursuing a "small but beautiful" knowledge-livestream path — small in scale, but not cash-burning; its annual loss is contained to the tens of millions of dollars, not seriously eroding the cash reserve. Charles Zhang's physics-lecture IP genuinely created a distinctive brand awareness for Sohu.

**This counterargument holds up**: Sohu Video's losses are manageable — it's not a "bottomless pit." The problem isn't how much it burns; it's that **it doesn't grow** — a non-growing business creates no value, it only consumes resources.

## III. Two Important Assets Already Divested

### Sogou (Sold to Tencent in 2021)

| Dimension | Detail |
|------|------|
| Sale date | September 2021 |
| Sale price | **$1.18 billion** |
| Buyer | Tencent |
| Sohu's proceeds | ~$670 million (based on ownership stake) |
| Timing assessment | Reasonable — the search business was being squeezed by WeChat Search, Baidu, and ByteDance's products |

Sogou was once Sohu's most technically substantial asset (Sogou Input Method, Sogou Search). But under Baidu's dominant position in search and the pressure from ByteDance's products, Sogou's room for independent development was already narrow. Selling to Tencent for $1.18 billion was reasonably well timed.

**But the key question is**: Sogou sold for $670 million and Changyou was bought back — where did that money go afterward? The answer is that most of it ended up sitting on the balance sheet as part of today's $1.2 billion cash pile. Management didn't put that money to any value-creating reinvestment.

### Changyou Privatization (2020)

| Dimension | Detail |
|------|------|
| Completion date | April 2020 |
| Acquisition price | $10.80/ADS, roughly a **68%** premium |
| Total consideration | ~$579 million |
| Purpose | Sohu's full ownership control of Changyou |

The Changyou privatization was **the most successful capital operation Charles Zhang has ever executed**: he bought back his most profitable subsidiary at a reasonable premium, avoided the cost of maintaining a separate listing, and gave Changyou's minority shareholders a decent exit price.

**The significance of this deal**: it proves Zhang has the capability and experience to execute a privatization — and Sohu's own net-cash discount today is even more extreme than Changyou's was back then. This is an important reference point for the later discussion of Sohu's own privatization possibility.

## IV. Business Map Summary: A "Shrinking Map"

Mapping out Sohu's business evolution over the past 10 years:

```
Sohu in 2015
├── Sohu Portal (news)             → 2026: marginalized
├── Sohu Video (long-form video)   → 2026: MAU <20M, effectively exited
├── Sogou (search + input method)  → 2021: sold to Tencent for $1.18 billion
├── Changyou (gaming, independently listed) → 2020: privatized, delisted, brought back in-house
└── Sohu Focus (real estate)       → long since marginalized

Sohu in 2026
├── Changyou gaming (Tian Long Ba Bu IP)  → 86.6% of revenue, profitable but the IP is aging
├── Portal + video advertising             → 10.3% of revenue, -18% per year
├── Charles Zhang's personal brand         → physics lectures, not monetized
└── Cash pool                              → ~$1.2 billion, sitting quietly
```

**Over the past 10 years, Sohu has sold everything that could be sold (Sogou) and bought back everything that could be bought back (Changyou); what's left in hand is one aging game IP, one shrinking advertising business, one non-monetized CEO personal livestream, and a large pile of cash.**

This is "the business map after Sogou's sale" — a continuously shrinking map, whose final remaining core asset is not a business, but cash.

## V. A Key Question: What Is Changyou Actually Worth?

This question matters, because Changyou is Sohu's only business still making money.

A rough estimate:

| Method | Changyou valuation | Logic |
|------|---------|------|
| Operating profit × multiple | $237 million × 5-8x = **$1.19-1.9 billion** | Game companies typically get 5-10x operating profit |
| Revenue × multiple | $506 million × 1.5-3x = **$760 million-$1.52 billion** | Reference P/S for small-to-mid-size game companies |
| Reference: Changyou privatization valuation | 2020 total consideration ~**$579 million** | Source: Changyou privatization announcement |

**Taking a conservative median: Changyou's reasonable valuation is roughly $800 million-$1.2 billion.** (Detailed analysis in Part 03)

Then:

| Item | Valuation |
|------|------|
| Changyou gaming | $800 million-$1.2 billion |
| Net cash | $1.15 billion |
| Media/video business | ~$0 (ongoing losses) |
| **Sohu's reasonable total value** | **$1.95-2.35 billion** |
| Current market cap | **$360 million** |
| **Discount magnitude** | **~82-85%** |

**Sohu's market cap is only about 15-18% of its reasonable value.** This degree of discount would be nearly impossible to see in a normally functioning capital market.

**But it has, in fact, appeared — which means the market is pricing certain factors in an extreme way.** What are those "certain factors"? The next part expands on this when discussing implied asset value, and the fourth part gives the full answer when discussing risk.
