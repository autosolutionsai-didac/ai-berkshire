## Doing the Math

Of the $1.2 billion in cash on the books, how much actually "belongs to shareholders"? That's the core question for understanding Sohu's valuation. The VIE structure, the cost of moving onshore cash offshore, ongoing operating losses — each factor takes a discount. This piece quantifies each discount, then sums the parts to arrive at as conservative an estimate range as possible.

Sohu isn't in the first two categories, but the third is worth discussing (covered in the next part).

## II. The "Discounts" on the $1.2 Billion Cash Pile

$1.2 billion sounds like a lot, but it can't all be counted at face value. Here are the factors that warrant a discount:

### 2.1 Cost of Moving Onshore Cash Offshore

The vast majority of Sohu's cash sits in RMB inside VIE entities within mainland China. Getting that cash to the Cayman Islands-incorporated listed entity — and from there to shareholders — requires the following path:

```
VIE entity → dividend to WFOE → remitted to HK/Cayman → dividend to shareholders
```

Every step carries a cost:
- **Withholding tax**: dividends from VIE profits to an offshore entity are subject to a **10%** withholding tax
- **Foreign exchange controls**: requires approval from China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange, with quota limits on large outbound remittances
- **Time cost**: the whole process can take months or longer

### 2.2 The Drain of Ongoing Operating Losses

Sohu's media/video business loses money every year, and while Changyou is profitable, after deducting parent-level management expenses, overall Non-GAAP results are still a loss:

| Year | Non-GAAP net loss |
|------|----------------|
| 2023 | -$66 million |
| 2024 | -$83 million |
| 2025 | -$51 million |

Source: Sohu annual reports, various years

**The good news is the loss is narrowing** (down from $83 million to $51 million, a 40% improvement), but it's still a loss. At a burn rate of roughly $50 million per year, **$1.2 billion in cash could theoretically last 24 years** — timing isn't the pressing issue.

**But flip it around**: if the company loses $50 million a year and buys back/pays out $100-200 million, cash would fall from $1.2 billion to $400-500 million within 5 years — the discount advantage would be eroded by time.

### 2.3 Estimating Adjusted "Distributable Net Cash"

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Book net cash | $1.15 billion | |
| Less: withholding tax (10%) | -$115 million | Paid on outbound dividends |
| Less: 3 years of operating loss reserve | -$150 million | At ~$50 million/year average |
| Less: privatization/liquidation transaction costs | -$50 million | Lawyers, banks, SEC compliance |
| **Adjusted distributable net cash** | **~$840 million** | |
| **Adjusted net cash per share** | **~$32** | On 26M ADS |
| **Current share price / adjusted net cash per share** | **~44%** | Still less than half |

**Even after the most conservative adjustments, Sohu's share price is still only 44% of adjusted net cash per share.** That's still an extreme discount.

## III. Changyou Gaming's Implied Value

### 3.1 Changyou's "Implied Valuation" Within Sohu's Market Cap

Sohu's market cap = Changyou's value + media/video value + net cash - discount

Working backward:

| Assumption | Implied figure |
|------|------|
| Sohu market cap | $360 million |
| Less: net cash | -$1.15 billion |
| **Implied valuation of Changyou + media** | **-$790 million** |

**The market's implied valuation of Sohu's operating businesses is negative $790 million.** In other words: the market doesn't just think Changyou is worthless — it thinks these businesses are a "liability" that will burn cash and drag down value.

### 3.2 What Is Changyou Worth Standing Alone?

| Valuation method | Valuation range | Logic |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Operating profit multiple** | $1.19-1.9 billion | $237 million operating profit × 5-8x |
| **Revenue multiple** | $760 million-$1.52 billion | $506 million revenue × 1.5-3x |
| **Changyou privatization reference** | ~$579 million | 2020 total consideration, source: Changyou privatization announcement |
| **A "lifespan discount" for TLBB** | $500 million-$1 billion | If the IP has 5-7 years of strong monetization left |

Source: Changyou privatization announcement, Sohu financial reports, comparable industry companies

**Reasonable median valuation: $800 million-$1.2 billion** (note: the $579 million privatization reference was the consideration paid for the minority stake at the time, and does not represent a ceiling on Changyou's overall valuation)

**Counterargument**: TLBB is a 2007-vintage PC game IP with declining MAU and shouldn't be given too high a multiple. Under "declining-stage game company" standards, 3-5x operating profit would be more appropriate, implying $710 million-$1.19 billion.

**Counter-counterargument**: Changyou's 47% operating margin is very healthy, and the 2025 new TLBB Return version proves the IP still has the ability to be refreshed — it shouldn't be valued as if it were "dying."

On balance, **$800 million-$1.2 billion is a range both sides can accept**.

### 4.2 The Sohu Brand + the sohu.com Domain

As one of China's earliest internet brands, "Sohu" still carries some residual brand value. The sohu.com domain itself also has value.

Rough estimate: brand + domain together are worth **$50-200 million**. But this kind of intangible asset is hard to monetize on its own, absent an acquisition.

### 4.3 Interest Income

The $1.2 billion cash pile, invested at a 3-4% annualized yield, generates roughly **$36-48 million** in interest income per year. This is "hidden income" — not part of the core business, but it genuinely subsidizes the losses.

## VI. Why Is the Discount So Extreme?

If Sohu's asset value far exceeds its market cap, why doesn't the market price it reasonably?

The answer is: **it's not that the market doesn't know Sohu has cash — it's that the market doesn't believe that cash can reach shareholders.**

There are four root causes of the discount:

| Reason | Weight | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| **1. VIE structure + onshore cash lock-up** | High | Most of the $1.2 billion sits in mainland China; remitting dividends out involves taxes and controls |
| **2. Management discount** | High | Charles Zhang has historically not prioritized shareholder returns, and his strategic instincts are weak |
| **3. No business growth** | Medium | All core businesses are shrinking, with no "story" to attract capital |
| **4. Small-cap China-concept-stock predicament** | Medium | Low average daily trading volume, dropped from major ETFs, institutions can't build positions |

**Counterargument (the bull case)**: In 2025, Sohu completed a $150 million buyback program — management is already changing. If buybacks continue, the discount should narrow over time.

**This counterargument carries weight** — in fact, the 2025 buyback is Sohu's most important positive development in recent years. A $150 million buyback equal to 30-40% of market cap sends a strong signal. But the key question is: **is this a one-off, or an ongoing policy?** As of now, Sohu has not announced a new buyback program.

## VII. Liquidation Scenario: If Sohu Shut Down Today, How Much Would Shareholders Get Back?

This is an extreme thought experiment, but it's useful for understanding Sohu's "floor price":

| Step | Amount |
|------|------|
| Realize net cash | $1.15 billion |
| Less: withholding tax 10% | -$115 million |
| Realize Changyou (sold at a discount) | $500-800 million |
| Realize real estate + brand | $100-200 million |
| Less: liquidation costs (lawyers + banks + severance) | -$100-200 million |
| Less: reserve for contingent liabilities | -$50 million |
| **Total distributable to shareholders** | **$1.4-1.8 billion** |
| Liquidation value per share (26M ADS) | **$54-69** |
| Current share price | **~$14** |
| **Share price / liquidation value** | **20-26%** |

**The current share price is only 20-26% of liquidation value.**

Of course, liquidation won't happen — Charles Zhang isn't going to voluntarily shut Sohu down. But this number illustrates one thing: **even if you're extremely pessimistic about Sohu's business prospects, its assets still far exceed its market cap.**

## VIII. Core Conclusions of This Part

1. **Sohu's net cash on the books is about $1.15 billion, 3.2x its market cap.** Even after deducting outbound taxes, loss reserves, and transaction costs, adjusted net cash is still about $840 million — 2.3x market cap.

2. **Changyou gaming's standalone valuation is about $800 million-$1.2 billion**, but the market's implied valuation is negative. That means the market considers the business a "liability" rather than an "asset."

3. **The sum-of-the-parts reasonable valuation is $1.4-2.2 billion, versus a market cap of only $360 million — a 75-83% discount.** This degree of discount is extremely rare among globally listed companies.

4. **The root cause of the discount isn't "the market doesn't know" — it's "the market doesn't believe the cash can reach shareholders."** The VIE structure, management history, lack of business growth, and small-cap China-concept-stock liquidity troubles — all four factors compound each other.

5. **The 2025 $150 million buyback is a key positive signal**, but it needs to be sustained to truly change the discount.
