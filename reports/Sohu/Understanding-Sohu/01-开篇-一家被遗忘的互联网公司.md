## A Set of Numbers That Leave You Puzzled

Sohu listed on the Nasdaq in 2000, and 26 years later, there's a rift between the company's actual state and any normal logic that could explain it. It's sitting on $1.2 billion in cash, yet its market cap is only $360 million. Spend one dollar, and you get $3.20 of net cash on the books in return — the market's valuation of the operating business is negative.

This isn't a typo. Here is the real data as of May 2026:

| Metric | Latest figure | Note |
|------|----------|------|
| Current share price (May 2026) | **approx. $14** | Source: Yahoo Finance |
| Current market cap | **approx. $360M** | Approx. 26M ADS outstanding |
| Cash + short-term investments + long-term time deposits on the books | **approx. $1.2B** | Source: 2025 annual report |
| Net cash (after deducting all liabilities) | **approx. $1.16B** | Interest-bearing debt only $36M |
| Net cash / market cap | **approx. 3.2x** | Cash on the books is more than 3x the market cap |
| Enterprise value (EV) | **approx. -$800M** | Negative — the market is effectively paying you to take the business off its hands |
| Full-year 2025 revenue | **$584M** (-2.4%) | Cumulative shrinkage of about 30% over 5 years |
| 2025 Non-GAAP net loss | **-$51M** | The true loss after stripping out a one-time tax reversal |
| P/B | **0.32x** | Trading at 32% of book value |

**The market isn't just valuing the business at zero — it's paying you an extra $800 million to "take it off its hands." That's an extreme case even by global capital-market standards.**

But if you think this is "free money" — you probably haven't fully understood it.
And if you think this is a "value trap" — you may not have fully understood it either.

## What Exactly Is Sohu Today?

Most people's impression of Sohu is still stuck in the era of the "big three portals." But the Sohu of 2026 is a completely different company from the one people remember.

### Revenue Composition (2025 annual report, source: PR Newswire)

| Business segment | 2025 revenue | YoY | Share | True nature |
|---------|-----------|------|------|---------|
| Online games (Changyou) | **$506M** | +1% | **86.6%** | Cash cow, riding the Tian Long Ba Bu IP |
| Marketing services (advertising) | $60M | **-18%** | 10.3% | In persistent decline |
| Other | $18M | — | 3.1% | Negligible |

**The truth is: nearly 90% of Sohu's revenue comes from its gaming subsidiary, Changyou, whose core is a single IP — *Tian Long Ba Bu* (Demi-Gods and Semi-Devils), which launched in 2007.**

The portal website? Long since marginalized. Sohu Video? Fewer than 20 million monthly active users, a rounding error next to iQiyi. Sogou? Sold to Tencent for $1.18 billion in 2021.

So the real picture of Sohu today is:

> **Sohu = a mid-sized gaming company wearing a portal's clothing + a $1.2 billion cash management fund + Charles Zhang's personal-IP livestreaming studio.**

Don't let the four words "internet portal" mislead you. Sohu and the company you imagine are no longer the same thing.

## Three Common Misconceptions About Sohu

### Misconception One: Sohu Is Still One of the "Big Three Portals"

Around 2000, Sina, Sohu, and NetEase were known as China's big three internet portals. But 25 years later, the three companies' fates have diverged dramatically:

| Company | 2000 positioning | 2026 status | Current market cap |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **NetEase** | One of the portals | Transformed into a gaming empire (Fantasy Westward Journey, Egg Party) | **approx. $70B** |
| **Sina** | The portal king | Went private and delisted in 2020, now living within Weibo | Delisted |
| **Sohu** | One of the portals | Portal shrinking, gaming living off past glory, video marginalized | **approx. $360M** |

**NetEase and Sohu started from almost identical positions — founded the same year, both portal-born, both listed overseas. 25 years later, the market-cap gap is roughly 194x.** (Source: each company's public financial reports, Yahoo Finance)

The core difference lies in this: NetEase found gaming as a trillion-yuan-scale second curve, while Sohu tried three second curves — Sogou, video, Changyou — all of which either failed or were spun off.

**Counter-argument**: although Sohu's market cap is small, its on-balance-sheet cash far exceeds its market cap, and its net assets are solid — from an asset perspective, it isn't a "failure." This view has merit, but it points to liquidation value, not operating value — and those are fundamentally different things.

### Misconception Two: Charles Zhang's Physics Lectures Can Save Sohu

Since 2021, Charles Zhang has livestreamed physics lectures (quantum mechanics, relativity) weekly on Sohu Video, unexpectedly going viral. As of the end of 2025, he had delivered a cumulative **240+ lectures, 24,000 minutes of content**, with about **15 million** followers network-wide, and has also published a companion book. (Source: China National Radio, China Science Communication)

The 2025-2026 New Year's Eve keynote theme was "the Solar System," with a striking hardcore-science-popularization effect.

**But the problem is: the hotter the physics lectures get, the more Sohu's ad revenue shrinks.**

Marketing services revenue fell **18%** YoY in 2025. Charles Zhang's personal IP traffic has not effectively converted into commercial monetization for Sohu.

More fatal still: Charles Zhang's IP is a **personal asset**, not a company asset. Fans follow "Charles Zhang" the person, not the "Sohu" platform. Once Charles Zhang (62 this year) stops broadcasting, Sohu will lose its last shred of public awareness.

**Counter-argument**: Charles Zhang's IP has brought differentiation to the Sohu brand; although knowledge-marketing CPMs are low, brand recall is 23 percentage points higher (source: Sohu public materials), suggesting room for brand premium over the long run. This has some merit, but the scale is simply too small — within China's **RMB 850 billion** internet advertising market, Sohu's share is already below six-hundredths of a percent (source: industry research estimate). A personal IP can't move an advertising business that's already down at the level of a statistical rounding error.

### Misconception Three: $1.2 Billion in Cash Equals a Safety Cushion

This is the bulls' most commonly used argument: $1.2 billion on the books against a market cap of just $360 million — isn't that "buying a dollar for forty cents"?

The math is correct. But this argument carries a hidden premise: **the cash has to be able to reach shareholders.**

Sohu's situation is:

- Most of the cash sits in RMB within a domestic China VIE structure
- Repatriating dividends is subject to foreign-exchange controls plus a 10% withholding tax
- Sohu has **historically not paid dividends** for a long stretch
- Non-GAAP losses were still $51M in 2025 — the business is consuming cash

**But there is also a positive development**: Sohu completed a **$150 million share buyback program** in 2025 (as of February 2026, it had repurchased about 8.1 million ADS at a cost of roughly $106 million). This is an important signal of management shifting from "silence" to "returning capital to shareholders." (Source: Sohu 2025 Q4 earnings report)

**But the counter to the counter also needs to be said**: while the buyback amount as a share of market cap is high (roughly 25-30%), suggesting management believes the shares are undervalued, the question remains — is the buyback a one-time act or a sustained policy? If it doesn't become a long-term mechanism, the discount won't disappear.

## What Is Sohu's Fundamental Problem?

Having gone through the data above, Sohu's core contradiction is now clear:

**On one side: an extremely cheap valuation + cash reserves far exceeding its market cap + management beginning to buy back stock.**
**On the other side: all core businesses are in structural decline + the flagship IP has an expiration date + the 62-year-old founder has no successor.**

This isn't a company "waiting to be revived." It's a company that needs to answer the question, "what is the endgame?"

There are three possible endgames:

| Endgame | Probability assessment | Impact on shareholders |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Going private / delisting** | Fairly high (see the Changyou and Sina precedents) | Depends on the premium offered; retail shareholders could be cashed out at a low price |
| **Continued buybacks + dividends unlocking value** | Moderate | The discount narrows gradually, but requires patience |
| **Status quo maintained ("the walking dead")** | Fairly high | Business losses erode cash every year, and the discount never closes |

**Different endgame assumptions lead to completely different investment conclusions.** That's exactly the question the next three parts will address.
