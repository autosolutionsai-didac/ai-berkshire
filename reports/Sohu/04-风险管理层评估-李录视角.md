## I. Deep assessment of Charles Zhang (the core issue)

### 1.1 Personal background and career trajectory

Charles Zhang (Zhang Chaoyang), born in 1964 (now 62), holds a bachelor's degree in physics from Tsinghua and a PhD in physics from MIT (1993). He returned to China in 1995 and founded ITC (Sohu's predecessor) in 1996, formally renaming the company Sohu in 1998.

**Three phases of his career evolution**:

| Phase | Period | Image |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | 2000-2008 | Godfather of China's "Big Four portals," briefly China's richest man, rang the Nasdaq bell |
| Dormant | 2010-2018 | Missed the mobile internet wave; Sohu's market cap evaporated 90% from its peak of $5 billion |
| Rebirth | 2019-present | A "livestream celebrity CEO" — the "Physics Class" livestream IP, running, English study, knowledge sharing |

### 1.2 Assessment of strategic vision (**Li Lu's core dimension**)

What Li Lu values most is a CEO's "long-term compounding ability" and "ability to seize opportunities." Over the past 20 years, Zhang has **systematically missed nearly every major wave in Chinese internet**:

| Wave | What was missed | Benchmark company |
|---|---|---|
| E-commerce (2003-2010) | Completely absent | Alibaba |
| Mobile social (2011-2014) | Weibo launched later than Sina's version; failed to incubate an IM product | Tencent/Weibo |
| Search (2004-2013) | Sogou did get built, but was ultimately sold to Tencent | Baidu |
| Short-form video (2016-2020) | Completely absent | ByteDance/Kuaishou |
| Livestream e-commerce (2019-2024) | Zhang livestreams personally, purely for brand IP, with no monetization | Douyin/Kuaishou |
| Long-form video (2014-2020) | Sohu Video went from leader to laggard | iQiyi/Tencent Video/Bilibili |
| Large AI models (2023-2026) | Completely absent | Baidu/Alibaba/ByteDance/Zhipu |

**Li Lu-style judgment**: This amounts to "an almost 100%-hit-rate contrarian indicator." Zhang's strategic instincts rate a **D grade**.

### 1.3 Assessment of capital-allocation ability

Capital allocation is one of the top dimensions Buffett and Li Lu use to evaluate a CEO. Zhang's capital-allocation record:

**Positive record**:
- **Sogou divestiture (2021)**: sold to Tencent for $1.18 billion, reasonable timing (the search business was already being squeezed by WeChat Search, Baidu, and ByteDance)
- **Changyou take-private (2020)**: taken private for $579 million at roughly a 69% premium, reasonably fair to minority shareholders
- **Share buybacks**: Sohu bought back roughly $150 million cumulatively from 2020-2024

**Negative record**:
- **Failed to take Sohu Video public separately** (missed the window when iQiyi listed independently)
- **Overly conservative cash management**: has held $1 billion+ in cash on the balance sheet for years, without a major buyback program, dividend, or acquisition
- **Missed the reinvestment window for the proceeds from Sogou and Changyou** (the 2021-2023 AI/new-energy/overseas-expansion opportunity window)

**Li Lu-style judgment**: Capital allocation rates a **C grade**. **Core problem: over $1 billion in cash sits on the balance sheet (close to the current market cap), but the CEO lacks the ability to deploy it, so the money just sits idle.**

### 1.4 Assessment of willingness to step back

Comparing the "stepping back from the front line" paths of Chinese internet founders:

| Founder | When they stepped back | Succession arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Jack Ma (Alibaba) | 2019 | Daniel Zhang → Joe Tsai/Eddie Wu |
| Zhang Yiming (ByteDance) | 2021 | Liang Rubo |
| Wang Xing (Meituan) | Partial delegation | Wang Huiwen (has since departed)/Mu Rongjun |
| Colin Huang (Pinduoduo) | 2021 | Chen Lei |
| **Charles Zhang (Sohu)** | **Never** | **No clear successor** |

Zhang, 62, still appears on camera daily as livestreamer, physics-class host, and hands-on CEO. **There is no sign whatsoever of successor development.** In Li Lu's "100-year institution" framework, this is a **major deduction**.

### 1.5 Integrity assessment

- No SEC investigation record
- No major financial scandal
- Was criticized for "Olympics marketing" in 2008, but that was a business decision, not a scandal
- Relatively low-profile personal life, with few divorce/relationship controversies

**Integrity rating: B+**. This is Zhang's biggest strength — **he doesn't steal, cheat, or falsify.**

### 1.6 Overall rating for Charles Zhang: **C grade**

**Scoring logic**: integrity earns credit (B+), but strategic instincts are extremely poor (D), capital allocation is mediocre (C), and there is no succession plan (D). The final composite rating is **C grade**. **Li Lu would very likely not invest behind this kind of founder.**

## II. Charles Zhang's dual identity as "personal IP + CEO"

### 2.1 The actual business impact of the livestream and physics-class content

Zhang's "Physics Class" livestream has run since 2021, has accumulated over 100 million views, and has roughly 15 million followers across Bilibili, Douyin, and WeChat Video Accounts combined. But **its impact on Sohu's financials is close to zero**:

- The physics-class content has no monetization (no product sales, no advertising)
- Sohu Video's daily active users have not risen meaningfully as a result
- Zhang's personal IP traffic has not converted into Sohu media advertising revenue

**Conclusion**: Zhang's personal IP is a "**social-image repair project**," not a "**business growth engine**."

### 2.2 Does the personal IP hold the company hostage?

**Yes, deeply so.**
- Roughly 20-30% of Sohu's current stock price reflects a "Charles Zhang premium" (the market believes "as long as Zhang is here, the company is here")
- Should Zhang exit due to health or other reasons, Sohu's stock would likely fall another 30-50%
- **This is a textbook case of "key man risk"**

### 2.3 What would be left of Sohu if Zhang retired?

| Asset | Value |
|---|---|
| Net cash | ~$1 billion (the core value) |
| Sohu Media (portal) | <$100 million (already left behind by the times) |
| Sohu Video | $200-300 million (persistently loss-making) |
| The Sohu.com domain | $100-200 million (residual brand value) |
| **Total** | **~$1.4-1.6 billion** |

Current market cap is roughly $800 million-$1 billion. **Liquidation value exceeds market cap** — this is the so-called "cigar-butt stock," but **a cigar-butt stock with no catalyst is just a value trap.**

## III. Ownership structure and governance

### 3.1 Major shareholder structure (as of the latest 2026 disclosure)

- **Charles Zhang**: holds roughly **25-26%** (including options), the clear controlling shareholder
- **Institutional shareholders**: BlackRock, Vanguard, and other passive index funds together hold roughly 15%
- **Public float**: roughly 60%
- **Dual-class structure**: Sohu **does not** have a dual-class share structure (unlike Alibaba/JD.com/Baidu)
- **VIE structure**: Yes, a standard Chinese-ADR VIE (Sohu's entity is registered in the Cayman Islands)

### 3.2 Board composition

- Zhang holds both the Chairman and CEO roles (a governance red flag — a deduction in Li Lu's framework)
- The number of independent directors meets Nasdaq requirements, but lacks any high-profile independent directors
- **No publicly disclosed "succession committee"**

### 3.3 Governance rating: **C grade**

Having no dual-class structure is a plus, but the combined Chairman+CEO role, lack of a succession plan, and weak independent board are clear shortcomings.

## V. Deep dive on take-private probability (**the core issue**)

### 5.1 Historical precedents for Chinese-portal take-privates

| Company | Take-private date | Price | Premium | Lead party |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sina** | September 2020 | $43.30/share | Roughly 18% | Charles Chao's MBO |
| **Changyou** | April 2020 | $10.80/share | Roughly 69% | Led by Sohu |
| **Renren** | 2018 | Asset divestiture | - | Joseph Chen |
| **Dangdang** | 2016 | $6.70/ADS | Roughly 8% | Li Guoqing/Yu Yu |

### 5.2 Financial feasibility of taking Sohu private

**Extremely high.**
- Current market cap: roughly $800 million-$1 billion
- Net cash on the balance sheet: roughly $1 billion
- **In theory, Zhang could "buy Sohu using Sohu's own money"**
- Adding a 30% premium, total acquisition consideration would be roughly $1.3 billion; Zhang already holds 25%, so actual financing needed would be roughly $1 billion — **entirely manageable**

### 5.3 Does Zhang have an incentive to take the company private?

**Yes, and the incentive is growing stronger**:

**Factors favoring take-private**:
1. The cost of maintaining the listing (audit/compliance/IR) runs roughly $5-10 million a year
2. Chronic undervaluation in the secondary market (P/B < 0.6)
3. Zhang has already executed a Changyou take-private, **so he has proven experience**
4. US-China uncertainty adds to the appeal of the delisting option
5. Taking the company private removes quarterly-earnings pressure, letting Zhang focus on the IP business

**Factors against take-private**:
1. Zhang **enjoys the status that comes with being a public-company CEO**
2. Part of his livestream/physics-class influence relies on the "public-company CEO" label
3. Taking the company private carries an opportunity cost (what if the AI pivot succeeds?)

**Li Lu-style judgment**: **Probability of take-private is 60-70%, most likely within the next 3-5 years.**

## VI. Long-term certainty assessment

### 6.1 Will Sohu still exist in 10 years?

**As a commercial entity — most likely not, or it will have already been taken private.**
- Media business: most likely shut down or divested within 5 years
- Video business: most likely sold or shut down within 5 years
- Cash assets: will either be paid out as dividends, used in a take-private, or the company will be acquired

### 6.2 Forces disrupting Sohu's business model

Sohu has already been mostly disrupted. The remaining disruptive pressures:
- AI's fundamental reshaping of search/content (already happening)
- Short-form video's continued displacement of long-form video
- Web3/metaverse further marginalizing portal media

### 6.3 Predicted timeline for Zhang's retirement

- **2026-2028**: Continues as CEO + personal-IP figure; will not retire
- **2028-2030**: Around age 65, may initiate a take-private
- **After 2030**: After going private, runs a family office plus personal IP; the Sohu entity most likely disappears

## VIII. Overall risk rating and Top 3 risks

### 8.1 Overall investment risk rating: **C grade (leaning C-)**

**Rating logic**:
- Financial risk is low (ample cash) → B+
- Governance risk is moderate (no dual-class structure, but CEO holds both top roles) → C
- Strategic risk is extremely high (management has no ability to break through) → D
- Take-private risk / delisting risk is high → C-
- **Overall: C grade, leaning C-**

### 8.2 Top 3 core risks

#### **Top 1: Take-private risk (High)**
- Zhang will most likely take Sohu private at a **limited premium** within 3-5 years (referencing Changyou's 69% and Sina's 18%)
- Secondary-market investors could be forced out at a low valuation
- This is the **biggest "ceiling on the upside"** — even if you correctly identify the liquidation value, you may never actually capture it

#### **Top 2: Founder personal-IP hostage risk (High)**
- Zhang is 62, has no successor, and his personal health/attention is the company's invisible ceiling
- Should Zhang "disappear" (health, retirement, shifted attention), the stock would likely sell off again
- **This is a textbook case of "key man risk"**

#### **Top 3: Continued business decline plus management's inability to break through (High)**
- Media, video, and gaming — all three core businesses — are in structural decline
- Zhang's strategic track record over the past 20 years proves he **lacks a second-act capability**
- Cash will be consumed at a rate of roughly $50-100 million a year, and **the liquidation-value advantage disappears in 5-7 years**

## IX. Final conclusion (Li Lu's lens)

> "**Sohu is a clean, cheap, small company in a declining industry, run by an honest but mediocre CEO. It does not fit any characteristic of a great business. Its 'cheapness' is precisely priced — the market has already priced in the take-private discount, the management discount, and the industry discount. This is a textbook case of something that looks safe but is actually dangerous — a value trap.**"

**Recommended action** (if action must be taken):
- **Do not recommend holding long-term** (no compounding, and there is take-private tail risk)
- Only suitable for **event-driven arbitrage** (betting on a take-private premium)
- Keep the position strictly under **5% of a single stock**
- A stop-loss must be set (consider exiting if the price falls below 70% of net cash before a take-private plan is announced)

**Would Li Lu buy it? Answer: 100% no.**
