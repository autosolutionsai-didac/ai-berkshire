# Sohu (NASDAQ: SOHU) Business Model Deep Dive (Duan Yongping's Lens)

> Analyst: business-analyst | Date: 2026-04-26 | Data confidence: 🟢 High / 🟡 Medium / 🔴 Low

---

## I. The Truth About the Business Mix: A Company That's Been Mis-Categorized

### 1.1 Full-Year 2025 Revenue Composition (🟢 High)

| Business segment | 2025 revenue | YoY | Share | Nature |
|---------|---------|-----|------|------|
| Online games (Changyou) | $506M | +1% | **86.6%** | Cash cow, standalone subsidiary |
| Marketing services (media advertising) | $60M | **-18%** | 10.3% | In persistent decline, cash-burning |
| Other | $18M | - | 3.1% | Marginal |
| **Total** | **$584M** | -2% | 100% | - |

### 1.2 Key Inflection-Point Data (🟢 High)

- **2025 GAAP net income of $394M** — but this is a "phantom profit": it comes from a one-time gain from the reversal of $285M in withholding tax accruals at Changyou. Stripping that out, **Non-GAAP loss was $51M** (versus a 2024 loss of $83M, a 40% narrowing of losses)
- **Games' share of revenue jumped to 90% in Q3 2025**: TLBB Return (*Tian Long Ba Bu: Return*) launched in July 2025, with single-quarter gaming revenue of $162M, up 27% YoY
- **Extremely high dependence on TLBB**: TLBB PC + Legacy TLBB Mobile contribute **60% of total revenue and 69% of gaming revenue** (source: 20-F risk factors section). This means a single IP holds the entire company hostage

### 1.3 What's Making Money, What's Burning It (🟢 High)

- **Making money**: Changyou's gaming segment generated roughly $237M in operating profit in 2025 (47% operating margin)
- **Burning money**: Sohu's media segment (video + advertising + portal) remains unprofitable, propped up by Changyou's "subsidy for retirement"
- **Neutral**: Cash management income (net cash of $930M+, at 3-4% annual yield that alone contributes $30M+)

> **Duan Yongping's first question**: "What business is this company actually in?" Answer: **Sohu is, in essence, a mid-sized gaming company wearing a portal's clothing, plus a billion-dollar-class cash management fund**; the media business is the founder's "personally subsidized hobby project."

## II. Defining the Core Business: A "Portal in Name Only" With a Split Identity

**The real picture**:

> Sohu (SOHU) = Changyou (contributing 90% of revenue, its life tied to the TLBB IP's lifespan) + a content/media business driven by Charles Zhang's personal IP (weak traffic, advertisers fleeing, kept alive on the founder's life support) + ~$930M in net cash (equal to **160%+** of market cap).

**Revenue model**:
1. **In-game purchases** (the mainstay, with paying-user ARPU heavily concentrated among TLBB veteran players)
2. **Brand advertising / marketing services** (in persistent decline, as the industry's top 10 players already hold 96% of the market, squeezing Sohu out) 🟢 High
3. **Membership / content licensing** (video business, negligible scale) 🟡 Medium
4. **Interest income** (a hidden cash cow)

**Duan Yongping's interrogation**: "Will the 'business' you're describing still exist in 10 years?" Can this 2007-vintage PC-game IP, TLBB, really hold up for another 10 years? Almost certainly not — meaning Sohu's **true core business has an expiration date**.

## III. The Value of Charles Zhang's Personal IP: A Double-Edged Sword

### 3.1 State of the IP (🟢 High)
- **Physics lectures**: as of 2025, a cumulative **270+ livestreamed sessions, 26,000+ minutes**, plus a companion popular-science book that has been collected by the National Library
- **English lessons**: launched in 2016, a steady, long-running output
- **New Year's keynote**: the 2024-25 New Year's Eve address on "quantum mechanics" achieved significant crossover reach
- The 61-year-old CEO personally livestreams weekly, cementing his status as a network-wide science-popularization influencer

### 3.2 Actual Lift to the Business (🔴 Low - 🟡 Medium)
- **Positive**: it has driven a differentiated "interest-follow" community on Sohu Video (hanfu culture, K-pop, science content), creating a distinct label; it has given marketing services a "content marketing" differentiation angle for pricing
- **Negative evidence**: marketing services revenue fell 18% YoY in 2025 — **the hotter Charles Zhang's personal IP gets, the more Sohu's advertising shrinks** — proof that the IP's traffic has not translated into effective monetization
- **36Kr's sharp assessment**: "Charles Zhang loves playing the professor; Sohu Video is coasting tactically"

### 3.3 The Risk of the IP Decoupling From Company Value (🟢 High)

**This is the most consequential non-consensus point**:
- Charles Zhang's IP is a **personal asset**, not a company asset. The copyright to the physics lectures, publishing income, and personal fanbase all belong to Charles Zhang himself
- Once Charles Zhang stops broadcasting (health, retirement, or a career pivot), Sohu's media business has **no Plan B**
- This structure — "the founder is the company's only brand asset" — is exactly what Buffett and Duan Yongping would avoid. A good business is "one any fool can run"; Sohu is the opposite

> **Key judgment**: Charles Zhang's personal IP valuation ≠ Sohu's enterprise valuation. If the IP were valued at $500M, that $500M belongs to **Charles Zhang personally**, not to SOHU shareholders.

|------|------|------|
| **Brand** | ⭐⭐ (2/5) | The "Sohu" legacy brand still carries some recognition, but has lost all pricing power. Media advertisers are voting with their feet (-18%). Outside of Charles Zhang's personal IP, the Sohu parent brand has essentially become a "portal fossil" |

**Composite moat score: 1.6/5 (extremely weak)**

> Duan Yongping's standard: "Don't invest in what you don't understand. And for what you do understand, if you find it has no moat, invest in it even less." Sohu falls into the second category.

## V. Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Assessment

### 5.1 Differentiation (🔴 Low)
- Media: the differentiation angle is "knowledge livestreaming," but the niche is too narrow to scale, and it competes head-on with Xiaohongshu, Bilibili, and Douyin's education channels without any scale advantage
- Games: TLBB is a 2007 IP, differentiated by "veteran-player nostalgia," but nostalgia isn't a moat — it's an expiration date

### 5.2 Pricing Power (🔴 Extremely Low)
- Advertisers can flow to leading platforms at zero cost → no pricing power
- In-game payments rely on veteran-player stickiness, but 2025 MAU fell 27% YoY (mobile), indicating **the paying-user base is shrinking**, which further erodes pricing power
- No subscription-based cash flow of any kind (versus iQiyi membership, Tencent Video membership)

### 5.3 Sustainable Competitive Advantage (🔴)
- **Unsustainable**: the TLBB IP is aging, Charles Zhang's personal IP can't be inherited, and the advertising business is being crushed by giants
- The 2025 "turn to profit" was driven by a **one-time book gain from a tax reversal**, not by business improvement
- Q1 2026 gaming guidance: $123M-$130M (YoY -4% to +5%), meaning the TLBB Return windfall is about to normalize downward

### 5.4 The Key Counter-Question: What Would Happen If Sohu Shut Down Today?

**Hard conclusion**: **Almost no impact at all.**
- Users: would migrate to Douyin/WeChat/Tencent News/Bilibili at zero migration cost and zero loss of experience
- Advertisers: have already moved their budgets to ByteDance, Tencent, and Alibaba
- Industry: one fewer TLBB game server; veteran players would find substitutes at NetEase or Tencent
- Employees: talent would flow to leading internet companies
- **The only party affected**: Charles Zhang's personal physics livestream would lose its platform (but he could keep lecturing on any other platform)

> **Duan Yongping's judgment**: "If a company's disappearance would have zero impact on the world, it isn't a good business — no matter how much cash sits on its balance sheet."
> This counter-test effectively delivers a death sentence to Sohu's status as a "business" — **it is no longer a going concern; it's a liquidation asset.**

## VI. Possible Business Extensions / Transformations

### 6.1 Livestream Commerce / Knowledge Monetization (🟡 Medium)
- Charles Zhang has explicitly **rejected the livestream e-commerce route**, sticking to a "small but refined" knowledge-livestreaming path
- No public revenue disclosure for knowledge monetization currently; scale is negligible
- This is Charles Zhang's personal values-based choice, but from a business standpoint, it means voluntarily walking away from a trillion-yuan-class market

### 6.2 AI / Large-Model Positioning (🔴 Extremely Low)
- **No public disclosure of any proprietary large-model initiative** (zero search results) 🟢 High
- Compared with ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Tongyi, Tencent's Hunyuan, Baidu's Ernie, DeepSeek, and Moonshot AI, Sohu is **entirely absent from the AI table**
- As a physics PhD, Charles Zhang would seem one of the best-positioned CEOs to talk about AI, yet Sohu has been absent from the AI wave — confirming the company **lacks offensive capital-allocation capability**

### 6.3 The Hidden Value of Going Private (🟡 Medium-High)

**This is the most noteworthy non-consensus value point for Sohu**:
- **Market cap of $440-470M vs. net cash of $930M** — book cash is **2x** the market cap
- Charles Zhang's stake has risen to 26%+ (historical trend: 20% in 2016 → 26% in 2024)
- There is already precedent: Sogou's privatization (acquired by Tencent), Changyou's privatization (bought back by Sohu)
- 2024 buyback of 4.18M ADS costing $52M; a two-year, $80M buyback plan was announced in 2023
- Charles Zhang's most likely eventual path is **going private** — reclaiming control at a price below net cash

> **Duan Yongping's lens**: this is a "buy a dollar for fifty cents" cigar-butt stock, but only if you can actually collect that dollar. Sohu's path to cash distribution or a delisting cash-out is unclear, so the discount persists.

### Three Core Conclusions

**Conclusion 1: Sohu is no longer an "internet portal" — it is "a TLBB gaming company wearing a media costume, plus a cash management box"**
- The media business (10% of revenue) is Charles Zhang's personally funded hobby, continuously subsidized by the gaming business (87% of revenue) and cash interest income
- Don't be misled by the "internet portal" narrative — Sohu's comparability to Sina and NetEase has long since broken down; the only true comparable is late-stage Yahoo!: cash on the balance sheet, but a business beyond saving

**Conclusion 2: Duan Yongping's "good business" dimensions all fail across the board, but the Duan framework doesn't really apply here — Sohu follows liquidation logic, not operating logic**
- No differentiation, no pricing power, no sustainable advantage, and its disappearance would have zero impact on the world
- The real investment thesis is: **can the discount between $930M net cash and $470M market cap actually be realized?** This is not a Buffett/Duan-style good-business stock; it's a Graham-style cigar butt
- The key variable: will Charles Zhang go private? Will the pace of buybacks accelerate? What's the probability of a special dividend?

**Conclusion 3: Charles Zhang's personal IP is the company's largest "unrecognized liability" — it props up the valuation narrative while also creating decoupling risk**
- The hotter Charles Zhang's personal IP gets, the more awkward its contribution to Sohu's enterprise value becomes: it hasn't produced real monetization in advertising or paid content (marketing services down 18%), and it can't be transferred to shareholders
- Once Charles Zhang stops broadcasting, Sohu's media business would "instantly go to zero"; the more he invests in it personally, the more it actually proves Sohu **cannot exist without its founder** — this is an anti-moat

---

### Duan Yongping's Final Self-Interrogation

> **The "Stop Doing List" test**: what should Sohu stop doing? The answer is: shut down the media business, sell Changyou to NetEase or Tencent, return the cash to shareholders, then wind down. But Charles Zhang's personal values mean none of this will happen — that's a corporate-governance risk, and it's also the fundamental reason for the valuation discount.

> **Duan Yongping would not buy Sohu.** The reason is simple: what he buys is a good business + a good company + a good price. Sohu, at best, qualifies as a "bad business + a passion project + a cheap price" — one out of three criteria met, which doesn't clear the bar.
