## A set of numbers that will make you rethink Ping An

RMB 94 to RMB 36, then to RMB 74, then back down to RMB 58 — over the past five years, no blue-chip stock has torn investor perception apart quite like China Ping An.

- Early 2021: about **RMB 94** — the peak of the "insurance sector's Moutai" narrative, with a market cap of RMB 1.7 trillion
- October 2022: about **RMB 36** — a triple blow from the China Fortune Land Development (CFLD) blow-up, the property-sector collapse, and falling interest rates
- September 2024: about **RMB 56** — the earnings inflection confirmed, the market still half-convinced
- October 2025: about **RMB 74** — high NBV growth plus a capital-market rebound, a 52-week high
- May 2026: about **RMB 58** — Q1 net profit down 7.4%, the market hesitating again

**The same company has traded at 1.5x P/EV and at 0.4x P/EV — a pricing gap of nearly 4x.** This isn't market noise; it's a genuine disagreement — one rooted in how hard this company is to understand.

Open the 2025 annual report, and Ping An shows an extremely contradictory set of indicators:

| Metric | 2025 | What the scale tells you |
|------|---------|---------|
| Revenue | **RMB 1,050.5 billion** (+2.1%) | Averaging **RMB 2.88 billion** a day |
| Net profit attributable to shareholders | **RMB 134.8 billion** (+6.5%) | Positive growth for two straight years |
| Operating profit attributable to shareholders | **RMB 134.4 billion** (+10.3%) | Back to double-digit growth |
| Group embedded value | **RMB 1,504.3 billion** (+5.7%) | RMB 1.5 trillion in "book net worth" |
| New business value (NBV) | **RMB 36.9 billion** (+29.3%) | Nearly doubled in two years |
| Dividend per share | **RMB 2.70** (14 consecutive years of growth) | Total dividends of **RMB 48.9 billion** |
| Individual customers | **251 million** | 1 in every 5.5 Chinese people is a Ping An customer |
| Insurance funds under management | **RMB 6.49 trillion** | Larger than the GDP of many countries |
| **A-share P/EV** | **~0.65x** | Historically, P/EV below 0.8 has usually marked a major bottom |
| **A-share dividend yield** | **~4.8%** | Far above the 10-year government bond yield |

Sources: China Ping An 2025 annual report, Eastmoney, Stcn (China Securities Journal's Star Rating site)

Put all of this together — **a company that earns RMB 2.88 billion a day, holds RMB 1.5 trillion in embedded value, and has raised its dividend for 14 straight years, has a market cap of just over RMB 1 trillion and a P/EV of only 0.65x.**

If you think this is "severely undervalued" — you may be overlooking how falling interest rates are eroding that RMB 1.5 trillion of embedded value.
If you think this is a "value trap" — you may be overlooking what two straight years of 30% NBV growth actually means.

**Both views have merit, but neither is complete.** That is Ping An's contradiction.

## What exactly is Ping An?

Many people call Ping An an "insurance company." That label covers about 70% of Ping An — but the 30% it misses is precisely the key to understanding this company.

A more accurate definition is:

> **Ping An = life insurance (the profit core) + P&C insurance (the underwriting-profit engine) + banking (the stabilizer) + technology/health & elderly care (the future story), four business lines bound together through cross-selling to 251 million customers.**

**2025 operating data by segment:**

| Segment | Core metric | Operating profit growth | Profit contribution (estimate) |
|----------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| Life & health insurance | NBV of RMB 36.9 billion, +29.3% | +3% | ~55% |
| Property & casualty insurance | Insurance service revenue of RMB 338.9 billion, COR of **96.8%** | +13% | ~15% |
| Banking | Net profit attributable to shareholders -4% y/y | -4% | ~18% |
| Asset management | Losses narrowing | Losses narrowing | ~-3% |
| Technology/other | 8.18 billion large-model API calls | Turned from loss to profit | ~5% |

Sources: China Ping An 2025 annual report, Sina Finance, Stcn

**A few key facts:**

1. **Life insurance is the absolute core.** It contributes more than half of operating profit. Ping An's valuation is, in essence, the market pricing its life insurance business.
2. **P&C insurance is already profitable.** COR fell from 100.7% in 2023 to 96.8% — three straight years of improvement. Underwriting losses have turned into underwriting profits.
3. **Banking is a drag.** Ping An Bank's profit fell 4% in 2025, but Q1 2026 already delivered a "double increase" inflection — both revenue and net profit turned positive year-on-year.
4. **Technology is still telling a story.** 8.18 billion large-model API calls and 650+ deployed use cases, but the technology segment's contribution to profit remains small.

## Three common misconceptions about Ping An

### Misconception one: Ping An just sells insurance

This is the most common cognitive bias.

Ping An did start out in insurance, but today's Ping An is a **fully licensed, diversified financial conglomerate** — holding insurance, banking, securities, trust, and fund licenses simultaneously. That is nearly unique in China's financial industry.

More importantly, Ping An's business model isn't "sell insurance, collect premiums" — it's:

**Step one:** Acquire customers through insurance agents or bancassurance channels (251 million individual customers)
**Step two:** Cross-sell so customers open bank accounts, buy wealth-management products, and use Ping An Good Doctor
**Step three:** The more products a customer holds, the higher the retention (customers holding 3+ product categories have a **99%** retention rate)
**Step four:** Internal customer-acquisition costs run **35%-45%** lower than external ones

This "customer migration" path is Ping An's deepest moat — not insurance itself, but the diversified-financial-services ecosystem.

**The counterargument:** Cross-selling sounds good, but in practice the conversion rate of bank customers into insurance buyers isn't that high. How much overlap actually exists between Ping An Bank's retail customers and Ping An Life's customers? The annual report has never disclosed this in detail. And excessive cross-selling could draw regulatory attention — as data-privacy rules tighten, data-sharing across subsidiaries may face restrictions.

### Misconception two: RMB 1.5 trillion in embedded value = Ping An is worth RMB 1.5 trillion

Embedded value (EV) is an insurer's "book net assets, supercharged" — it equals adjusted net assets plus the value of in-force business. At the end of 2025, Ping An Group's EV stood at **RMB 1,504.3 billion**.

The current market cap is about **RMB 1.05 trillion**, implying a P/EV of about **0.65x**. That looks like "buying a dollar of assets for 65 cents."

But EV has one fatal precondition: **it rests on actuarial assumptions, especially the long-term investment-return assumption.**

In 2025, Ping An lowered EV's long-term investment-return assumption from 5% to **4.0%-4.5%**. If actual investment yields stay below this assumption for a long time — say, if falling rates push fixed-income yields below 3% — EV will "shrink."

In other words: **EV isn't a bank deposit; it's an estimate built on assumptions.** A 0.65x P/EV looks cheap, but if the assumptions are wrong, true EV could be 20%-30% lower than the book figure.

**The counter-counterargument:** Ping An has already cut its assumption from 5% to 4.0%-4.5%, the most conservative level in recent years. The actual comprehensive investment yield in 2025 was **6.3%**, far above the assumption. If the assumption is 4% and the actual result is 6.3%, then EV, if anything, is being **understated**.

### Misconception three: the China Fortune Land Development blow-up = Ping An can't invest

In 2021, China Fortune Land Development (CFLD) blew up, and Ping An took a hit of about **RMB 54 billion**. This was Ping An's worst investment mistake on record, and it directly drove a 29% collapse in net profit attributable to shareholders in 2021.

Many investors have since labeled Ping An as "bad at investing."

But the facts are:

1. Impairments related to CFLD have largely been worked through and are no longer an ongoing drag
2. The share of real-estate investment has kept falling, from 4.5% to **3.1%** (estimated as of end-2025)
3. The real-estate portfolio has been restructured to focus on rent-generating properties (commercial offices, logistics real estate, long-term rental apartments), which account for **81.7%** of the total
4. The 2025 comprehensive investment yield of **6.3%** suggests the investment team's overall capability isn't actually weak

**CFLD was a serious misjudgment, but a single failure shouldn't define an investment team managing RMB 6.49 trillion.**

**The counterargument:** CFLD exposed more than just one bad investment — it revealed Ping An's overconcentration risk in real estate. The RMB 54 billion loss shows that internal risk controls failed at a critical moment. Even though the exposure is being wound down, 3.1% of RMB 6.49 trillion still leaves roughly **RMB 200 billion** of real-estate investment outstanding — if the property market takes a second hit, that money isn't entirely safe.

## The diversified-financial-services moat: how deep is it, really?

| Moat type | Strength | Analysis |
|-----------|------|------|
| Licensing barriers | Very strong | Holds full licenses across insurance, banking, securities, trust, and funds simultaneously — nearly impossible for new entrants to replicate |
| Brand | Very strong | Ranked the world's most valuable insurance brand for multiple consecutive years |
| Scale economies | Strong | 251 million customers spread technology and operating costs across a large base |
| Customer switching costs | Moderately strong | Multi-product bundling drives 99% retention, but single-product customers face low switching costs |
| Data barriers | Moderately strong | Massive financial, medical, and driving-behavior data used for precision pricing, but data-privacy regulation is tightening |
| Network effects | Moderate | There is some internal ecosystem synergy, but it's weaker than the network effects of internet platforms |

**Will the moat still be there in 5 years?** Most likely yes. Licensing barriers are institutional and won't disappear in the short term.

**Will the moat still be there in 10 years?** Two risks bear watching: (1) if Ant Group or Tencent's financial arm obtain full licenses, they could erode the differentiation of diversified financial services; (2) AI and digitization could erode the channel advantage of traditional insurers. Overall, though, the moat is likely to remain fairly solid over a 10-year horizon.

## A core framework for understanding Ping An

If you had to define Ping An in one sentence, the closest version might be:

> **Ping An = a diversified financial machine powered by life insurance as its core engine (70% of profit) + a bank in the midst of a recovery (18% of profit) + a technology/health-and-elderly-care story still in its investment phase (5% of profit), all bound together on top of 251 million customers and RMB 6.49 trillion of insurance funds.**

What makes this company hard to price is that you must simultaneously assess life insurance (actuarial assumptions and the interest-rate cycle), banking (asset quality and net interest margin), and technology (return on investment) — and also judge how much the synergy of the diversified-financial ecosystem is actually worth.

Look at any single dimension in isolation, and the Ping An you see is **correct, but incomplete**.

## Q1 2026: short-term headwinds

At the time of writing, Ping An had just released its Q1 2026 results. A brief update:

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Year-on-year |
|------|--------|------|
| Revenue | RMB 218.4 billion | **-6.2%** |
| Net profit attributable to shareholders | RMB 25.02 billion | **-7.4%** |
| Operating profit attributable to shareholders | RMB 40.78 billion | **+7.6%** |
| Life insurance NBV | RMB 15.57 billion | **+20.8%** |
| Total assets | RMB 14.17 trillion | +2.0% versus start of year |

Sources: China Ping An Q1 2026 report, Caizhongshe, Eastmoney

**The core tension:** net profit fell 7.4% (mainly due to capital-market volatility affecting investment income), while operating profit rose 7.6% and NBV grew 20.8%.

In other words: **the fundamentals are improving even as the headline numbers look worse.** This is the "noise" that's peculiar to insurance stocks — swings in investment income obscure the underlying improvement in the quality of the business.

Understanding this tension is the entry point for understanding insurance stocks.
