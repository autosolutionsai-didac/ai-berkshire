## Risk One: Real Estate Exposure — the Scar Has Healed, but the Aftereffects Linger

### The Facts

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 (estimate) |
|------|---------|---------|---------------|
| Real estate investment share | 4.5% | 3.5% | ~**3.1%** |
| Real estate investment balance (RMB 100M) | — | 2,025 | ~**2,000** |
| Structure | — | Equity/property investment 81.7% | Mostly rent-generating properties |
| China Fortune Land Development impairment | — | Essentially cleared | — |

Sources: Ping An 2025 annual report, Securities Times, Guandian

The RMB 54 billion China Fortune Land Development default happened 4 years ago, and impairments have been essentially cleared. Real estate exposure has continued to decline from 4.5% to approximately 3.1%, with the structure also shifting from development-oriented real estate to rent-generating properties (commercial offices, logistics real estate, long-term rental apartments).

**The optimistic take:** "The worst is behind us" — management's own words. The direction is correct, exposure is shrinking, and the structure is improving.

**The pessimistic take:** 3.1% of RMB 6.49 trillion still amounts to roughly **RMB 200 billion** in real estate investment balance. If China's real estate market experiences a second systemic crisis (say, a crash in commercial property prices), that RMB 200 billion is not necessarily safe. And whether Ping An still has indirect exposure to other property developers like Country Garden (through trusts, bonds, and other channels) isn't disclosed in enough detail in the annual report.

**Risk rating: Medium.** The direction is right, but tail risk remains.

## Risk Two: Interest-Spread Loss — the Insurance Industry's "Gray Rhino"

This is currently the biggest systemic risk in the insurance industry, and it's worth spelling out in full.

### What Is an Interest-Spread Loss?

Put simply: when an insurer sells a policy, it promises a return rate (the guaranteed rate). If investment returns stay below the promised rate for a long time, the insurer is losing money on that business. That is an "interest-spread loss."

Japan's insurance industry went through a brutal interest-spread-loss crisis in the 1990s — rates plunged from 6% to 0%, and multiple insurers went bankrupt as a result.

### Ping An's Situation

| Dimension | Facts |
|------|------|
| Guaranteed rate on in-force policies | Historically, some policies carry guaranteed rates of **3.0%-3.5%** |
| Guaranteed rate on new policies (2026) | The floor rate on participating (dividend-paying) policies has dropped to **1.25%-1.75%** |
| EV long-term investment return assumption | Cut to **4.0%-4.5%** |
| 2025 total investment yield | **6.3%** |
| 2025 net investment yield | **3.8%** (down 0.4pp YoY) |
| 10-year average total investment yield | **4.9%** |

Sources: Ping An 2025 annual report, Jiemian, Securities Times

**Management's position: there is no interest-spread loss.** Their reasoning:
1. The share of participating policies is rising fast (2025 premium volume for participating policies grew more than 40%), with floor guaranteed rates of only 1.25%-1.75%
2. Mortality margin (claims experience better than assumed) and expense margin (actual costs below assumed costs) are taking a larger share, reducing reliance on the interest spread
3. The 2025 total investment yield of 6.3% far exceeds the EV assumption

**The market's concern: what about the interest-spread loss on in-force policies?**

New policies genuinely aren't a problem — guaranteed rates have already been cut to extremely low levels. But among in-force policies, a large volume sold before 2020 carries guaranteed rates of 3.0%-3.5%. These policies still have 20-30 years of claims to pay out. If average investment returns over the next 20 years come in at only 3%, this cohort of in-force policies will keep losing money.

**The key number:** the 2025 net investment yield of **3.8%**, down 0.4 percentage points YoY. If this trend continues (declining 0.3-0.4 percentage points per year), it could fall below the critical 3.0% line within 2-3 years.

**A total investment yield of 6.3% looks great, but it includes unrealized gains from the 2025 capital-market rally.** If the stock market flattens or declines, the total investment yield will swing sharply. **What's truly stable is the net investment yield — and that is declining.**

### Interest-Spread-Loss Risk Assessment

| Scenario | Probability (subjective judgment) | Impact |
|------|---------------|------|
| Rates stabilize at 2%-3%, no interest-spread loss | 40% | EV grows steadily, PEV recovers to 0.8-1.0x |
| Rates continue to decline modestly but remain manageable | 40% | Some interest-spread loss on in-force policies, but offset by participating policies and non-spread income |
| Rates "Japanify," approaching 0% | 20% | Severe interest-spread loss, EV shrinks substantially, PEV could fall to 0.3-0.5x |

**Risk rating: Medium-high.** This is the core variable that will determine Ping An's long-term value, and it's also the least controllable one.

## Risk Three: Succession — Ping An After Ma Mingzhe

### The Facts

Ma Mingzhe, **68 years old**, founded Ping An in 1988 and has led it for 38 years. He took it from a small insurer in Shekou to a trillion-yuan financial empire; his strategic vision and execution ability are Ping An's greatest intangible asset.

Several key decisions:
- 2002: brought in HSBC as a strategic investor — internationalization
- 2008: positioned in technology — a fintech first-mover
- 2018: launched life-insurance reform — painful but correct
- 2020: launched the healthcare/eldercare strategy — a long-term bet

**Current management structure:**
- A **co-CEO system** has been in place since 2018
- A management team led by Xie Yonglin handles day-to-day operations
- Ma Mingzhe has moved to Chairman + Head of the Strategic Development Center, focusing on strategy, talent, and culture

### Succession Risk Assessment

**The optimistic view:** the co-CEO system has been running for 8 years, and day-to-day operations no longer depend on Ma Mingzhe at all. Senior management is highly international and professional. Ping An's degree of institutionalization is top-tier among Chinese private enterprises.

**The pessimistic view:** many of Ping An's key strategic decisions — when to "cut" and when to "invest" — carry a strong imprint of Ma Mingzhe's personal judgment. Co-CEOs can execute strategy, but can they set it? Especially when facing major uncertainty (say, the next decision on the scale of "China Fortune Land Development"), what happens without Ma Mingzhe making the final call?

**Historical reference:** succession at large Chinese private enterprises has almost no "perfect precedent." Succession at Legend Holdings, Vanke, and Fosun all produced varying degrees of strategic wavering. Ping An's degree of institutionalization is higher, but the risk remains.

**Risk rating: Medium.** Limited impact in the short term (within 3 years); significant uncertainty in the long term (5-10 years).

## Risk Four: Industry Regulation — the Visible "Hand"

| Risk type | Probability | Impact | Response |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Further cuts to guaranteed rates | High | Hurts sales short term, favorable long term for interest-spread management | Shift to participating policies already underway |
| Cut to the illustrative rate ceiling for participating policies | Already happened | Ceiling cut from 3.9% to 3.5% | A uniform industry-wide policy — Ping An is affected the same as its peers |
| Tighter regulation of integrated-finance cross-selling | Medium | May restrict data interconnection between subsidiaries | Needs to watch data-privacy regulatory developments |
| Restrictions on the equity-investment share of insurance funds | Low | May cap the ceiling on equity asset allocation | Current equity allocation ratio has not exceeded the cap |

Sources: Xinhuanet, Sina Finance, regulatory filings

**Overall judgment:** regulatory risk is manageable. And many regulatory measures (cutting guaranteed rates, capping illustrative dividend rates) look like a short-term negative (hurting sales) but a long-term positive (lowering industry-wide interest-spread-loss risk, weeding out aggressive small insurers, and further raising concentration among leaders).

## Ping An in 10 Years

### Bull scenario (probability 30%)

- China's interest rates stabilize at 2%-3%, the economy grows moderately
- Insurance penetration rises from 4.5% to 6%+, industry scale grows 50%
- Ping An's NBV keeps growing, group EV reaches **RMB 2.5 trillion**
- The technology/healthcare-eldercare segments begin contributing meaningful profit
- PEV reverts to 1.0x, market cap reaches **RMB 2.5 trillion**
- Annualized return (including dividends): **~12%-15%**

### Base scenario (probability 45%)

- Rates fluctuate at a low level in the 1.5%-2.5% range
- NBV grows moderately (8%-12% annually), group EV reaches **RMB 2 trillion**
- The banking segment recovers slowly, the technology segment breaks even
- PEV holds at 0.7-0.8x, market cap RMB 1.4-1.6 trillion
- Annualized return (including dividends): **~8%-10%**

### Bear scenario (probability 25%)

- Rates approach 0-1%, "Japanification"
- Severe interest-spread losses emerge, EV stalls or even shrinks
- Real estate sees another systemic risk episode, further impairments on property investments
- Strategic direction drifts after Ma Mingzhe's retirement
- PEV falls to 0.4-0.5x, market cap falls below RMB 800 billion
- Annualized return (including dividends): **~-5% to 0%**

**Note:** the probabilities in these scenarios are subjective judgments, not precise forecasts. For reference framing only.

## Final Judgment

Ping An is not a thrilling stock. It doesn't have AI's sexy narrative, doesn't have consumer staples' certain growth, doesn't have an internet platform's network effects.

**It's a name with "unremarkable odds but a high win rate."**

0.65x PEV + 4.8% dividend yield + NBV still growing — this combination is hard to find a second example of among Chinese financial stocks.

**But it comes with one unavoidable precondition: you need to form a view on China's interest-rate environment.** If you believe China will not "Japanify" (that rates will ultimately stabilize above 2%), Ping An offers a fairly attractive risk/reward profile at the current price. If you cannot rule out "Japanification," then Ping An's "margin of safety" may not be as safe as it looks.

**This is not an investment that lets you sidestep the macro — it is itself a macro judgment.**

## Closing Thoughts

Across four articles, Ping An doesn't come across as "absolutely cheap," nor as a "value trap," but as a name **that requires you to form your own judgment.**

Its business direction is right (life-insurance reform is paying off, P&C profitability is improving, technology is being positioned for the future), its valuation is low (PEV of 0.65x), and its dividends are real cash (14 consecutive years of growth, RMB 48.9 billion in cash dividends).

But its core risks are just as real — falling rates, interest-spread loss, succession — these are not "tail risks you can ignore," but "core variables whose probability you must assess yourself."

**Understanding Ping An isn't about understanding one company — it's about understanding the future of China's financial industry.**
