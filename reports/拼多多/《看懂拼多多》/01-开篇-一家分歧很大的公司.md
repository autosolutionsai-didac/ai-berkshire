## A set of facts that will make you rethink Pinduoduo

Over the past six years, Pinduoduo's stock price has traced a dizzying curve:

- Early 2020: about **$40** — the "China's third e-commerce player" narrative begins
- February 2021: **$212** all-time high — the "beating Alibaba" narrative peaks
- October 2022: **$26** all-time low — the "Chinese ADRs are done for" narrative is everywhere
- August 2024: **$88** — earnings beat + single-day stock surge + Duan Yongping adds to his position
- May 2026: **~$100** — market sentiment returns to "hard to read"

**In six years, the same company has been re-labeled by the market at least four times.** Almost every one of those labels turned out, in hindsight, to be wrong.

But Pinduoduo is different from Tencent — Tencent gets misread because it's too complex; Pinduoduo gets misread because it **deliberately keeps you from seeing clearly**.

Open the 2025 annual report, and Pinduoduo has a set of metrics so counterintuitive they look like data errors:

| Metric | 2025 | Scale interpretation |
|------|---------|---------|
| Total revenue | **431.8 billion yuan** (+10%) | Growth rate has plunged from +59% in 2024 |
| GAAP net profit | **99.36 billion yuan** (**-12%, first-ever decline**) | A signal of divergence |
| ROE | **31%** (vs. 49% in 2024) | Still among the top tier of global e-commerce platforms |
| Free cash flow | **~106.9 billion yuan** | FCF/net profit >100% |
| **Net cash** | **422.3 billion yuan (~$60.4 billion)** | **42% of the current market cap** |
| **Current P/E** | **10.5x** | Near the low end of the historical median |
| **P/E excluding cash** | **6.3x** | **Extremely cheap** — but for a reason |
| Buybacks + dividends | **0 + 0** | **Two zeroes** |

Put this set of numbers together — **a company with 106.9 billion yuan in annual free cash flow and 422.3 billion yuan (~$60.4 billion) in net cash, yet with a market cap of only about $143.2 billion (roughly 1.03 trillion yuan), a P/E of 10.5x, and not a single cent returned via dividends or buybacks.**

If you think this is "undervaluation" — you probably haven't understood it.
If you think this is a "value trap" — you probably haven't understood it either.

What this series aims to do is **first lay out clearly "what it is," then lay out clearly "why it is that way"** — so you can reach your own judgment on whether it's worth buying.

## Three common misconceptions about Pinduoduo

Before we start taking it apart, let's debunk the three most widespread misconceptions.

### Misconception one: Pinduoduo is "discount e-commerce"

This is the most common perception, and also the most inaccurate.

Open the 2025 annual report: Pinduoduo's 431.8 billion yuan in revenue comes from only two lines:

| Revenue segment | Revenue (billion yuan) | Share | Nature |
|---------|------------|------|------|
| Online marketing services (advertising) | 217.8 | **50%** | Merchant ad spend |
| Transaction services (commissions + Temu) | 214.1 | **50%** | GMV commission take |

**Pinduoduo's actual business isn't "selling cheap goods for a markup" — it's an algorithmic company that matches Chinese manufacturing capacity with price-sensitive consumers, and it makes money from advertising fees and commissions.**

Its business model is closer to **Google + Meta's advertising model** (high gross margin, high ROE) than to Taobao (self-operated e-commerce) or Walmart (brick-and-mortar retail).

That distinction determines that Pinduoduo's financial texture (gross margin 60%+, ROE 30%+, FCF/net profit >100%) has nothing to do with "discount e-commerce" — it sits in **the tier of financial efficiency second only to Kweichow Moutai** among Chinese internet companies.

### Misconception two: Pinduoduo's moat is "low prices"

This is an argument **used by both bulls and bears** — and both are wrong.

The bulls say: Pinduoduo has already built low-price mindshare with users, so its moat is deep.
The bears say: low price isn't a moat — buyers just go with whoever is cheapest.

The more accurate judgment sits in the middle: **low price itself is not a moat — but the operating efficiency, algorithmic distribution, and merchant ecosystem that make low prices possible are a moat of medium strength.**

Analogy: Walmart's moat isn't "low price" either — it's "the world's most efficient supply chain + lowest operating expense ratio." The result is low prices, but the moat isn't the low price itself.

But be wary: **this moat is currently being eroded from multiple directions**.

- **Douyin e-commerce** reached 4.3 trillion yuan in GMV in 5 years (24% market share in 2024), growing at 30%+, and is taking away Pinduoduo's "low-price + impulse purchase" users
- **JD.com** is fighting back with "9.9 yuan free shipping"; **Taobao** has relaunched its "100 billion subsidy" program
- **Overlapping users across the three platforms are up 19.9% year-over-year** — meaning users are "price arbitrageurs" rather than brand loyalists
- **AI shopping-agent price-comparison tools** — on a 5-10 year horizon, these could render the "algorithmic recommendation + group buying" playbook ineffective

The moat hasn't disappeared, but its depth has gone from "very deep" to "medium." **This shift has happened over the past two years, but a lot of people's perceptions are still stuck in 2021.**

### Misconception three: 422.3 billion yuan in cash = a safety cushion

This is the bulls' favorite argument: Pinduoduo has over 420 billion yuan of net cash on its balance sheet, equal to 42% of its market cap, and its P/E excluding cash is only 6.3x — extremely cheap.

The math is correct. But there's a **fatal implicit assumption** here — that "the cash can actually be converted into value for shareholders."

Pinduoduo's actual situation is:

| Comparison | What comparable internet companies do | What Pinduoduo does |
|------|----------------------|-----------|
| Alibaba | Bought back $11.9 billion + paid $4.6 billion in dividends in 2025 | **0 + 0** |
| JD.com | Bought back $3.0 billion + paid $1.4 billion in dividends in 2025 | **0 + 0** |
| Tencent | Bought back HK$80 billion + paid HK$41 billion in dividends in 2025 | **0 + 0** |
| Pinduoduo | — | **Zero yuan in cumulative buybacks, zero yuan in dividends over 6 years** |

Pinduoduo is **the only Chinese internet company holding 400+ billion yuan of cash that has never made any shareholder return** whatsoever.

**Does the 422.3 billion yuan in cash actually protect shareholders? That depends on one thing: whether management is willing to distribute it to shareholders.**

The current factual evidence is: the likelihood of that willingness is low.

- Colin Huang stepped down from the board in 2021 and has maintained public silence for a long time
- Current CEO Chen Lei holds less than 1% of shares, giving him limited say over capital allocation
- Management has never publicly responded to "why no buybacks, why no dividends"
- Investor conference calls are **almost never held**, and the company does not accept sell-side analyst phone visits

If a company has cash but **holds onto it tightly without distributing it to shareholders**, then in essence shareholders aren't really buying a 1.03 trillion yuan market cap — they're buying **over 600 billion yuan of "operating assets"** plus 422.3 billion yuan of "cash you can see but can't touch."

The real value of the latter **depends on management's future choices** — and management has so far given no signal at all.

## Understanding Pinduoduo requires several different lenses

If you had to define Pinduoduo in one sentence, the closest version might be:

> **Pinduoduo = an algorithmic advertising machine with financial efficiency among the world's best (domestic main battlefield) + a political option whose valuation swings from $0 to $70 billion (Temu) + 420 billion yuan of cash sitting on the balance sheet that can't be taken out.**

Each of these three things has been written about separately by others, but few people have assessed them together. That is exactly why the market is so deeply divided on Pinduoduo, and why its P/E has oscillated around 10x for so long — valuation models can't handle this kind of composite company that combines "a high-quality core business + high overseas uncertainty + a trust discount."

Understanding this company requires putting on several lenses at once:

Let's leave a hook and close with a set of numbers. These will be explained in detail in Parts 04 and 05 — for now, just remember the scale:

| Metric | 2025 data |
|------|------------|
| Revenue | 431.8 billion yuan (+10%) |
| GAAP net profit | 99.36 billion yuan (**-12%, first-ever decline**) |
| Free cash flow | ~106.9 billion yuan |
| Shareholder returns (buybacks + dividends) | **0** (running completely counter to every peer) |
| Net cash | **422.3 billion yuan (42% of market cap)** |
| Gross margin | 60%+ |
| ROE | 31% (still among the top tier globally in e-commerce) |
| Current P/E | **10.5x** |
| P/E excluding cash | **6.3x** |
| FCF yield | **~7.0%** |
| Historical median P/E | ~15x |
| Current share price (May 2026) | ~$100 |

Put all of this together, and the Pinduoduo you see looks like this:

- A company with financial quality among the best in global e-commerce
- Both the current P/E (10.5x) and the P/E excluding cash (6.3x) are clearly below the historical median
- Free cash flow remains above the 100 billion yuan level
- But net profit declined for the first time in 2025, down 12%
- 420 billion yuan of cash is not being distributed to shareholders
- The Temu overseas business faces structural policy risk

What does adding all these facts together mean? **Different lenses give different answers.** That is precisely the question the next four parts will answer.

## Comparison with the *Understanding Tencent* series

If you've read *Understanding Tencent* — Pinduoduo and Tencent are two completely different types of holdings, and it's **worth comparing them side by side**:

| Dimension | Tencent | Pinduoduo |
|------|------|--------|
| Asset quality | WeChat is irreplaceable + 14 evergreen games | Algorithmic advertising machine + political option |
| Valuation | 14.5x P/E, reasonable to cheap | 10.5x / 6.3x ex-cash, **extremely cheap** |
| Management | Highly transparent + generous buybacks | Low transparency + zero buybacks, zero dividends |
| Risk structure | Regulatory tail risk | Temu political gamble + trust discount |
| Suitable investor | Steady types who want to "sleep well at night" | Value-arbitrage types who can tolerate high uncertainty |

Tencent is like "a top-quality Moutai" — you pay a reasonable price for certainty.
Pinduoduo is like "a luxury item at 40% off" — cheap, but you're not sure whether there's a hole in the fabric.

**The biggest difference between the two series is this**: the Tencent series is telling you "why this company is worth holding for the long term"; the Pinduoduo series is telling you "before you decide to hold it, first think carefully about whether that 420 billion yuan in cash can actually ever be recovered."
