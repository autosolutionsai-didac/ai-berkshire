## A Set of Numbers That Will Change How You See Pinduoduo

Open the 2025 annual report, and Pinduoduo's revenue composition looks like this:

| Revenue Segment | Revenue (RMB 100M) | Share |
|---------|------------|------|
| Online marketing services (advertising) | 2,178 | **50%** |
| Transaction services (commissions + Temu) | 2,141 | **50%** |
| Total revenue | 4,318 | 100% |

**50% of Pinduoduo's revenue is merchant advertising fees** — and that fact should change how you think about the company.

It isn't "buying low, selling low, and pocketing the spread" — it's an algorithmic company that makes money from advertising fees and commissions, a business model much closer to Google + Meta (high gross margins, zero marginal cost) than to JD.com (self-operated e-commerce) or Walmart (brick-and-mortar retail).

This piece is devoted to breaking down Pinduoduo's domestic main battlefield — explaining clearly why it's so profitable, how deep its moat really is, and whether it can hold up five years from now.

The next piece will cover overseas (Temu) separately — because that's an entirely different story.

## I. The Fundamental Difference Between Pinduoduo, Alibaba, JD.com, and Douyin

| Platform | Merchant Structure | Traffic Distribution | Core Business Model |
|------|---------|---------|------------|
| Taobao/Tmall | Mix of brand owners + small and midsize merchants | User search + follows + livestreaming | Platform advertising + commissions |
| JD.com | Self-operated + third-party brands | User search + self-operated recommendations | Capital-intensive self-operation |
| Douyin E-commerce | Content creators + brand owners | Interest-based video content recommendations | Interest-triggered purchasing |
| **Pinduoduo** | **White-label factories + direct-from-origin agricultural produce** | **Algorithm-driven distribution** | **C2M algorithmic matchmaking** |

**At its core, Pinduoduo is an algorithmic matchmaking machine that pairs China's excess manufacturing capacity with price-sensitive consumers.** Its core capability isn't "selling goods cheaply" — it's:

1. **Eliminating middlemen** — factories sell directly to consumers
2. **Precise algorithmic matching** — users see cheap, desirable products within 30 seconds of browsing
3. **Group buying + recommendations** — lowering customer acquisition costs through social virality and algorithmic distribution

### A Striking Contrast: Revenue per Employee

| Platform | Employees (approx.) | Total Revenue | **Revenue per Employee** |
|------|----------|------|-----------|
| Alibaba | 190,000 | 980 billion yuan | 5.1 million yuan/person |
| JD.com | 500,000 | 1.18 trillion yuan | 2.4 million yuan/person |
| Meituan | 117,000 | 340 billion yuan | 2.9 million yuan/person |
| **Pinduoduo** | **17,000** | **431.8 billion yuan** | **25.4 million yuan/person** |

**Pinduoduo's revenue per employee is 5x Alibaba's and 10x JD.com's** — that isn't the product of "low prices," it's the product of **algorithms + a lean organization**.

This kind of "operational efficiency" is Pinduoduo's real moat.

## II. The Real Profit Structure of the Main Battlefield

Breaking advertising and transaction services apart (**estimated basis, for order-of-magnitude reference only**):

### Advertising Business (217.8 billion yuan) — the Real Money Printer

Merchants buy ads on Pinduoduo (traffic packages, bid-ranking, recommendation slots), with gross margins above 80%:
- Revenue: 217.8 billion yuan
- Direct costs (servers + algorithms + customer service): approximately 20-25 billion yuan
- **Gross profit approximately 193-198 billion yuan (gross margin 88%+)**

This segment's structure resembles Google search advertising — **essentially a "zero marginal cost" money machine**.

### Transaction Services (214.1 billion yuan) — a Mixed Bag

This comprises two entirely different businesses:
- Domestic main-site commissions (charged on GMV, at rates of 0.6-1%)
- Temu platform revenue (accounts for 40-50% of transaction services, but Pinduoduo does not disclose it separately)

Temu's gross margin is far lower than domestic advertising's (it involves logistics and tariffs), so the overall gross margin of "transaction services" is heavily diluted.

### Main Battlefield Operating Margin: 35-40% (Among the World's Best)

Strip out Temu, and the domestic main site's standalone operating margin is approximately **35-40%** — for reference:

- Alibaba's Taotian Group operating margin is approximately 25-30% (the basis shifts with financial disclosures; for order-of-magnitude reference only)
- JD.com self-operated business: 3-5% (inherently low under the self-operated model)
- JD.com third-party business: 15-20%
- Amazon third-party marketplace: 10-15%
- **Pinduoduo: 35-40% — among the best in global e-commerce**

## III. Why "Low Prices" Aren't the Moat — Efficiency Is

Open any bullish research report on Pinduoduo and you'll find this line:

> "Pinduoduo's core moat is its 'low-price mindshare.'"

This sounds right, but **it is in fact both the bulls' favorite argument and the bears' easiest one to refute** — because the moment any other platform matches the price cuts, "low-price mindshare" is instantly shaken.

A more precise judgment requires unpacking "low prices":

- **Low prices are the result** — the price consumers see
- **Efficiency is the cause** — the underlying capability that makes low prices possible

The moat isn't in the result — it's in the cause.

Analogy: **Both Walmart and Kmart pursued "low prices," but Walmart survived while Kmart went bankrupt** — the difference wasn't who was cheaper, but who could sustain low prices long-term with lower operating costs.

### Pinduoduo's Real Moat Score (Composite: 2.9/5)

Assessed using the classic "five forces" moat framework (**subjective assessment, for order-of-magnitude reference only**):

| Moat Type | Pinduoduo's Score | Notes |
|-----------|----------|------|
| Scale effects | 3.5 / 5 | 700 million+ user base; GMV scale brings supply-chain bargaining power |
| Network effects | 2.5 / 5 | Weak network effects (not as strong as WeChat) |
| Algorithm/data | 3.5 / 5 | Years of accumulated user-behavior data + C2M algorithm |
| **Operating efficiency** | **4.5 / 5** | **Industry-leading revenue per employee, its strongest dimension** |
| Brand | 2 / 5 | "Pinduoduo = cheap" is a perception, not a brand |
| Switching costs | 1.5 / 5 | Near-zero cost for users to switch platforms |
| **Composite** | **2.9 / 5** | **Medium strength** |

Note: **this score is noticeably lower than Tencent's (close to 4.5/5)** — Pinduoduo lacks the kind of irreplaceable "relationship-chain lock-in" that WeChat has.

But that doesn't mean it has "no moat" — a 2.9/5 means it's unlikely to be disrupted within 5 years, but there is erosion risk on a 10-year horizon.

## IV. Three Current Threats

### Threat 1: Douyin E-commerce — From Zero to 4.3 Trillion Yuan in 5 Years

| Platform | 2024 GMV | 5-Year Growth Rate |
|------|------------|---------|
| Alibaba | ~7.5 trillion yuan | -2% |
| Pinduoduo | ~4.5 trillion yuan | +30% |
| JD.com | ~3.8 trillion yuan | +12% |
| **Douyin E-commerce** | **~4.3 trillion yuan** | **+200%** |

Douyin E-commerce went from zero to 4.3 trillion yuan in five years — that fact alone proves **the barriers to entry in e-commerce are lower than people assume**.

Douyin is targeting precisely Pinduoduo's core users: **impulse spenders + low-price preference + content-driven shoppers**.

> **Counterpoint**: Douyin E-commerce's "low prices" rely mainly on livestreamer-driven sales and livestream-room subsidies; Pinduoduo's "low prices" rely on algorithms and direct-from-factory shipping. The two have entirely different merchant ecosystems — Douyin isn't stealing 100% of Pinduoduo's users, only the subset that is "willing to watch videos to make purchase decisions." But even on a conservative estimate, a 15-25% user diversion from Pinduoduo to Douyin is reasonable.

### Threat 2: JD.com + Taobao's Low-Price Counterattack

The most important change in 2024-2025 is that **every top e-commerce platform is now competing on low prices**.

- JD.com: launched a "9.9 yuan, free shipping" section, and spun off Jingxi to operate independently
- Taobao: relaunched its "10-Billion-Yuan Subsidy" program, with subsidies exceeding 20 billion yuan in 2025
- Kuaishou E-commerce: followed with its "Five-Star Goods" low-price strategy

Result: **user overlap across the three platforms rose 19.9% year-over-year** — meaning **users are "price arbitrageurs"**: they buy wherever it's cheapest, with no platform loyalty.

On a 5-year view, **it's reasonable to expect Pinduoduo to hold a 22-25% market share; but pushing past 35%+ would be extremely difficult**.

### Threat 3: Longer Term — AI Agent Price Comparison + Instant Retail

This won't happen within 2 years, but must be considered on a 5-10 year horizon:

**AI agent price-comparison tools**:
- Once AI assistants can compare prices across every platform in seconds and place orders automatically, platform-level algorithmic recommendations could, in theory, lose their meaning
- But Pinduoduo users' shopping habits lean more toward "scroll-and-discover" browsing rather than "goal-directed price comparison," so an AI agent's ability to substitute for that behavior may be limited
- Platforms centered on goal-directed shopping (such as JD.com) are more likely to be disrupted

**Instant retail**:
- Meituan Instashopping handles 120 million orders per day (2025), with a focus on delivery within 30 minutes
- Pinduoduo is entirely absent from instant retail
- "Cheap and slow to arrive" versus "cheap and delivered in 30 minutes" — the latter is clearly more appealing

On a 10-year view, **Pinduoduo's moat could erode further from 2.9/5 to 2-2.5/5**.

## V. So Why Is Pinduoduo Still Making Money?

Having covered the threats, let's return to the most important question: **with Douyin, JD.com, and Taobao all counterattacking on low prices, why did Pinduoduo still earn 99.4 billion yuan in profit in 2025?**

The answer is the **combined effect of habit and efficiency**:

### 1. User Habits Are Sticky (Not Brand Loyalty, But Inertia)

Although users aren't "loyal to Pinduoduo," their shopping behavior has inertia:
- Open Pinduoduo → the algorithm recommends familiar, cheap products → order placed in 30 seconds
- Switch to Taobao/Douyin → search again / watch videos / compare prices → at least 3-5 minutes

**Habitual inertia is not brand loyalty, but it can sustain a 5-10% switching cost**.

### 2. Factory-Side Capacity Dependence

The "white-label factories + industrial-cluster merchants" that Pinduoduo has built up over the years have formed a distinctive supply-side structure:
- Factories depend on Pinduoduo to move volume
- Factories' cost structures have already been reverse-engineered from Pinduoduo's low-price positioning
- Switching to Douyin/JD.com requires readjustment (traffic structure, return policies, and commission structures all differ)

Supply-side "inertia" is the reason merchants are reluctant to switch.

### 3. Algorithmic Efficiency Is Hard to Replicate in the Short Term

Refined over 9 years of iteration, Pinduoduo's algorithm can specifically:
- Push precisely-targeted, cheap products to new users within 30 seconds of entry
- Build user behavior profiles more precise than most competitors'
- Match factory-side supply with user demand in real time

Douyin's algorithm is "content-driven," Taobao's is "search + follow" — **in the specific niche of "white-label goods + price-sensitive users," neither can catch up with Pinduoduo in the short term**.

## VI. A 5-Year Outlook for the Main Battlefield

Putting all the analysis together, here is a reasonable projection for Pinduoduo's domestic main battlefield over the next 5 years:

### Base Case

| Dimension | 2025 Actual | 2030 Base Case |
|------|----------|------------|
| Domestic MAU | ~900 million | 900-930 million (near the ceiling) |
| Domestic GMV | ~5.5-6 trillion yuan | 7-8 trillion yuan (+25-35% cumulative) |
| Main battlefield revenue | ~280-300 billion yuan (estimate) | 380-420 billion yuan |
| Main battlefield operating margin | 35-40% | **30-35%** (compressed by Douyin/Taobao low-price competition) |
| Main battlefield operating profit | ~100-120 billion yuan | 130-150 billion yuan |

**Under the base case, the domestic main battlefield's profitability will retreat modestly from "best-in-the-world" to "top-tier globally but no longer in a class of its own"** — though the absolute figures remain very substantial.

### Risk Scenario

If any of the following occur, expectations for the main battlefield would need to be revised downward:
- Douyin E-commerce's market share breaks past 30% and stays there
- Instant retail extends from "30-minute delivery" into the "low-ticket, fast-moving consumer goods" category
- Regulators intervene forcefully against user-favoring policies such as "refund-only" claims
- Large-scale attrition of top merchants

## Key Takeaways from This Piece

1. **50% of Pinduoduo's revenue is advertising fees** — it's fundamentally an algorithmic advertising machine, not a "discount e-commerce" business
2. **Revenue per employee of 25.4 million yuan, 5x Alibaba's and 10x JD.com's** — an efficiency moat
3. **Main battlefield operating margin of 35-40%** — among the best in global e-commerce
4. **"Low prices" are not the moat — the operating efficiency that supports low prices is** — composite moat score 2.9/5 (medium strength)
5. **Three threats**: Douyin's 4.3 trillion yuan GMV, JD.com + Taobao's low-price counterattack, and AI agents + instant retail
6. **The core business can hold up over the next 5 years** — but growth will slow from +30% to +5-10%
7. **Tencent's core business vs. Pinduoduo's main battlefield**: Tencent has the deeper moat, Pinduoduo the higher profitability
