## 2. Profitability Metrics Trend

### 2.1 Margin Trends

| Metric | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| **Gross margin** | 66.3% | 75.9% | 63.0% | 60.9% | 56.3% | 55.9% |
| **Operating margin** | 7.3% | 23.3% | 23.7% | 27.5% | 21.9% | 18.4% |
| **Net margin** | 8.0% | 24.3% | 24.2% | 28.5% | 23.0% | 11.8% |

**Data source:** StockAnalysis annual financial data, calculated from SEC filings

**Key trends:**

1. **Gross margin has been in sustained decline**: from a peak of 75.9% in FY2022 down to 55.9% in Q1 2026. The core driver is the rising share of the Temu business — Temu's fully-managed/semi-managed models involve fulfillment, logistics and warehousing, all asset-heavy costs that drag down blended gross margin. At the same time, the domestic "RMB 100 billion support program" fee reductions are also compressing gross margin.

2. **Operating margin is volatile but still healthy**: Q1 2026's 18.4% is below the FY2024 peak of 27.5%, but this mainly reflects expense allocation during Temu's expansion phase. Notably, the selling-expense ratio fell from about 33% in FY2024 to 31.8% in Q1 2026, suggesting Temu's customer-acquisition efficiency is improving.

3. **The "illusion" of a collapsing net margin**: Q1 2026's net margin is just 11.8%, but this is severely distorted by non-operating losses. Stripping out the RMB 5.3 billion deterioration in the "other gains/losses" line, the adjusted net margin would be about 16.8% — consistent with the operating-margin trend.

### 2.2 ROE and ROA Trends

| Metric | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (annualized) |
|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------------|
| **ROE** | ~10% | ~35% | ~41% | ~36% | ~26% | ~12% (annualized) |
| **ROA** | ~5% | ~18% | ~20% | ~22% | ~17% | ~8% (annualized) |

**Data source:** GuruFocus, MacroTrends, calculated from financial statements. Q1 2026 ROE = RMB 12.55 billion x 4 / RMB 423.4 billion (average shareholders' equity), for reference only.

**Note:** the Q1 2026 annualized ROE of just 12% is far below historical levels, but this is a single-quarter figure driven by non-operating losses, not representative of the full-year level. Using FY2025 full-year net profit of RMB 99.4 billion divided by period-end shareholders' equity of RMB 413.4 billion, ROE works out to about 24%, still an excellent level.

**Structural causes of the ROE decline:**

PDD's ROE fell from 41% in FY2023 to about 26% in FY2025 (and to an annualized 12% in Q1 2026), which looks concerning on the surface. But a DuPont decomposition shows:

- **Falling net margin**: from 24% down to 23% (FY2025), and further down to 12% in Q1 2026 — this is the primary drag
- **Falling asset turnover**: as RMB 436.1 billion in cash piles up on the balance sheet, a large pool of low-yielding assets is dragging down turnover efficiency
- **Extremely low financial leverage**: total assets of RMB 637.7 billion against shareholders' equity of RMB 423.4 billion, an equity multiplier of just 1.51x — PDD uses almost no leverage at all

The core tension is this: **PDD's growing profits are increasingly turning into cash sitting idle on the balance sheet, rather than being returned to shareholders or redeployed into high-return investments, so ROE keeps getting diluted by an ever-expanding denominator.** This is the financial mirror of a capital-allocation efficiency problem.

## 5. Line-by-Line Breakdown of the Q1 2026 Income Statement — Solving the Puzzle of "Operating Profit Up 22% but Net Profit Down 15%"

This is the single most important — and most widely misread — data point in this quarter's earnings. Let's use a bridge analysis to unpack it:

### 5.1 The "Bridge" from Operating Profit to Net Profit

```
Operating profit, Q1 2026:        RMB 19.57 billion (+RMB 3.48 billion, +22% YoY)
  + Interest and investment income/loss:   -RMB 0.63 billion (Q1 2025 was +RMB 0.22 billion, a deterioration of RMB 0.85 billion)
  + Foreign exchange gain/loss:              -RMB 0.15 billion (Q1 2025 was -RMB 0.24 billion, an improvement of RMB 0.09 billion)
  + Other gains/(losses), net:         -RMB 2.03 billion (Q1 2025 was +RMB 3.26 billion, a deterioration of RMB 5.29 billion)
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  = Pre-tax profit:              RMB 16.76 billion (Q1 2025 was RMB 19.33 billion, down RMB 2.57 billion)
  - Income tax:                -RMB 4.12 billion (Q1 2025: -RMB 4.48 billion)
  - Equity-method investment losses:            -RMB 0.10 billion
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  = Net profit:                RMB 12.55 billion (-15% YoY)
```

### 5.2 "Other Gains" Swung from +RMB 3.26 Billion to -RMB 2.03 Billion — What Exactly Is This?

This is the single most consequential variable in the entire earnings report: **"other gains/(losses), net" swung from +RMB 3.26 billion in Q1 2025 to -RMB 2.03 billion in Q1 2026, a combined deterioration of RMB 5.29 billion.** This single line item alone completely swallowed the RMB 3.48 billion increase in operating profit, and then dragged results down by an additional RMB 1.8 billion.

**What exactly does this line item include?**

Based on the accounting-policy disclosures in PDD's historical 20-F annual reports and analysis by Chinese financial media, "other gains/(losses), net" mainly comprises the following categories:

1. **Fair-value changes in the investment portfolio**: PDD holds a large volume of short-term investments (RMB 313.0 billion), including wealth-management products, structured deposits, and money-market funds. Under accounting standards, some of these investments must be measured at fair value, with market-value changes flowing directly through the income statement. Q1 2026 (January-March 2026) coincided with a sharp U.S. stock market selloff (all three major indices fell in tandem in March); if PDD holds dollar-denominated equity or bond-type investments, fair-value declines could have produced a book loss.

2. **Government subsidies**: Chinese technology companies commonly receive R&D subsidies and tax rebates from local governments. The RMB 3.26 billion of "other gains" in Q1 2025 may have included a large government subsidy or tax incentive. In Q1 2026, this source of gains may have shrunk or its recognition may have been delayed.

3. **Non-recurring gains/losses related to the business**: this may include gains/losses on asset disposals, or compensation/impairment items related to suppliers or merchants.

4. **The initial capital injection into the new Duoduo Maimai brand subsidiary**: management mentioned injecting RMB 15 billion of seed capital into a new Shanghai entity in March 2026. While the equity injection itself would not directly affect the consolidated income statement (since it's a subsidiary), if there are unconsolidated related-party investments or external equity investments subject to impairment, some of that could be reflected in "other losses."

**The most likely primary driver:** based on analysis from Eastmoney — **fair-value changes in the investment portfolio are the biggest factor.** The U.S. stock market crashed in March 2026, and part of the investment portfolio within PDD's massive RMB 436.1 billion cash pool, marked to market, generated unrealized losses. Q1 2025 was the exact opposite — markets were rising then, and investment gains were substantial. This explains why the same line item swung by RMB 5.3 billion between the two quarters.

**Implications for investors:** this type of fair-value swing is fundamentally a **non-cash, non-operating, non-sustainable fluctuation** — it neither reflects business deterioration nor consumes cash on an ongoing basis. If markets recover next quarter, this line item could turn positive again. But it exposes a structural problem: **if RMB 436.1 billion in cash is mainly parked in low-yield wealth-management products or passively held financial assets, the income statement will inevitably be buffeted by market swings — this is the hidden cost of not distributing a mountain of cash.**

### 5.3 Interest and Investment Income Swung from +RMB 0.22 Billion to -RMB 0.63 Billion

"Interest and investment income/(loss), net" also swung from +RMB 0.22 billion in Q1 2025 to -RMB 0.63 billion in Q1 2026. Per the 20-F disclosures, this line item includes:
- Interest income on cash and deposits
- Realized and unrealized gains/losses on short-term investments
- Fair-value changes on convertible-bond investments

Similarly, the unfavorable investment environment in Q1 2026 (high dollar interest rates but falling asset prices) pushed investment income into negative territory.

### 5.4 Comprehensive Assessment: The Combined Impact of Non-Operating Items

| Non-operating item | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | YoY Deterioration |
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Interest and investment income | +RMB 0.22 billion | -RMB 0.63 billion | RMB 0.85 billion |
| Foreign exchange gain/loss | -RMB 0.24 billion | -RMB 0.15 billion | +RMB 0.09 billion (improvement) |
| Other gains/losses | +RMB 3.26 billion | -RMB 2.03 billion | RMB 5.29 billion |
| **Non-operating items, total** | **+RMB 3.25 billion** | **-RMB 2.81 billion** | **Deteriorated by RMB 6.05 billion** |

**Conclusion:** operating profit grew RMB 3.48 billion year over year, but non-operating items swung from a net gain of RMB 3.25 billion to a net loss of RMB 2.81 billion, a deterioration of RMB 6.05 billion — the sole cause of the net-profit decline. Stripping out non-operating items, PDD's Q1 2026 operating performance was in fact **accelerating its improvement.**

## 8. Capital-Allocation Efficiency Assessment — RMB 436.1 Billion in Cash, No Dividends, No Buybacks

### 8.1 Return on Cash

The return generated by PDD's RMB 436.1 billion in cash/short-term investments can be inferred indirectly from the income statement:

- Q1 2025 interest and investment income was only +RMB 0.22 billion (annualized, about RMB 0.9 billion)
- Q1 2026 interest and investment income was -RMB 0.63 billion (a loss)

Even in a normal quarter, the annualized interest income on RMB 436.1 billion in cash is only about RMB 5.0-7.5 billion, implying a yield of roughly 1.2-1.7%. **This is far below China's one-year government bond yield (about 2%), and far below PDD's own cost of capital (WACC of 10-12%).**

### 8.2 Comparison with Peer Capital Returns

|------|---------|---------|---------|-----------|
| **PDD** | RMB 436.1 billion (US$63.2 billion) | **No** | **No** | **0%** |
| Alibaba | ~RMB 470 billion | Yes | Yes (large-scale buybacks) | ~5-8% |
| JD.com | ~RMB 210 billion | Yes | Yes | ~3-5% |

### 8.4 A Quantitative Assessment of Capital-Allocation Efficiency

**Note:** "invested capital" here excludes excess cash (defined as cash beyond what the business needs for operations), because those funds are not participating in core-business value creation.

An ROIC of 33-48% is still an extremely excellent level (Buffett's standard: ROIC > 15% qualifies as an excellent company). This shows that **PDD's core business is exceptionally capital-efficient, and the problem lies in the massive idle cash sitting outside the core business.**

**Comparing against a full-scope ROIC (without excluding excess cash):**

### 8.5 What Would Happen If PDD Launched a Buyback?

Suppose PDD deployed RMB 200 billion (about US$28 billion, just 46% of its cash reserves) toward a buyback:

- Against a current market cap of ~RMB 840 billion, a RMB 200 billion buyback could retire roughly 24% of shares outstanding
- Post-buyback EPS would rise by about 31% (1/(1-0.24))

This is exactly why Buffett has always emphasized: **for a company with high ROIC but a low valuation, a buyback is the most efficient form of capital allocation.** PDD happens to satisfy every condition (high ROIC, low valuation, enormous cash reserves), yet has chosen the least efficient path — parking the money in wealth-management products yielding 1-2%.
