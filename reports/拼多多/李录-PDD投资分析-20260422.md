## II. In-Depth Analysis of the Buy/Sell Rationale

### First Position (Q4 2020): An Exploratory Bet, Sized Conservatively

**Market backdrop:**
- Q4 2020 marked the tail end of China's mobile-internet boom. PDD had already completed its transformation from a group-buying app into a nationwide e-commerce platform, with annual active buyers surpassing 700 million, overtaking Alibaba's domestic DAU to become a leading e-commerce player.
- PDD's stock rallied hard throughout 2020, from about $35 in February to a high of $179 in November. Li Lu's average purchase price of about $119 fell within the historically high range.
- Most value investors had not yet come around to PDD; it was still widely dismissed as a "cheap and low-quality" fad.

**Inferred buy thesis:**
1. Penetration into lower-tier/rural markets plus a user base of a billion-plus gave it a wider moat than the market expected
2. Colin Huang's low-price algorithmic flywheel (traffic → competition → low prices → users → more traffic) created a systemic advantage
3. At only 2% of the portfolio, this was a "research-sized" position, indicating Li Lu was testing the waters rather than betting big

**Valuation context at the time:** PDD's P/S ratio in Q4 2020 was about 15x; P/E could not be calculated because the company was still unprofitable. Li Lu was clearly evaluating this investment through a growth lens rather than a traditional valuation lens.

### Full Exit (Q4 2021): Stopped Out by the Regulatory Storm

**Market backdrop:**
- 2021 was the most regulation-heavy year in China: the "Double Reduction" policy hit the education sector (July), Didi was forced off app stores, antitrust probes targeted Tencent, Alibaba, and Meituan, and oversight of internet platforms tightened across the board.
- PDD's stock was cut in half over the course of 2021, falling from a high of $179 to around $55.
- In August 2021, Colin Huang announced he was stepping down as chairman.

**Inferred sell rationale:**
1. Regulatory uncertainty fell outside Li Lu's circle of competence — he had no reliable way to gauge the direction of Chinese policy
2. At only a 2% position, the $32 million stop-loss was an acceptable tuition fee for Himalaya Capital
3. At the time, Li Lu was broadly rotating toward U.S. markets (Google, Bank of America); China's regulatory risk had moved outside his comfort zone
4. Cutting the position rather than holding through shows that Li Lu distinguishes between "deteriorating company fundamentals" and "external policy risk" — since he could not forecast the latter, he chose to exit

> Key quote (inferred rationale): Li Lu has said, "We only invest in companies within our circle of competence" — when external policy risk moves outside that circle, he chooses to leave.

## IV. Detailed Buy Rationale for Each Core Holding

### 1. BYD (2002, the greatest win of his career)

**Background:** In 2002, BYD had just completed its Hong Kong IPO. Its core business was nickel-cadmium batteries, and it was virtually unknown. Li Lu discovered through a news article that BYD was supplying rechargeable batteries to Motorola, flew to Shenzhen specifically to investigate, and held in-depth talks with Wang Chuanfu.

**Core thesis:**
- An engineering-driven culture: Wang Chuanfu himself was an engineer, and a workforce of a hundred thousand engineering and science graduates was the core competitive advantage
- China's labor costs combined with engineering capability formed the most efficient combination in global manufacturing
- A battery-technology barrier: BYD's ability to develop its own batteries in-house was extremely rare in 2002
- Vertical-integration capability: moving from batteries into automobiles was a natural extension, not a reckless leap into a new industry

**When it became a huge winner:**
- In the 2007 A-share bull market, BYD's stock began to surge
- During the 2008 financial crisis, the stock fell from HK$88 to HK$14, and **Li Lu added to the position against the tide**
- In September 2008, Li Lu convinced Munger, who then "pounded the table" to get Buffett to act, and Berkshire bought a 10% stake at HK$8
- In the 2020-2021 new-energy-vehicle bull market, BYD's stock rose above HK$300

**The cost of a 20-year hold:** along the way the stock fell 47% from its highs, Wang Chuanfu faced a reputational crisis, and the electric-vehicle business struggled for years to turn a profit. Li Lu never sold through any of it, and only began trimming in 2021.

**In Li Lu's own words:** "When I first bought BYD, what I valued was its accumulated battery technology and its vertical-integration capability. Its later moves into automobiles and new energy were all natural extensions of that capability."
