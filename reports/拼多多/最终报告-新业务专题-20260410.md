## 1. One-Sentence Conclusion

> PDD is spending roughly $18-21 billion per year on Temu (2025), with losses narrowing from $3.5-6.0 billion in 2024 to $1.0-3.0 billion. **The most counterintuitive finding: if Temu were to fail completely and shut down, PDD's profit would actually surge 20-30%.** The company sits on $60.4 billion in cash yet pays zero dividends and does zero buybacks — the core reasons being that Temu's globalization still requires continuous investment, founder Colin Huang's "delayed gratification" philosophy, and a governance structure that leaves shareholders with no voice. At the current PE of ~10x — just 6.1x after netting out cash — the margin of safety is ample, but three catalysts are still awaited: a Hong Kong listing, a first buyback/dividend, and Temu profitability.

## 5. Core Question Three: What If It Fails?

### 5.1 Scenarios in Which Temu Could Fail

| Scenario | Probability | Trigger Condition |
|------|:----:|---------|
| Continued contraction of the US market | **Already occurring** | DAU plunged 58% after the de minimis exemption was eliminated |
| EU follows suit and eliminates de minimis | 80% | Planned to take effect in July 2026 |
| Massive DSA fine | 50% | Up to 6% of global annual revenue (~$4.0 billion) |
| Localization transition fails | 40% | Loses the China-direct-shipping price advantage, becomes "an Amazon without Prime" |
| Full shutdown | 15% | Simultaneous deterioration across multiple markets plus persistent heavy losses |

### 5.2 PDD's Ability to Cut Losses

**PDD has extremely strong loss-cutting capacity**:
- **$60.4 billion in cash reserves** — even if Temu were shut down entirely, the wind-down cost (layoffs, contract termination, warehouse lease exits) is estimated at no more than $10-15 billion
- **The core platform business is fully independent** — Temu's operations do not affect the domestic Pinduoduo core platform
- **Duoduo Grocery is already profitable**, providing additional profit support
- **Optional loss-cutting path**: doesn't have to shut down entirely — could retreat to tariff-friendly markets (Southeast Asia, Latin America, Middle East)

### 5.3 The Real Impact on the Company If Temu Fails

| Dimension | Impact |
|------|------|
| Profit | **Positive** — saves the $18-21 billion invested annually, profit surges |
| Revenue | **Negative** — loses about 50% of revenue, but this portion of revenue is loss-making |
| Valuation narrative | **Negative** — loses the "globalization growth story," PE could compress further |
| Team | Overseas teams would need to be cut, but the core technology team is domestic |
| Competitive landscape | Positive for Shein and AliExpress, but doesn't affect PDD's domestic position |

> **Buffett's lens: If a business is worth more to the company shut down than running, that tells you the business is currently "destroying value." But if that business could plausibly become a large enterprise in the future, then the current spend is "buying an option." PDD is, in essence, using an annual loss of $1.0-3.0 billion to buy an option on a global e-commerce empire.**

## 6. Core Question Four: Why No Dividends and No Buybacks?

### 6.1 Peer Comparison: PDD Is the Only Chinese Internet Giant With "Zero Shareholder Return"

| Company | 2024-2025 Dividends | 2024-2025 Buybacks | Total Shareholder Return | Shareholder Yield |
|------|:----------:|:----------:|:----------:|:--------:|
| **Tencent** | HK$5.30/share (+32%) | HK$112 billion + $10 billion program | ~$16.9 billion | ~4.8% |
| **Alibaba** | $1.98/ADS | $25 billion buyback program | ~$15.0 billion+ | ~2.5% |
| **JD.com** | $1.5 billion (+32%) | $3.6 billion + $5.0 billion new program | ~$5.0 billion/year | ~6-8% |
| **PDD** | **Zero** | **Zero** | **Zero** | **0%** |

### 6.2 Four-Factor Analysis

#### Reason 1: Temu Still Requires Continuous Investment (Plausibility: High)
- Temu is transitioning from "asset-light cross-border direct shipping" to "asset-heavy local warehousing," which requires substantial capital
- Building warehouses and logistics networks across 80+ countries is capital-intensive
- **But counterargument**: taking $10 billion out of $60.4 billion in cash for buybacks would still leave $50 billion to support Temu

#### Reason 2: Management Believes Reinvestment Returns Exceed Buyback Returns (Plausibility: Medium)
- At the current PE of ~10x, buybacks imply a return of roughly 10%
- Management needs to demonstrate that new-business investment returns exceed 10%
- **But counterargument**: with Temu's US DAU down 58%, does the reinvestment return really exceed the buyback return? **Questionable**

#### Reason 3: Cash Repatriation Constraints Under the VIE Structure (Plausibility: Medium)
- Most of the cash sits within onshore Chinese VIE entities
- Dividending upward from the VIE incurs a 10% Chinese withholding tax
- Cross-border transfers are subject to foreign-exchange controls
- **But counterargument**: Alibaba, JD.com, and Tencent operate under the same VIE structure, yet still pay dividends and buy back shares

#### Reason 4: Colin Huang's "Delayed Gratification" Philosophy + Governance Structure (Plausibility: High, and Concerning)
- Colin Huang holds 26.5% of shares and 80.7% of voting power — effectively a veto right
- His philosophy is "delayed gratification" and "long-term investment"
- **Core contradiction: the founder has already retired to pursue scientific research, yet demands that shareholders continue to practice "delayed gratification"**
- Even if every external shareholder unanimously demanded a dividend, Colin Huang alone could veto it

### 6.3 Under What Conditions Might Dividends/Buybacks Begin?

| Catalyst | Timeline | Probability |
|--------|:------:|:----:|
| Temu reaches breakeven in major markets | 2027-2028 | 60% |
| Hong Kong listing completed | 2026-2027 | 70% |
| PE staying below 10x long-term forces management action | Ongoing | 40% |
| Chinese government requires tech companies to return capital to shareholders | Uncertain | 30% |

> **Li Lu's lens: Investing in PDD essentially means "trusting that a management team you cannot oversee will do the right thing." $60.4 billion in cash sits idle, with neither buybacks nor dividends even as the PE sits at just 10x — this is either far-sighted long-termism, or a governance failure.**

## 7. Investment Thesis (Bull vs. Bear)

### The Bull Case
1. **PE ex-cash is just 6.1x** — buying a money-printing machine with 30% ROE and zero debt
2. **Asymmetric bet**: Temu succeeds → profit doubles; Temu fails → profit still rises 20-30%
3. Temu's loss has already narrowed from $6.0 billion to $1.0-3.0 billion — **the profitability inflection point is approaching**
4. Duoduo Grocery, with 62% share, is already profitable and underappreciated by the market
5. $60.4 billion in cash equals 42% of market cap — "buy the company, get half the cash for free"
6. Duan Yongping has kept adding to his position, up to 7.75 million shares ($917 million) — voting with real money

### The Bear Case
1. **Zero dividends, zero buybacks** — the $60.4 billion in cash is of no use whatsoever to shareholders
2. Colin Huang's 80% voting power plus his retirement to pursue scientific research is a **governance black box**
3. Temu's US DAU has plunged 58% — the original model has already failed
4. **HFCAA delisting risk**: the only giant without a Hong Kong-listing backstop
5. EU DSA fines plus the elimination of de minimis mean tightening global regulation
6. Growth has fallen off a cliff from 59% to 10%, and the market may continue to assign a low valuation
7. Disclosure is extremely poor; investors cannot see the true profit/loss of each business line

## 10. Summary

Pinduoduo is a combination of "a good business + a free option + questionable governance."

**A good business**: the core platform has 30% ROE, $15.7 billion in FCF, and zero debt, making it China's most efficient e-commerce platform. Duoduo Grocery, with 62% share, is already profitable.

**A free option**: for an annual loss of $1.0-3.0 billion (and narrowing), the company is buying the possibility of a global e-commerce empire. If it fails, profit rises; if it succeeds, profit doubles.

**Questionable governance**: $60.4 billion in cash generating zero return, Colin Huang holding 80% of the vote while retired to pursue scientific research, and industry-worst disclosure. What you're buying isn't a business that will pay you back — it's an asset you're betting will appreciate.

**Final judgment**: at a PE of 10x, with cash equal to 42% of market cap, the margin of safety is ample. But this is not a "buy and forget" investment — it requires ongoing tracking of catalysts. It's suited to a "low-valuation + optionality" position within a portfolio, not a large concentrated position.
