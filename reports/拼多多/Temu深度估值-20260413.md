**Date: 2026-04-13**
**Core fact: PDD has never disclosed Temu data separately in its financial reports; any Temu valuation is an external estimate**

**Loss scenario**:

### 3.3 Estimating Temu's drag on PDD's profit
- PDD's GAAP net profit fell 13 billion RMB (≈$1.8 billion) from 2024 to 2025
- Cost increase: +23% vs. revenue +10%, a ~13pp gap × 431.8 billion RMB revenue ≈ **56 billion RMB in additional cost**
- That 56 billion RMB is split between: Temu losses + the main platform's hundred-billion-yuan investment program + other items
- **If Temu accounts for half (roughly $4 billion)**, the main platform's hundred-billion-yuan investment accounts for the other half (~$4 billion)
- **But this is only a guess — the actual split is entirely unknowable**

## IV. Temu valuation: three scenarios + explicit assumptions

### Methodological choice

For valuing a loss-making or breakeven business, the industry generally uses three methods:
1. **DCF (discounting future profit)** — not applicable, because future profit cannot be forecast
2. **EV/GMV multiple** — using comparable cross-border e-commerce company multiples
3. **Strategic option value** — treating Temu as an "option on future profitability," using Black-Scholes-style thinking

**This report adopts: EV/GMV multiple + option adjustment**

### Comparable data (SHEIN, AliExpress, Amazon International)

| Company | 2024 GMV | Valuation | EV/GMV | Notes |
|------|---------|------|--------|------|
| **SHEIN** (private market) | ~$45 billion | $50-70 billion | 1.1-1.6x | Profitable but with losses in parts of the business |
| **AliExpress** (Alibaba AIDC) | ~$50 billion (including Lazada) | Not separately valued by Alibaba | ~0.5-1.0x | Growth slowing |
| **Amazon International** | $150 billion+ | ~1.5x on a PE-implied basis | 1.5x | Already profitable |
| **Temu** (this report's estimate) | $40-50 billion | To be determined | 0.5-1.5x? | Currently loss-making |

**Key judgment**: for a breakeven/slightly-loss-making Temu, a reasonable EV/GMV multiple should be **0.5-1.0x** (below that of the profitable SHEIN).

### Three-scenario valuation

#### Scenario A: Profitability scenario (probability 25%)
**Trigger conditions**:
- Tariff policy does not escalate further in 2026
- Temu's local-warehouse + semi-managed model proves out
- **Net margin of 3-5%** achieved by 2027-2028

**2028E figures**:
- GMV $70 billion
- Revenue $18 billion (take rate 26%)
- Net profit $0.54-0.9 billion

**Valuation**:
- At PE 20x (benchmarked against SHEIN): **$10.8-18.0 billion**
- Or EV/GMV 1.0x: **$70 billion** (clearly too high)
- **Take the conservative value: $15 billion**

#### Scenario B: Breakeven / persistent small loss (probability 50%)
**Trigger conditions**:
- Tariff pressure persists but is manageable
- GMV holds at $40-50 billion, no further significant growth
- Hovers near breakeven in 2027-2028

**Valuation**:
- **This is the real crux of the question**
- If Temu stays at breakeven forever, its DCF value = **$0** (no future cash flow)
- But as a "strategic option" (potential to turn profitable), it merits some premium
- EV/GMV 0.3-0.5x × $45 billion = **$13.5-22.5 billion**
- **Take the midpoint: $18 billion**

> **Note**: an earlier report gave this scenario $60 billion — that was based on overly optimistic assumptions. If the business stays at breakeven with no cash flow, $60 billion is unsupportable.

#### Scenario C: Collapse scenario (probability 25%)
**Trigger conditions**:
- Tariff policy keeps escalating (>200%)
- Local-warehouse gross margin turns negative
- PDD is forced to sharply scale back Temu, GMV halves to below $20 billion

**Valuation**:
- Temu's standalone business valuation = **$0**
- **But** PDD's overall profit actually rises (no more subsidies): saving $3-5 billion in annual losses → at PE 12x → **+$36-60 billion added to the parent company**
- **Net effect: roughly +$40 billion to PDD as a whole**

### Three-scenario weighted average (revised)

| Scenario | Probability | Temu standalone valuation | Contribution to PDD |
|------|------|-------------|------------|
| A: Profitable | 25% | $15 billion | +$15 billion |
| B: Breakeven | 50% | $18 billion | +$18 billion |
| C: Collapse | 25% | $0 | +$40 billion (profit rebound) |
| **Weighted average** | | | **+$22.8 billion** |

## VI. Core judgment on Temu's valuation

### Why does Temu's valuation swing so widely?
Because Temu is an **option-like asset**:
- Option value is highly sensitive to volatility and time-to-expiry
- Tariff policy, the success or failure of the semi-managed model, and the competitive landscape are all uncertain factors
- The reasonable valuation range is so wide it loses meaning ($0 to $100 billion have both been cited by analysts)

### Duan Yongping's perspective
In his 2025-2026 remarks, Duan Yongping **rarely discusses Temu's valuation on its own**. His investment logic mainly rests on:
1. The main platform's cash flow being strong enough
2. Trusting Colin Huang
3. Judging the success or failure of the new Duoduo Mu brand 10 years from now

In other words, **Duan Yongping essentially does not treat Temu as a major source of value**. On top of the "domestic main platform + cash" base case, he treats Temu as a free option.

### Recommendation for readers
**When valuing PDD, one should**:
1. **Use the most conservative Temu valuation** (the $0-20 billion range)
2. **Treat Temu's option value as a "bonus thrown in with the purchase"**
3. **If a price just above $100 already prices in a reasonable Temu option premium, the current price is not cheap**

## Appendix: Key uncertainties

**Where this report is most likely to be wrong**:
1. Temu's true 2025 GMV could be far higher than $50 billion (if PDD's deliberate non-disclosure is because the number is very large rather than very small)
2. The true margin profile of the local-warehouse model is unknown (it could be better than assumed)
3. Growth in Europe, Latin America, and Southeast Asia could exceed expectations
4. Colin Huang/management's true strategic intent for Temu cannot be judged from the outside

**Recommended basis for decisions**:
- Do not make decisions based on a single-point valuation — look at the valuation **range**
- The current $100 sits at the upper edge of my conservative range — **don't rush to buy; wait for a better price**
- If the share price falls back to **$75-85** (assuming fundamentals haven't deteriorated), the margin of safety improves significantly, and building a position could begin
