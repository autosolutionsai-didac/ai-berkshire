### 1.3 The Impact of Huawei's Comeback

Huawei reclaimed the top annual spot in China's smartphone market in 2025 with 46.7 million units shipped, capturing 69% share of the foldable-phone segment and effectively monopolizing the ultra-premium segment above RMB 6,000. Xiaomi's Q1 2026 shipments plunged 19% year over year — the steepest decline among the top five vendors.

**Competitiveness Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> Third globally but slipped to fourth in China; premiumization is still in its early stages, and the pressure from Huawei's comeback continues. The 19% plunge in Q1 2026 is a serious warning sign.

## V. Munger's "Invert, Always Invert": What Could Kill Xiaomi?

### Five Fatal Scenarios

**Scenario One: Smartphone shipments keep sliding, undermining the foundation of internet services**
The 19% plunge in Xiaomi's Q1 2026 smartphone shipments is already a warning bell. Smartphones are the "entry point" to the whole ecosystem; a narrowing entry point means the foundation of the business model is being eroded.

**Scenario Two: The vehicle business suffers large-scale losses that feed back into the rest of the group**
In 2025 the vehicle business barely turned a profit — RMB 900 million, a margin below 1%. If gross margin falls from 24.3% to below 15%, it will slide back into heavy losses. The cliff-edge drop in SU7 Ultra's monthly sales, from 3,000+ to just 45, exposes real product-lifecycle risk.

**Scenario Three: All-out pressure from Huawei**
Huawei is Xiaomi's common rival across three battlefields at once: smartphones (38% vs. 7% at the premium tier), IoT (HarmonyOS Connect), and vehicles (AITO's 589,100 units). This is the rare, difficult situation of "facing the same strongest competitor on every front simultaneously."

**Scenario Four: Cross-category expansion spreads resources thin**
Xiaomi's R&D spending of RMB 33.1 billion looks large, but once split across four business lines, the investment in each falls short of more focused rivals: BYD's R&D spending of over RMB 50 billion goes entirely into vehicles; Huawei's R&D spending of RMB 164.7 billion is concentrated mainly in ICT.

**Scenario Five: The smartphone industry a decade from now**
Shipments shrink slowly, with the high end concentrated into a "tri-oligopoly" of Apple, Huawei, and Samsung. If new device forms like AI glasses and AR devices rise, the smartphone's role as the "entry point" could be partly displaced.

---

## VI. Munger's Final Verdict

**"This company is fighting in four red oceans simultaneously, and in three of them it has no pricing power."**

The one thing that might genuinely interest Munger is the IoT ecosystem — the network effect created by 1.079 billion connected devices has produced a real switching cost. But whether that moat is wide enough to support the whole company's valuation narrative depends on: can Xiaomi convert the ecosystem stickiness of IoT into sustainable profit growth, rather than letting the massive capital expenditure on vehicles and AI devour those profits?

**The core conclusion of inverting the question**: Xiaomi's biggest industry risk is not the failure of any single business, but resource dilution caused by "fighting on four fronts." The proof that "ecosystem synergy" value exceeds the cost of "divided focus" is, so far, incomplete.
