## I. Five-Year Revenue and Profit Trend

### 1.1 Core Financial Data Overview

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Total revenue (RMB billion) | 328.3 | 280.0 | 271.0 | 365.9 | 457.3 |
| YoY growth | +33.5% | -14.7% | -3.2% | +35.0% | +25.0% |
| Adjusted net profit (RMB billion) | 22.0 | 8.5 | 19.3 | 27.2 | 39.2 |
| YoY growth | +69.5% | -61.4% | +126.3% | +41.3% | +43.8% |
| GAAP net profit (RMB billion) | ~22.0 | ~8.5 | ~19.3 | ~27.2 | 41.6 |
| Overall gross margin | ~17.7% | ~17.0% | 21.2% | 20.9% | 22.3% |

**Data sources**: Xiaomi Corporation annual reports (2021-2025), cross-validated with Xinhuanet, Securities Market Weekly, and iyiou.com.

### 1.2 Trend Interpretation

**Revenue traced a "V-shaped reversal"**: 2022-2023 saw two consecutive years of declining revenue amid weak global smartphone demand and macroeconomic headwinds. A strong recovery began in 2024, and 2025 revenue hit an all-time high of RMB 457.3 billion.

**Profit growth has consistently outpaced revenue growth**: in 2024-2025 profit grew 41.3% and 43.8% respectively, well above revenue growth of 35% and 25%, reflecting improved operating efficiency and a better product mix.

However, note that the gap between 2025 GAAP net profit (RMB 41.6 billion) and adjusted net profit (RMB 39.2 billion) reached RMB 2.4 billion, driven mainly by fair-value gains on equity investments. Buffett is wary of this kind of non-operating gain — "paper profit is not real profit."

**Rating: ★★★★ (4/5)**
> Revenue has recovered strongly and profit growth is respectable, but the company still sits in the low-margin band typical of hardware businesses. The gap between GAAP and adjusted profit warrants ongoing attention.

### 2.2 Key Profitability Metrics

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| ROE (return on equity) | 11.3% | 13.3% | ~19.0% |
| ROA (return on assets) | — | — | ~8.2% |
| Overall gross margin | 21.2% | 20.9% | 22.3% |
| Adjusted net margin | 7.1% | 7.4% | 8.6% |

**Data sources**: GuruFocus, Alpha Spread, Xiaomi annual reports.

### 2.3 Key Observations

**Declining smartphone gross margin is the biggest concern**: the 2025 phone gross margin fell from 12.6% to 10.9%, while revenue declined 2.8% year over year. The main driver was a global surge in memory chip prices (prices for the same specification nearly quadrupled year over year), which pushed up costs. Xiaomi internally expects phone shipments to decline 13% in 2026, and IDC forecasts a 12.9% decline in global smartphone shipments — a significant headwind for a company where phones still account for 41% of revenue.

**Internet services is the "profit cash cow"**: it contributes only 8% of revenue but carries a gross margin as high as 76.5%, generating a disproportionate share of profit. This is the part of Xiaomi's business model that most resembles what Buffett prefers — asset-light, high repeat usage, close to a "toll-booth" economy.

**Automotive gross margin is rising quickly**: it climbed from 18.5% in 2024 to 24.3% in 2025, and operating profit turned positive for the first time (RMB 900 million). On the other hand, full-year operating profit of only RMB 900 million on RMB 106.1 billion of revenue implies an operating margin below 1% — still a long way from genuinely "making money."

**The improvement in ROE deserves attention**: it rose from 11.3% in 2023 to roughly 19% in 2025. Note, however, that part of this improvement is attributable to the non-operating investment gains included in 2025 GAAP net profit. Calculated on adjusted net profit, ROE is roughly 17-18%, still a solid level.

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> Overall gross margin is trending upward, with internet services the standout. But declining phone margins combined with rising memory prices are a near-term headwind. For Buffett, the ceiling on a hardware company's profitability is the central concern — "making hardware is like riding a bicycle into the wind."

## III. Cash Flow Analysis

### 3.1 Key Cash Flow Data

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Net operating cash flow | ~RMB 39.3bn | ~RMB 36.4bn | -7.4% |
| Capital expenditure | ~RMB 10.9bn | ~RMB 21.1bn | +93.6% |
| Free cash flow (FCF) | ~RMB 28.4bn | ~RMB 15.2bn | -46.5% |
| Cash reserves (incl. investments) | ~RMB 175.1bn | ~RMB 232.6bn | +32.8% |

**Data sources**: Xiaomi annual reports, Investing.com cash flow statements, GuruFocus.

### 3.2 The Impact of the Automotive Business on Capex

2025 capital expenditure jumped from RMB 10.9 billion to RMB 21.1 billion, nearly doubling. This was driven mainly by capacity expansion in the automotive business — Xiaomi delivered 411,000 vehicles in 2025 (versus just 137,000 in 2024), a 200% increase.

**The "deterioration" in free cash flow needs to be viewed dialectically**:

- On the surface, FCF plunged from RMB 28.4 billion to RMB 15.2 billion, a 46.5% decline, which looks concerning
- In substance, however, this is deliberate, strategic capital deployment. The automotive business turned operating-profit-positive in 2025, meaning this spending is starting to generate returns
- Q4 2025 operating cash flow was only RMB 614 million (versus RMB 5.47 billion in Q3), suggesting there may have been large year-end inventory build or seasonal swings in receivables

**Buffett's view**: "When a company needs continuous, heavy capital investment just to maintain its competitiveness, its economic goodwill is heavily discounted." Xiaomi's automotive business is in a heavy capital-investment phase, which runs counter to the capital-light model Buffett favors. The question is: when does the automotive business turn from a "cash-devouring beast" into a "money-printing machine"?

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> Operating cash flow holding above RMB 36 billion is a positive signal, but heavy capital investment in the automotive business has sharply reduced FCF. For a value investor, FCF has limited reference value at this stage — steady-state FCF should be reassessed once automotive capex peaks and rolls over.

| Debt-to-assets ratio | ~49% | Healthy |
| Current ratio | ~1.33 | Reasonable |
| Quick ratio | ~0.90 | Acceptable |

**Data sources**: Xiaomi 2025 annual report, Zhihu financial analysis, Investing.com balance sheet.

### 4.2 Analysis

**Xiaomi's balance sheet is one of its biggest highlights**. RMB 196.5 billion in net cash means:
- Even if revenue fell 30%, the cash reserves could sustain operations for more than 10 years
- There is ample "ammunition" to fund continued investment in the automotive business
- The company has the capacity for counter-cyclical M&A or buybacks (in fact, from September 2025 to January 2026 Xiaomi has repurchased roughly 206 million shares, about 0.79% of total shares outstanding)

**Buffett would like this balance sheet** — "Only when the tide goes out do you discover who's been swimming naked." Xiaomi is clearly wearing a heavy-duty swimsuit.

One point does warrant attention, though: a quick ratio of 0.90, below 1, means that excluding inventory, current assets are slightly below current liabilities. That is not unusual for a manufacturing business, but it bears ongoing monitoring.

**Rating: ★★★★★ (5/5)**
> Net cash of RMB 196.5 billion far exceeds interest-bearing debt. Financial condition is extremely sound — this is the "safety cushion" Buffett values most.

## VI. Margin-of-Safety Assessment

### 6.1 DCF Valuation (Based on Free Cash Flow)

Using 2025 free cash flow of RMB 15.2 billion as the base, we ran a three-scenario DCF valuation:

| Scenario | FCF growth (Yr 1-5) | FCF growth (Yr 6-10) | Terminal growth | WACC | Value per share (HK$) | Margin of safety |
|------|--------------|---------------|-----------|------|-------------|---------|
| Conservative | 10% | 6% | 2.0% | 12% | 18.36 | **-24%** |
| Base case | 15% | 8% | 2.5% | 10% | 25.74 | **11%** |
| Optimistic | 20% | 10% | 3.0% | 9% | 36.58 | **38%** |

**Key findings**:

- **In the conservative scenario, the current share price already exceeds the DCF valuation** — meaning that if Xiaomi's FCF grows at just 10% (with a 12% WACC), the current price carries no margin of safety
- **In the base case, the margin of safety is only 11%** — far below the 30%+ margin of safety Buffett typically requires
- **Only in the optimistic scenario is there a 38% margin of safety** — but that requires FCF to grow at 20% for five straight years

### 6.2 Reverse DCF: What Does the Current Share Price Imply?

Working backward with a WACC of 10% and terminal growth of 2.5%:

- The current HK$22.80 price implies an EV of roughly RMB 343.8 billion
- EV / adjusted net profit = 8.8x
- **The market's implied pessimism**: with zero FCF growth, the DCF value would be about HK$15.60; the current price implies the market is pricing in roughly 5-8% annual FCF growth

This suggests the market is essentially pricing Xiaomi as "a low-growth hardware company," almost entirely ignoring the growth potential of the automotive business and the high margins of internet services.

### 6.3 Fair P/E Valuation

| Method | Fair P/E | Implied share price (HK$) | vs. current |
|------|--------|-------------|---------|

### 6.4 Composite Margin-of-Safety Judgment

| Method | Fair value range (HK$) | Current price | Margin of safety |
|------|------------------|---------|---------|
| FCF-DCF (conservative-base) | 18-26 | 22.80 | Minimal to none |
| Fair P/E method | 20-33 | 22.80 | 0-31% |
| SOTP (see next section) | 41-45 | 22.80 | 44-50% |

**Buffett-style conclusion**: A strict FCF-based valuation shows an insufficient margin of safety. But the SOTP method suggests the market may be systematically undervaluing the sum of Xiaomi's parts. This valuation gap is itself a signal worth pondering.

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> An insufficient margin of safety under the DCF method is the biggest concern. SOTP points to potential undervaluation, but that depends on whether the automotive and internet businesses can unlock standalone value.

| Net cash | — | — | Face value | 196.5 |
| **Total** | | | | **970.0-1,076.1** |

### 7.2 Per-Share Value Calculation

| Scenario | Auto P/S | Total value (RMB bn) | Per share (HK$) | vs. current |
|------|--------|-------------|------------|--------|
| Low valuation | 0.8x | 970.0 | 40.93 | +80% |
| Mid valuation | 1.2x | 1,012.4 | 42.72 | +87% |
| High valuation | 1.8x | 1,076.1 | 45.41 | +99% |

### 7.3 Key Assumptions and Uncertainties in the SOTP

**Issues that need to be honestly noted**:

1. **Standalone valuation of internet services is contentious**: internet services (advertising + gaming + fintech) are highly dependent on the phone and IoT user base, and should not be valued as a standalone internet company. If phone users churn, internet services would shrink too. A 20x P/E may be too high.

2. **The estimated phone operating margin may be overly optimistic**: 5% operating margin is an estimate. Given the expected 2026 memory-price surge and shipment decline, actual margins could be lower.

3. **Comparability of the auto P/S multiple is questionable**: NIO trades at roughly 0.8x P/S, BYD at roughly 1.2x. Assigning Xiaomi's auto business 1.2x P/S already implies fairly high growth expectations.

4. **The conglomerate-discount problem**: diversified business conglomerates typically carry a 15-25% conglomerate discount. Applying a 20% discount would bring the SOTP valuation down from HK$42.72 to roughly HK$34.

**If internet services P/E is lowered to 15x plus a 20% conglomerate discount**:
- Total value ≈ (111.6+129.0+336.0+127.3+196.5) x 0.80 = RMB 720.3bn
- Per share ≈ HK$30.3
- Margin of safety ≈ 25%

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> On the surface, SOTP shows substantial undervaluation, but once business interdependence and the conglomerate discount are factored in, the actual margin of safety narrows to roughly 25%. How to value internet services is the crux of the SOTP disagreement.

## VIII. Core Financial Judgment from a Buffett Lens

### 8.1 The "Four Pockets" Check

Buffett evaluates a company by first asking four core questions:

| Check item | Xiaomi's showing | Assessment |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Is it understandable?** | Phones/IoT/internet are understandable; the automotive business adds complexity | Moderate |
| **Does it have a durable competitive advantage?** | Value-for-money plus ecosystem stickiness, but lacks an Apple-style brand-premium moat | Weak |
| **Is management honest and capable?** | Lei Jun has strong founder spirit and heavy R&D investment (RMB 33.1bn/year) | Fairly good |
| **Is the price reasonable?** | P/E of 13.8x is near a historical low, but the DCF-based margin of safety is insufficient | To be determined |

### 8.2 Core Conclusion

**Bullish factors**:
- The balance sheet is extremely healthy, with net cash of RMB 196.5 billion providing top-tier resilience
- P/E of 13.8x is near a historical bottom, and market sentiment is extremely pessimistic (RSI 23.53, severely oversold)
- The automotive business achieved positive operating profit, validating the viability of the business model
- Internet services' high margin (76.5%) provides a profit safety cushion

**Bearish factors**:
- The phone business faces a double blow from rising memory prices and falling shipments (expected to decline 13% in 2026)
- The automotive business remains in a heavy capital-investment phase, pressuring FCF
- 2025 FCF was only RMB 15.2 billion, down 46.5% from 2024
- High R&D spending (RMB 33.1bn/year), while a long-term investment, erodes near-term profit

**Buffett's likely judgment**:

> "Xiaomi is an interesting company, but it's not within my circle of competence. It makes phones, home appliances, internet services, and cars all at once — it reminds me of those conglomerates that do a bit of everything. When a company tries to fight on four fronts at once, I'd rather invest in a business that is absolutely the best at one thing."

> "That said, its balance sheet is impressive. Net cash of RMB 196.5 billion means that even in a worst-case scenario, this company won't go bankrupt. If the price is low enough — low enough that, net of cash, you're getting the business almost for free — that's worth serious consideration."

### 8.3 Composite Valuation Range

| Method | Fair value range (HK$) |
|------|------------------|
| FCF-DCF (conservative-base) | 18-26 |
| Fair P/E method | 20-33 |
| SOTP (with discount) | 30-45 |
| **Composite judgment** | **25-35** |

**The current share price of HK$22.80 sits at the low end of the composite fair-value range.**

If the share price falls further into the HK$18-20 range (i.e., EV / adjusted net profit < 7x), it would enter a value-investing zone with a significant margin of safety.
