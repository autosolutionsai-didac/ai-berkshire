## One-Line Conclusion

Corning is a core beneficiary of the AI infrastructure wave, with strong growth in its optical communications business (+35% in 2025), and massive long-term agreements with NVIDIA and Meta locking in years of growth visibility. But the current valuation (GAAP PE of ~113x, Core PE of ~82x) already fully reflects — and may overdraw — optimistic expectations, leaving a very thin margin of safety. This is a classic case of "a good company but not a good price."

## Dimension One: Business Model Analysis (Duan Yongping's Perspective)

### 1. Nature of the Business Model and Revenue Structure

At its core, Corning is a **materials-science platform company** that applies core capabilities in glass, ceramics, and optical physics to multiple high-barrier end markets.

### 2. Moat Verification Point by Point

- **Technological barrier ★★★★★**: 50 years of accumulated fiber-optic technology; Contour fiber product is exclusive
- **Switching costs ★★★★☆**: deeply locked in via long-term agreements with Meta ($6.0 billion), NVIDIA ($3.2 billion), and Apple ($2.5 billion)
- **Scale effects ★★★★☆**: world's largest fiber/cable producer, vertically integrated
- **Brand ★★★☆☆**: strong brand recognition in the B2B market
- **Network effects ★★☆☆☆**: inherently lacking for a materials manufacturer

### 3. Assessment Against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

- **Differentiation**: Strong. Exclusive position in the Contour fiber market
- **Pricing power**: Moderately strong (cyclical pricing power, not permanent)
- **Sustainable competitive advantage**: Moderately strong

## Dimension Three: Industry and Competition Analysis (Munger's Perspective)

- **Global fiber and cable market**: projected to exceed $150 billion by 2033
- **AI data center optical interconnect**: AI data centers require 5x the connectivity of traditional data centers
- Corning holds a clear leadership advantage in high-end, AI-data-center-grade products
- Chinese competitor Yangtze Optical Fibre and Cable (YOFC) has a price advantage in the commodity fiber market, but the gap at the high end persists

## Dimension Four: Risk and Management Assessment (Li Lu's Perspective)

- **CEO Wendell Weeks**: in the role for over 20 years, strong execution; the Springboard plan has repeatedly been over-delivered
- **Heavy management selling**: the CEO cashed out $250 million in early 2025, and executives collectively sold over $10 million
- **Geopolitics**: 34 U.S.-based factories form a "geopolitical moat"
- **10-year certainty**: fiber's foundational position is solid, but there is uncertainty around the AI capex cycle and silicon-photonics technology

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (7 points)
1. AI optical interconnect represents decade-scale structural demand
2. Massive long-term agreements (Meta $6.0 billion, NVIDIA $3.2 billion) lock in growth
3. Vertical integration plus U.S. manufacturing = a dual moat
4. Margins continue to improve
5. The solar business is becoming a second growth engine
6. Strong management execution
7. Supply-demand tightness supports pricing power

### Bear Case (7 points)
1. GAAP PE of 113x is far above the historical average
2. Cyclicality risk in AI capex
3. Heavy management selling
4. Non-optical-communications businesses are a drag
5. Excessive customer concentration
6. Long-term threat from Chinese competitors
7. Heavy capex cycle
