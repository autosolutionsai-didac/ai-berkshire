## Core Conclusion Upfront

> "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get." -- Warren Buffett

Wuliangye is in the midst of an **unprecedented financial-narrative rupture**: the 2025 annual report retroactively reduced revenue by 30.3 billion yuan and net profit by 16.9 billion yuan due to an accounting-error correction, causing headline 2025 results to fall off a cliff. On the other hand, the annualized 2026 Q1 profit has already recovered to the level of 32.2 billion yuan, and the company has committed to an annual dividend of no less than 20 billion yuan. At the current share price of 92.26 yuan, the 2024 PE is only 11.2x, with a dividend yield above 5.5%. **On the surface this looks like an accounting disaster; in substance it means the valuation framework needs to be re-anchored.**

*Data source: Wuliangye's annual reports for each year, 21jingji.com, Securities Times*

### 1.2 Trend Analysis

**2020-2024: A golden period of five consecutive years of double-digit growth.** Revenue grew from 57.3 billion to 89.2 billion yuan, a CAGR of roughly 11.7%; net profit grew from 20.0 billion to 31.9 billion yuan, a CAGR of roughly 12.4%. EPS grew from 5.14 yuan to 8.21 yuan, a compound growth rate of roughly 12.4%.

**2025: An accounting-error correction caused headline results to "fall off a cliff."** On April 30, 2026 (the very last moment before the annual-report disclosure deadline), Wuliangye issued an "Announcement on Correction of Prior-Period Accounting Errors," changing its revenue-recognition basis from "recognized upon shipment" to "recognized after completion of terminal sell-through." This reduced 2025 revenue by 30.3 billion yuan and net profit by 16.9 billion yuan.

**Key judgment:** The 2025 "cliff" in the annual report is not operational deterioration but a one-time shock from switching accounting bases. However, this also exposes **the possibility that Wuliangye may have recognized revenue prematurely for years** -- i.e., counting goods as sold once they were pushed into distributor warehouses, rather than actually sold to consumers. The Shenzhen Stock Exchange has already issued an inquiry letter demanding an explanation.

**On the other hand**, this adjustment can also be viewed as management "scraping the poison off the bone" -- a positive signal of returning to conservative accounting. Under the new basis, 2026 Q1 delivered revenue of 22.838 billion yuan and net profit attributable to parent of 8.063 billion yuan, an annualized figure of roughly 32.2 billion yuan, essentially flat with 2024's 31.85 billion yuan, indicating that underlying earnings power has not collapsed.

**Counter-argument:** Although Wuliangye's brand power is weaker than Moutai's, it holds an absolute leadership position within the strong-aroma (nongxiang) baijiu category. A 77% gross margin would be top-tier in almost any other industry. The question is whether the "eternal number two" positioning can continue to create value growth.

## III. Cash Flow Analysis -- Is Baijiu the Best Cash-Flow Business?

### 3.1 Core Cash Flow Data

| Year | Net operating cash flow (billion yuan) | Capital expenditure (billion yuan) | Free cash flow (billion yuan, estimate) | Free cash flow / net profit |
|------|-------------------|-------------|-------------------|----------------|
| 2020 | ~33.0 (estimate) | ~2.0 (estimate) | ~31.0 | ~155% |
| 2021 | ~37.0 (estimate) | ~2.2 (estimate) | ~34.8 | ~149% |
| 2022 | ~40.0 (estimate) | ~2.5 (estimate) | ~37.5 | ~140% |
| 2023 | 41.742 | 2.957 | 38.785 | 128% |
| 2024 | 33.940 | 2.666 | 31.274 | 98% |

*Data source: Wuliangye annual reports. 2020-2022 figures are estimates based on industry data and are so labeled.*

### 3.2 Cash Flow Quality Assessment

**Operating cash flow / net profit ratio:** Has stayed above 100% over the long run, indicating high earnings quality. In 2024, operating cash flow of 33.9 billion yuan was still slightly above net profit of 31.9 billion yuan (a ratio of 106%, though roughly 98% after adjusting for changes in advance receipts). The 18.7% decline in 2024 operating cash flow was mainly due to:
1. Higher dividend payouts (19.2 billion yuan vs. 15.9 billion yuan the prior year)
2. Changes in the pace of distributor payments

**Extremely low capital expenditure:** Over the past 7 years, cumulative capex / net profit has totaled only about 6%. This is the core appeal of the baijiu business -- **competitiveness can be maintained with almost no reinvestment.** The older the distillery cellars, the more valuable they become; the older the brand, the more valuable it becomes -- there's no need to keep burning cash the way tech companies do.

> "The ideal business is one that requires no significant capital reinvestment yet generates high returns." -- Buffett

**Free cash flow:** Estimated 2024 free cash flow of roughly 31.3 billion yuan, against the current market cap of 358.1 billion yuan, gives an **FCF yield of about 8.7%**. This means that if Wuliangye distributed all of its free cash flow to shareholders, investors would recoup their entire investment in under 12 years.

### 3.3 Is Baijiu the Best Cash-Flow Business?

**Conclusion: Yes, baijiu is one of the business models in the Chinese market that comes closest to being a "money-printing machine."**

Reasons:
1. **Asset-light operations:** Capex/revenue is only about 3%, far below manufacturing's 20-30%
2. **Advance-payment model:** Distributors pay before they take delivery, so the company enjoys long-term use of upstream and downstream funds
3. **Inventory appreciates the longer it sits:** Unlike electronics, which depreciate, baijiu inventory is an asset that "gets better with age"
4. **Almost zero accounts receivable:** The baijiu industry is cash-before-goods, so receivables are extremely low

**On the other hand:** The baijiu industry faces long-term structural challenges -- China's declining population, changing drinking habits among young people, and restrictions on government-consumption spending. High-end baijiu is fundamentally a form of **social currency**, and demand elasticity depends on macroeconomic conditions and shifts in social culture.

### 4.2 In-Depth Interpretation

**Cash and cash equivalents of 127.4 billion yuan -- "so much money it doesn't know what to do with it"**
Wuliangye holds 127.4 billion yuan of cash on its books, 67.6% of total assets. This far exceeds what's needed for day-to-day operations and is a double-edged sword:
- Upside: extremely strong risk resilience -- it could sustain itself for years even if revenue fell to zero
- Downside: a huge pile of cash sitting in the bank earning interest represents low capital efficiency. If management neither pays dividends nor buys back shares, it is effectively "destroying shareholder value"

**Contract liabilities of 11.69 billion yuan (+70.3%) -- stronger distributor willingness to prepay**
Contract liabilities are advance payments made by distributors and reflect channel confidence. The 70% surge in contract liabilities in 2024 suggests, on one hand, that distributors still have confidence in Wuliangye, but on the other hand may also reflect year-end "channel stuffing" that inflated advance receipts. Contract liabilities rose further to 13.46 billion yuan at the end of 2025.

**Interest-bearing debt near zero -- "zero-leverage" operations**
Wuliangye carries almost no bank loans or bonds, running its operations entirely on its own capital and upstream/downstream float. This is a textbook Buffett-favored enterprise type.

> "I've always liked companies that don't need to borrow money." -- Buffett

### 7.4 Margin of Safety Assessment

> "The margin of safety always depends on the price you pay." -- Buffett

**Margin of safety viewed across multiple dimensions:**

1. **PE dimension:** 11.2x PE (2024 EPS basis) vs. a 10-year midpoint of 25x, a 55% discount
2. **Dividend-yield dimension:** A 5.6% dividend yield, far above the 10-year government bond yield (~2.0%), providing a "downside floor" of support
3. **Book-cash dimension:** 127.4 billion yuan of cash on the books, 35.6% of the 358.1-billion-yuan total market cap -- buy Wuliangye, and 35% of it is cash
4. **FCF dimension:** An 8.7% free-cash-flow yield, implying payback in under 12 years
5. **Probability-weighted valuation dimension:** Even in the bear scenario (15x PE, profit declining 3% annually), the target price is still 113.6 yuan (+23%)

**Conclusion: The current share price carries a significant margin of safety.** Even if Wuliangye's profit continues to decline slightly over the next 3 years, investors are highly likely to still achieve a positive return through dividends alone (5.6%/year) plus valuation recovery.

**Risks to watch, however:**
1. The accounting-error correction could trigger regulatory penalties, and the investor-trust crisis may persist
2. If the baijiu industry enters a "lost decade" (similar to Japanese sake), the valuation midpoint could shift permanently lower
3. Management governance quality is in question -- why was the correction made only at the very last moment before the annual-report deadline?

But if you have fundamental doubts about management's integrity, no price is cheap enough.
