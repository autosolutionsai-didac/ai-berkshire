## II. Deep Dive into the Profitability Trend

### 2.1 Evolution of Core Margins

| Net profit growth | +8.42% | +8.68% | +73.76% | +17.33% | +8.87% | Growth rate gradually converging |

**Key finding**: Net profit grew from RMB 908 million to RMB 2.191 billion, a 141% increase, while revenue over the same period grew only 22.4%. This means profit growth came **almost entirely from margin expansion rather than revenue growth**.

### 2.2 Analysis of the Gross Margin Improvement

Gross margin rose from 15.51% in 2021 to 24.78% in 2025, a cumulative gain of 9.27 percentage points. Drivers:

**(1) Falling steel prices (cyclical factor)**
- The main raw material for forklifts is steel (hot-rolled coil, sections, etc.); steel prices have been falling steadily since their 2021 peak
- The average rebar price was about RMB 5,000/ton in 2021, falling to roughly RMB 3,500-3,800/ton by 2025, a decline of about 25-30%
- Based on industry experience, steel accounts for roughly 15-20% of forklift manufacturing costs, so the price decline likely lifted gross margin by about 3-5 percentage points
- **Opinion**: This is a cyclical external tailwind and is not sustainable. If steel prices rebound, gross margin will come under pressure.

**(2) Product-mix upgrade (structural factor)**
- Hangcha Group has been transitioning toward electric forklifts, high-end warehouse trucks, and AGV driverless forklifts in recent years
- Electric forklifts typically carry gross margins 3-5 percentage points higher than internal-combustion forklifts
- According to data from the China Association of Construction Machinery, Hangcha Group's share of electric-forklift sales has been rising year after year
- **Evidence**: From 2021 to 2025, the company has kept ramping up R&D spending and its push toward intelligent equipment
- **Opinion**: This is a structural improvement factor with high sustainability.

**(3) Scale effects**
- Revenue grew from RMB 14.49 billion in 2021 to RMB 17.739 billion in 2025, an increase of 22.4%
- However, given that the gross-margin improvement far outpaced revenue growth, the contribution from scale effects is relatively limited
- **Opinion**: Scale effects contributed somewhat but were not the main driver of the gross margin improvement.

**(4) Exchange-rate factor (export business)**
- Overseas revenue accounts for roughly 30-35% of Hangcha Group's total, and the RMB depreciation cycle has been favorable for export-margin economics
- Since 2022 the RMB has generally weakened against the US dollar, which benefits export settlement

**Overall assessment**: Roughly 60% of the gross-margin improvement is attributable to structural factors (product upgrade + exports), and about 40% to cyclical factors (falling steel prices). **The structural factors are sustainable, but a rebound in steel prices is a potential risk.**

2. **Inventory buildup**: inventory turnover days rose from 45.61 in 2021 to 73.59 in 2025, an increase of 28 days, tying up a substantial amount of cash

### 4.2 Accounts Receivable — the Most Alarming Signal

| Year | Receivable turnover days | Revenue for the year (RMB 100M) | Estimated receivables (RMB 100M) |
|:---:|:--------------:|:-----------:|:---------------:|
| 2021 | **23.36 days** | 144.90 | ~9.3 |
| 2022 | 30.96 days | 144.12 | ~12.2 |
| 2023 | 33.29 days | 162.99 | ~14.9 |
| 2024 | 44.52 days | 167.50 | ~20.4 |
| 2025 | **54.66 days** | 177.39 | ~26.6 |
| Q1 2026 | **57.92 days** | 49.17 (Q1) | ~31.3 (annualized estimate) |

**Extent of deterioration**: Receivable turnover days surged from 23 days in 2021 to 55 days in 2025 — **more than doubling** in four years. Q1 2026 deteriorated further to 58 days.

**Estimated accounts-receivable balance** (based on turnover days / 365 × revenue):
- 2021: 23.36/365 × RMB 14.49 billion ≈ RMB 930 million
- 2025: 54.66/365 × RMB 17.739 billion ≈ RMB 2.66 billion
- **Increase: 186%**, versus revenue growth of only 22.4% over the same period

**Severity assessment**:
- This is a **major red flag**. Receivables growth (186%) far outstrips revenue growth (22.4%)
- This suggests the company is significantly loosening credit terms for downstream customers in order to sustain revenue growth
- Alternatively: downstream customers (particularly small and mid-sized logistics and manufacturing firms) may be facing cash-flow strain, slowing their payments

**Impact on net-profit quality**:
- Had the 2021 turnover level of 23 days held, 2025 receivables would be roughly RMB 1.1 billion; the actual figure is roughly RMB 2.7 billion — about RMB 1.6 billion higher
- The revenue behind this "excess receivables" of RMB 1.6 billion has already been booked to the income statement but has not yet converted to cash
- If part of these receivables eventually turns into bad debt, it would materially hit net profit

> Buffett's words: "**Rapid growth in accounts receivable is often the result of management loosening credit policy to mask a slowdown in revenue — this is a danger signal that value investors must watch closely.**"

**Possible mitigating explanation (counter-argument)**:
- Part of the increase in turnover days may reflect the growing share of export business — overseas customers' payment cycles are inherently longer than domestic customers'
- Large enterprises (such as logistics giants) are an increasing share of the forklift industry's downstream customer base, and payment terms of 60-90 days are common industry practice for such customers
- Even so, a jump from 23 days to 55 days remains a striking increase

The simultaneous deterioration in inventory and receivables suggests **overall working-capital efficiency is declining**.

## V. DuPont ROE Decomposition

### 5.1 Overall ROE Trend

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| ROE (diluted) | 16.22% | 15.73% | 19.96% | 19.89% | 18.88% |
| ROE (weighted average) | 17.22% | 16.70% | 22.23% | 21.64% | 20.34% |

**ROE has held steady in a 16-22% range**, staying above 15% every year since 2021, peaking in 2023 before easing back slightly while remaining at a fairly high level overall.

### 5.2 Three-Factor DuPont Decomposition

DuPont formula: ROE = Net margin × Total asset turnover × Equity multiplier

| Year | Net margin | Total asset turnover (est.) | Equity multiplier | = **ROE** |
|:---:|:-----:|:---------------:|:-------:|:--------:|
| 2021 | 6.84% | ~1.2x | 1.72 | ~14.1% (actual 16.2%) |
| 2022 | 7.45% | ~1.1x | 1.72 | ~14.1% (actual 15.7%) |
| 2023 | 11.27% | ~1.1x | 1.50 | ~18.6% (actual 20.0%) |
| 2024 | 13.11% | ~1.0x | 1.50 | ~19.7% (actual 19.9%) |
| 2025 | 13.07% | ~1.0x | 1.59 | ~20.8% (actual 18.9%) |

> Note: total asset turnover is estimated from known data. Equity multiplier = 1/(1 - debt-to-assets ratio).

**DuPont decomposition conclusions**:

1. **Net margin (the primary driver of the ROE improvement)**:
   - Rose from 6.84% to 13.07%, more than doubling
   - The core reason ROE climbed from 16% to 19%
   - See Section II for a sustainability assessment

2. **Total asset turnover (flat to slightly declining)**:
   - Fell from ~1.2x to ~1.0x, a modest decline
   - Caused by the rapid buildup in receivables and inventory, which has weighed on asset-turnover efficiency
   - This is a **negative signal**: the same asset base is now generating less revenue

3. **Equity multiplier (leverage came down, then stabilized)**:
   - Fell from 1.72 to 1.50-1.59, stabilizing after deleveraging
   - Indicates the company is not "dressing up" its ROE with borrowed money — a trait **Buffett favors**
   - Leverage sits at a conservative level

**Core judgment**: Hangcha Group's high ROE (18-20%) comes mainly from **a high net margin (13%) × a reasonable turnover rate (1.0x) × moderate leverage (1.5x)**. This is a healthy combination, not an inflated ROE propped up by leverage.

### 5.3 ROE Sustainability Analysis

| Factor | Assessment | Direction of impact |
|:---|:---:|:--------:|
| Can net margin be sustained? | Partly cyclical (steel prices), partly structural (electrification) | Stable to slightly lower |
| Can turnover improve? | Receivables and inventory deteriorating; unlikely to improve near-term | Drags on ROE |
| Will leverage rise? | Management currently conservative, no clear sign of increased leverage | Neutral |
| **Overall ROE trend** | **ROE expected to be in the 16-19% range in 2026-2027** | Slightly lower |

**Rating: ★★★★** — The quality of ROE is good (not leverage-driven), but declining turnover and cyclicality in margins are potential drags.

> Buffett's words: "**If a company can sustain an ROE above 15% over the long run without relying on heavy borrowing, it's an excellent compounding machine.**" Hangcha Group displayed this characteristic from 2020 to 2025.
