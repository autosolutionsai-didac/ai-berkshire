## III. Product Flywheel: How Do Scale Effects Operate?

### 3.1 The foundation of scale effects

Hangcha Group ships more than 300,000 units a year (2025 industry-wide sales were 1.4518 million units, giving Hangcha a market share of roughly 20-25%), making it the world's No. 7-8 forklift manufacturer by ranking.

Sources of scale effect:

| Source of scale advantage | Specific manifestation | Quantitative evidence |
|------------|---------|:-------:|
| Procurement | Bulk-purchase discounts on steel, lithium batteries, motors, and other commodity inputs | 2025 gross margin of 24.78%, above the industry average |
| Production | Automated lines at bases such as the Hangcha Henghan Science & Technology Park and Shiqiao Science & Innovation Park | 600+ robots and 20+ automated production lines |
| R&D | R&D spending amortized over production of millions of units | R&D expense ratio of roughly 3-4% — a sizeable absolute figure but low once amortized |
| Distribution | 20+ overseas subsidiaries plus a dense domestic dealer network | Customer-acquisition cost per unit far below smaller manufacturers |
| Service | Coverage of the parts and repair network | The parts business shows high-margin characteristics |

### 3.2 How electrification is reshaping the flywheel

This is the most critical structural change within Hangcha's business model.

**The flywheel in the internal-combustion era:**
```
Scale production → cost advantage → price advantage → higher volume → greater scale
```
This is a **cost-driven flywheel** under commoditized competition, and its moat is relatively shallow.

**The flywheel in the electrification era (which Hangcha is building):**
```
In-house "three-electric" system → lower cost + performance differentiation → surge in electric forklift sales →
scale effects further reduce cost → more R&D investment → technology leadership → higher premium
```

**Key chain of facts**:
1. In 2018, Hangcha formed a joint venture with CATL (Pengcheng New Energy) to develop custom lithium batteries for industrial vehicles
2. By 2025, full in-house development and manufacturing of the "three-electric" system (battery, motor, controller) was achieved
3. The share of electric forklifts in total sales continues to rise, with industry-wide electrification now at 73.61%
4. Among lithium-battery forklifts, the higher the share of Hangcha's self-developed batteries, the more pronounced the cost advantage

**Quantitative analysis**:
- Internal-combustion forklifts: the engine and transmission account for roughly 15-20% of cost, and Hangcha must buy these externally (mainly from Xinchai Co., which is also controlled by Qiu Jianping)
- Electric forklifts: the battery, motor, and controller account for roughly 30-40% of cost, and **Hangcha now develops and manufactures all of these in-house**
- Implication: every 10-percentage-point rise in electrification lifts Hangcha's supply-chain self-sufficiency by a corresponding notch

> Duan Yongping quote: "**Differentiation is the foundation of a moat. A product with no differentiation ends up in nothing but a price war.**" Electrification has opened a window for differentiation in forklifts — battery range, charging speed, and intelligence level have all become competitive dimensions, and Hangcha moved early and has executed well here.

### 3.3 Quantitative verification of scale effects

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Change over 4 years |
|:----|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:-------:|
| Revenue (RMB billion) | 14.490 | 14.412 | 16.299 | 16.750 | 17.739 | +22.4% |
| Gross margin | 15.51% | 17.78% | 20.78% | 23.55% | 24.78% | +9.27pp |
| Period expense ratio (estimated) | ~5.5% | ~7.0% | ~6.5% | ~7.0% | ~8.5% | +3.0pp |
| Net margin | 6.84% | 7.45% | 11.27% | 13.11% | 13.07% | +6.23pp |

**Analysis**:
- Revenue grew only 22.4%, yet gross margin rose 9.27 percentage points — **this is not entirely due to scale effects**
- Of the gross-margin improvement, roughly 60% comes from product-mix upgrading (electrification + higher-margin overseas sales) and roughly 40% from the decline in steel prices (a cyclical factor)
- The period expense ratio rose from 5.5% to 8.5%, indicating the company has not achieved a lower expense ratio as it scaled up in R&D, sales, and administration — spending actually increased
- **The true scale effect may not be as strong as it appears**

**Evidence indicates**: Hangcha's scale effect shows up mainly on the **cost side** (lowering per-unit material cost through bulk procurement and in-house development), not on the **expense side** (expense growth has roughly tracked revenue growth).

### 3.4 Fragile points in the flywheel

1. **The electrification technology barrier will erode** — once the whole industry masters in-house "three-electric" capability, the differentiation advantage will weaken
2. **Competition within the lithium-battery supply chain** — lithium-battery prices fell sharply in 2025, narrowing the cost gap between externally sourced and self-developed batteries
3. **A ceiling on scale effects** — the global forklift market is worth roughly RMB 100 billion; Hangcha already accounts for RMB 17.7 billion of that, and doubling again will require going global

## IV. Moat Analysis: Item by Item

Duan Yongping applies the strictest standard when judging a moat. As he put it: "**A so-called 'moat' means competitors find it very hard to take your business away from you.**"

### 4.1 Brand power ★★★☆

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Domestic brand awareness | Hangcha and Anhui Heli are jointly the top tier of domestic forklift brands, with high brand recognition |
| International brand awareness | Far behind Toyota, Kion, and Jungheinrich, but has some recognition in emerging markets |
| Brand pricing power | Limited — forklifts are a B2B industrial good, and customers care more about TCO than brand |
| Brand loyalty | Medium — the dealer network is relatively stable, but the switching cost for end users to change brands is not high |

**Judgment**: Hangcha has a brand, but not enough to support a meaningful brand premium. In the B2B industrial-goods space, a brand functions more as a "trust mark" than a "premium tool."

### 4.2 Switching costs ★★★

Analysis of customer switching costs:

| Source of switching cost | Level | Notes |
|-------------|:---:|------|
| Operating habits | Low | Operating logic is similar across brands, and drivers adapt quickly |
| Parts compatibility | Medium | Parts are not interchangeable across brands, so switching means writing off existing parts inventory |
| Service network | Medium-high | Hangcha's nationwide and global service network means switching brands requires rebuilding the service relationship |
| Telematics/fleet management system | Medium | Hangcha's telematics platform (the HC series), once integrated with a customer's management system, creates some stickiness |
| Finance-lease contracts | Low | Forklifts are often purchased via finance leases, and customers are free to switch brands once the lease ends |

**Judgment**: Switching costs are moderate. If a customer is deeply tied into Hangcha's service network and parts system, changing brands involves real friction, but it is not insurmountable. **In particular, large logistics companies typically run mixed fleets across multiple brands**, so their switching cost is lower.

### 4.3 Network effects ★★

The forklift industry has almost no network effect in the traditional sense:
- More people using Hangcha equipment does not make it more convenient for other users
- The only "weak network effect" that may exist:
  - Parts supply network: higher sales volume means denser parts outlets, making repairs more convenient for users
  - Used-equipment market: brands with a larger installed base command higher resale values

**Judgment**: Hangcha has essentially no network effect. This is one of the fundamental reasons its moat is not wider.

### 4.4 Scale effects ★★★★

This is Hangcha's strongest moat dimension.

**Specific advantages**:
1. **Procurement leverage**: With annual sales of RMB 17.7 billion, the company has meaningful bargaining power over suppliers
2. **Capacity utilization**: Producing 300,000+ units a year, fixed costs are amortized effectively
3. **Channel coverage**: 20+ overseas subsidiaries plus a dense domestic network keep per-point costs low
4. **R&D amortization**: Annual R&D spending of roughly RMB 500-600 million works out to only about RMB 2,000 per vehicle

**But note**:
- Anhui Heli is comparable in scale to Hangcha, so the two are in "symmetric competition" at the scale level
- A differentiated scale advantage requires product mix and technology leadership to actually materialize

### 4.5 Technology barriers ★★★

| Technology area | Barrier height | Notes |
|---------|:-------:|------|
| Three-electric system (battery/motor/controller) | ★★★★ | In-house development plus 8 years of joint-venture experience with CATL |
| Vehicle integration | ★★★ | 60 years of forklift manufacturing experience; tuning for reliability takes time |
| Hydrogen fuel-cell forklifts | ★★★ | 400+ units already deployed, industry-leading |
| AGV/smart logistics | ★★☆ | Capability gained through the acquisition of Guozi Robotics; integration results still to be verified |
| Software/telematics | ★★ | Some presence, but a clear gap versus technology companies |

**Key judgment**:
- In-house "three-electric" development is the most substantive technology barrier today — but it is not permanent
- The tuning experience for vehicle reliability (NVH, durability, safety) takes time to accumulate — this is a "hidden barrier"
- In AGV and smart logistics, Hangcha is still in a "buying capability" phase, and technology leadership has not yet been established

### 4.6 Composite moat score

| Dimension | Rating | Weight | Weighted score |
|:----|:---:|:---:|:--------:|
| Brand power | ★★★☆ | 15% | 0.525 |
| Switching costs | ★★★ | 20% | 0.60 |
| Network effects | ★★ | 10% | 0.20 |
| Scale effects | ★★★★ | 30% | 1.20 |
| Technology barriers | ★★★ | 25% | 0.75 |
| **Composite** | **★★★☆** | **100%** | **3.28** |

**Final judgment**: Hangcha's moat is **moderately above average, but not wide**.

Duan Yongping's standard is: **"The best moat is one that competitors cannot replicate quickly, even with unlimited money."** Applying that standard:

- Can unlimited money replicate Hangcha's production capacity? **Yes** (build factories, buy equipment) — but it would take 3-5 years
- Can unlimited money replicate Hangcha's channel network? **Yes** — but it would take 5-10 years and enormous resources
- Can unlimited money replicate Hangcha's technical accumulation? **Partially** — the three-electric system can be bought, but 60 years of industrial know-how cannot
- Can unlimited money replicate Hangcha's position in the duopoly? **No** — customer mindshare and industry structure are not things money can change in the short term

> Duan Yongping quote: "**A moat is dynamic. Every few years you have to re-examine whether a company's moat is widening or narrowing.**" Hangcha's moat is evolving from "scale + channel" toward "technology + globalization" — the direction is right, but whether the new moat will prove deep enough remains to be verified.

## VII. Core Risks — Fragile Points at the Business-Model Level

### 7.1 Risk from weak pricing power

If steel prices rebound sharply, Hangcha's gross margin will come under pressure, and the company has limited ability to raise prices in response. Historically, gross margin was only 15.51% at the 2021 steel-price peak; if steel prices returned to 2021 levels, gross margin could fall back by 3-5 percentage points.

### 7.2 "Bad customer" risk — ballooning accounts receivable

Accounts receivable turnover days rose from 23 days in 2021 to 55 days in 2025, and continued to worsen to 58 days in 2026 Q1. This is a signal of **deteriorating business-model quality** — the company appears to be loosening credit policy to sustain revenue growth.

### 7.3 Concentration of overseas risk

43.38% of revenue comes from overseas, and that overseas revenue is concentrated in European and American markets. An escalation in trade friction would directly hit the company's growth engine.

### 7.4 Technology-route risk

If the forklift industry undergoes a major shift in technology route (for example, solid-state batteries completely replacing lithium batteries, or large-scale adoption of hydrogen fuel), Hangcha's in-house three-electric system might require fresh reinvestment.

### 7.5 Counter-evidence — positive factors that should not be ignored

| Risk factor | Counter-evidence |
|---------|---------|
| Receivables ballooning | Partly because overseas customers (43% of revenue) have longer payment terms, and overseas collection credit is generally better |
| Weak pricing power | The duopoly structure itself has maintained industry order, so the probability of a destructive price war is low |
| Technology disruption | The threat from new entrants such as BYD is limited — the B2B channel barrier in forklifts is very high |
| Trade risk | The Thailand and France plants are building "localized" capacity, which can help avoid tariffs |
