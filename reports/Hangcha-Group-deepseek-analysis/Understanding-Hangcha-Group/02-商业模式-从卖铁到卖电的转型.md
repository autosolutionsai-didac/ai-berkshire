# From "Selling Iron" to "Selling Electricity": A Transformation

## How does Hangcha make its money?

Gross margin rose from **15.51%** to **24.78%** over four years, and net profit grew **141%** — yet revenue grew only **22.4%**. Where exactly is this company earning more money?

The answer lies in its revenue structure. Hangcha doesn't disclose a precise product-line breakdown in its annual report, but based on public information we can reconstruct a rough picture:

| Business segment | Revenue share (estimated) | Gross margin characteristics | Growth trend |
|---------|:-------------:|:---------:|:-------:|
| Electric forklifts (complete units, lithium + lead-acid) | About **35-40%** | Medium-high (about 25-30%) | Rising rapidly |
| Internal-combustion forklifts (complete units) | About **25-30%** | Lower (about 18-22%) | Continuing to decline |
| Parts and aftermarket (components, repair, rental) | About **15-20%** | High (about 30-40%) | Stable |
| Intelligent logistics products (AGVs, unmanned forklifts, etc.) | About **3-5%** | Relatively high (about 30-35%) | High-speed growth |
| Other (aerial work platforms, cleaning equipment, etc.) | About **5-10%** | Medium | Stable |

(Source: Estimates based on Hangcha Group's 2025 annual report business descriptions and industry research reports; not a precise figure disclosed in the annual report)

**Revenue from the intelligent/smart business reached about 1 billion yuan in 2025**, and is expected to maintain high-speed growth going forward.

(Source: Brokerage research estimates)

But the more critical breakdown is: domestic vs. overseas.

| Region | 2025 revenue share | Year-over-year growth | Gross margin |
|:----|:-------------:|:-------:|:-----:|
| Domestic | **56.62%** | Low single digits | About **20-22%** |
| **Overseas** | **43.38%** | **+27%** | About **30.7%** |
| Total | 100% | +5.90% | **24.78%** |

(Source: Hangcha Group 2025 annual report)

**Core finding**: overseas revenue carries a gross margin roughly **10 percentage points** higher than domestic revenue. This means every yuan of overseas revenue is worth far more than a yuan of domestic revenue. Overseas revenue's share rose from about 20-25% in 2021 to 43.38% in 2025, making it one of the core engines behind Hangcha's gross margin rising from 15.51% to 24.78%.

**Counterargument**: overseas revenue is growing fast, but it also brings foreign exchange risk and trade-barrier risk. With 43% of revenue dependent on overseas markets, profits would take a direct hit if Europe and the US impose tariffs.

## "In-house three-electrics R&D" — Hangcha's most important strategic move

If you had to pick one decision to represent Hangcha's smartest move over the past eight years, it would be **the 2018 push into in-house R&D of the "three electrics."**

### What are the "three electrics"?

The "three electrics" refers to the three core components of an electric forklift: **the battery, the electric drive (motor), and the electric control system**. These three components account for about **30-40%** of an electric forklift's manufacturing cost. Whoever controls the three electrics controls the cost lifeline of electric forklifts.

### Hangcha's timeline

| Time | Event | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| 2018 | Formed joint venture Pengcheng New Energy with CATL | One of the earliest forklift makers in the industry to move into lithium batteries |
| 2018 | Strategic investment in Henan Jiachen Intelligent | Positioning in electric control and smart instrumentation |
| 2021 | Raised funds via convertible bonds to build a "60,000-unit annual capacity new-energy forklift" project | Capacity expansion |
| 2022 | Launched the full XH lithium-specific forklift lineup (covering 1.5-48 tons) | Completed the product line |
| 2025 | Full in-house R&D and manufacturing of the three electrics (battery, drive, control) | Supply chain self-sufficiency |
| 2025 | Hydrogen fuel cell forklifts deployed, exceeding **400 units** | Forward-looking positioning in new technology |

(Source: Hangcha Group annual reports, public announcements, China Forklift Network reports)

### Why is in-house three-electrics R&D a moat?

A comparison makes it clear:

| Comparison item | Internal-combustion forklift era | Electric forklift era (Hangcha has achieved this) |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| Core components | Engine + transmission (15-20% of cost) | Battery + motor + electric control (30-40% of cost) |
| Hangcha's in-house R&D level | Engines had to be purchased externally (mainly from Xinchai Co.) | **Full in-house R&D and manufacturing of all three electrics** |
| Cost control | Dependent on suppliers | Self-sufficient, stronger economies of scale |
| Room for differentiation | Limited (engines are broadly similar across manufacturers) | Battery range, charging speed, and intelligence can all differentiate |

For every 10-percentage-point rise in electrification, Hangcha's self-sufficiency in its supply chain climbs another notch. And the industry's electrification rate has already surged from **37%** in 2017 to **73.6%** in 2025 — a clear and irreversible trend.

**Counterargument**: the barriers created by in-house three-electrics R&D aren't permanent. As lithium battery prices have fallen sharply (down roughly **50%** over the past five years), the cost gap between buying batteries externally and developing them in-house is narrowing. Once the whole industry masters three-electrics technology, the differentiation advantage will fade. Hangcha's first-mover window may only last **3-5 years**.

## Hangcha vs. Heli: A Duopoly Rivalry

This is the question investors ask most often: what's the real difference between Hangcha and Anhui Heli?

| Comparison dimension | Hangcha Group | Anhui Heli | Who's stronger |
|---------|---------|---------|:-----:|
| 2025 revenue | **17.739 billion yuan** | About **18-19 billion yuan** (estimate) | Heli slightly larger |
| Net profit | **2.191 billion yuan** | About **1.8-2.0 billion yuan** (estimate) | **Hangcha higher** |
| ROE | **20.34%** | About **15-18%** (estimate) | **Hangcha clearly ahead** |
| Gross margin | **24.78%** | About **22%** (estimate) | **Hangcha better** |
| Overseas revenue share | **43.38%** | About **35-40%** | Hangcha slightly higher |
| Actual controller | Qiu Jianping (**private enterprise**) | Anhui Provincial SASAC (**state-owned enterprise**) | Each has pros and cons |
| R&D expense ratio | About **4.7%** | About **6.3%** | Heli invests more |
| Product line coverage | 0.75-25 tons | 0.5-48 tons | Heli more comprehensive |
| China sales ranking | 2nd | **1st** (for 30 consecutive years) | Heli |

(Heli data is an estimate based on its 2024 annual report and brokerage consensus forecasts, marked "estimate")

**Key judgment**: at the product and technology level, the differences between Hangcha and Heli are **not large**. Their competition looks more like a "war of position" — fought over channel density, service response speed, payment terms, and other details.

For end customers, the cost of switching from Hangcha to Heli is limited. This means: **pricing power is weak**. There's essentially no room to raise prices in the domestic market, because Heli is right next door.

Hangcha's core advantages over Heli come down to two points:

1. **Flexible private-enterprise mechanisms**: faster decision-making, more aggressive international expansion
2. **Stronger profitability**: at comparable revenue scale, Hangcha's net margin and ROE are clearly higher than Heli's

**Counterargument**: Heli's state-owned background is an advantage in certain scenarios — large infrastructure projects and state-owned enterprise procurement tend to favor state-owned suppliers. Heli's R&D investment is larger, with over 3,000 patents. Over the long run, the gap between the two may narrow further.

## How strong are the economies of scale?

Hangcha sells more than **300,000 units** annually and is the world's **8th-largest** forklift manufacturer. Its economies of scale show up as follows:

| Source of scale advantage | Specific manifestation |
|------------|---------|
| Procurement | Large-volume purchasing of steel, lithium batteries, and motors gives strong bargaining power |
| Production | **600+** intelligent robots, **20+** automated production lines |
| R&D | Annual R&D spend of about **500-600 million yuan**, amortized to only about **2,000 yuan** per unit |
| Channels | **20+** overseas subsidiaries globally, dense domestic distribution network |
| Service | High coverage of parts network; genuine parts deliver high margins |

(Source: Hangcha Group 2025 annual report, ESG report)

**But is the scale effect overstated?**

One set of numbers tells the story:

| Metric | 2021 | 2025 | 4-year change |
|:----|:---:|:---:|:-------:|
| Revenue (billion yuan) | 14.490 | 17.739 | **+22.4%** |
| Gross margin | 15.51% | 24.78% | **+9.27 percentage points** |
| Period expense ratio (estimated) | About 5.5% | About 8.5% | **+3.0 percentage points** |

(Source: Estimates derived from Hangcha Group's 2021-2025 annual report data)

Revenue grew 22.4%, while the period expense ratio actually rose from 5.5% to 8.5%. In other words, expense growth kept pace with revenue growth — or even outpaced it. **The real economies of scale aren't as powerful as they look.** The improvement in gross margin comes more from product-mix upgrades and falling raw material prices than from expense dilution driven by scale.

## The Aftermarket — An Underappreciated Profit Pool

A forklift isn't a one-and-done sale. A forklift typically lasts 5-10 years, during which it continuously generates demand for parts replacement, maintenance, and battery replacement.

| Aftermarket business | Gross margin | Characteristics |
|-----------|:-----:|------|
| Genuine parts sales | **30-40%+** | Extremely strong customer stickiness, brand trust |
| Maintenance and repair | **30%+** | Recurring revenue, counter-cyclical |
| Forklift rental | Medium | Rising penetration, lowers the barrier to customer purchase |
| Lithium battery recycling/swapping | Yet to be validated | A new market emerging from electrification |

(Source: Estimates based on public industry information)

Aftermarket revenue contributes **30-50%** of profit for European and American forklift companies, while Hangcha's aftermarket revenue share is estimated at only **15-20%**. This is potential room for Hangcha's gross margin to keep rising in the future — but it will take time and investment to build out the service network.

**Counterargument**: growth in aftermarket revenue is a slow process, not something achieved overnight. Competition among third-party parts suppliers is intense, and genuine parts' pricing premium is also being eroded.

## How wide is the moat?

Scoring each dimension:

| Moat dimension | Score | Notes |
|-----------|:---:|------|
| Economies of scale | Strong | 300,000+ units sold annually; clear procurement and production cost advantages |
| Brand recognition | Medium | Solid duopoly position domestically, but forklifts are a B2B industrial product with limited brand premium |
| Switching costs | Medium | Non-interchangeable parts plus a sticky service network, but customers don't lose much by switching brands |
| Technology barriers | Medium | In-house three-electrics R&D is a current advantage, but not permanent |
| Network effects | Weak | The forklift industry has essentially no traditional network effects |

**Overall judgment**: Hangcha's moat is **medium to above-average** — sufficient, but not deep. The economies of scale are clear, but a competitor with enough time (5-10 years) could replicate them; it is not a permanent barrier.

**The single most important sentence**: if a Hangcha customer switches to Heli, the loss is limited — real pricing power is weak.

## Key conclusions from this installment

1. **Hangcha's business model is upgrading**: transitioning from "selling iron" (low-margin internal-combustion forklifts) toward "selling electricity + selling services" (high-margin electric forklifts + aftermarket). The rise in gross margin from 15.51% to 24.78% over four years is real progress.
2. **Overseas markets are the core engine of profit growth**: overseas gross margin is about 10 percentage points higher than domestic, and overseas revenue share has reached 43.38%.
3. **In-house three-electrics R&D is the most important strategic move**: but the first-mover window may only last 3-5 years.
4. **The moat is sufficient but not wide**: the duopoly structure is stable and economies of scale are clear, but differentiation is limited and pricing power is weak.
5. **The aftermarket is an underexploited profit pool**: currently at 15-20% share, far below the 30-50% seen at European and American peers.
