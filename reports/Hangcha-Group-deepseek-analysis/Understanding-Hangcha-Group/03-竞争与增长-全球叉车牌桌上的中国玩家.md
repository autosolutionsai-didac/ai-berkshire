## The Global Forklift Market: One Dominant Leader, Many Strong Contenders

China accounts for 50% of global forklift production, but the two leading Chinese companies together hold only 8% of global revenue share. Production accounts for half the world's total, yet the money earned is only a fraction — this contradiction is the starting point for understanding Hangcha's future growth potential.

First, look at the seating order at the global table:

| Rank | Company | Country | Global Share (Revenue Basis) | Core Advantage |
|:----:|------|:----:|:------------------:|----------|
| 1 | Toyota Industries | Japan | approximately **25-29%** | Broadest product line, strongest global service network |
| 2 | KION Group (Linde + Dematic) | Germany | approximately **13-15%** | European high-end segment + warehouse automation |
| 3 | Jungheinrich | Germany | approximately **8-10%** | Europe's leading electric-forklift maker |
| 4 | Crown Equipment | United States | approximately **3-4%** | Deep roots in the North American market |
| 5 | Hyster-Yale | United States | approximately **2-3%** | Heavy-duty forklifts |
| 6 | Mitsubishi Logisnext | Japan | approximately **2-3%** | Comprehensive Japanese manufacturing strength |
| 7 | Anhui Heli | China | approximately **4-5%** | China's top seller by volume, scale advantage |
| 8 | **Hangcha Group** | China | approximately **3-4%** | First-mover in lithium-battery electrification, flexible mechanisms |

(Source: World Industrial Trucks Statistics (WITS) 2024 data, MMH magazine's global forklift company rankings, individual company annual reports. Rankings are by sales revenue and differ from unit-sales-based rankings published by Zhongcha.net.)

**A counterintuitive fact**: China accounts for more than **50%** of global forklift production, yet China's duopoly (Hangcha plus Heli) together hold only about **8%** of global revenue share.

Production accounts for half the world's total, but revenue is only 8% — where does the gap come from?

The answer: **Chinese companies sell a lot but earn little**. Chinese forklift export unit prices are far below those of international brands like Toyota and KION, and in the global market they remain in a "trading volume for price" phase.

But the good news is: **electrification is changing how value is distributed.** Chinese companies' cost advantage across the entire lithium-battery supply chain is shifting them from "low-end substitution" toward "technology leadership."

## Going Overseas: Hangcha's Biggest Growth Pole

First, a set of trend data:

| Metric | 2021 | 2023 | 2025 |
|:----|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Hangcha's overseas revenue share | approximately **20-25%** | approximately **35%** | **43.38%** |
| Number of Hangcha overseas subsidiaries | approximately 9 | approximately 15 | **20+** |
| China's annual forklift export volume | — | — | approximately **530,000 units** (263,000 units in H1 2025) |
| Export growth y/y | — | — | **+15-18%** |

(Source: Hangcha Group annual reports, China Construction Machinery Association data)

China's forklift exports have posted positive year-over-year growth for **15 consecutive months**, setting a new record for that calendar month every single month. Electric forklifts now account for more than **78%** of exports.

(Source: Industrial Vehicles Branch of the China Construction Machinery Association, Zhongcha.net reporting)

### The Core Logic of Hangcha's Overseas Push

Hangcha's overseas expansion is not simply "dumping at low prices" — it is a phased strategy:

| Stage | Strategy | Progress |
|------|------|------|
| Stage 1: Export complete units | Open markets on value for money, priced roughly **30-50%** below Toyota/KION | Completed |
| Stage 2: Localized production | Building a plant in Thailand (production starts 2026Q2, annual capacity of **10,000 units**) | Underway |
| Stage 3: Technology export | Joint venture with France's Manitou, exporting lithium-battery technology to Europe | Underway |
| Stage 4: Brand upgrade | Entering the supply chains of international companies such as Tesla and Coca-Cola | Early stage |
| Stage 5: Smart logistics goes overseas | Established Hangcha America Smart Logistics (HASL) in 2025 | Just starting |

(Source: Hangcha Group announcements, brokerage research reports)

This path closely resembles Toyota Motor's globalization in the 1970s-80s — first exporting complete vehicles, then building assembly plants, and finally establishing R&D centers. Hangcha is currently in the transition period from "trade-based" overseas expansion to "localized" overseas expansion.

### Overseas Market Structure

Hangcha's overseas revenue breakdown (estimated from public information):

| Region | Share of Overseas Revenue (est.) | Characteristics |
|------|:------------------:|------|
| Europe | approximately **40-45%** | Largest overseas market, electrification rate of 85%+, joint venture with Manitou |
| Southeast Asia | approximately **35-40%** | Fastest-growing, Thailand plant underway |
| Americas | approximately **15-25%** | The U.S. market is tariff-sensitive, share is relatively low |
| Other | approximately **5-10%** | Emerging markets such as the Middle East and Africa |

(Source: Estimate based on Hangcha Group's annual report disclosures on overseas operations and industry reporting)

**Key assessment**: Hangcha's direct exports to the U.S. market are already a small share, and with staged inventory building, the near-term impact of U.S. tariffs is limited. Its long-term positioning leans more toward Europe and Southeast Asia.

**The counter-argument**: Overseas expansion sounds good, but execution risk is substantial. Cultural differences, compliance costs, the long timeline for brand building, and inadequate localized service capability are all real obstacles. Toyota has service outlets in more than 200 countries worldwide, while Hangcha has only 20-plus overseas subsidiaries — that gap cannot be closed in a year or two.

## Electrification: A Structural Turning Point Has Been Confirmed

| Year | Share of Electric Forklifts in China | Share of Lithium-Battery Forklifts Among Electric Forklifts |
|:----:|:----------:|:--------------------:|
| 2017 | approximately 37% | approximately 4.3% |
| 2020 | approximately 51% | approximately 15% |
| 2023 | approximately 67.9% | approximately 38% |
| 2024 | **73.6%** | approximately **47%** |
| 2025E | approximately 78% | approximately 55% |

(Source: Industrial Vehicles Branch of the China Construction Machinery Association)

In 2024, absolute sales volume of internal-combustion forklifts began to decline (down **10.06%** year over year) — an irreversible structural turning point.

### Why Is Electrification a Structural Tailwind for Chinese Companies?

| Dimension | The Internal-Combustion Forklift Era | The Electric Forklift Era |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Core technology barrier | Engines (Japanese and German companies lead) | Lithium batteries (**China leads globally**) |
| Supply chain advantage | Japan and Germany hold the core components | **China controls the entire chain: batteries, motors, and controllers** |
| Cost structure | High manufacturing costs in developed countries | China has the world's lowest lithium-battery costs |
| Competitive dynamic | Chinese companies are followers | **Chinese companies become the technology leaders** |

Lithium-battery electrification is an "asymmetric advantage" for China's forklift industry. Unlike the solar industry's overcapacity problem (Chinese companies competing brutally among themselves), this is an opportunity to gain global share driven by a "technology generation gap."

### But Watch Out: the Electrification-Rate Figures Have Some "Padding"

Within the industry's 73.6% electrification-rate figure, there is a widely overlooked detail:

- **Class III trucks** (electric walkie warehouse forklifts) account for **77%** of electric forklift sales — a large portion of these are simple, low-priced, low-tech small pallet trucks
- Excluding Class III trucks, the penetration rate of electric rider forklifts (Class I+II) in China is only about **38.85%**, versus **72%** in Europe and **45%** in the Americas

(Source: Industrial Vehicles Branch of the China Construction Machinery Association)

This means the "real electrification rate" still has room to double. The replacement of large-tonnage, high-value Class I trucks will be the main battleground over the next five years — and where Hangcha's real opportunity lies.

## Breaking Down the Competitors One by One

### Anhui Heli — a Peer in the Same Tier

The detailed comparison was covered in the previous article. In one line: **the two companies are in the same tier, with little gap between them.** The duopoly structure is good for both — the probability of a price war is low, and the competitive focus has shifted to product upgrades and overseas expansion.

### BYD Forklifts — "Crying Wolf," or a Real Threat?

BYD has a complete electrification supply chain (self-supplying batteries, motors, and controllers), which in theory gives it a structural advantage in entering the forklift business.

| Dimension | BYD Forklift | Assessment |
|------|----------|------|
| Lithium-battery technology | Full in-house stack from cells to BMS to battery packs | The strongest in the entire industry |
| Market share | Approximately **3-4%** in China | Far smaller than the duopoly |
| Service network | Still underdeveloped compared with traditional forklift companies | This is the biggest weakness |
| Group resources | BYD's 2025 revenue exceeded **RMB 800 billion** | Can absorb losses in the forklift business even if it isn't profitable |

**Assessment**: The threat from BYD's forklift business **has been overstated**. There are three reasons:

1. **A forklift is not just "battery plus motor."** Forklifts are B2B industrial products, and what customers care about most is after-sales response speed — when a forklift stops, the production line stops. BYD's dealer and service network is far behind Hangcha's and Heli's.
2. **BYD has never separately disclosed forklift business data**, suggesting it is not a high priority within the group.
3. **Electrification has already reached its mid-to-late stage** (industry penetration of 73.6%), so the window for new entrants to leapfrog the incumbents through electrification is closing.

**The counter-argument**: If BYD decided to treat forklifts as a strategic-level business and used its battery cost advantage to wage a price war, the current structure could be rewritten. There is no sign of this at present, but it cannot be ruled out.

### Toyota — the Global Titan, but Only 3% Share in China

Toyota's forklift business ranks first globally, with market share of about **25-29%**. But in China it holds only about **3%** market share. The reason is simple: Chinese domestic brands have too great a value-for-money advantage, and Toyota's high-end positioning in China has caused it to miss the low- and mid-end market.

Toyota's global position is unassailable in the near term, but in emerging markets like China and Southeast Asia, Chinese companies are rapidly eating into Toyota's space. Toyota is more of a "defender," while Hangcha is the "attacker."

### KION (Linde + Baoli) — Gatekeeper of the High-End Market

KION has strong pricing power and technological advantages in the high-end market through its Linde brand, and competes in China's mid-market through its Baoli brand.

But KION faces a challenge: China's lithium-battery electric forklift solutions are penetrating upward from the low end. Linde is lagging Chinese companies in the pace of lithium-battery adoption. KION's net margin in 2024 was about **5%**, far below Hangcha's **13%**.

## Intelligence: the Next Variable

The future of the forklift industry is not just "electrification" — it is also "intelligence": AGVs (automated guided vehicles), driverless forklifts, and logistics robots.

| Participant | Type | Characteristics |
|--------|------|------|
| Hangcha Group | Traditional forklifts + AGVs | Acquired Guozi Robotics in 2025; intelligence-related revenue of approximately **RMB 1 billion** |
| Anhui Heli | Traditional forklifts + AGVs | Acquired Yufeng Intelligent |
| Geek+, Quicktron, Hikrobot | Pure-play AGV makers | Stronger software and algorithm capabilities |
| Toyota / KION | Traditional forklifts + automation | Globally leading warehouse-automation systems-integration capability |

(Source: individual company announcements, industry reporting)

The AGV/driverless forklift market is still in its early stages (China penetration below **5%**), but growing fast (annual growth rate above **30%**).

Hangcha has an advantage in chassis and vehicle engineering, but its software and algorithm capabilities lag pure-play AGV makers. In 2025, Hangcha also unveiled the X1 humanoid robot, testing the waters in embodied intelligence.

**Assessment**: Intelligence is a "mandatory question" for the forklift industry — skip it and you get left behind, but doing it doesn't guarantee excess returns either. The real moat isn't the technology itself, but the combination of "hardware reliability + software capability + service network." Hangcha currently has an advantage in its service network, but its software capability still needs strengthening.

## Industry Outlook for the Next Five Years

| Trend | Impact on Hangcha | Certainty |
|------|------------|:-----:|
| Continued deepening of electrification | Positive (in-house "three electrics," cost advantage) | High |
| Accelerating internationalization | Positive (large room to grow beyond a 3-4% global share) | Medium-high |
| Continued rise in industry concentration | Positive (the duopoly squeezes smaller makers' room to survive) | High |
| Intelligence / autonomy | Neutral to positive (already has a foothold, but not a leader) | Medium |
| Rising trade protectionism | Negative (43% of overseas revenue faces tariff risk) | Medium-high |

**Overall assessment**: The forklift industry is not one that will make anyone rich overnight, but it is **an industry that is hard to lose money in**. Against the backdrop of continued growth in global manufacturing and logistics, forklift demand's medium-pace growth carries relatively high certainty. As one of China's two dominant players, Hangcha is well positioned on both key tracks — electrification and internationalization.

**The biggest growth variable is internationalization** — Hangcha's global revenue share is only about **3-4%**, while China accounts for more than 50% of global production. The huge gap between global revenue share and production share is Hangcha's growth runway for the next 5-10 years.

**The biggest risk variable is trade barriers** — if Europe and the U.S. impose steep tariffs on Chinese forklifts, the overseas-expansion thesis would take a serious hit. The Thailand plant and the France joint venture are countermeasures, but near-term capacity is limited.
