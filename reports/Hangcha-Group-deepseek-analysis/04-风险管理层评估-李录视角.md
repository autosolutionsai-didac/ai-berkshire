## II. Management assessment

### 2.1 CEO profile: Zhao Limin — a state-owned-enterprise veteran turned private-enterprise leader

**Facts:**
- Zhao Limin, born October 1956, is 69 years old as of 2026. He holds the title of senior economist and is a member of the Communist Party of China.
- Career path: deputy director of the dispatch office at Hangzhou Forklift General Plant → branch factory director → head of manufacturing → deputy general manager
- April 2000-May 2003: general manager of Hangzhou Forklift Co., Ltd.
- June 2003-December 2008: general manager of the company
- December 2008 to present: chairman and general manager (17 years in the role), also serving as Party committee secretary since May 2018
- In September 2025, the eighth board of directors re-elected Zhao Limin as chairman and general manager (a three-year term)

**Inference and analysis:**
- Zhao Limin rose from a grassroots position at the forklift plant to general manager, has worked at Hangcha for over 40 years, and has an extremely deep understanding of the industry.
- However, at 69 years old in 2026 — despite being reappointed in 2025 — the succession question is pressing. Li Lu pays very close attention to succession: management with no clear succession plan is a potential risk.
- Holding both "chairman" and "general manager" concentrates power in one person, a gap relative to governance best practices.
- Zhao Limin's long tenure is a double-edged sword: on one hand it means deep industry experience and stability; on the other, at 69 he still holds both the chairman and general manager roles, and whether a successor is being groomed and whether a bench of talent exists are questions investors need to keep tracking.

## III. Governance structure risk  ★★★★☆ (High)

### 3.1 Ownership structure: the actual controller is not the state-owned assets commission, but Qiu Jianping

**This is one of the most important findings in this report.** The background information provided by the user states that "the actual controller is the Hangzhou Municipal State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission," but **this is not the case.**

**Facts:**
- Controlling shareholder: Zhejiang Hangcha Holding Co., Ltd. (holding approximately 41.34%)
- Qiu Jianping holds 98.98% of Zhejiang Hangcha Holding, thereby controlling approximately 41.34% of Hangcha Group's shares
- The Hangzhou Municipal State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission controls approximately 20.39% of the company's shares through Hangshi Group (the second-largest shareholder)
- Qiu Jianping is the actual controller of Hangcha Group. He is chairman of Juxing Technology (002444.SZ), the actual controller of Xinchai Co. (301032.SZ), and is advancing an IPO for Zhongce Rubber
- Qiu Jianping went into business in 1993, founding Juxing Group, and is a classic Zhejiang-merchant "acquisition king"
- In 2011, Qiu Jianping completed the acquisition of 98.8% of Hangcha Holding's equity, taking control of Hangcha Group

**Source:** Huafu Securities January 2024 research report, Qichacha, Ruicaijing reporting

**Analysis from Li Lu's perspective:**
- When Qiu Jianping took control of Hangcha, it was an old-line state-owned forklift enterprise. After he took over, Hangcha transformed from a local state-owned enterprise into a privately-controlled one, and subsequently grew revenue from roughly 7 billion yuan to 17.739 billion yuan. This transformation itself was a success.
- But Qiu Jianping simultaneously controls or holds stakes in multiple enterprises (Juxing Technology, Xinchai Co., Zhongce Rubber, Guozi Robotics, and others), forming a complex "Juxing system" capital network. A complex holding structure inherently increases channels for related-party transactions and interest transfer.
- Qiu Jianping himself does not hold an operating management position at Hangcha (he is a director but not an executive); actual operations at Hangcha are run by Zhao Limin's team. Would Li Lu like this "owner not on the ground" model? No. Li Lu prefers enterprises where the founder and management team are highly unified.

### 3.2 Related-party transaction risk ★★★★☆ (High)

**Facts:**
- July 2025: subsidiary Hangcha Intelligent acquired 99.23% of Guozi Robotics through a capital increase, for approximately 398 million yuan
  - Counterparties included: Kunxia Investment (controlled by Qiu Jianping) and Juxing Technology (controlled by Qiu Jianping)
  - Qiu Jianping serves as a director of both Guozi Robotics and Juxing Technology
  - Qiu Fei (Qiu Jianping's daughter) also serves as a director of Guozi Robotics
  - This transaction constituted a related-party transaction; the interested parties recused themselves from the vote
- Hangcha Group has ongoing day-to-day related-party transactions (purchases, sales, etc.) with "Juxing system" enterprises such as Juxing Technology, Guozi Robotics, and Zhongce Rubber
- The 2024 forecast disclosure of routine related-party transactions listed multiple "Juxing system" companies, including Hangzhou Lianhe Tools and Changzhou Huada Kejie Optoelectronic Instruments

**Inference and analysis:**
- The Guozi Robotics deal: strategically sound (smart logistics is a natural extension of forklifts), but the transaction structure is relatively complex — structured as a capital increase by a subsidiary rather than a direct acquisition, with the counterparties being affiliates of the actual controller.
- Qiu Jianping simultaneously controls Hangcha Group and Juxing Technology, and Hangcha Group co-invested with Juxing Technology in Guozi Robotics, forming a cross-shareholding network. This structure increases the potential for conflicts of interest.
- **An area with insufficient data:** Are the prices in routine related-party transactions fair? Tianjian Certified Public Accountants' audit found no anomalies, but investors need to continuously monitor the scale, pricing basis, and growth trend of related-party transactions.

## IV. Regulatory risk

### 4.1 Environmental policy (National IV emission standard) ★★★☆☆ (Medium)

**Facts:**
- The National IV emission standard has been fully implemented for non-road mobile machinery since December 1, 2022
- Hangcha's internal-combustion forklifts meet the National IV standard as well as international emission standards such as the EU and US EPA
- 2024 industry-wide sales of internal-combustion forklifts: 339,300 units, down 10.06% year over year
- Electric forklift sales: 946,300 units, up 18.79% year over year, an 73.61% share

**Judgment:**
- Implementation of the National IV standard is accelerating the phase-out of internal-combustion forklifts, which is actually favorable for Hangcha, given its lead in electrification.
- In 2025, Hangcha's clean-energy usage rate reached 55.01%, and greenhouse gas emissions fell 48.37% year over year.
- Environmental policy is a structurally positive factor for Hangcha, accelerating the industry's shift to electrification and favoring increased concentration among leaders.
- **The risk level is low from a product standpoint but medium from a compliance-cost standpoint** — the company must continue investing in green retrofits and carbon-footprint management.

### 4.2 Export tariffs/trade barriers ★★★★☆ (Relatively high)

**Facts:**
- Overseas revenue share of 43.38% (2025), indicating high dependence on overseas markets
- The Thailand plant is expected to begin production in Q2 2026, with annual capacity of 10,000 units, aimed at mitigating tariff risk
- A joint venture with France's Manitou for local production in Europe
- Industry backdrop: trade protectionism continued to intensify globally in 2026. The US imposed anti-dumping duties of 85%-159% on Chinese MDI, the EU pursued anti-dumping measures on titanium dioxide, and multiple countries launched anti-dumping investigations into chemical products
- Direct anti-dumping cases against China's forklift industry are not yet prominent, but escalating trade friction is the broader backdrop

**Inference:**
- A large share of Hangcha's overseas revenue (over 43%) comes from Europe and the US. If the US or EU imposes tariffs on Chinese-made forklifts, it would directly affect the company's profitability.
- The Thailand plant and the France joint venture are strategic moves to address trade barriers — but the Thailand plant will not begin production until 2026, and its 10,000-unit annual capacity is limited relative to total sales.
- **Key uncertainty:** there is currently no dedicated anti-dumping case against Chinese forklifts, but one cannot be ruled out. Forklifts, as construction/engineering machinery, have historically been relatively less affected by trade friction, but the risk trend is rising.
- Hangcha's export gross margin is approximately 30.7% (2024 overseas gross margin), significantly higher than domestic, leaving room for margin compression.

### 4.3 Workplace safety regulation ★★☆☆☆ (Low)

**Facts:**
- Per Hangcha's 2025 ESG report, its internal control system is operating effectively with no material deficiencies for the year
- 7,233 employees, with 100% coverage of labor contracts/social insurance
- Ongoing intelligent and automated retrofits have reduced workplace injury risk (600+ smart robots, 20+ automated production lines)

**Judgment:** Workplace safety risk is low. Manufacturing enterprises carry inherent risk, but Hangcha's high level of automation and intelligence, combined with a compliance culture inherited from its state-owned-enterprise transformation, keeps this risk manageable.

## V. Competitive risk

### 5.1 Direct competition from Anhui Heli ★★★☆☆ (Medium)

**Facts:**
- China's forklift market shows a "duopoly" structure: Anhui Heli and Hangcha Group together hold more than 50% market share
- 2024 industry data: Anhui Heli's revenue was approximately 17.3 billion yuan (estimated), Hangcha Group's revenue was 16.49 billion yuan (excluding tax) — the two are similar in scale
- Anhui Heli is likewise advancing electrification and internationalization, with a similar strategic path to Hangcha
- 2024 industry-wide sales volume: 1.2855 million units, up 9.52% year over year

**Judgment:**
- The duopoly structure benefits both parties — there is limited room for price wars, and the shared competitive focus has shifted to upgrading electric products, which favors margin expansion.
- Heli is state-owned (Anhui state-owned assets commission), while Hangcha is effectively controlled by private businessman Qiu Jianping — a contest between two different systems, each with its own advantages and disadvantages.
- Heli's revenue scale is slightly larger than Hangcha's, but Hangcha is not behind in electrification speed or overseas expansion — if anything, slightly ahead.
- **Competitiveness rating: the two are in the same tier, with a small gap.**

### 5.2 The disruptive threat from new-energy automakers like BYD ★★★★☆ (Relatively high)

**Inference (this section is more "speculation" than "fact"):**
- BYD has a complete electrification supply chain (battery, motor, electronic control all self-supplied), which theoretically gives it a structural cost and technology advantage in entering the forklift space.
- Forklifts are a B2B industrial product, fundamentally different from BYD's passenger-vehicle business — requiring an entirely different dealer network, after-sales system, and customer relationships.
- BYD's forklift business currently has some presence, but its market share still lags far behind the duopoly.
- The technical barrier in the forklift industry does not lie in electrification itself (batteries can be bought), but in understanding of industrial use cases, product reliability, and a global service network — precisely the core advantages that Hangcha and Heli have accumulated over 60 years.
- **But Li Lu would say:** no moat is permanent. If BYD truly decides to make a major push into forklifts and wages a price war leveraging its structural battery-cost advantage, the current landscape could be upended. There is no such sign yet, but it needs continuous tracking.

**Key fact:** 2024 industry data shows electric forklifts already account for 73.61% of sales volume, indicating that industry electrification has reached its late-middle stage. The window for new entrants to leapfrog via electrification is closing.

### 5.3 Global competition (Toyota, KION, etc.) ★★★★☆ (Relatively high)

**Facts:**
- The top three players in the global forklift market: Toyota, KION, and Jungheinrich
- Hangcha has ranked 7th-8th globally (a position held since 2017)
- Toyota and KION still hold significant advantages in the high-end market, technology accumulation, and global service networks
- But domestic Chinese forklifts have a clear cost-performance advantage, especially a supply-chain lead in the lithium-battery segment

**Judgment:**
- In overseas markets, Hangcha's brand recognition and service network fall well short of Toyota's and KION's. This is a long uphill climb.
- Hangcha's strategy is to use "cost-performance plus lithium-battery technology advantage" as an entry point, first capturing the mid-tier market and then gradually extending upward.
- The joint venture with France's Manitou is a smart move — leveraging a local company's channels and brand. Manitou is a globally known manufacturer of aerial work platforms and rough-terrain forklifts.
- **The risk is:** if international giants also start aggressively promoting lithium-battery forklifts and competing on price, Hangcha's window of advantage is limited.

**Facts:**

**Judgment:**

- But the rising self-sufficiency rate for core components (especially the three-electric self-development) has significantly reduced upstream dependence
- The gross-margin improvement against the trend in 2025 indicates that product-mix upgrading (a rising share of high-margin electric forklifts) is offsetting raw-material cost pressure
- **Risk is manageable but steel price trends need continuous tracking**

### 6.3 Foreign exchange risk in overseas business ★★★☆☆ (Medium)

**Facts:**
- Overseas revenue share of 43.38%, with overseas revenue predominantly denominated in US dollars and euros
- The company's 2025 semi-annual report explicitly mentions foreign exchange risk
- The company has overseas subsidiaries that provide a degree of natural hedging, but the extent is limited

**Judgment:**
- For every 5% appreciation of the renminbi, the potential impact on net profit is roughly 2-3% (estimate: overseas revenue of 7.7 billion yuan, gross margin 30%, net margin roughly 13%; FX impact calculated on the revenue side is roughly 300-400 million yuan, and on the net-profit side roughly 100-200 million yuan)
- In the current cycle of dollar strength (2025-2026), FX has actually been favorable for Hangcha — a relatively weak renminbi supports export competitiveness
- **The risk is:** if the renminbi appreciates suddenly and sharply, it would deliver a significant short-term hit to profit. There is no public information indicating Hangcha uses foreign-exchange derivatives to hedge at scale.

## VII. Macro risk

### 7.1 Manufacturing PMI cyclicality dragging on forklift demand ★★★☆☆ (Medium)

**Facts:**
- Forklift demand is closely tied to manufacturing sentiment
- 2025 industry data shows the industrial-vehicle sector is in an adjustment phase of "shrinking demand and intensifying competition, marked by internal involution and external pressure"
- Yet Hangcha still achieved revenue growth of +5.90% and net profit growth of +8.87% in 2025
- Hangcha's non-domestic revenue share has reached 43.38%, and internationalization has effectively diversified domestic cyclical risk

**Judgment:**
- Hangcha's cyclical exposure is weakening (unlike a purely domestic-demand manufacturer), because the rising share of overseas revenue smooths out domestic cyclical fluctuations
- Structural growth from electrification replacing internal-combustion forklifts is also offsetting cyclical downturns
- But a severe economic recession would still compress corporate capital expenditure, delivering a systemic hit to forklift demand — an uncontrollable macro risk

### 7.2 Contraction in capital expenditure during economic downturns ★★★☆☆ (Medium)

**Inference:**
- Forklifts are a capital-goods expenditure for businesses, and during periods of economic uncertainty, companies tend to postpone equipment replacement
- But forklifts also have a "consumable" quality (average replacement cycle of 5-7 years), giving demand a degree of rigidity
- China's ongoing growth in logistics (warehousing, e-commerce, express delivery) provides underlying support for forklift demand
- In an extreme economic downturn scenario, Hangcha may face 1-2 quarters of order softness, but a sharp decline like that seen in engineering machinery is unlikely

## VIII. Ten-year outlook: Hangcha in 2036

### 8.1 Will the forklift industry still exist in 10 years?

**Judgment: yes, but its form will change fundamentally.**

- As the core equipment for material handling, forklifts are irreplaceable for the foreseeable future. As long as factories, warehouses, and ports exist, handling equipment will be needed.
- **What could be disruptive?**
  - **Autonomous/AGV replacement of manually driven forklifts**: this is already happening, but Hangcha is actively positioning itself (Guozi Robotics, the X1 humanoid robot)
  - **Changes in warehousing and logistics models**: automated high-bay warehouses reduce reliance on traditional forklifts, but simultaneously create new demand for AGVs and smart logistics equipment — Hangcha is transitioning in this direction
  - **Disruptive technologies**: the low-altitude economy and drone delivery may replace some ground logistics equipment, but heavy-duty handling and warehousing scenarios are unlikely to be disrupted within 10 years

### 8.2 Can Hangcha maintain its position amid the waves of electrification and intelligence?

**Positive factors:**
- Electrification: the duopoly structure is stable, and leadership is likely to continue
- Intelligence: the Guozi Robotics acquisition plus the X1 humanoid robot launch show forward-looking positioning in intelligence
- Globalization: the Thailand plant, the France joint venture, and 20+ overseas subsidiaries — a global network is being built
- Self-developed and self-manufactured three-electric system: fully proprietary battery, motor, and electronic control, building a supply-chain moat

**Negative factors:**
- Management aging: Zhao Limin is 69, and the core team averages 57 — most will have retired in 10 years. The succession question remains unresolved.
- Actual-controller risk: Qiu Jianping's "Juxing system" control structure could bring succession risk. If Qiu Jianping exits or has a need for capital, Hangcha could become a cash cow to be drained — this is the "ownership structure risk" Li Lu would worry about most.
- Technological iteration: if the forklift industry rapidly evolves toward fully autonomous driving, are Hangcha's software and AI capabilities sufficient? The Guozi Robotics acquisition is a first step, but its scale and capability still need to be proven.

**Estimated 10-year survival probability: 85%**

> **Li Lu-style closing remark:** Hangcha will most likely still be around in 10 years, and possibly bigger than it is today. But investing requires more than "still being around" — it requires sustained ability to generate excess returns. If Qiu Jianping uses his controlling position to make decisions unfavorable to minority shareholders, or if poor succession planning erodes management capability, Hangcha could degrade from an excellent manufacturer into a mediocre "cash cow" — continuously drained by its parent structure.

### The three risks most worth watching (in order of priority)

1. **Governance risk (highest priority):** Qiu Jianping controls 41.34% of equity through Hangcha Holding, while simultaneously controlling multiple affiliated enterprises (Juxing Technology, Xinchai Co., Zhongce Rubber, Guozi Robotics, and others), creating a potential channel for interest transfer via related-party transactions. The Guozi Robotics acquisition is a textbook case of being "both buyer and seller." Investors must continuously monitor the scale and pricing fairness of related-party transactions.

2. **Management succession risk:** the 69-year-old Zhao Limin still holds both chairman and general manager roles, and the core executive team averages 57 years old. There is no clear succession plan. Manufacturing succession risk has historically been underestimated.

3. **Trade barrier risk:** 43.38% of revenue comes from overseas, amid rising global trade protectionism. The Thailand plant and the France joint venture are countermeasures, but near-term capacity remains limited.

### Relationship to the current valuation

At a current PE of 15.5x and a market cap of 34.7 billion yuan, the valuation is not expensive for a manufacturing leader with ROE above 20% and net-profit growth of 9% per year. But after accounting for governance risk, a reasonable margin of safety should be larger.

**Li Lu's investing law: buying a stock means buying a company, and buying a company means buying management.** Hangcha's management (the operating layer) is of qualified quality, but actual controller Qiu Jianping's "Juxing system" capital-operations style — heavy acquisitions, complex related-party transactions, high dividends supporting acquisition loans — falls short of the "honest, capable management that treats shareholders as partners" profile that Li Lu prefers.
