The global forklift market was valued at approximately USD 81.8 billion in 2024 (Fortune Business Insights data), with a projected CAGR of about 5.6% for 2025-2034. China has been the world's largest forklift producer and consumer for 14 consecutive years, with total domestic sales of 1.2855 million units in 2024 — about 57% of the global total.

**Key fact**: The global forklift industry is a classic "slow-growth but perennial" track. As basic material-handling equipment, forklifts are closely tied to manufacturing/warehousing/logistics activity — they won't be disrupted the way consumer electronics can be, nor are they as violently affected by construction cycles as engineering machinery.

### 2.2 Structural Characteristics of the China Forklift Market

2024 China forklift sales structure (per Industrial Vehicles Branch of the China Construction Machinery Association data):

| Category | Units sold | YoY growth | Share |
|------|:---------:|:--------:|:----:|
| Internal-combustion forklifts (Class IV+V) | 339,251 | -10.06% | 26.39% |

**Trend judgment**: The electrification rate jumped from 67.9% in 2023 to 73.6% in 2024, and absolute sales of internal-combustion forklifts have begun to decline — an irreversible structural inflection point. The speed and depth of the electrification trend is the "industry-transformation" variable Munger cares about most.

> Munger-style thinking: "**When a trend is clear and irreversible, get on the right side of it as early as possible.** The impact of electrification on the forklift industry is similar to film giving way to digital photography — except forklift electrification isn't moving as fast as digital cameras did, but it is more certain. China's full-chain advantage in lithium batteries makes this transition a tailwind rather than a threat for domestic firms."

**A realistic assessment of the BYD Forklift threat**:
BYD Forklift is the industry's biggest "wild card." Its full-stack in-house lithium-battery capability is something neither Hangcha nor Heli possesses. BYD Group's 2025 revenue exceeded CNY 800 billion, so even a loss-making forklift unit is sustainable for the parent. That said:

## VI. In-Depth Reading of Industry Trends

### 6.1 Lithium-Ionization — Chinese Companies' "Asymmetric Advantage"

- China is the country with the most concentrated lithium-battery production capacity in the world; leaders such as CATL and BYD lead globally on cell costs
- The cost of forklift lithium-battery packs has fallen by about 50% over the past five years
- Hangcha has partnered with CATL and Pengcheng New Energy to develop forklift-specific lithium batteries
- Lithium-battery forklifts offer longer range, faster charging (1-2 hours to full versus 8 hours for lead-acid), and longer life (5-8 years versus 2-3 years)

**Munger's interpretation**: Lithium-ionization is a "structurally favorable" event for Chinese forklift companies. Unlike the overcapacity seen in the solar industry (where Chinese firms compete against each other), this is a "technology-generation gap" opportunity that brings pricing power and share gains. The window for Chinese firms to shift from "low-end substitution" to "technology leadership" is right now, over the next 3-5 years.

### 6.2 Intelligence/Smart Features — A Double-Edged Sword

- AI/IoT technology is reshaping the forklift industry: fleet management, remote monitoring, fault warning, route optimization
- Hangcha has already deployed an IoT platform supporting remote monitoring and energy-consumption management
- Intelligent features require software capability, which is not a traditional manufacturer's strength
- Cross-industry entrants (Hikvision, Geek+, even internet companies) could take a share of the incremental market

**Munger's interpretation**: For traditional forklift manufacturers, intelligent features are more of a "necessary investment" than a source of excess returns. Software technology is a hard thing to turn into a moat — because competitors can buy the same sensors and algorithms. The real moat lies in the combination of "hardware reliability x service density x customer stickiness" — and that is precisely where a traditional leader like Hangcha has the advantage.

### 6.3 Internationalization — The Biggest Growth Pole

Overseas revenue share for major Chinese forklift companies (2024 data/estimates):

| Company | Overseas revenue share | Key markets |
|------|:----------:|----------|
| Hangcha Group | ~38-40% | Europe (44%), Southeast Asia (41%), Americas (23%) |
| Anhui Heli | ~35% | Southeast Asia (Thailand/Vietnam #1 for years running), Europe/US |
| Zhongli | ~50%+ | Global value king in Class III trucks |
| BYD Forklift | ~40%+ | European new-energy market |

**Trends**:
- China exported 480,500 forklifts in 2024, up 18.53% YoY, far outpacing the 4.77% growth in domestic sales
- Electric forklifts already account for more than 78% of exports, and have become the core driver of China's forklift exports
- Hangcha has established more than 20 overseas subsidiaries, and set up Hangcha America Smart Logistics (HASL) in 2025
- The SKD (semi-knocked-down) export model reduces tariff costs and local-assembly costs

**Munger's interpretation**: The internationalization path resembles Toyota's global expansion in the 1970s-80s — first exporting complete vehicles, then building assembly plants, and finally establishing R&D centers. Hangcha's overseas footprint is currently in the transition "from trade to localization," and the direction is right, but execution difficulty should not be underestimated. Cultural friction, compliance risk, and tariff barriers are real obstacles.

### 6.4 Aftermarket — An Underexploited Gold Mine

| Aftermarket business | Estimated market size | Characteristics |
|-----------|:----------:|------|
| Parts sales | ~CNY 10 billion+ per year | High margin (>40%), extremely strong customer stickiness |
| Maintenance and repair | ~CNY 8 billion+ per year | Recurring revenue, counter-cyclical |
| Forklift leasing | ~CNY 6 billion+ per year | Rising penetration, lowers the barrier to customer adoption |
| Battery recycling / battery swap | ~CNY 3 billion+ per year | New market created by lithium-ionization |
| Used equipment / remanufacturing | ~CNY 2 billion+ per year | Standardization is improving |

> Note: The figures above are rough estimates based on total industry size and penetration rates, not precise statistics.

**Judgment**: The aftermarket is the "hidden profit pool" of the forklift industry. Forklifts typically have a 5-10 year service life, and the installed base continually generates demand for parts and service. At Western forklift companies, the aftermarket can contribute 30-50% of profit, while Chinese firms' aftermarket revenue share remains low (Hangcha is estimated at 15-20%). This is a source for further gross-margin improvement at Hangcha.

|--------|:---:|-----------|:--------:|
| Steel (plate/section) | ~25-30% | Baosteel, Ansteel, Hunan Valin Steel | Fell in 2024, moderate rebound expected 2025-26 |

**Key insights**:
- Lithium-battery costs are much higher than lead-acid battery costs, but with falling lithium prices and scale effects, battery-pack costs have declined noticeably in 2024-2025
- Steel, the largest cost item, has price swings that directly affect forklift gross margin. Hangcha's gross margin rose from 15.51% in 2021 to 24.78% in 2025, and falling steel prices were an important contributor
- Domestic substitution in motors/motor controllers (e.g., Inovance Technology) is lowering electric-forklift manufacturing costs and narrowing the price gap between electric and internal-combustion forklifts

### 7.2 Downstream Customer Distribution

| Downstream industry | Share of demand | Characteristics |
|---------|:-------:|------|
| Manufacturing (in-plant logistics) | ~35% | Moderate cyclicality, high customer stickiness |
| Logistics/warehousing/express delivery | ~25% | Fastest growing, driven by e-commerce |
| Wholesale and retail | ~15% | Stable demand, many individual customers |
| Ports/airports | ~10% | Large-tonnage, high-end demand |
| Construction/mining | ~8% | Highly cyclical, mainly internal-combustion demand |
| Food and pharmaceuticals | ~5% | Special-environment needs (cold chain/clean rooms) |
| Other | ~2% | — |

**Ranking of customer decision factors** (based on industry survey estimates):
1. **Price** (weight ~30%) — most sensitive in the low-to-mid-end market
2. **After-sales response speed** (weight ~25%) — a stopped forklift means a stopped production line
3. **Brand reputation** (weight ~20%) — matters most to large customers
4. **Product performance** (weight ~15%) — differences are minor
5. **Payment terms** (weight ~10%) — the importance of leasing/installment plans is rising

**Munger's interpretation**: The forklift industry's downstream customer base is extremely fragmented (almost no single customer accounts for more than 5% of revenue) — a hallmark of a "good business," since low customer concentration means pricing power sits with the manufacturer. On the other hand, customer price-sensitivity means the industry rarely commands a brand premium — a hallmark of an "ordinary business." Hangcha's strategy should be to raise the share of aftermarket services to lock in customers, gradually lowering the weight of price in the purchase decision.

## VIII. Barrier-to-Entry Analysis (Munger's Core Framework)

### 8.1 Assessing the "Lollapalooza Effect" in Munger's Investment Framework

Munger believes a true moat comes from the compounding resonance of multiple factors:

| Barrier factor | Strength | Explanation |
|---------|:----:|------|
| **Scale effects** | ★★★★ | Hundreds of thousands of units produced annually; clear economies of scale mean new entrants cannot compete on cost |
| **Brand recognition** | ★★★ | A forklift is a capital good, so brand value is not like that of a consumer product; "Hangcha" has high recognition among customers but commands no premium |
| **Service-network density** | ★★★★ | Hundreds of dealers and service points nationwide; new entrants would need 5-10 years to replicate this |
| **Technology accumulation** | ★★★ | Manufacturing craft has a barrier but is not disruptive; lithium-battery technology can be acquired through procurement |
| **Customer switching costs** | ★★★★ | Once a customer buys a given brand's forklift, parts, maintenance, and operating habits create dependency, raising switching costs — though not as high as SaaS-level lock-in |
| **Capital barriers** | ★★★ | Reaching economies of scale requires investment in the billions of CNY, but this is not insurmountable |

**Overall judgment**: The forklift industry's moat is of the "wide but not deep" type — wide enough to block small-scale new entrants, but not enough to stop a sufficiently determined cross-industry challenger (an opponent at the scale of BYD, for instance).

> Munger's words: "**If a company's moat needs to be re-dug every year, it's not really a moat.**" The forklift industry's distribution network and service system do require ongoing investment to maintain, but once built they don't get wiped out easily — that is the essence of a "wide but not deep" moat: it requires upkeep costs, but it does not disappear.

### 8.2 "Antifragility" Assessment

| Risk type | Probability | Impact | Hangcha's response |
|---------|:----:|:----:|-----------|
| Technology-path disruption | Low | High | Lithium direction is correct; hydrogen fuel cells still need watching |
| Trade war/tariff escalation | Medium | Medium | Overseas plants + SKD model can mitigate |
| Sharp rise in raw-material prices | Medium | Medium | Leading companies have pricing power and cost pass-through ability |
| Price war | Medium | Low | Probability of destructive price war is lower under a duopoly structure |
| Cross-industry challenger (BYD-scale) | Low | High | Service network and installed customer base are the biggest defense |

**Conclusion**: The forklift industry is not easily disrupted (it is not a technology-intensive industry), but nor can it earn excess returns (it is not a high-ROIC industry). Hangcha's 20% ROE is excellent among Chinese manufacturers.

### 9.3 Munger's Three Questions

**Question 1: Is the industry easy to understand?**
Yes. A forklift is simply an industrial vehicle; the products, customers, competitive landscape, and technology path are all clear. There is no risk of "not knowing what the company will be doing in five years."

**Question 2: Does the industry have a durable competitive advantage?**
Partially. The duopoly structure provides some pricing power (low probability of destructive price wars), but the industry does not have a "very wide moat" characteristic. Hangcha's and Heli's advantage lies in the combination of scale + distribution + service.

**Question 3: Will the industry be better or worse in five years?**
**More favorable**. The core logic:
1. Lithium-ionization + internationalization give Chinese forklift leaders a "volume and price both rising" dynamic — export unit prices exceed domestic prices, and electric-forklift unit prices exceed internal-combustion prices
2. Concentration in the duopoly structure continues to rise, and small manufacturers are being weeded out faster
3. China's electric-forklift penetration rate has already reached 73%+ in 2025, but excluding Class III trucks, the real electrification rate still has room to roughly double
4. Overseas revenue share rising from the current 35-40% to 50%+ is a high-probability event

**Munger-style final judgment**:

> "Forklifts are not an industry that will make you rich overnight, but they are an industry **where it's hard to lose money**. Against a backdrop of continued growth in global manufacturing and logistics, moderate growth in forklifts carries high certainty. As one of China's two dominant players, Hangcha is well positioned on the two key tracks of lithium-ionization and internationalization.
>
> The biggest risks come from two sources — first, an escalating trade war could disrupt the internationalization process; second, a cross-industry challenger at BYD's scale, if it commits serious capital, could change the competitive landscape. But on balance, this industry is better suited to being 'bought at a reasonable price' than to waiting for an 'extremely undervalued price.'"
