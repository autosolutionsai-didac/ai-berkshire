# Series Notes: "Understanding AGIBOT"

> 4 main articles, taking apart layer by layer a humanoid robot company that is 3 years old, valued at 15 billion yuan, and not yet profitable.
>
> The series takes about 50 minutes to read; reading in order is recommended.

---

## Series positioning

AGIBOT is not a company that "lacks attention" — it may be one of the **most talked-about and most divisive** companies in China's hard-tech startup scene from 2023 to 2026.

- The bulls say: the world's largest shipment volume, 10,000 units off the production line, backing from Tencent, Hillhouse, and Sequoia, and Zhihui Jun as China's strongest hardware founder
- The bears say: volume without profit, suspiciously inflated related-party orders, an unseemly reverse-merger listing, and 140 competitors locked in a race to the bottom

**Both camps are looking at the same company but reaching opposite conclusions.** What makes this harder is that because AGIBOT is not publicly listed, has no audited financial statements, and its core financial data is entirely missing, both camps' arguments are "plausible but unfalsifiable."

What this series aims to do is **lay out the known facts and data clearly, and flag the unknown blind spots** — so you can judge for yourself whether this company is worth following.

## Writing principles for this series

This series follows these hard rules:

1. **Lay out the data first, then the logic, then the conclusion** — no bullish or bearish stance is assumed in advance
2. **Every core judgment is paired with a counter-argument** — so readers can weigh it themselves
3. **Unreliable data is deleted rather than kept** — AGIBOT's core financial data (net profit, gross margin, cash flow) have not been disclosed; this series uses only the most credible sources as a baseline and does not fill in false precision
4. **No investment advice is given** — every article ends with a disclaimer
5. **"Fact" is distinguished from "speculation"** — estimated figures are explicitly labeled as such
