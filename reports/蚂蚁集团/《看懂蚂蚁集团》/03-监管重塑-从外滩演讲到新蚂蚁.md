# Regulatory Reshaping — From the Bund Speech to the New Ant

> "Understanding Ant Group" series · Part 03
> Reading time: about 15 minutes

## It all started with that speech

On October 24, 2020, exactly two days after the IPO cleared its final regulatory review, Jack Ma took the stage at the Bund Finance Summit in Shanghai. At that moment, Ant was the hottest company on the planet: less than two weeks remained before what would have been the largest IPO in history, at $34.4 billion, and thousands of employees were waiting for their wealth to be realized. No one anticipated what came next.

Jack Ma's 21-minute speech shattered the calm. He said:

- "The Basel Accords are like an old-folks' club."
- "China's finance has no systemic risk, because Chinese finance basically has no system."
- "Good innovation isn't afraid of regulation, but it is afraid of being regulated using yesterday's methods."

Eleven days later, on November 3, 2020, the Shanghai and Hong Kong stock exchanges simultaneously announced the suspension of Ant Group's listing. The IPO, which was set to raise **$34.4 billion**, came to an abrupt halt at the last moment.

**From that day on, Ant entered a regulatory overhaul that would last three years and whose aftershocks are still being felt today.**

## Five rectification requirements: each one changed Ant's DNA

After the four regulators' summons in December 2020, Ant received five explicit rectification requirements. Breaking them down one by one:

### Requirement one: return to the core business of payments, severing improper links between payments and financial products

**Before**: opening Alipay, the homepage would recommend Huabei installment plans, Jiebei loans, and Yu'ebao wealth-management products — payments and finance flowed together seamlessly, and users found themselves "financialized" without quite realizing it.

**After**: Huabei/Jiebei were folded into Ant Consumer Finance (a licensed consumer-finance company), separated from the Alipay entity itself. Financial-product recommendations on the Alipay homepage were sharply reduced. New rules from eight ministries in April 2026 further restricted the bundling of payments with credit.

**Impact**: Huabei usage frequency may have fallen **30-40%**, but the credit business is now more compliant and healthier.

### Requirement two: establish a financial holding company

**Before**: Ant operated payments, consumer lending, funds, insurance, banking, and other financial businesses under the guise of a "technology company," sidestepping bank-level capital and regulatory requirements.

**After**: Ant submitted its financial holding company license application in July 2023. But as of May 2026, **the license still has not been approved**. So far the PBOC has approved only three financial holding company licenses — CITIC Financial Holdings, Beijing Financial Holdings, and China Merchants Financial Holdings — all with state-owned/central-enterprise backgrounds. If approved, Ant would be **the largest privately-owned financial holding company case**.

**This is the single biggest obstacle to Ant's overall listing.** Without the financial holding company license, an IPO prospectus cannot give a clear account of regulatory risk. Ant's status — "each individual business is running normally, but the group level still lacks its legal identity card" — has now persisted for nearly three years.

### Requirement three: reduce credit leverage

**Before**: Huabei/Jiebei used roughly RMB 3 billion of its own capital to lever up more than **RMB 2 trillion** in outstanding loan balances, a leverage ratio of nearly 100x. The core technique was revolving loans funded through ABS (asset securitization).

**After**: Ant Consumer Finance's registered capital rose to RMB 18.5 billion, with a leverage cap of 10x, and roughly RMB 300 billion in loans on its own balance sheet. ABS-driven leverage was thoroughly compressed, and the credit business shifted from "lending on its own book to earn the spread" to "helping banks lend and collecting a technology fee."

**Impact**: lower profit margins, but far lower risk. It's like someone going from "borrowing money to trade stocks" to "helping others pick stocks for a consulting fee" — you earn less, but you don't get wiped out.

### Requirement four: personal-information protection and credit-reporting compliance

**Before**: Alipay had accumulated data spanning payments, spending, wealth management, and social activity for 1 billion users, and Sesame Credit scores were used broadly without a credit-reporting license.

**After**: Qiantang Credit Reporting was established (holding a personal credit-reporting license), bringing Ant's data assets under the regulatory framework.

### Requirement five: adjustments to management and governance structure

**Before**: Jack Ma exercised actual control over Ant through his shareholding and voting agreements, with voting rights reaching **53.46%**.

**After**: Jack Ma's voting rights fell to **6.2%**, and 10 members of management received dispersed voting rights. Ant moved into a state of having "no actual controlling person." Jack Ma appeared at Ant's 20th-anniversary celebration in December 2024 and returned to Alibaba's campus in 2025 — a nominal exit, with "invisible influence" still present.

## A 65% evaporation in valuation: where did the money go?

Ant's valuation has fallen from **RMB 2.1 trillion** in 2020 to roughly **RMB 700-750 billion** currently, an evaporation of about **RMB 1.4 trillion**. This isn't attributable to any single cause, but to four layers stacking together:

### Layer one: forced changes to the business structure (contributing ~40% of the evaporation)

Before the overhaul, Ant was, in substance, a "low-capital, high-leverage quasi-bank" — using RMB 3 billion in capital to lever up RMB 2 trillion in credit, with enormous room for profit. After the overhaul it became a "technology company providing technical services to banks" — steadier revenue, but a much lower ceiling.

**Profit potential before the overhaul**: if the RMB 2 trillion in outstanding loans is calculated at a 3% spread, the theoretical upper bound on profit was RMB 60 billion.

**Profit reality after the overhaul**: Ant Consumer Finance plus MYbank together earned RMB 6.2 billion in net profit — an entire order of magnitude lower.

### Layer two: a regulatory-uncertainty premium (contributing ~25% of the evaporation)

With the financial holding company license still undecided, the listing timeline opaque, and Jack Ma's political sensitivity, investors are demanding a higher "uncertainty discount" on Ant.

### Layer three: changes in the market environment (contributing ~20% of the evaporation)

2020 was the peak of the global tech-stock bubble; Ant's RMB 2.1 trillion valuation implied a **67x P/E** on that year's RMB 31.2 billion net profit — a bubble valuation in its own right. Even absent the rectification, that valuation would not have been sustainable.

### Layer four: a deteriorating competitive landscape (contributing ~15% of the evaporation)

WeChat Pay's share rose from 39% to 59.7%, while Alipay's fell from 55% to 36.2%. Douyin Pay's registered capital surged 20x to RMB 3.15 billion. Digital yuan 2.0 launched on January 1, 2026.

**Bear case**: a 65% valuation evaporation also means "the bubble has been wrung out." The current valuation of RMB 700-750 billion implies an **18-20x P/E** on 2024's RMB 38.3 billion net profit — though using 2025's RMB 15.3 billion net profit, the P/E balloons to **46-49x**. But if 2025 was a "year of strategic investment" and profit recovers to RMB 40 billion-plus, the current valuation isn't expensive.

## Did the rectification make Ant "worse" or "healthier"?

This is the key question. Two fully opposing readings:

### The case for "worse"

| Dimension | Before | After | Direction |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Credit leverage | ~100x | 10x | Profit potential sharply compressed |
| Jack Ma's influence | Absolute control | Nominal exit | Loss of founder-driven spirit |
| Pricing power | Relatively strong | Constrained | Payment fees trending down, lending rates controlled |
| Valuation | RMB 2.1 trillion | RMB 700 billion | -65% |
| Payments share | 55% | 36.2% | Continuing decline |

### The case for "healthier"

| Dimension | Before | After | Direction |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Credit risk | Extremely high (high leverage) | Low (asset-light) | Risk resilience sharply improved |
| Compliance | Operating in a gray zone | Licensed operations | Regulatory risk sharply reduced |
| Business structure | Heavily dependent on credit spreads | Diversified (international + AI + wealth management) | More balanced |
| Competitive edge | Regulatory arbitrage | Compliance first-mover | Smaller rivals get weeded out |
| Internationalization | Almost nonexistent | RMB 27 billion in revenue | Opens new room to grow |

Both readings are backed by data, but they operate on different time horizons. In the short run it looks "worse" (profit and valuation have shrunk sharply); in the long run it looks "healthier" (lower leverage, more diversified, compliance-first). **The rectification turned Ant from a "high-risk, high-return quasi-bank" into a "moderate-return but more sustainable fintech platform."**

It's like someone forced to move from "leveraged stock trading" to "systematic index-fund investing" — the short-term thrill is gone, but the long-term footing is steadier.

## The financial holding company license: the last door to a listing

The financial holding company license is the key to understanding Ant's current predicament. In plain terms:

**What is a financial holding company license?** Any non-financial enterprise that controls two or more categories of financial institutions above a certain scale must set up a financial holding company, subject to unified PBOC supervision. Ant controls payments, consumer finance, banking, funds, insurance, and credit reporting — squarely triggering the application requirement.

**Why does it matter?** No financial holding company license = the group level lacks its "legal identity card" = the IPO cannot proceed. Each subsidiary holds its own individual license and can operate normally, but the parent company needs this "household registration booklet" to go public.

**Why hasn't it been approved yet?** As of May 2026, the PBOC has approved only three financial holding company licenses (CITIC, Beijing Financial Holdings, China Merchants Financial Holdings) — all with state-owned backgrounds. Ant is the largest private-sector applicant, so the approval process is inevitably more cautious. Political sensitivity (the Jack Ma factor) is also a variable.

| Scenario | Probability | Impact |
|------|------|------|
| Approved in 2026-2027 | **40%** | Clears the path to listing, valuation recovers 20-30% |
| Approved after 2028 | **30%** | Listing delayed, greater risk of talent attrition |
| Shelved indefinitely | **20%** | No prospect of listing, valuation remains under pressure |
| Rejected / approved with onerous conditions | **10%** | Could be forced to spin off financial businesses |

**Bear case**: the importance of the financial holding company license is overstated. Ant is already pushing Ant International toward an independent Hong Kong listing — a path that does not require the parent company's license. And even if the license is approved, it comes with higher capital requirements and tighter regulatory constraints, which isn't necessarily a good thing.

## Alibaba and Ant: a permanent "tax"

One detail that is often overlooked: Alibaba holds a **33%** equity stake in Ant and shares in Ant's profits under the equity method. More critically, under an early-stage agreement, Alibaba actually receives roughly **37.5% of Ant's pre-tax profit** as a profit share.

That means: for every RMB 10 billion Ant earns, about RMB 3.75 billion goes to Alibaba. This is a "permanent tax" that Ant's management cannot change.

For Ant's employees and other shareholders, **Ant's valuation is inherently discounted by Alibaba's profit-sharing right**.

But conversely, for Alibaba's shareholders — **owning Alibaba already means indirectly owning 33% of Ant**. At Ant's neutral valuation of RMB 750 billion × 33% = **RMB 247.5 billion**, that's roughly 10-15% of Alibaba's current market capitalization. Ant is effectively a "free option" embedded in Alibaba.
