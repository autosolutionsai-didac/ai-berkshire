## Summary analysis by dimension

### Business model & moat (Duan Yongping's lens)

1. **A qualitative shift in the business model**: from "selling commodity memory chips" to "selling a core AI infrastructure component (HBM)." HBM accounted for 77% of total revenue in Q2 2025
2. **The strongest moat is switching costs plus technology barriers**: the HBM qualification cycle runs 12-18 months, and MR-MUF packaging is the only process in the world capable of mass production at scale
3. **A flywheel spinning at high speed**: technology leadership → exclusive supply to NVIDIA → capacity lock-in → excess profit → heavier investment → continued leadership in the next generation
4. **The core tension**: HBM's 77% share of revenue means extreme concentration — once the HBM demand cycle turns, the impact will be amplified
5. **Duan Yongping's ultimate judgment**: "A good business, but it's uncertain whether it's a business that stays good forever"

### Financials & valuation (Buffett's lens)

1. **Margins went from a loss to 72% in just two years**: OPM was -24% in 2023 → 49% in 2025 → 72% in Q1 2026 — a stunning swing
2. **An extremely healthy balance sheet**: net cash of KRW 35 trillion at the end of Q1 2026, with the interest-bearing debt ratio down to ~16%
3. **Normalized-earnings valuation is expensive**: five-year average net income of KRW 13.12 trillion/year implies a normalized PE of roughly 100x+
4. **Three-scenario valuation**: bull +118% (PE 12x), base +17% (PE 8x), bear -48% (PE 5x)
5. **Buffett's take**: "When margins go from a loss to 72%, the instinctive reaction should be fear, not greed"

### Industry & competition (Munger's lens)

1. **A DRAM oligopoly of three controls 95% of the market**: Samsung 32%, SK Hynix 36%, Micron 25% — a good industry structure
2. **The HBM market is exploding**: projected at $54.6 billion in 2026, with SK Hynix's share of 62% expected to fall to ~54% (Samsung catching up to 28%)
3. **A contrarian finding**: Samsung's Q1 2026 margin on conventional DRAM was actually higher than on HBM — due to quarterly pricing versus annual price-lock mechanisms
4. **The memory shortage could persist through 2027-2028**: producers' cautious capacity expansion may be the reason this cycle proves more durable
5. **NAND is a worse business than DRAM**: more competitors, lower differentiation, and the low-cost threat from YMTC

### Risk assessment (Li Lu's lens)

1. **Excellent management (low risk)**: CEO Kwak Noh-jung has a strong technical background and has made precise HBM strategic calls; DRAM revenue surpassed Samsung's for the first time in 2025
2. **Geopolitics (medium-to-high risk)**: China-based plants account for 35-40% of DRAM capacity, and the annual licensing regime brings persistent uncertainty
3. **NVIDIA concentration (medium-to-high risk)**: 27% of revenue comes from a single customer, and pricing power may tilt toward the large buyer
4. **CXMT gray rhino (medium risk)**: 2025 revenue doubled to $8 billion, and the technology gap has narrowed to 3-4 years
5. **Yongin cluster capital bet (medium risk)**: total investment could reach KRW 600 trillion, spanning 10+ years

## Investment thesis (bull vs. bear)

### Bull case
1. AI infrastructure build-out is a long-term trend, and HBM is an irreplaceable "must-have" component
2. HBM's technology barrier is rising (HBM4 is more complex), widening the moat
3. Oligopoly structure + producers' cautious capacity expansion = improved supply discipline, which could make the cycle milder
4. Management has bet precisely on HBM, with best-in-class execution
5. At Forward PE 6.9x, if the AI super-cycle persists for 2-3 years, the stock is significantly undervalued
6. An extremely healthy balance sheet, with net cash of KRW 35 trillion providing downside protection
7. DRAM supply growth is lagging demand, and the shortage could persist through 2028

### Bear case
1. Over the memory industry's thirty-year history, every "this time is different" call has ultimately proven wrong — the cycle always returns
2. Q1 2026's 72% operating margin is the most extreme level ever recorded in the memory industry, and mean reversion is inevitable
3. NVIDIA accounts for 27% of revenue — customer concentration is too high, and pricing power could be squeezed
4. Samsung's HBM4 received NVIDIA's top rating, and the competitive landscape could deteriorate significantly in the HBM4 generation
5. CXMT's capacity is doubling, which could trigger a DRAM supply glut in 2027-2028
6. The KRW 600 trillion Yongin cluster investment is a long-term bet, with large cycle-mismatch risk
7. On a normalized-earnings basis, the current share price offers almost no margin of safety

## Summary

SK Hynix is a business that is "currently excellent" — the world's number-one in HBM, with customers chasing after its capacity, and margins at the highest level ever seen in the memory industry. But it is also a business that is "questionable long term" — the memory industry has never escaped its boom-bust cycle, 27% of revenue depends on a single customer (NVIDIA), and although the moat is deep, it is built on "technology leadership" rather than "brand loyalty." Measured against normalized earnings, the current share price offers an insufficient margin of safety. For value investors, a better strategy is to wait for a cyclical pullback before entering — buying a "good company" at a "good price," rather than chasing it at the height of its glory.
