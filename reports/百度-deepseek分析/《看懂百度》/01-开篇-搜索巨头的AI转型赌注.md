## A Set of Numbers That Will Make You Rethink Baidu

**A company with net cash of 239.4 billion yuan equal to 70% of its market cap, yet whose free cash flow just turned negative and whose profit has collapsed 76% — it is simultaneously "the world's cheapest AI company" and possibly a value trap slowly bleeding out.** This is Baidu in 2026.

Over the past 5 years, Baidu's stock price has traced a bewildering curve:

- February 2021: an all-time high of **$354** — the peak of the "China's leading AI stock" narrative
- October 2022: **$75** — panic selling of Chinese ADRs
- April 2024: **$117** — a brief revival on the Ernie Bot narrative
- September 2025: **$81** — profit cliff-dive + free cash flow turns negative, market confidence hits rock bottom
- May 2026: **~$141** — $5 billion buyback + first-ever dividend + Ernie 5.0 launch, sentiment recovers

**Over 5 years, Baidu went from "AI front-runner" to "AI-era laggard," and is now climbing back.** But in hindsight, each narrative shift looks like it overreacted.

Opening the 2025 annual report, Baidu shows a set of counterintuitive metrics:

| Metric | 2025 | Scale Assessment |
|------|---------|---------|
| Total revenue | **129.1 billion yuan** (-3%) | First full-year decline since listing |
| Net income attributable | **5.6 billion yuan** (**-76%**) | A cliff-edge drop |
| Non-GAAP net income | **18.9 billion yuan** (-30%) | Still profitable after stripping out impairments |
| Free cash flow | **-15.1 billion yuan** | **Turned negative for the first time ever** |
| Net cash | **239.4 billion yuan (~$33 billion)** | **About 70% of current market cap** |
| New AI business revenue | **~40 billion yuan** (+48%) | 31% of total revenue, reaching 43% in Q4 |
| Non-GAAP P/E | **~16x** | Looks cheap on the surface |
| P/B | **1.16x** | Close to "liquidation value" |
| Online marketing revenue | **~63 billion yuan** (**-14%**) | Core cash cow shrinking faster |

Put these numbers together — **a company with net cash of 239.4 billion yuan (70% of market cap) and a P/B of just 1.16x, whose core advertising business is shrinking 14% a year and whose free cash flow just turned negative, yet whose new AI business is growing at 48%.**

If seeing "P/B 1.16x" makes you think it's cheap — you're probably only seeing half the picture.
If seeing "profit -76%" makes you think it's finished — you're probably also only seeing half the picture.

**What this series sets out to do is put the two halves together, so you can see the whole of Baidu.**

## What Exactly Is Baidu?

Before we start taking things apart, let's answer a basic question: what kind of company is Baidu in 2026, exactly?

Many people's mental model is still stuck on "a search engine company." That was accurate before 2020, but it is no longer accurate today.

Look at the 2025 revenue structure:

| Business Segment | Revenue (billion yuan, est.) | Share | YoY | Nature |
|---------|---------------|------|------|------|
| Online marketing (search advertising) | ~63.0 | 49% | **-14%** | Old engine, stalling |
| AI Cloud + infrastructure | ~20.0 | 15% | **+34%** | One new engine |
| AI applications + AI-native marketing | ~20.0 | 15% | **+70%+** | Another new engine |
| iQIYI | ~27.0 | 21% | ~flat | Non-core, but contributes cash flow |

> Note: AI segment revenue figures are estimates; Baidu's annual report does not fully disclose the breakdown by sub-segment. Sources: Baidu annual report, Wall Street CN, 36Kr.

**Baidu in 2025 = a search-advertising machine that is stalling (49%) + a set of new AI engines that are firing up (30%) + a legacy video business (21%).**

More precisely, Baidu is going through a "life-swap" — not a "transformation."

Where's the difference?

- **Tencent's AI is "empowerment"**: AI makes WeChat more useful and games more profitable — the old business and the new technology point in the same direction
- **Baidu's AI is "disruption"**: AI search reduces the number of ad impressions — the better the AI gets, the harder ads are to sell

**This is Baidu's unique "left hand fighting the right hand" dilemma: igniting the new engine is precisely what accelerates the stalling of the old one.**

## Three Core Contradictions About Baidu

### Contradiction One: Strongest Technology vs. Weakest Monetization

Baidu's accumulated technical depth in AI genuinely puts it in the first tier among Chinese internet companies:

- **Kunlunxin**: The No. 2 domestic AI chip by shipment volume (behind only Huawei Ascend), with 60,000 P800 units shipped and orders exceeding 200,000 units, valued at **21 billion yuan**, and pushing toward an "A+H" listing
- **PaddlePaddle**: China's No. 1 deep learning framework by market share
- **Ernie foundation model**: version 5.0's official release has **2.4 trillion** parameters; version 5.1's pre-training cost is just 6% of the industry standard; its search capability leads domestically
- **Apollo Go**: The world's No. 1 Robotaxi platform by order volume (**20 million+ orders**), entering London in 2026

But what about monetization?

| Comparison | Baidu AI revenue (2025) | ByteDance AI-related revenue (est.) | Alibaba Cloud revenue (2025) |
|------|-------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Scale | ~40 billion yuan | far exceeds Baidu | ~110 billion yuan+ |
| AI Cloud market share | **6.1%** (Omdia) | 14.8% (Volcano Engine) | **36%** (No. 1) |

**Counter-argument**: Strong technology does not equal a strong business. In AI, monetization depends on distribution channels and user scale, not model parameters. ByteDance has 700 million DAU, Tencent has 1.3 billion WeChat users — what does Baidu have? Of its 679 million MAU, only **4.79 million** use Ernie Bot — a conversion rate of **0.7%**.

**Rebuttal**: Losing the consumer-facing entry point doesn't mean losing the enterprise-facing one. Baidu AI Cloud has ranked No. 1 in large-model bid-winning for two consecutive years, serving **65% of central state-owned enterprises** and every systemically important bank. Enterprise customers don't look at MAU — they look at technical depth and delivery capability.

### Contradiction Two: Enormous Cash Pile vs. Extremely Fast Cash Burn

Baidu sits on **239.4 billion yuan of net cash**, roughly 70% of its current market cap. In February 2026 it also announced its first-ever **$5 billion buyback plus its first dividend**.

Looks very safe? But look at the other side:

- 2025 free cash flow: **-15.1 billion yuan** — turning negative for the first time ever
- Capital expenditure up **+49%** YoY to 12.1 billion yuan — AI infrastructure spending is accelerating
- Operating cash flow: **-3.0 billion yuan** — it's not just the investing side burning cash; operations themselves are bleeding

**At the current burn rate, if free cash flow stays negative, the 239.4 billion yuan of net cash will shrink materially within 2-3 years.**

**Counter-argument**: A $5 billion buyback sounds generous, but if the company is burning 15 billion yuan+ a year, the buyback is just moving money from one pocket to the other. The real safety cushion isn't "how much cash is on the balance sheet" — it's "how much the company earns each year."

**Rebuttal**: The 2025 negative cash flow reflects one-off factors (a 16.2 billion yuan asset impairment, plus a concentrated investment period for Apollo and cloud infrastructure). 2026Q1 has already rebounded — revenue of 32.5 billion yuan and core net income of 7.6 billion yuan, up +48% YoY. A single year's data shouldn't be linearly extrapolated.

### Contradiction Three: Extremely Low Valuation vs. Extremely Low Certainty

Baidu's P/B is **1.16x**, and its Non-GAAP P/E is **~16x**. If you strip out net cash, Baidu's "operating assets" are valued at only about **100 billion yuan** — for a company with annual revenue of 129.1 billion yuan, the market is assigning less than 1x P/S.

What does this valuation imply? **The market is essentially saying: Baidu's search advertising business will keep shrinking, it's uncertain whether the new AI business will ever be profitable, so I'm only willing to pay for the net cash.**

| Valuation Metric | Baidu | Tencent | Alibaba |
|---------|------|------|------|
| P/B | **1.16x** | ~4.5x | ~1.8x |
| P/E (Non-GAAP) | ~16x | ~18x | ~12x |
| Net cash / market cap | **~70%** | ~10% | ~30% |

**Counter-argument**: A low P/B doesn't mean cheap. If the core business keeps losing money and burning cash, net assets themselves are shrinking. The combination of "low P/B + negative FCF" is called a "value trap" in value investing, not "undervaluation."

**Rebuttal**: Kunlunxin's IPO is imminent (J.P. Morgan forecasts a 2026 valuation exceeding **80 billion yuan**), and that alone could unlock 25%+ of Baidu's market cap. If Apollo Go succeeds in going global, the optionality is even greater. The market isn't pricing in these assets that are "visible but not yet realized."

## Understanding Baidu Requires Looking at "Two Baidus" Separately

If you had to define Baidu in 2026 in a single sentence:

> **Baidu = a search-advertising machine that is stalling + a set of new AI engines firing up (foundation models + autonomous driving + AI Cloud) + a 239.4-billion-yuan cash cushion + a chip company about to IPO.**

These four things must be examined separately before being weighed together. The reason the market is so divided on Baidu is precisely that most people only look at one or two of them:

## Preview: Signals from Baidu's 2026Q1

A hook to leave you with. The Q1 earnings just released on May 21, 2026 contained a few noteworthy signals:

| Metric | 2026Q1 | YoY |
|------|--------|------|
| Total revenue | **32.5 billion yuan** | +3% (reversing the full-year decline trend) |
| Baidu Core revenue | **25.5 billion yuan** | +7% |
| Baidu Core net income | **7.63 billion yuan** | **+48%** |
| AI Cloud revenue | up **+42%** YoY | accelerating |
| Online marketing revenue | **16.0 billion yuan** | **-6%** (narrowing decline) |

The signal from the Q1 data is: **the old engine's decline is decelerating, the new engine's growth is accelerating, and profit is warming up.** But one quarter doesn't make a trend — 2025Q1 also looked optimistic at the time, only for full-year profit to then collapse 76%.

**Is Baidu "bottoming out and rebounding" or is this a "dead-cat bounce"?** That's the question the next 4 essays will try to answer.
