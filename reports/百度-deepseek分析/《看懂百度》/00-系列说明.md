# "Understanding Baidu" Series Overview

> 5 essays that take apart Baidu — a company where "the old engine is stalling and the new engine is firing up" — layer by layer.
>
> Total reading time for the series is about 50 minutes; recommended to read in order.

---

## Series Positioning

Baidu is **one of the hardest companies to price** in the Chinese internet sector — not because there isn't enough information, but because "old Baidu" and "new Baidu" exist simultaneously, moving in completely opposite directions.

- The bulls say: **net cash of 239.4 billion yuan** equals 70%+ of market cap, Baidu is China's only company with full-stack AI capability, Kunlunxin is about to IPO, and Apollo Go has the largest global scale — it's absurdly cheap
- The bears say: search advertising is in irreversible decline, free cash flow turned negative in 2025, the consumer-facing AI entry point has already been lost to Doubao, and management "can see the future but can't get there" — the cheapness is cheap for a reason

**Both camps are looking at the same company, but one is looking at the future and the other is looking at the past.** That is the real root of the disagreement.

What this series sets out to do is **explain "old Baidu" and "new Baidu" separately, then let you judge for yourself which Baidu carries more weight** — rather than handing you a conclusion.

## Series Writing Principles

This series follows these hard rules:

1. **Lay out the data first, then the logic, then the conclusion** — no preset bullish or bearish stance
2. **Every core judgment carries a counter-argument** — so readers can weigh it themselves
3. **Unreliable data is better deleted than kept** — many of Baidu's key figures (AI Cloud gross margin, Apollo Go's full P&L, etc.) are not publicly disclosed; this series uses only the most credible sources and does not force false precision
4. **No "probability-weighted expected return" calculations** — assigning weights to scenarios is purely subjective and gives readers a false sense of precision
5. **This does not constitute investment advice** — a disclaimer appears at the end of every essay
