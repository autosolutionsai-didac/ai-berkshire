### 1.2 Why Sell at a Loss, Then Buy Back at a Higher Price?

**Context at the time of the Q4 2021 sale:**
- July 2021: Didi was forcibly pulled from app stores for a rectification review just three days after its IPO
- August 2021: the "double reduction" education policy was introduced; New Oriental and TAL Education shares fell more than 90%
- Chinese internet platforms faced sweeping regulatory scrutiny; Alibaba, Tencent, and PDD all fell sharply
- March 2021: Colin Huang resigned as CEO and gave up most of his wealth, for reasons that were never disclosed
- PDD's fundamentals at the time: revenue was growing fast but the company was barely profitable, and the business model was still being validated

**Context at the time of the Q2 2025 buyback:**
- PDD's full-year 2024 revenue was 393.8 billion yuan (versus approximately 90 billion yuan in 2021 — roughly a 4x increase)
- 2024 net profit was 112.3 billion yuan (PDD was close to unprofitable in 2021)
- Temu had already built scale in major markets worldwide (it didn't exist at all in 2021)
- The P/E ratio was approximately 9–10x; in 2021 PDD had almost no profit to calculate a P/E from
- China's tech regulatory storm had largely subsided

**Logic:** the absolute share price of $104.95 was higher than the $78.86 exit price, but what was being bought was a fundamentally different company — profit went from nothing to over 100 billion yuan a year, and the P/E was actually cheaper (profit grew far faster than the share price).
