## I. Nature of the Business (Duan Yongping's Lens)

### 1.1 One-sentence definition

> PDD is **a traffic allocator for China's excess manufacturing capacity**: it uses algorithms to connect price-sensitive consumers with white-label factories, capturing an efficiency spread in China (the main platform) and replicating that logic globally (Temu).

### 1.2 Revenue-mix breakdown

| Business | 2025 revenue share (estimate) | Growth rate | Gross margin | Business model |
|------|-------------------|------|-------|---------|
| Domestic main platform (advertising + commissions) | ~55% | ~5-8% | ~75% | Platform matchmaking, asset-light |
| Temu (cross-border international + local warehousing) | ~40% | ~30% | ~35% | Hybrid of self-operated and matchmaking, growing more asset-heavy |
| Duoduo Grocery (group-buying for agricultural produce) | ~5% | ~20% | ~15% | Fulfillment-heavy, near-monopoly |

### 1.4 Duan Yongping-style probing question

> **What's good about this business, in one sentence?**
>
> Its strength is **an extremely powerful scale-effect flywheel** — more users draw more merchants, which drives prices lower, which draws more users. But "low price" is a brand discount rather than a brand premium — PDD cannot charge consumers a premium, and that is the core reason Duan Yongping would not build a large position here: this business depends on efficiency, not trust.

## II. Moat Assessment (Buffett's Lens)

### 2.1 Verification of the five moat types

| Moat type | Strength | Evidence | Counter-evidence |
|-----------|:----:|------|------|
| **Brand / pricing power** | ★★☆ | "PDD equals cheap" is deeply ingrained | "Cheap" is a discount brand, unable to raise prices; users are loyal to low prices, not to the platform |
| **Switching costs** | ★★☆ | Users have purchase history and habits | Users routinely have 5 e-commerce apps installed at once; switching cost is zero |
| **Network effects** | ★★★☆ | More users draw more merchants and richer selection | Weaker than WeChat or Meituan; Douyin built RMB 4 trillion in GMV in 3 years, proving network effects can be bypassed |
| **Scale effects** | ★★★★ | Traffic and warehousing costs fall with scale; C2M direct sourcing carries a cost advantage | Alibaba has comparable scale; the differentiator is algorithms, not scale alone |
| **Technology / patents** | ★★★☆ | Recommendation algorithms (Temu's "gamified shopping") provide some barrier to entry | High risk of being caught up with; ByteDance's recommendation algorithm is no weaker than PDD's |

**Overall moat rating: B+** (the real moat is scale effects, but it is much narrower than Alibaba's or Tencent's)

### 2.2 Moat trajectory

- **Past 3 years**: due to Douyin's rise, the domestic moat has actually been narrowing (market share fell from ~24% to ~19%)
- **Next 3 years**: hinges on whether "New PDD" (C2M branding) can upgrade from an efficiency moat to a brand moat

### 2.3 Buffett-style probing question

> **Will this moat still be here in 10 years? What could destroy it?**
>
> Three paths to destruction: (1) ByteDance/Douyin fully replacing PDD's recommendation-driven shopping experience with content plus algorithms; (2) AI-native shopping changing user behavior (users no longer rely on a platform to find low prices — AI directly matches supply and demand); (3) domestic consumption upgrading reducing price sensitivity, causing the "low price" moat to lose its foundation. **10-year certainty: low.**

### 3.2 The most critical "hidden harm": cash hoarding

**This is the single issue most worth analyzing in isolation**:

- **Fact**: as of year-end 2025, PDD's net cash on the balance sheet was $60.4B (roughly RMB 422B), equal to **42%** of market cap
- **The problem**: PE is only 10.5x, and management has never announced any buyback or dividend plan
- **Implication**: shareholders are effectively buying the operating business at roughly 6.3x earnings (PE net of cash), but whether that $60B in cash can actually make its way back to shareholders remains highly uncertain

**Why might this money never come back?**

1. **VIE structure blockage** (see Section IV): a large portion of the cash sits within Chinese operating entities, and repatriation requires regulatory approval
2. **Doubts about management's willingness to allocate capital**: Colin Huang's public statements have never included a commitment to shareholder returns
3. **Strategic-reserve hypothesis**: the cash may be earmarked for acquisitions, new business expansion, or to absorb regulatory fines or political risk
4. **A history of zero buybacks**: the company has never bought back stock even at low valuations, which is a strongly negative signal

> **What Duan Yongping might say**: a platform business trading at 10x PE with 32% ROE, sitting on $60B of net cash, yet neither buying back stock nor paying a dividend — that tells you either management doesn't care about shareholders, or they themselves don't believe in the company's long-term value. Neither is good for shareholders.

## IV. Deep Dive into VIE Structure Risk (Key Section)

### 4.1 PDD's VIE structure diagram

```
U.S. shareholders
    | hold equity
PDD Holdings Inc. (Cayman Islands) <- what you actually hold when you buy the U.S.-listed stock
    | wholly owned
PDD Hong Kong Limited (Hong Kong)
    | wholly owned
Shanghai Xunmeng Information Technology Co., Ltd. (China VIE entity)
    | contractual control (not equity)
Shanghai Pinwuwu Information Technology Co., Ltd. (operating entity, held by Chinese natural-person shareholders)
    |
Pinduoduo main-platform business + Duoduo Grocery business
```

**Key fact**: part of Temu's business operates through non-VIE subsidiaries. The share of revenue running through the VIE fell from 56.2% (2022) to 22.5% (2024) — this is actually a signal that risk is being structurally reduced.

### 4.2 VIE revenue-share trend (a risk-reduction signal)

| Year | VIE revenue as % of total revenue | Meaning |
|------|--------------|------|
| 2022 | 56.2% | Domestic main platform dominant |
| 2023 | 45.7% | Temu began contributing revenue through direct subsidiaries |
| 2024 | 22.5% | Temu's international business expanded rapidly, bypassing the VIE |
| 2025E | ~15-20% | Reliance on the VIE continues to decline |

> **Constructive reading**: VIE risk is structurally declining on its own, because Temu's international business does not depend on the VIE but instead operates through wholly owned subsidiaries held directly by the parent.

### 4.3 The six-layer VIE risk matrix

| Risk type | Trigger condition | Probability | Impact | Overall rating |
|---------|---------|:-------:|:-------:|:-------:|
| **VIE agreement invalidity risk** | Chinese courts rule the VIE agreements violate foreign-investment access restrictions | Low (5-10%) | Extremely high (could mean losing operational control) | ⚠️⚠️ |

| **HFCAA delisting risk** | PCAOB audit inspection cannot be completed, forcing delisting from a U.S. exchange | Low-medium (China and the U.S. reached an agreement in 2022, but it is reversible) | High | ⚠️⚠️ |

### 4.4 "Can that $60B in cash actually come back?" — a dedicated VIE-lens analysis

**Estimated distribution of the $60.4B in net cash:**

| Location held | Estimated amount | Difficulty of repatriation | Likelihood of being returned to shareholders |
|---------|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Cash held in China VIE/subsidiaries | ~$30-35B | High (requires SAFE approval, subject to tax) | Medium |
| Cash held in offshore subsidiaries (Temu operations) | ~$15-20B | Low (can be distributed directly) | High |
| Cash held at the Cayman/Hong Kong level | ~$10B | Extremely low (already at the listed-company level) | High |

**Historical reference**: Alibaba launched a $25B buyback in 2024, proving that returning cash to shareholders is feasible even under a VIE structure — but it requires both political will and regulatory approval. **PDD's record of zero buybacks to date suggests this is not a matter of inability, but of unwillingness.**

### 4.5 Correctly pricing VIE risk

The market's pricing of VIE risk typically shows up as a **Chinese-ADR discount** (relative to comparable Hong Kong-listed/A-share peers):

- Historical Chinese-ADR discount: 15-30%
- Current PDD PE of 10.5x vs. 20-25x for comparable Hong Kong-listed e-commerce peers -> a discount of roughly 50%
- This implies the market is applying a discount to PDD that **far exceeds** pure VIE risk, and also embeds concerns about slowing growth, Temu's uncertain outlook, and capital-allocation doubts

> **Conclusion**: VIE risk is real, but the current share price may already over-discount that risk. The real question is: even if VIE risk were eliminated (e.g., via a Hong Kong listing), would management be willing to give the money back to shareholders?

## V. Temu's Global Expansion Risk (Key Section)

### 5.1 A fundamental shift in Temu's business model

**Original model (2022-2024)**:
```
Chinese factories -> direct shipping -> global consumers
Dependent on: the U.S. de minimis exemption (duty-free for shipments under $800)
```

**Current model (2025-2026, forced transition)**:
```
Chinese factories -> overseas local warehouses -> local delivery -> consumers
Cost structure: substantially higher inventory costs, warehousing costs, and local operating costs
```

**The cost of this transition**:
- Capital spending is shifting from asset-light to asset-heavy
- Requires building out warehousing networks across many countries (billions of dollars of investment)
- Inventory risk has gone from zero to extremely high (unsold goods cannot be returned)
- Operational complexity has risen exponentially

### 5.2 Detailed review of global regulatory risk

| Region | Risk event | Current status | Potential impact |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **United States** | De minimis exemption eliminated; 145% tariff on Chinese goods | Already in effect | U.S. market costs have surged sharply, driving the shift to local warehousing |
| **European Union** | Preliminary DSA violation finding (product safety / supply-chain transparency) | Under investigation | Fines of up to 6% of global revenue (~$3.7B) |
| **European Union** | Elimination of the EUR 150 duty-free threshold effective July 2026 | Confirmed to take effect | The European model is coming under across-the-board pressure |
| **Turkey** | Antitrust regulator raided the Istanbul office | Under investigation | Potential exit or large fines |
| **Multi-country coalition** | 21 U.S. states jointly investigating data security | Ongoing | Risk of GDPR-scale fines |
| **UK / Australia** | Following the EU's regulatory framework | Watching and waiting | Further tightening likely |

### 5.3 Assessment of Temu's path to profitability

### 5.4 Temu's impact on PDD's overall valuation

**Conclusion**: the market is currently valuing Temu at a **negative** number (-$80B to -$130B), meaning Temu is being treated as a "toxic asset" rather than a value-creating one. If Temu proves it can be profitable, there is enormous room for a valuation re-rating.
