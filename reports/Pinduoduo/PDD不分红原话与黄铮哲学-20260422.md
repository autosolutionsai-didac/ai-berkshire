# Deep Dive: PDD Management's Own Words on Not Paying Dividends × Huang Zheng's Philosophy

> **Research date**: 2026-04-22
> **Methodology**: 3-person team (archaeology of management's own statements / deconstruction of Huang Zheng's philosophy / philosophy-to-behavior mapping)
> **Core questions**:
> 1. What exactly did PDD management say between 2022 and 2026 about not buying back stock and not paying dividends?
> 2. From the perspective of Huang Zheng's philosophy, why does this pattern occur?

---

## 0. One-line core conclusion

> **The evolution of PDD management's "no buyback, no dividend" statements runs as follows: from Chen Lei's hardline stance in Q2 2024 — "not appropriate + no need for several years" — to Jiazhen Zhao's counterattack in Q3 2024 that raising a "red flag" is "absurd," to the philosophically packaged framing in Q4 2025 — "faced with short-term financials versus long-term ecosystem value, the company will resolutely choose the latter." This evolution fits perfectly with Huang Zheng's "mechanism-design idealism": he does not believe "returning capital to shareholders" is the company's core mission; he believes in "using mechanisms to make money compound more efficiently inside the enterprise, then flow back to society in reverse." But this sits in tension with Duan Yongping's concept of benfen (doing right by one's duty), which includes strong approval of Apple's buybacks and dividends.**

# Part One: Archaeology of Management's Own Statements (2022-2026)

## I. Key statements along the timeline

### 1.1 2024 Q2 earnings call (2024-08-27) — **The historic "not appropriate" declaration**

**Background**: At the time of the earnings release, PDD's cash balance exceeded RMB 284.9 billion, with operating cash flow of RMB 43.793 billion.

**Chen Lei (Co-Chairman and CEO), in his own words**:

> "Right now is **not the appropriate time for share buybacks or dividends**. And **for the foreseeable next few years, we also don't see this need**."

> "We have said many times that the profit growth of the past few quarters should not be taken as long-term guidance — it's the result of a mismatch between the timing of business investment and financial reporting cycles."

> "Facing a complex environment, we will focus on our core strengths and continuously improve our supply chain capabilities."

**Jiazhen Zhao (Co-CEO), in her own words**:

> "High revenue growth **is not sustainable**."

**Market reaction**: The stock fell **-28.51%** in a single day, wiping out **$55 billion** in market value.

**Key rhetoric**:
- "Not appropriate" = not a matter of timing, but "shouldn't in principle"
- "The foreseeable next few years" = at least 3 years
- "Don't see this need" = not merely a strategy, but a conviction

---

### 1.2 2024 Q3 earnings call — "Long-term value > short-term results"

**Chen Lei**:

> "**As always, we prioritize long-term value over short-term results**."

> "Against a backdrop of intense industry competition, a complex global environment, and our continued investment in the ecosystem, our quarterly profitability will fluctuate and is inherently difficult to predict."

**Jiazhen Zhao**:

> "This quarter's profit **should not be taken as guidance for the quarters ahead**; performance in coming quarters may well continue to swing back and forth."

**Key shift**: From the Q2 hardline "not appropriate" stance to a philosophically packaged "long-term value" framing — the beginning of a long-term narrative structure.

---

### 1.3 2024 Fortune interview — Jiazhen Zhao: "a red flag is absurd"

**Background**: Wall Street widely questioned whether "$6 billion in cash not being returned = a red flag."

**Jiazhen Zhao, in her own words**:

> "Every company makes decisions based on its own unique circumstances and strategic considerations. **To suggest there's a 'red flag' simply because a company doesn't follow what other companies do is absurd**."

**Key rhetoric**:
- "Absurd" = an extremely strong emotional counterattack
- "Unique circumstances" = rejects peer comparability
- Reframes "questioning PDD" as "failing to understand PDD"

---

### 1.4 2025 Q4 earnings call (2026-03-26) — **"The new PDD" + philosophical packaging**

**Jiazhen Zhao**:

> "Over the past three years, Temu's business has expanded to more than 90 countries worldwide — **in 3 years it has covered ground that took Pinduoduo's domestic e-commerce business 10 years**."

> "**Faced with short-term financial performance versus the platform ecosystem's long-term value, the company will resolutely choose the latter**, so margins will continue to fluctuate and swing between quarters, and **this will be the new normal**."

**Chen Lei**:

> "We believe corporate governance and internal talent development have not kept pace with the growth of the business, and in many areas we are stretched thin. Combined with the fast-changing international environment right now, we believe there is a need for a **systemic overhaul of organizational culture and corporate governance**."

**Major shifts**:
- From "not appropriate" to "resolutely choosing the latter" — a firmer stance
- From "no need for a few years" to "this will be the new normal" — institutionalizing the non-return of capital
- The introduction of "overhauling organizational culture and corporate governance" — in a sense, a "philosophical response" to all the skepticism

---

### 1.5 Duan Yongping's defense (2026-03-29, Xueqiu)

> "**Why on earth would you worry about this**, or rather, why should they worry about short-term traders like you? It's already been said as clearly as possible on the conference call — read it carefully, and whether you understand or not is on you."

**Key observation**: Duan Yongping's own core logic for holding Apple includes the fact that "share count has fallen by roughly 40% over the past 10 years" (he places great weight on buybacks and cancellation), yet toward PDD's non-payment of dividends he responds with sarcasm rather than data — a rare "non-data-driven" defense from Duan Yongping.

## II. Rhetorical analysis of the evolving statements

### 2.1 Keyword frequency (Q2 2024 → Q4 2025)

| Keyword | Q2 2024 | Q3 2024 | Q4 2025 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Not appropriate" | High | Weakened | Gone | ↓ |
| "No need" | High | Weakened | Gone | ↓ |
| "Long-term value" | Weak | **High** | **High** | ↑ |
| "Ecosystem" | Weak | Medium | **Very high** | ↑ |
| "Resolutely" | None | None | **Appears** | ↑ |
| "New normal" | None | None | **Appears** | ↑ |
| "Overhaul" | None | None | **Appears** | ↑ |

**Evolutionary logic**: From "hard refusal" → "philosophical defense" → "institutionalized new normal" — over two years, management has completed the maturation and packaging of the "no capital return" narrative.

### 2.2 Has the stance ever wavered?

**Answer: never**.

- Q2 2024, original stance: "not appropriate + no need for a few years"
- Q3 2024, packaging upgraded: "long-term value > short-term results"
- 2024 media counterattack: skepticism is "absurd"
- Q4 2025, institutionalized: "this will be the new normal"

**No signal whatsoever** has indicated: "this may change in 3-5 years" or "a buyback will start once a certain milestone is reached."

### 2.3 Response when asked about peers' buybacks

PDD management has never, in public:
- Explained why Alibaba can execute a $30 billion buyback under a VIE structure while PDD cannot
- Addressed the technical VIE question of whether cash sits onshore or offshore
- Given clear guidance on "under what conditions a buyback would be launched"

**This silence itself is a key signal.**

# Part Two: The 8 Key Words of Huang Zheng's Philosophy

## I. 8 core keywords (each with an original quote)

### 1. **Differently**

> "Our generation will ultimately be pushed by the rushing tide of this era toward a new era that belongs to us — a different one." — 2019 Letter to Shareholders

> "In China's competitive market environment, every table, everywhere, is already full." — *Caijing* interview

**Core logic**: All the old rules can be overturned.

### 2. **Benfen ("doing right by one's duty")** — Huang Zheng's 5-layer definition (2018 Letter to Shareholders)

> 1. Be honest, and become a person worthy of trust
> 2. Do your own job well, regardless of what others are doing
> 3. Shut out outside noise, return to first principles, and focus on doing what you ought to do
> 4. **Do not take advantage of others, even when you can**
> 5. When problems arise, **look to yourself first**

**Key extension**: Compared with Duan Yongping's benfen (personal ethics), Huang Zheng extends benfen to the organizational and societal level.

### 3. **Misalignment**

> "We compete with Taobao through misalignment."
> "What they're fighting over is territory; what I want is misalignment."

**Core logic**: Don't compete within existing rules — define new rules.

### 4. **Slow**

> "Choosing the right path matters more than working hard... a 20% annual return sustained for 20 straight years is remarkable, and delivers far greater returns than a 100% gain this year followed by a 50% loss next year."

> "Right now we don't want growth — I want to bring GMV growth down."

**Core logic**: Reject growth for growth's sake; compounding matters more than short-term results.

### 5. **Long-term**

> "Looking ten years into the future, the level of service we provide today may still be at its crudest stage." — 2018 Letter to Shareholders

> "If you've thought through your long-term social meaning clearly, then these things become routine, and business competition itself stops mattering so much."

**Core logic**: A time horizon starting at 10 years.

### 6. **Simple**

> "I like reading his (Buffett's) letters to shareholders — decades of repeating the same simplicity."

> "A score of 60 out of 100 being good enough — 'good enough is great' — is a good philosophy."

**Core logic**: Common sense + rationality + an even keel.

### 7. **For the People**

> "We hope Pinduoduo can be a **public institution** that exists to create value for the broadest base of users. **It should not be a tool for showcasing individual ability, nor should it carry too much personal color**." — 2018 Letter to Shareholders

> "The biggest characteristic of new e-commerce is 'inclusiveness'... its historical mission is to serve the broadest mass of ordinary people." — 2019 Letter to Shareholders

**Core logic**: Consumers > merchants > society > employees > **shareholders (last)**

### 8. **No Free Ride**

> "**Do not take advantage of others, even when you can**."

> "Any attempt to exploit someone's misfortune or take advantage of a loophole for one's own benefit looks extremely foolish in the face of time."

**Core logic**: Do not profit from any informational, positional, or opportunistic advantage.

## II. Huang Zheng's "view of capital"

### Key original quotes

> "**Money is a tool, not an end in itself**." — *My Middle School and College Years*

> "Google showed me the side effects of getting rich overnight... because once people suddenly had so much money, many of them lost the motivation to work."

> "He (Buffett) clearly understands that money is not the end goal. On one hand he enjoys the pleasure the game of capital brings him; on the other hand, he **wisely gave the vast majority of his money to Bill Gates, who is younger than him**, trusting Gates to carry out the redistribution of wealth that wealth deserves." — *Turning "Capitalism" Upside Down*

> "Could there be mechanisms that let the poor also 'sell insurance' to the rich... so as to achieve more fine-grained feedback, **a shorter-cycle flow of money circulating back from the rich to the poor**?" — *Turning "Capitalism" Upside Down*

### Actions on record

- 2018 IPO prospectus: pledged to donate 2.3% of shares to set up a charitable foundation
- July 2020: actually donated 2.37% (about 113 million shares), plus allocated another 7.74% to the partnership collectively
- March 2021, resignation: voluntarily gave up **10-to-1 super-voting rights**
- March 2024: stake fell from 50.7% at IPO to **about 26.5%**
- Total shares given up: about **24%** (based on PDD's market cap of $141 billion as of April 2026, roughly **$34 billion**)

### Comparison with Buffett and Duan Yongping

| Dimension | Buffett | Duan Yongping | **Huang Zheng** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position on money | A game of compounding capital | A tool for giving family a good life | **"A tool, not an end in itself"** |
| Disposition of wealth | 99% donated to the Gates Foundation | Angel investing + family foundation | **Gave up 24% of his stake before age 42; now focused on life sciences** |
| Core view of capital | Insurance + compounding (positive accumulation) | Buying stock = buying a business | **"Reverse insurance" — letting the poor sell certainty to the rich** |
| Wealth redistribution | Advocates higher taxes on the rich | No public stance | **Mechanism design lets wealth flow back naturally** |

**Key difference**:
- Buffett: efficient accumulation → concentrated donation (two linear stages)
- Huang Zheng: through mechanism design → wealth flows back naturally in circulation (one integrated loop)

# Part Three: Philosophy-to-Behavior Mapping (Why no dividends?)

## I. Precise mapping across 8 angles

### Angle 1: Huang Zheng's "compounding" philosophy vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "Choosing the right path matters more than working hard... a 20% annual return sustained for 20 straight years is remarkable"

**Mapping**:
- Huang Zheng believes money can generate a higher ROE inside the company (PDD's ROE > 30%)
- Returning capital to shareholders = letting shareholders reinvest after-tax proceeds into other lower-ROE assets
- **Logically self-consistent** — but only if PDD can sustain a high ROE
- **Counter-example**: Berkshire Hathaway also paid no dividends in its early years, but Buffett at least made large-scale acquisitions and capital reallocation; the bulk of PDD's $60.4 billion in cash just sits on the balance sheet

### Angle 2: Huang Zheng's "social experiment" mindset vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "The company should not be a tool for showcasing individual ability, nor should it carry too much personal color"
> "If you've thought through your long-term social meaning clearly, then these things become routine, and business competition itself stops mattering so much"

**Mapping**:
- If PDD is not a purely commercial company but a "social experiment," shareholder returns will **necessarily** rank below "the experiment itself" in priority
- In 2021 Huang Zheng donated half his stake into a life sciences fund — in effect, he has already "exited" the role of shareholder himself
- **Key evidence**: Huang Zheng has turned himself into someone who is "neither CEO, nor majority shareholder, nor experimenter" — he has no incentive to actively push for shareholder returns

### Angle 3: Huang Zheng's "long-termism" vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "Looking ten years into the future, the level of service we provide today may still be at its crudest stage"

**Mapping**:
- Long-termism rationalizes "not returning capital in the short term"
- **Counter-example**: A true long-termist (Buffett) has, over the past decade, bought back a large amount of Berkshire's own stock (although he opposed buybacks early on)
- Apple represents a perfect combination of Buffett's long-termism and shareholder returns

**Key tension**: Long-termism does not necessarily equal "no capital return" — Huang Zheng's binding of the two may be a **philosophical blind spot**.

### Angle 4: Huang Zheng's "misalignment" strategy vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "What they're fighting over is territory; what I want is misalignment"

**Mapping**:
- Alibaba, Tencent, and JD.com all buy back stock → PDD, in "misaligned" fashion, does not
- Treating "not playing by Wall Street's rules" as a source of differentiated advantage
- **Logical trap**: misalignment is a business strategy — it should not be extended to capital allocation

### Angle 5: Huang Zheng's "differently" philosophy vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "Our generation... a different new era"

**Mapping**:
- Apple and Microsoft both buy back stock → "we want to be different"
- But "different" should point toward "doing the right thing," not "being different for the sake of being different"
- **Key question**: PDD's "differentness" may, on some dimensions, be sliding into "rebellion for rebellion's sake"

### Angle 6: Huang Zheng's "benfen" vs. Duan Yongping's "benfen"

**Duan Yongping's benfen**: includes strong endorsement of Apple's buybacks and dividends
**Huang Zheng's benfen**: all 5 points are about product/users/merchants/self-accountability, **with no mention of shareholders whatsoever**

**Mapping**:
- Huang Zheng's "benfen" framework, from the outset of its design, **left out the shareholder dimension**
- This is not malicious — it's a philosophical blind spot
- Huang Zheng has taken "benfen toward the consumer" to the extreme (inclusiveness + low prices), but neglected "benfen toward the shareholder"

**Key evidence**:
- The prospectus's letter to shareholders explicitly ranks stakeholders as "consumers > merchants > society > employees > shareholders"
- This is the reverse of traditional Wall Street "shareholders first"
- It's close to Amazon's "customer obsession," but Amazon eventually did carry out large-scale buybacks anyway

### Angle 7: Huang Zheng's "no free ride" vs. no dividends

**Huang Zheng, in his own words**:
> "Do not take advantage of others, even when you can"

**Reverse reading**:
- Is keeping $60.4 billion in cash inside the company without returning it, itself a form of "taking advantage" of shareholders?
- Shareholders invest in PDD stock expecting a return; the company's profits not being returned effectively amounts to using shareholders' capital without compensation
- **A key irony**: Huang Zheng's own "no free ride" principle may apply in reverse to the question of shareholder returns

**Huang Zheng's possible defense**:
- "No free ride" refers to "not exploiting an informational, positional, or opportunistic advantage"
- Not returning cash is not "taking advantage" — it's "deferring a decision"
- This line of defense is relatively weak

### Angle 8: Huang Zheng's "retired but not really retired," "no responsibility" status vs. no dividends

**Facts**:
- Resigned as CEO in 2020
- Resigned as chairman and gave up super-voting rights in 2021
- Still holds a 26.5% stake and remains the largest shareholder
- Class B voting rights were given up, but actual influence remains

**Mapping**:
- Huang Zheng occupies a strange position of being "neither the one bearing decision-making responsibility, nor giving up being the ultimate beneficiary"
- Chen Lei and Jiazhen Zhao are not PDD's true "owners" — they each hold less than 1% of the stock
- **No one has any incentive to push for shareholder returns**: Huang Zheng has already given away his wealth and doesn't need more; Chen Lei and Jiazhen Zhao hold too little stock
- This **"incentive vacuum"** is a structural problem

## II. 3 core contradictions

### Contradiction 1: "Long-termism" in philosophy vs. inefficient capital allocation

- Buffett's long-termism includes "buying back and retiring stock"
- Why doesn't Huang Zheng's long-termism include this?
- The truth may be: Huang Zheng's long-termism leans more toward "product + users," not "capital"

### Contradiction 2: "Benfen" in philosophy vs. shareholders also being a stakeholder

- The prospectus explicitly states "benfen = not taking advantage of others"
- Is not returning $60.4 billion in cash itself a form of taking advantage of shareholders?
- Huang Zheng's "benfen" framework may carry a **structural blind spot**

### Contradiction 3: "Differently" in philosophy vs. "different" not meaning "irresponsible"

- Apple's buybacks are also quite "different" (in scale)
- Why is PDD's "differentness" expressed as "not doing it at all"?
- "Different" should mean differentiation that creates value, not an excuse to dodge responsibility

## III. 5 root causes (ranked by likelihood)

### Cause 1: **Huang Zheng genuinely believes "money kept in the company can do better things"** (probability 30%)

Evidence:
- Huang Zheng donated half his wealth into a life sciences fund; he believes "money can accomplish big things"
- The "hundreds of billions [RMB] investment" 3-year plan embodies this conviction
- **Logical gap**: yet the bulk of the $60.4 billion in cash just sits on the balance sheet, not actually deployed toward big things

### Cause 2: **Huang Zheng has a deep aversion to "Wall Street short-termism"** (probability 25%)

Evidence:
- Huang Zheng has repeatedly criticized being "driven by stock price fluctuations"
- The prospectus explicitly states the "team should not be unsettled by stock price swings"
- **Possible motive**: treating "no buybacks" as a kind of stance against Wall Street

### Cause 3: **As the largest shareholder, Huang Zheng no longer needs a cash return** (probability 20%)

Evidence:
- Has already given away 24% of his stake
- He himself is focused on life sciences research and doesn't need cash
- Chen Lei and Jiazhen Zhao hold less than 1% each, with no incentive to push for buybacks
- **A structural incentive vacuum**

### Cause 4: **Deep concern over Chinese regulation / geopolitics** (probability 15%)

Evidence:
- Cash = survival insurance (against Temu risk + regulation + fines)
- The precedent of Ant Group's IPO being halted
- The fragility of the VIE structure
- The RMB 1.522 billion fine in April 2026 proves the risk is real

### Cause 5: **Huang Zheng treats PDD as a "spiritual practice"** (probability 10%)

Evidence:
- Huang Zheng's philosophy of stepping back
- The positioning of the company as a "public institution"
- Commercial success is merely a byproduct
- **If true**, shareholder returns will never become a priority

## IV. Blind spots in Huang Zheng's philosophy

### Blind spot 1: Concrete arrangements for shareholders as a stakeholder

- Among the 8 core keywords, **"shareholders" is entirely absent**
- The prospectus ranks shareholders last
- No writing has ever discussed "when capital should be returned to shareholders"

### Blind spot 2: A concrete philosophy of returning capital

- Huang Zheng has written extensively about "what money is," but never about "when to give money back to those who provided it"
- Buffett has a complete philosophy of "buyback trigger conditions" (when the stock price is below intrinsic value)
- Huang Zheng has none

### Blind spot 3: Commitment to information transparency

- Huang Zheng emphasizes that "benfen" includes "honesty"
- Yet PDD has never separately disclosed Temu's financials, which sits in tension with "honesty"
- The information asymmetry itself may violate the "no free ride" principle

# Part Four: Overall Assessment

## I. The true reason PDD does not pay dividends (overall assessment)

**The most likely truth is a combination of factors**:

1. **Huang Zheng's philosophical blind spot** (30%): his benfen framework simply leaves out the shareholder dimension
2. **Incentive vacuum** (25%): Huang Zheng has already given away his wealth, and management holds too small a stake — no one genuinely pushes for buybacks
3. **Strategic reserve needs** (20%): Temu, regulation, and fines really do require ammunition
4. **VIE technical constraints** (15%): most of the cash sits onshore, and repatriation is costly
5. **Regulatory fear** (10%): a philosophy of keeping wealth hidden

## II. The essential difference from Duan Yongping's benfen

**Duan Yongping's benfen**: includes shareholder returns (the core logic behind his Apple position)
**Huang Zheng's benfen**: does not include shareholder returns (entirely absent from the public text)

This is not Huang Zheng "violating" benfen — it's that **Huang Zheng's version of benfen never had a shareholder dimension to begin with**.

**Duan Yongping's compromise**:
- Duan Yongping's own benfen framework includes shareholder returns
- But for PDD, he has "let go of this standard"
- Because he believes Huang Zheng's character matters more than institutionalized shareholder-friendliness

## III. Predicted evolution over the next 1-3 years

### Scenario A: Philosophy solidifies (probability 55%)
- Huang Zheng's philosophy stays unchanged + management's incentives stay unchanged = no-dividend becomes the permanent normal
- Cash continues accumulating past $70 billion
- Valuation stays permanently discounted

### Scenario B: Passive buyback (probability 30%)
- Regulatory fines and Temu losses eat into cash → cash pressure eases
- But the business is also under pressure at the same time → still no dividend
- Net effect: the "cash black hole" problem naturally stops on its own

### Scenario C: Philosophy softens (probability 15%)
- Continued buybacks by Alibaba, Tencent, and JD.com prove VIE structures are workable
- Sustained investor pressure
- Huang Zheng is persuaded, or management changes → a buyback is launched
- Stock rises +20-30% in the short term

## IV. Practical recommendations for investors

### 1. Valuation approach
- Apply a **50-70% discount** to the $60.4 billion cash pile in the valuation
- Do not assume "a dividend will come eventually"

### 2. Key signals to watch
- 🟢 Huang Zheng publicly speaking about shareholder returns (extremely low probability)
- 🟢 Any genuine buyback authorization announcement (>$5 billion)
- 🔴 Earnings continuing to show $10 billion+ net cash increases with no capital-return action

### 3. Lessons from Duan Yongping's perspective
- Don't treat Duan Yongping's large position as a signal that "PDD will pay dividends"
- What he's betting on is Huang Zheng's character, not capital allocation discipline
- When investing in PDD yourself, form your own independent judgment

---

## V. Final characterization

**PDD's "no dividend" policy is not a strategy — it's a philosophy.**

Huang Zheng's philosophical system is a complete, internally consistent system — **it's just that this system, from the moment of its design, never had a place for "shareholder returns."** This is not malice — it's a philosophical blind spot. He cares more about the cycle of "consumers + merchants + society," treating shareholders as a passive role that "provides initial capital and then waits for long-term value."

Chen Lei's and Jiazhen Zhao's public statements — "not appropriate," "long-term value," "resolutely choosing the ecosystem" — are all executions of this philosophy of Huang Zheng's. It's not that they don't know Alibaba can execute a $30 billion buyback; it's that they believe "PDD should not follow that rule."

**For investors**: investing in PDD is, in essence, **a bet on the correctness of Huang Zheng's philosophy** —
- If Huang Zheng is right, PDD's "mechanism design" will eventually return the $60.4 billion in cash to a "broader set of shareholders" (human society) through another channel (scientific research + social redistribution)
- If Huang Zheng is wrong, then it is exactly the "governance problem" Wall Street has been questioning, and the valuation stays permanently discounted

**Duan Yongping's real bet**: not a bet on PDD's commercial success, but a bet that **Huang Zheng's philosophy will ultimately be understood by the market**. This is a very long-term, very philosophical wager.
