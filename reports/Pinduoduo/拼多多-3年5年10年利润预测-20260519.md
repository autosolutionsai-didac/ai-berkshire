# PDD Holdings (PDD) 3-Year/5-Year/10-Year Profit Forecast

**Research date**: May 19, 2026
**Data as of**: full-year FY2025 (released March 25, 2026)
**Core question**: What will PDD's annual net profit be in 2028, 2030, and 2035?
**Method**: bottom-up business segmentation + driver-variable derivation + three-scenario probability weighting
**Confidence**: 3-year moderately high / 5-year moderate / 10-year low (Temu's political variables dominate the long-term variance)

---

## One-Sentence Conclusion

> **Probability-weighted forecast — net profit of approximately RMB 130 billion in 2028, approximately RMB 155 billion in 2030, and approximately RMB 210 billion in 2035. Three years out, PDD will very likely earn more than it does now (the end of the "100-billion-yuan support program" plus narrowing Temu losses), but the increase will be limited. Ten years out, the variance is enormous (RMB 85 billion to RMB 320 billion) — almost entirely driven by Temu's political fate and how much of its profit potential actually materializes.**

## II. Segment Breakdown: Where Does the Profit Come From?

PDD does not disclose P&L by business line separately. The breakdown below is based on estimates from investment-bank research (Bernstein, HSBC, Kaiyuan Securities) and public data, with an **error band of ±15-20%**.

### 2.1 Overview of the Three Core Segments

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue (est.) | Share | Operating Profit (est.) | Margin (est.) |
|------|----------------|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Domestic marketplace** (advertising + commissions) | ~RMB 275.0 billion | 64% | ~RMB 142.0 billion | ~52% |
| **Temu** (global) | ~RMB 130.0 billion | 30% | ~-RMB 22.0 billion | ~-17% |
| **Duoduo Grocery** | ~RMB 27.0 billion | 6% | ~-RMB 1.0 billion | near breakeven |
| **Consolidated** | RMB 431.8 billion | 100% | RMB 94.6 billion | 21.9% |

*Note: Operating profit includes allocated expenses (SBC of RMB 7.9 billion, etc., not broken out separately above but embedded within each segment). Different brokerages allocate expenses differently, producing wide variation in estimated domestic-marketplace margins (anywhere from 35% to 65%). Temu's FY2025 loss, per Xueqiu/investment-bank breakdowns, is approximately RMB 22.0-24.0 billion (this report uses RMB 22.0 billion) — narrower than the roughly RMB 25.3 billion loss in FY2024, though the narrowing fell short of previously optimistic expectations.*

**Interpretation**: The consolidated operating margin of 21.9% is the result of the marketplace's high ~52% margin being dragged down by Temu's ~-17% loss margin. Understanding this "profit-blending effect" is the key to forecasting future profit.

### 2.2 Domestic Marketplace: A Mature Cash Machine

**Core metrics:**
- FY2025 GMV: approximately RMB 5.0 trillion (market share of roughly 19-22%, second only to Taobao/Tmall and close to Douyin e-commerce)
- Blended take rate: approximately 4.8-5.1% (advertising ~4.0% + commissions ~1.0%, on a domestic-marketplace-only basis; on a group-wide basis including Temu, roughly 5.5%)
- Active merchants: over 14 million (already surpassed 14.2 million in 2024)
- Annual active buyers: approximately 900 million (nearing the ceiling)

**Profit driver formula:**
> Marketplace revenue = Domestic GMV × Blended take rate
> Marketplace profit = Marketplace revenue × Operating margin - Allocated expenses

### 2.3 Temu: The Biggest Variable

**Current status (FY2025):**
- GMV: approximately $92.5 billion (+30% YoY, missing the $100 billion target)
- Global MAU: 416 million
- **Europe has overtaken the U.S. as the largest market** (GMV share >40%)
- Semi-managed's share of GMV rose from <10% to 34%, with an AOV of $55 vs. $32 for fully managed
- FY2025 operating loss narrowed to approximately -RMB 22.0 billion (vs. approximately -RMB 25.3 billion in FY2024 and -RMB 58.0 billion in FY2023), though the narrowing fell short of the market's earlier optimistic expectations

**Major shock — elimination of the U.S. de minimis exemption (May 2025):**
- Previously, packages under $800 entered the U.S. duty-free — a core pillar of Temu's low-price model
- After the exemption was removed, Temu's U.S. DAU plunged 52-58% (Reuters reported 52%, Sensor Tower reported 58%; sources diverge)
- The U.S. currently imposes a 145% tariff on Chinese goods
- Temu's response: accelerating local-warehouse buildout, sharply raising the U.S. semi-managed share (targeting 80%), targeting 80% local-warehouse fulfillment in Europe, and compressing delivery times to three days

**The "New Pinmu" strategy (announced March 2026):**
- A new company was established in Shanghai, with a first-phase capital injection of RMB 15.0 billion
- Total planned three-year investment of RMB 100.0 billion
- Goal: to "strive to build another Pinduoduo within three years"
- Focused on upgrading China's supply chain and overseas localization

### 2.4 Interest and Investment Income: An Overlooked but Precarious "Third Engine"

| Year | Cash + Short-term Investments | Interest & Investment Income | Implied Yield |
|------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| FY2024 | RMB 331.6 billion | RMB 20.6 billion | 6.2% |
| FY2025 | RMB 422.3 billion | RMB 25.6 billion | 6.1% |

**Breakdown of income composition (FY2024, based on analysis of 20-F footnotes):**

| Source | Amount | Nature |
|------|------|------|
| Time deposits + short-term bond interest | ~RMB 7.3 billion | Stable, predictable |
| **Equity investment gains** | **~RMB 8.7 billion** (cost basis RMB 35.7 billion, return of ~24.5%) | **Highly volatile, pro-cyclical** |
| Other (structured deposits, etc.) | ~RMB 4.6 billion | Moderate stability |
| **Total** | **~RMB 20.6 billion** | Mixed |

**Key warning**: The 6.1% "yield" is illusory. About RMB 8.7 billion of it came from equity investments (presumed to be U.S. stocks), benefiting from the 2024 U.S. equity rally. Stripping out the equity gains, the true risk-free yield on deposits and bonds is roughly 3-4%. In a bear market, the equity portion could swing to a loss, causing investment income to plunge from RMB 25.6 billion to RMB 10.0-15.0 billion or even lower.

PDD generates roughly RMB 70.0-100.0 billion in net cash inflow each year (net profit minus capex minus investments), and its cash pool keeps swelling. The forecast uses a **normalized yield of 4-5%** (not 6%+) to reflect the volatility of the equity-investment portion.

**But on the other hand**: if management never pays dividends or buys back shares, the true value of this RMB 400+ billion cash pile to shareholders is questionable — it merely inflates book profit without necessarily converting into shareholder returns.

## III. Analysis of Key Driver Variables

Forecasting PDD's future profit essentially comes down to the following six variables:

### Variable 1: Domestic E-commerce Market Growth Rate

| Data point | Value | Source |
|--------|------|------|
| 2025 growth in physical-goods online retail sales | +5.2% | National Bureau of Statistics (published February 2026) |
| 2024 | +6.5% | National Bureau of Statistics |
| Online penetration rate (2025) | ~26.1% (physical goods / total retail sales basis) | National Bureau of Statistics |
| **Forecast CAGR (2025-2035)** | **4-6%** | Penetration still has room to rise from 26% to 35%+, but growth is decelerating year by year |

**Assessment**: China's e-commerce market has entered a mature-growth phase, but online penetration stands at just 26% (lower than the 30%+ some institutions estimated previously), leaving structural room for further gains. Over the next 10 years, CAGR will most likely fall in the 4-6% range.

### Variable 2: PDD's Domestic Market Share

Current landscape (Goldman Sachs' Q4 2025 forecast): Taobao/Tmall 31% > Douyin 24% > **PDD 19%** > JD.com 16%

**The biggest threat is Douyin e-commerce** — it has covered in five years what took Alibaba twenty; its GMV reached approximately RMB 3.5 trillion in 2024 and surpassed RMB 4.3 trillion in 2025, growing at 30%+.

**Assessment**: PDD's domestic share will most likely slip modestly from 19% to 16-18% (squeezed by Douyin), but is unlikely to collapse — its no-name, low-price mindshare is a differentiating moat, and brand-upgrading efforts are also underway.

### Variable 3: Take Rate (Monetization Rate)

| Year | Marketplace blended take rate | Trend | Basis note |
|------|-----------------|------|---------|
| 2020 | ~3.1% | Rising rapidly | Domestic-marketplace basis |
| 2022 | ~4.2% | Rising | Domestic-marketplace basis |
| 2024 | ~4.7-5.1% | Approaching ceiling | Domestic-marketplace basis (estimates vary by brokerage) |
| 2025E | ~4.8-5.1% | Suppressed by the "100-billion-yuan support program" | Domestic-marketplace basis |

*Note: An earlier version cited 5.5-5.6%, which was on a group-wide basis (including Temu); the domestic-marketplace-only basis is roughly 0.5-0.8pp lower.*

**Assessment**: PDD's take rate has already **surpassed** Taobao/Tmall's (Taobao/Tmall's blended rate is roughly 3.5-4.5%, below PDD's rather than on par with it), meaning PDD's take-rate ceiling should be benchmarked against Douyin (8-10%, including livestream commissions) rather than Taobao/Tmall. That said, given that PDD is centered on low-price, no-name goods with merchants of limited fee tolerance, future upside is roughly +0.3-0.5pp. The "100-billion-yuan support program's" fee-cut policy is a near-term headwind in the opposite direction.

### Variable 4: Temu's Political and Commercial Fate (the Biggest Uncertainty)

| Scenario | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Temu effectively banned/exits the U.S. | 20% | UFLPA entity list, legislative ban |
| Temu's U.S. business shrinks sharply + EU tariff hikes | 30% | Tariffs persist; EU eliminates the duty-free threshold in July 2026 |
| Temu stabilizes globally after adjustment | 35% | Local-warehouse + semi-managed model proves out, with Europe as the mainstay |
| Temu becomes a top-3 global e-commerce player | 15% | Ad monetization unlocks profit, breakthroughs across multiple markets |

**Assessment**: Temu's future is roughly 60% determined by political variables and 40% by commercial execution. The current tariff environment is extremely unfavorable for Temu, but the shift to semi-managed fulfillment and its European expansion are positive signals.

### Variable 5: Operating Margin Trend

The margin plunged from 28.5% to 23.0% in 2025, for the following reasons:
1. **About 60% deliberate**: merchant fee waivers (the "100-billion-yuan support program," launched April 2025, a three-year plan spanning 2025-2027 with total investment of RMB 100.0 billion), increased R&D spending (+30%), and preparation for the "New Pinmu" initiative
2. **About 40% forced**: higher costs from Temu's shift from air to sea freight, extra tariff expenses, and defensive spending against Douyin competition

**Assessment**: Margins will stay under pressure in the near term (2026-2027), recover in the medium term (the "100-billion-yuan support program" expires in 2027 + Temu's losses narrow), and in the long run depend on the business mix — the domestic marketplace's margin is stable, and whether Temu can turn a positive profit is the key. Note, however, that in April 2026 management already indicated it would "continue heavy investment in the supply chain in 2026," so spending could persist even after the three-year program ends.

### Variable 6: Cash Accumulation and Shareholder Returns

PDD currently pays no dividends and conducts no buybacks (even after being fined RMB 1.522 billion in April 2026, it still has no buyback plan). If this policy remains unchanged:
- By 2028, the cash pool will swell to ~RMB 600.0-700.0 billion
- By 2035, it could exceed ~RMB 1,400.0 billion
- Interest income will become a significant component of net profit (15-25% of the total)

**But on the other hand**: if large-scale buybacks are launched in the future, the cash pool would shrink → interest income would fall, but per-share earnings would rise and the valuation could be re-rated.

  *Consolidated operating profit = RMB 390.0 billion × 24% = ~RMB 93.6 billion*

- Pre-tax profit: ~RMB 122.6 billion
- Income tax: ~RMB 23.3 billion
- **Net profit: ~RMB 99.0 billion**

**Neutral scenario (50% probability):**

Driving logic: Temu's U.S. GMV shrinks to roughly 40% of its prior level but does not exit (via the local-warehouse model), while Europe becomes the largest market and keeps growing; domestic share holds at 18-19%, and the "100-billion-yuan support program" investment gradually pays off

- Consolidated revenue: domestic ~RMB 330.0 billion + Temu ~RMB 160.0 billion + other ~RMB 35.0 billion = ~RMB 525.0 billion (CAGR +7%)
- Operating margin: ~24% (Temu just breaking even/slightly profitable; expenses fall after the "100-billion-yuan support program" expires in 2027)
  *Consolidated operating profit = RMB 525.0 billion × 24% = ~RMB 126.0 billion*
- Interest and investment income: RMB 620.0 billion × 5% = ~RMB 31.0 billion
- Pre-tax profit: ~RMB 157.0 billion
- Income tax: ~RMB 28.3 billion
- **Net profit: ~RMB 129.0 billion**

**Bullish scenario (25% probability):**

Driving logic: U.S. tariffs ease after the 2028 election (trade policy has historically been volatile); Temu's semi-managed + local-warehouse model proves out, with ad monetization contributing incremental profit; domestically, the "New Pinmu" supply-chain upgrade drives quality improvement and an influx of branded merchants

- Consolidated revenue: domestic ~RMB 360.0 billion + Temu ~RMB 220.0 billion + other ~RMB 40.0 billion = ~RMB 620.0 billion (CAGR +13%)
- Operating margin: ~26% (Temu contributes positive profit, helped by ad monetization plus scale effects)
  *Consolidated operating profit = RMB 620.0 billion × 26% = ~RMB 161.2 billion*
- Interest and investment income: RMB 650.0 billion × 5% = ~RMB 32.5 billion
- Pre-tax profit: ~RMB 193.7 billion
- Income tax: ~RMB 32.9 billion
- **Net profit: ~RMB 161.0 billion**

The recovery's main driver is not a revenue boom but rather **expense normalization** (the "100-billion-yuan support program" expiring in 2027) and **Temu's losses narrowing** (moving from -RMB 22.0 billion toward breakeven).

## IX. Key Risks and Catalysts

### 9.1 Upside Risks (which could push profit above the bullish scenario)

1. **Launching large-scale buybacks**: PDD sits on RMB 422.3 billion in cash; if it begins annual buybacks of over $5.0 billion, EPS growth would substantially outpace net profit growth while also triggering a valuation re-rating
2. **Temu's ad monetization exceeds expectations**: referencing PDD's marketplace ad-monetization rate rising from 2.5% to 4.6%, if Temu successfully replicates this trajectory, it could unlock tens of billions of yuan in pure profit
3. **U.S.-China trade détente**: restoration of the de minimis exemption or a substantial tariff cut would warm up Temu's U.S. business
4. **A secondary Hong Kong listing**: broadening the investor base and narrowing the valuation discount

### 9.2 Downside Risks (which could push profit below the bearish scenario)

1. **Temu is banned by U.S. legislation** (a TikTok-like path): 20% probability, which could trigger panic-driven valuation compression
2. **AI agents disrupt the recommendation algorithm**: if users bypass the platform and place orders directly via AI-driven price comparison, PDD's advertising-revenue model would be undercut at its root — **this is the single biggest structural threat on a 10-year horizon**
3. **Escalation of management integrity issues**: the April 2026 "violent resistance to law enforcement" incident is a warning sign; if financial fraud or a governance scandal further surfaces, the valuation could be permanently discounted
4. **VIE structure / Chinese ADR delisting risk**: PDD is one of the few large Chinese ADRs without a secondary Hong Kong listing; in an extreme scenario, this carries a 15% probability
5. **Colin Huang's complete exit**: the controlling shareholder has stayed out of the public eye for five years; if he formally severs all ties, management risk exposure would widen
