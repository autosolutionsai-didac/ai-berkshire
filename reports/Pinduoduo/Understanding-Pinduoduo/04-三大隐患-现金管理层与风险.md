# Three Overhangs — Cash, Management, and Risk

> *Understanding Pinduoduo* series · Part 04 · Overhangs
> Reading time: approx. 8 minutes

---

## The hardest part of Pinduoduo

The previous piece covered the essence of Pinduoduo's business — an algorithmic advertising machine plus Temu as a geopolitical option.

PE 10.5x, ROE 31%, FCF yield 7%. Looking at these numbers alone, Pinduoduo should be a textbook value stock. But the market simply refuses to price it that way.

The reason isn't the business itself — it's three things outside the business: cash that isn't returned to shareholders, a founder who stays out of the public eye, and risks that are hard to see through.

This piece takes them apart one by one.

---

## I. 422.3 billion yuan in cash

### Profit really did decline in 2025

Let's put the numbers on the table first:

| Item (100 million yuan) | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| Total revenue | 393.8 billion | 431.8 billion | +10% |
| Operating profit | 151.9 billion | 117.9 billion | -22% |
| Net profit | 113.5 billion | 99.4 billion | -12% |

Revenue grew 10%, costs grew 30%, and profit got cut by the scissors gap. Where did the money go? Temu promotion, the pivot forced by the tariff war, and defending the domestic core business. All of this is deliberate investment, not the business deteriorating.

ROE dropped from 49% to 31%, which sounds alarming. But that 49% in 2024 was itself abnormal to begin with — like a player who averages 35 points a game one season and drops back to 28 the next; you wouldn't say he's washed up. A 31% ROE is still top-tier across global e-commerce — Amazon's is only 25%, Alibaba's is 12%.

### How to think about this cash pile

422.3 billion yuan in net cash, equal to 42% of market cap. One year of free cash flow is 106.9 billion yuan — at that rate it would take just four years to build up another one.

This is the sticking point for many people — peers are all doing large-scale buybacks and dividends (Alibaba returns $16.5 billion a year, Tencent over HK$110 billion), while Pinduoduo hasn't spent a cent in six years.

But I lean toward not treating this as a problem.

Colin Huang's judgment on where to spend money has been validated again and again. In 2022, when everyone else was hesitating, he committed to Temu; when the tariff war hit in 2024, while others were still in meetings, he had already pivoted to semi-managed logistics and started building local warehouses. Every one of these decisions required burning cash, and every one of them has looked right in hindsight.

So the more sensible way to read this 422.3 billion yuan is: ammunition set aside by an extremely rational person for an uncertain future. Temu is still expanding, the tariff war could escalate at any time, and there might be an acquisition opportunity someday — all of this requires cash.

Of course, this judgment rests on one premise: you have to trust Colin Huang. Those who trust him see a safety cushion; those who don't see a black hole. The market pricing this at PE 10x instead of 15x is precisely because not everyone trusts him.

As for how this evolves going forward — most likely it keeps piling up untouched. If a buyback is ever suddenly announced, regardless of the amount, that would be a major signal, and the stock could jump 20% that day.

---

## II. Colin Huang and Chen Lei

He stepped down as CEO in 2020 and as chairman in 2021, and has barely appeared in public for five years. But he still holds the super-voting-power Class B shares, controlling more than 50% of the voting power in practice. Nominally retired, he's still the one with his hands on the steering wheel.

The most likely operating model is: Colin Huang sets the broad direction, Chen Lei runs the battles.

There's little to question about Chen Lei's execution ability. Temu went from zero to $70 billion in GMV under his watch, and the rapid pivot during the tariff war was also run by him. If forced to give a score, execution would rate 4.5 out of 5.

The shortfall is in communication. No investor calls, no interviews — to the outside world, the company is essentially a black box. This isn't a fatal problem, but it does mean the market can't fully price the company — when you don't speak, others can only guess conservatively.

Then there's Duan Yongping. His position in Pinduoduo is substantial — 150,000 shares in 2022, scaled up to 11.56 million shares two years later, a 77x increase, with a cumulative investment of $1.1-1.3 billion. But he has never publicly recommended it to others, and has even described it using the phrase "hard to read."

What does that tell us? Even an investor at Duan Yongping's level treats Pinduoduo as "I'm willing to bet on this myself, but I can't tell others this has high certainty." That attitude itself is telling.

## III. Risk

Pinduoduo's risks aren't like Tencent's — known and manageable. They're the low-probability, high-impact-if-it-happens kind.

There are really only two that matter:

First, Temu being substantively banned in the United States. If it follows TikTok's path, Temu's U.S. market (roughly 25% of GMV) would be wiped out overnight, and the valuation would take a 25-35% hit. Low probability, but not zero.

Second, a U.S.-China decoupling compounded by problems with the VIE structure. Pinduoduo still has no secondary listing in Hong Kong — if it were forced to delist from U.S. exchanges, the realizable value of that 420 billion yuan in cash would become a big question mark.

The structural threat is Douyin e-commerce. If Douyin keeps advancing in e-commerce and its market share crosses 30%, Pinduoduo's low-price mindshare would be gradually eroded. Not an overnight event, but worth watching on a five-year horizon.

Everything else — regulatory fines, merchant disputes, employee issues — is episodic; it dents the stock price for a moment but doesn't damage the foundation.

Taken together, these risks are why the market prices it at PE 10x. It's not cheap — it's a risk premium.

But flip it around: if you judge the probability of these tail risks to be lower than what the market is pricing in, then PE 10x is genuinely cheap. This isn't a question of right or wrong — it's whether your read on risk probability diverges from the market's.

---

## IV. What to watch

Six signals — if any one of them triggers, it's worth rethinking the whole picture:

1. Temu's U.S. GMV share falls below 20%
2. A major new UFLPA allegation surfaces
3. Douyin e-commerce's market share breaks through 30%
4. Pinduoduo's ROE stays below 22% for two consecutive quarters
5. Large-scale merchant disputes (more than 1,000 merchants in a single incident)
6. Management launches a buyback or dividend — this one is a positive signal; if it happens, it's worth adding to the position

---

## Summary

The profit decline is the result of deliberate investment, and 31% ROE is a return to normal, not deterioration. Whether the 422.3 billion yuan in cash sitting there — neither distributed nor bought back — is a problem comes down to whether you trust Colin Huang: trust him, and it's a strategic reserve; don't, and it's a value black hole. The market's PE of 10x is a neutral-to-pessimistic price. If you trust this business and this person more than the market does, Pinduoduo is undervalued.
