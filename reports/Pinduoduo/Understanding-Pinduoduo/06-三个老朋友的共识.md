# A Consensus Among Three Old Friends — Duan Yongping, Li Lu, and Ding Lei Simultaneously Build Large Positions in Pinduoduo

> *Understanding Pinduoduo* series · Piece 00 · Overview
> Reading time: about 12 minutes

---

## Something worth noting

In the second quarter of 2025, three things happened at the same time:

- **Duan Yongping** continued adding to his Pinduoduo position, accumulating a total holding of more than 8.66 million shares
- **Li Lu** bought back into Pinduoduo for the first time in three and a half years, building a position of 4.608 million shares in one go
- **Ding Lei** (of NetEase) bought Pinduoduo for the first time ever, building a position of 3.555 million shares in one go

Three people, the same quarter, the same stock.

If this were just three unrelated fund managers happening to buy the same stock, it would not be worth a dedicated piece. But these three people are connected by a relationship chain spanning 25 years — and at the other end of that chain sits Pinduoduo's founder, Huang Zheng (Colin Huang).

---

## A 25-year chain of relationships

**In 2001**, Ding Lei sent a message over MSN to a computer-science student at Zhejiang University named Huang Zheng, asking about a technical question. That is how the two men met.

**Over the following years**, Ding Lei introduced Huang Zheng to Duan Yongping. Duan Yongping had by then retired from BBK Electronics and moved to the United States, where he was investing. After Huang Zheng went to the US to study, his relationship with Duan Yongping grew closer.

**In 2006**, Duan Yongping paid $620,000 to win the charity lunch with Warren Buffett. He brought someone along with him — 26-year-old Huang Zheng.

Afterward, Duan Yongping became the "life mentor" Huang Zheng openly acknowledged. Huang Zheng once wrote on his public account that the single most important thing Duan Yongping taught him was "do the right thing, and do the thing right."

**In 2015**, Pinduoduo raised roughly $8 million in its angel round. There were four investors in total: **Ding Lei, Duan Yongping, Sun Tongyu (former CEO of Taobao), and Wang Wei (founder of SF Express)**.

**Duan Yongping and Li Lu** have been friends for many years, and both are practitioners of value investing. Li Lu has publicly praised Duan Yongping's "extremely deep understanding of the essence of business."

In other words: **Ding Lei discovered Huang Zheng, Duan Yongping shaped Huang Zheng, and Li Lu is Duan Yongping's like-minded friend**. These three people's depth of understanding of Huang Zheng and Pinduoduo far exceeds that of any outside analyst.

## The "coincidence" of the same quarter

Placing all three 13Fs side by side for Q2 2025:

| Investor | Q2 2025 PDD holding | Action |
|--------|-----------------|------|
| Duan Yongping | 8,662,694 shares | Continued adding, +909,000 shares |
| Li Lu | 4,608,000 shares | **Re-established a position** of 4.608 million shares (after 3.5 years) |
| Ding Lei / NetEase | 3,555,488 shares | **Initial position** of 3.555 million shares |

**In the same quarter, three people with a private friendship spanning over 20 years each independently decided to buy Pinduoduo.**

Is this a coincidence? There are two possibilities:

1. **They discussed it beforehand** — it is entirely normal for friends to discuss investment ideas. Ding Lei was the one who introduced Huang Zheng to Duan Yongping in the first place, so it would be natural for them to talk about Pinduoduo's business model
2. **They arrived independently at the same conclusion** — based on each person's own long-term tracking of Pinduoduo, each judged around the same time that the valuation had reached a range worth buying into

Whichever it is, **three people with deep insight into Huang Zheng and Pinduoduo reaching the same judgment at the same time** is, in itself, a signal worth paying attention to.

Given that Li Lu has allocated 14.64% of his portfolio to PDD, a natural question is: **how have his historically large positions performed?**

**5 wins and 1 loss, an 83% win rate.** The one blemish is Meta — bought in 2020, it declined steadily, and he sold out entirely at around $193 in Q2 2022 (Meta later rebounded above $600, making that sale look, in hindsight, like a mistake).

A few noteworthy patterns:

1. **Li Lu's large positions are extremely concentrated.** He is not a "50 stocks at 2% each" investor — he is a "think it through, then go big on a handful" investor. At the most extreme, Micron alone made up 100% of his entire US equity portfolio
2. **Google is his most successful large position**, returning +288-343%, and it is still his largest holding today at 44% of the portfolio
3. **He will admit a mistake and exit.** He walked away from Meta down 24%, and walked away from Pinduoduo the first time down 34% — but walking away does not mean never coming back
4. **When he comes back, he comes back bigger.** His second Pinduoduo purchase was 12.7 times the size of the first. That suggests the first exit was not because he "got the company wrong," but because "the environment at the time made it untenable to hold"

**But it must be said honestly: an 83% win rate does not mean 'going big always wins.'** The Meta loss is a reminder that Li Lu makes mistakes too. Whether this Pinduoduo position ultimately turns a profit or a loss, it is too early to say.

## What did each of the three see?

While none of the three has publicly explained the specific reasoning behind their Q2 2025 purchases, some clues can be inferred from their public statements and trading patterns.

### What Duan Yongping saw: Huang Zheng

Duan Yongping has said repeatedly on Xueqiu:

> "The truth is I invested in Pinduoduo because I like Huang Zheng."
>
> "I still don't fully understand PDD's business model, but the more I look at it over the years, the more interesting I find it."

Duan Yongping's investment framework is right business, right people, right price. He has said that "when you invest, you are investing in the people running the business, whom you approve of." His understanding of Huang Zheng dates back to 2001, when Huang Zheng was still a student at Zhejiang University. More than 20 years of tracking and observation gave him the conviction to add 3.8 million shares in a single quarter during the August 2024 crash.

### What Li Lu saw: a change in certainty

Li Lu's first Pinduoduo purchase was in Q4 2020, when Pinduoduo had only just started turning a profit, Temu did not yet exist, and the long-term certainty of the business model was relatively low. The 2021 regulatory storm, combined with Huang Zheng's resignation, led him to exit.

When he bought back in Q2 2025, Pinduoduo had been consistently profitable for multiple consecutive quarters, the advertising-monetization model on its domestic flagship platform had matured, and net cash exceeded 400 billion yuan. **The environment changed, certainty rose, and the position size scaled up by 12.7 times along with it.**

### What Ding Lei saw: a business he watched from beginning to end

Ding Lei was one of the four angel investors. He already knew what Pinduoduo was trying to do and what kind of person Huang Zheng was back in 2015. He established a position through NetEase's US-equity account in 2025, then added 149% to it in Q1 2026 with the stock around $100 — **this is not a "chasing the hot trend" move; it is a "waited ten years and finally felt the secondary-market price was right" move.**

## A few questions worth sitting with

By now you may be thinking: "if three smart people all bought in, it must be right." But a good investment decision cannot be built on "because so-and-so bought it." A few questions are worth thinking through for yourself:

**1. Their informational edge is not yours.**

Duan Yongping has known Huang Zheng for 25 years, Ding Lei was an angel investor, and Li Lu has his personal network. Their trust in Pinduoduo's management is built on long-term personal contact — while for outside investors, the transparency of Pinduoduo's management is essentially zero (no earnings calls, no roadshows, no meetings with investors). All you can rely on is public financial reports and SEC filings.

**2. Smart people make mistakes too.**

Li Lu's concentrated Meta bet lost 24%. Duan Yongping sold out of Pinduoduo entirely in Q2 2020 — had he held through the $212 high in February 2021, his return would have been far higher than what his current cost basis reflects. **Past actions do not prove future correctness.**

**3. Their cost basis is not your cost basis.**

Duan Yongping was an angel investor, and his cost in the primary market may have been just a few cents. The same goes for Ding Lei. Even if their secondary-market holdings suffer a substantial loss, their overall return could still be positive. If you buy in today, the risk-reward you face is entirely different from theirs.

**4. Pinduoduo's core uncertainties have not disappeared just because "someone bought in."**

- Temu's political risk (US tariff policy, elimination of the de minimis exemption)
- Management's intentions for the more than 400 billion yuan in cash remain unclear
- Huang Zheng is not on the front line, and the long-term execution ability of Chen Lei's team remains untested
- Domestic e-commerce growth is slowing, and the food-delivery war is consuming industry profits

None of these problems will be automatically solved simply because Duan Yongping, Li Lu, and Ding Lei bought in.

## What does this actually tell us?

Stripping out the emotion, **the fact that "three people who have known Huang Zheng for over 20 years bought Pinduoduo at the same time" does not tell us "Pinduoduo is certain to go up." It tells us:**

**In each of their own judgment frameworks, Pinduoduo's price in 2025 reached a range worth betting heavily on.**

Duan Yongping uses a "judge the person" framework — he trusts Huang Zheng.
Li Lu uses a "add more as certainty rises" framework — he has more confidence than the first time.
Ding Lei uses a "a familiar business finally reached a good price" framework — he waited ten years.

Three different frameworks, converging on the same conclusion. This kind of **convergence across multiple frameworks** carries more reference value than a single "it's cheap" indicator.

But ultimately, you have to make the call using **your own framework**.
