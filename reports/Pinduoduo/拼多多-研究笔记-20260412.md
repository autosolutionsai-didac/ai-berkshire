## I. Duan Yongping's analogy: PDD is like Disney + Costco

Duan Yongping said Pinduoduo resembles a combination of Disney and Costco.

**Disney's business model**: an IP flywheel — create IP → theatrical release → theme parks → merchandise licensing → streaming, reinforcing in a loop. Its moat is the IP itself: a cross-generational emotional connection that cannot be replicated and does not expire. In essence, it **sells emotion and an irreplaceable experience**.

**Costco's business model**: membership fees are where the profit is; merchandise itself barely makes money (gross margin ~11%). Membership fees account for 70%+ of operating profit, with a 93% renewal rate. It builds user trust through a curated SKU count (~3,700) and extremely low prices. In essence, it **saves users money, then charges them a fixed fee**.

**The core of Duan Yongping's analogy**: what these two companies have in common is that they "truly stand on the user's side." He hopes PDD's endgame is one where users believe Pinduoduo's goods are the cheapest and reliable — not just low price, but the combination of "low price + trust," similar to Costco.

## II. PDD's differentiated services (facts)

1. **Group-buying model**: users initiate a group purchase, and once enough people join, the deal closes at a lower price. After aggregating demand, the platform places large bulk orders with merchants, and merchants offer a lower factory price because of the larger order volume.
2. **Social-graph distribution**: reaches new users by sharing links through WeChat; group deals and price-cutting require inviting friends. The platform doesn't rely on search advertising for user acquisition — it relies on users actively spreading the word.
3. **Direct-from-origin agricultural produce**: partners directly with agricultural regions nationwide, bypassing wholesalers and distributors. More than 100 growing regions were onboarded in 2025.
4. **10-billion-yuan subsidy program**: a dedicated subsidy fund established in 2019, directly covering the price gap on branded goods (iPhone, Dyson, etc.) to guarantee the lowest price across the internet for authentic products.
5. **WeChat mini-program platform**: reaches users through the WeChat ecosystem without requiring a standalone app install; monthly active users exceed 500 million.
6. **Temu**: launched in 2022, using a fully managed model (merchants only supply goods; the platform handles pricing, logistics, and after-sales) to sell Chinese goods to consumers overseas.

## III. How competitors have imitated PDD

| Platform | What it imitated | Result |
|------|---------|------|
| Taobao Deals (Taote) | Imitated group-buying low prices | **Failed** — has since been marginalized, with merchants migrating back to the main Taobao platform |
| JD 10-billion-yuan subsidy | Imitated the 10-billion-yuan subsidy program | Prices caught up, but never surpassed PDD's |
| Douyin e-commerce | Did not directly imitate, instead pursued a content-commerce path | GMV has grown rapidly (projected to exceed 4 trillion yuan) and is the biggest variable |

**Competitors can and largely already have replicated Pinduoduo's differentiated services.** Differentiation at the feature level alone does not constitute a durable moat.

## IV. Why Taote failed

Its user base once reached 300 million, but it ultimately failed, for these reasons:

1. **Users came for the subsidies, and left once the subsidies stopped**: it never got users to stay for the platform's long-term low prices, resulting in low repeat-purchase rates.
2. **Poor product-quality control**: lax vetting of onboarded merchants led to high complaint volumes over counterfeit and low-quality goods, hurting the user experience.
3. **Poor merchant experience**: resource allocation relied on manual review with poor fairness, so many factory-based merchants couldn't get traffic and gradually gave up.
4. **Internal strategic wavering**: Alibaba never fully committed to running Taote as a truly independent operation, since its success would cannibalize Taobao's main platform.
5. **No WeChat traffic**: it had to rely on paid advertising for user acquisition, at a customer-acquisition cost far higher than early-stage Pinduoduo's.

## V. The real state of PDD's moat

**Moats that genuinely exist:**
- The WeChat social graph: user acquisition through WeChat sharing gives a customer-acquisition cost far below competitors' (but WeChat belongs to Tencent, not PDD)
- Direct connections to agricultural origins: established origin relationships and cold-chain networks that take time to build
- The low-price mindset: users' perception that "PDD = cheapest" is a real asset

**Flaws in the moat:**
- The deepest moat (the WeChat social graph) belongs to Tencent, not PDD
- Low prices can be matched (both JD and Taobao have already rolled out their own 10-billion-yuan subsidy programs)
- User loyalty is weak: PDD users go "wherever it's cheapest," so switching costs are low
- Temu has no WeChat overseas, so the social-distribution model doesn't apply — it relies purely on paid advertising for acquisition

## VI. Temu's current state

**Profitability:**
- Operating loss of approximately 32 billion yuan in 2024, approximately 50 billion yuan in 2025 (UBS estimate)
- The European market has been locally profitable since 2025 (net profit of about $110 million, gross margin of 37%)
- The U.S. market is close to breakeven, but conditions have worsened again after tariffs

**Tariff impact:**
- The U.S. eliminated the duty-free exemption for parcels under $800 in May 2025
- The air-freight direct-mail model has fundamentally lost its economics
- Temu's response: the share of air freight dropped from 85% to 2%, shifting to ocean-freight stocking; the semi-managed model has expanded to 75% of the business; ad spend has shifted its focus toward Europe

**Path to profitability:**
- UBS forecast: quarterly breakeven by the end of 2026, with an operating margin of about 6% by 2029

## VII. The 100-billion-yuan support program and "New Pinmu"

**100-billion-yuan support program**: 100 billion yuan over three years, across three main directions:
1. Agricultural supply chain (Duoduo Haote Chan): direct-from-origin sourcing, cold chain, standardization — agricultural product sales up 47% in 2025
2. Logistics into lower-tier markets (E-commerce Goes West): free-shipping coverage extended to remote administrative villages, waiving western-region transfer fees
3. Merchant ecosystem (100-Billion Payback Program): fee reductions and lower deposit requirements

**New Pinmu**: established in March 2026 in Shanghai, with an initial capital injection of 15 billion yuan and a planned 100 billion yuan over three years. It is positioned to integrate "PDD + Temu" supply-chain resources and incubate Chinese self-operated brands for global markets. The goal is to upgrade from "selling low-price white-label goods" to "selling branded Chinese-made goods," improving pricing power and boosting resilience against tariffs.

**Conditions for success**: cooperation from supply-chain factories in transforming, rebuilding channel perception, time needed to establish an overseas brand, and a stable tariff environment.

**Risk of failure**: failed cases of Chinese manufacturing going global with branding far outnumber successful ones; PDD's DNA is as a platform, not a brand; channel conflict; 15 billion yuan spread too thin to be enough; brand-building typically takes at least 5-10 years.

## VIII. New Pinmu vs. Huang Zheng's "matching principle" — are they in conflict?

**Huang Zheng's original words:**
> Full-category expansion is still the old traffic-driven logic, and so is "upgrading" — upgrading reflects a "looking down from above the Fifth Ring Road" mindset. I don't think Pinduoduo needs a brand upgrade or full-category expansion — that's wrong. What we should always be doing is matching: letting the right people find the right things in the right context.

**Apparent contradiction:** New Pinmu explicitly aims to incubate brands, while Huang Zheng said "don't pursue a brand upgrade" — on its face, this directly contradicts him.

**Digging deeper: they may not conflict in spirit**

What Huang Zheng opposed was the platform itself chasing brand cachet to please higher-end users (i.e., the "looking down from above the Fifth Ring Road" mindset). New Pinmu's logic is different:
- It helps the **supply-chain side** build brands, rather than repositioning the PDD platform itself
- The target users remain **globally price-sensitive consumers** (Temu's core demographic)
- It is still fundamentally "matching" — helping factories produce trustworthy goods and selling them to the right people

From this angle, New Pinmu is an evolution of the matching principle's execution path: from "matchmaking" to "building in-house," but the target user base hasn't changed.

**Where the real tension lies:**

Huang Zheng stepped down in 2021, and current management faces a different set of pressures:
1. Temu faces a white-label trust crisis overseas, and branding is a defensive measure
2. Tariff shocks have stripped the "low-price direct-mail" model of its economics; branding can improve pricing power
3. "Helping Chinese manufacturing go global" is a politically favorable strategic framing that facilitates domestic financing and compliance

**Conclusion:** in spirit, there is continuity (serving price-sensitive users), but in execution, this departs from Huang Zheng's "don't build brands" principle. What Huang Zheng opposed was "upgrading for the sake of upgrading"; New Pinmu is "upgrading to survive" — the motivation differs, but the outcome is branding either way. This is a strategic adjustment forced by current reality, not a continuation of Huang Zheng's philosophy.
