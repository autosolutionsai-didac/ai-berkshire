# Extended-Range Technology, Product Lineup, and the Family-User Positioning

> "Understanding Li Auto" series · Part 02
> Reading time: about 15 minutes

---

## Li Auto's success formula: a precisely engineered niche

Automaking is a famously brutal business — capital-intensive, long-cycle, and thin on margin. But in 2019 Li Auto found a path to make this brutal business sweet, by stacking three precise judgment calls:

> **Extended-range technology (solving range anxiety) × family-SUV positioning (locking in high-purchasing-power buyers) × extreme SKU discipline (controlling cost) = finding a sweet niche within a brutal business**

Li Xiang is the new-force founder with **the strongest product instincts** in China — each of these three choices was validated by the market as correct, and all three peaked simultaneously in 2023.

But the 2025 data is telling us: **all three variables in this formula are undergoing fundamental change.**

---

## The extended-range route: from an exclusive advantage to an industry standard

### Why did the extended-range approach succeed in 2019-2023?

The core value of extended-range wasn't technological sophistication — it was **delivering the best user experience while infrastructure was still inadequate**.

When the Li ONE launched in 2019, China had only **516,000** public charging points nationwide (source: China Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure Promotion Alliance), and charging queues were the norm. The "can refuel, can charge" nature of extended-range vehicles let users run on electricity for city commuting and refuel for long trips — perfectly covering every scenario.

The effect was immediate:

| Year | Number of charging points | Extended-range user pain point | Battery-electric user pain point |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| 2019 | 516,000 | Low (can refuel) | High (hard to charge) |
| 2021 | 1.14 million | Low | Medium (still not convenient enough) |
| 2023 | 2.77 million | Low | Medium-low (notably improved) |
| 2025 | **~5 million** (estimate) | Low | **Low (800V fast charging widespread)** |

**(Data source: China Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure Promotion Alliance)**

The key inflection point came in the second half of 2025: **800V fast charging began scaling up, delivering 300 km of range from a 15-minute charge**. This means the core pain point of battery-electric vehicles — slow charging — is being resolved by technology. Extended-range's "fuel as a backstop" advantage is turning from a "must-have" into a "nice-to-have."

### How much longer does the extended-range window last?

Two sets of data reveal the trend:

**Set one: the shift in battery-electric vs. extended-range mix among new-force automakers**

| Year | Battery-electric share | Extended-range share |
|------|---------|---------|
| 2023 | 49% | 51% |
| 2025 | **74%** | **26%** |

**(Source: China Association of Automobile Manufacturers)**

In two years, extended-range's share fell from half to a quarter. Battery-electric is now firmly dominant.

**Set two: the number of competitors in the extended-range lane**

| Point in time | Major extended-range brands |
|--------|-----------|
| 2020 | Li Auto (sole player) |
| 2023 | Li Auto, AITO |
| 2025 | Li Auto, AITO, Leapmotor, Voyah, Deepal, Changan Qiyuan |
| 2026 | **+Xiaomi, +7 multinational automakers have announced entry** |

**(Source: CnEVPost, automaker announcements)**

The extended-range route has gone from Li Auto's "exclusive advantage" to an "industry standard." What's more, Seres (AITO's extended-range powertrain supplier) already held a **37.5%** share of the extended-range powertrain market in 2025, overtaking Li Auto to rank first (source: Yicai).

**Counter-argument**: Extended-range isn't dying out — it's expanding, since the overall extended-range market pie is growing. Even if Li Auto's share of that market declines, if the total is growing fast enough, absolute sales volume can still increase. And extended-range still meets rigid demand in third- and fourth-tier cities and for long-distance use cases, where charging infrastructure will still take 3-5 years to mature.

**Our judgment**: Extended-range as a technology route won't disappear, but as a **competitive barrier** it already has. Li Auto can no longer command a premium by being "the extended-range specialist" — it needs to find new differentiation beyond extended-range.

## Product lineup: from "boutique store" to "inverted pyramid"

### The evolution of Li Auto's product structure

| Stage | Period | Products | Characteristics |
|------|------|------|------|
| Single hit product | 2019-2022 | Li ONE | One car carries the whole business, extreme focus |
| Extended-range lineup | 2023-2024 | L9/L8/L7/L6 | Four extended-range SUVs, covering RMB 250,000-500,000 |
| Extended-range + battery-electric | 2025-2026 | L series + i6/i8/i9 | Fighting on two fronts, product line expanding rapidly |

**(Source: Li Auto product launch records)**

The key inflection point came with the launch of the i6.

### The i6: it saved sales, but hurt the structure

The Li i6, launched in September 2025, starts at **RMB 249,800** — the cheapest car Li Auto has ever made.

The i6's sales performance has been genuinely impressive:

- **100,000 units** rolled off the line within 6.5 months of launch (source: ZOL), setting the fastest-delivery record among battery-electric SUVs in the RMB 200,000-300,000 range
- January 2026 deliveries of **16,883 units**, and March 2026 deliveries exceeding **24,000 units** (source: Li Auto monthly announcements)
- In March, it contributed nearly **60%** of Li Auto's total deliveries (source: NetEase/Securities Times)

But the i6's success has exposed a serious structural problem — **the product structure is turning into an "inverted pyramid."**

| Product | Positioning | Price range | Share of total sales (est., March 2026) |
|------|------|---------|--------------------------|
| L9 | Flagship large SUV | RMB 420,000-510,000 | ~5% (mid-generation transition) |
| L8 | Mid-large SUV | RMB 300,000-400,000 | ~10-15% |
| L7 | Mid-size SUV | RMB 280,000-350,000 | ~10-15% |
| L6 | Entry mid-size SUV | RMB 250,000-300,000 | ~15-20% |
| **i6** | **Battery-electric mid-size SUV** | **RMB 249,800-280,000** | **~55-60%** |

**(Source: composite estimate based on Li Auto's monthly delivery data and industry reporting)**

**The cheapest product contributing sixty percent of sales — a danger sign at any consumer-products company.**

The reason is simple: the i6's average selling price is **~RMB 250,000**, about **44%** lower than the L9's (~RMB 450,000). The higher the i6's share, the lower Li Auto's overall average selling price, and the more its gross margin gets dragged down.

The data bears this out:

| Year | Average selling price (RMB 10,000s) | Overall gross margin |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| 2023 | **~33** | **22.2%** |
| 2024 | ~28 | 20.5% |
| 2025 | ~25 | 18.7% |
| Q1 2026 guidance | ~22-23 (estimate) | **~5%** |

**(Data source: Li Auto annual report; Q1 guidance from TMTPost)**

Q1 2026 gross-margin guidance of just **5%** is the lowest since Li Auto's listing. Management attributes it partly to one-off factors — L9 inventory clearance and purchase-tax subsidies — but the trend direction is not encouraging.

**Counter-argument**: The i6's pricing strategy is a deliberate choice rather than something forced — Li Auto needs a presence in the mainstream RMB 250,000 market. Economies of scale on the i6 should gradually take hold, and long-term gross margin could recover to 15-18%. Also, the Q1 5% margin includes substantial one-off inventory-clearance costs and isn't representative of a steady-state level.

## Family-user positioning: the brand endures, but the premium is fading

### What Li Auto got right

Li Auto has built a distinctive brand perception in China's NEV market: **Li Auto = the family's first choice**.

This isn't just marketing copy. Li Auto has done several concrete things:

1. **Seat design built around the second row** — not "the driver's car," but "the whole family's car"
2. **In-car entertainment system** — independent rear screens and speakers so long trips aren't boring
3. **Space maximization** — six-seat layouts, with third-row usability far ahead of competitors
4. **User-community operations** — parent-child events, family road trips, and other programming deeply tied to family users

The result is an NPS (Net Promoter Score) of **79.1**, the best in the industry (source: Li Auto annual report). User satisfaction and word-of-mouth remain strong.

### Why the brand premium is fading

But "brand recognition" and "brand premium" are two different things.

Brand recognition = users know you and like you.
Brand premium = users are willing to **pay more** for you.

Li Auto's brand recognition remains strong, but its brand premium is fading — the evidence is the sustained decline in average selling price.

| Time | Flagship model pricing | Entry model pricing | Pricing power reflected |
|------|------------|------------|-------------|
| 2022 | L9: RMB 459,800 | L8: RMB 359,800 | Strong (no direct rival) |
| 2024 | L9: RMB 429,800 | L6: RMB 249,800 | Medium (starting to trend down) |
| May 2026 | New L9: RMB 459,800-509,800 (but cut RMB 20,000 right after launch) | i6: RMB 249,800 | **Weak (even the flagship needs promotional discounts)** |

**(Source: Li Auto product launch event information)**

One sign a brand has pricing power is that **a new product doesn't need a price cut to sell**. Li Auto achieved that in 2022 (the L9 sold out immediately at launch), but couldn't in 2026 (the new L9 was cut by RMB 20,000 right after launch).

**Counter-argument**: A price cut doesn't necessarily mean lost pricing power. In China's brutally competitive auto market, even BYD runs large-scale promotions. Li Auto's discount magnitude (RMB 20,000-30,000) is moderate by industry standards and shouldn't be over-read as brand erosion.

## Second half of 2026: the critical test of the product-refresh cycle

Li Auto's product roadmap shows 2026 as a "big product year":

| Timing | Product | Significance |
|------|------|------|
| May 2026 | All-new L9 | Flagship refresh, equipped with the Mach M100 chip, testing high-end pricing power |
| H2 2026 | i9 | Battery-electric flagship SUV, testing whether battery-electric can succeed above RMB 300,000 |
| H2 2026 | i8 | Battery-electric mid-large SUV, filling the price gap between the i6 and i9 |
| H2 2026 | L7/L6 refresh | The fight to keep the extended-range base alive |

**(Source: Li Auto product roadmap, CnEVPost)**

This marks Li Auto's first full product-refresh cycle in two years. If the new products succeed — especially if the i9 can gain a foothold in the above-RMB-300,000 battery-electric market — Li Auto could return to a trajectory of 500,000+ annual deliveries and 18-20% gross margin.

But the risks are equally significant:

1. **The i9 faces an extremely crowded field** — competing at the same price point as the AITO M9, NIO ES8, XPeng G9, and Tesla Model Y
2. **The L-series refresh can only "hold ground," not "expand it"** — the extended-range market already has too many competitors
3. **The product line is expanding from 4 models to 7-8** — SKU management difficulty grows exponentially, which runs directly counter to Li Auto's early "streamlined SKU" success formula

### Metrics to watch for this refresh cycle

| Indicator | Validation threshold | Interpretation |
|------|---------|------|
| New L9 monthly deliveries | Stable above **8,000 units** | Flagship pricing power still intact |
| i9 first-month orders at launch | **Above 10,000 units** | Battery-electric can succeed at the high end |
| Gross margin after L7/L6 refresh | Back **above 18%** | The extended-range base has stabilized |
| Total monthly deliveries | Stable at **45,000-50,000 units** | The 550,000-unit full-year target is achievable |

If the above indicators are met in Q3-Q4 2026, the "temporary trough" narrative for Li Auto holds; if not, the "structural decline" verdict will be further reinforced.

## Core conclusions of this piece

| Dimension | Judgment |
|------|------|
| Extended-range route | The technology still has a market, but as a **competitive barrier it has vanished** |
| Product structure | The i6 saved sales but produced an "inverted pyramid," systematically dragging down gross margin |
| Family positioning | Brand recognition remains strong (best NPS in the industry), but brand premium is eroding (flagship needs price cuts) |
| Refresh cycle | H2 2026 is the critical proving window; success or failure hinges on the combined performance of the i9 and the L-series refresh |

In one line: **Li Auto once found a sweet niche (extended-range + family SUV + high margin) within a brutal business (automaking), but that niche is shrinking, getting more crowded, and losing its sweetness. H2 2026 is the last window for it to prove it can find a new niche.**
