# Attacked on Three Fronts — Huawei, BYD, and Xiaomi

> "Understanding Li Auto" series · Part 03

## One chart to see Li Auto's competitive predicament

Li Auto's product problems can be solved by a new model generation, but a deteriorating competitive landscape cannot — rivals don't exit the market just because you launched a new car. This piece answers exactly one question: **how strong, and how durable, are the three forces closing in on Li Auto?**

First, a look at the numbers to understand how Li Auto's position among the new-force automakers has shifted:

### 2025 delivery rankings among new-force automakers

| Rank | Brand | 2025 deliveries (thousand units) | YoY change |
|------|------|------------------|---------|
| 1 | **Leapmotor** | ~420 | **+103%** |
| 2 | **XPeng** | 429 | **+126%** |
| 3 | **Xiaomi** | ~410 | **+204%** (partly a base-effect from H2 2024) |
| **4** | **Li Auto** | **406** | **-18.8%** |

**(Sources: 21st Century Business Herald, CnEVPost, company announcements)**

### January 2026 delivery rankings

| Rank | Brand | January deliveries | YoY |
|------|------|----------|------|
| 1 | **Harmony Intelligent Mobility** | **57,915** | Sharp growth |
| 2 | **Xiaomi** | ~39,000 | — |

The delivery champion of the new-force automakers two years ago is now ranked fourth — and it is the only top-tier new-force brand with negative full-year growth in 2025.

More critically, **the three brands ahead of Li Auto — Harmony Intelligent Mobility (Huawei), Xiaomi, and Leapmotor — are all growing rapidly.** This is not cyclical give-and-take; it is a structural shift in market share.

## First front: Huawei/AITO — the most lethal rival

### Why is Huawei Li Auto's biggest threat?

The answer is simple: **the product positioning is a near-perfect overlap**.

| Dimension | Li Auto L series | AITO M series |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Technology route | Extended-range EV (EREV) | Extended-range EV (EREV) |
| Target user | Families | Families |
| Price range | RMB 250,000-500,000 | RMB 250,000-500,000 |
| Vehicle positioning | Large-space SUV | Large-space SUV |
| Selling points | Fridge, TV, oversized sofa | Smart driving + HarmonyOS cockpit |

**(Source: product information from both companies)**

Li Auto has many competitors, but Huawei/AITO is the only one competing **in exactly the same lane, with exactly the same strategy, targeting exactly the same user group**.

And Huawei holds two cards Li Auto doesn't have:

**Card one: smart driving.** Huawei ADS (advanced smart driving) is widely recognized as tier-one in China's smart-driving race, on par with XPeng's XNGP. Li Auto lacks a distinctive label in smart driving — after the "three pillars" of its 2025 smart-driving team collectively departed, the team went through a near-total rebuild (sources: TMTPost, 21st Century Business Herald).

**Card two: brand endorsement.** Huawei enjoys extremely strong brand power and trust among Chinese consumers. "Far ahead of the pack" isn't just a slogan — it converts directly into purchasing power.

### Data comparison: AITO vs. Li Auto

| Metric | Harmony Intelligent Mobility (2025) | Li Auto (2025) |
|------|----------------|------------|
| Full-year deliveries | **589,000 units** | 406,000 units |
| YoY growth | — | **-18.8%** |
| Core models | AITO M7/M8/M9 | L9/L8/L7/L6 |
| Smart-driving capability | ADS (leading) | AD Max (catching up) |
| EREV powertrain supplier | Seres (37.5% market share) | In-house |

**(Sources: 21st Century Business Herald, Yicai)**

Harmony Intelligent Mobility's 2025 deliveries were already **1.45x** those of Li Auto. In particular, the AITO M9's precision strike on the L9 in the RMB 400,000-500,000 price band has made things increasingly difficult for Li Auto's flagship.

**Counter-argument**: Huawei's dominance won't necessarily last forever. AITO's products depend heavily on Huawei's brand pull and sales channels; should Huawei adjust its partnership model (as it did previously with the bumpy relationships with Arcfox and Avatr), AITO's growth could slow. Huawei's automotive business also has yet to turn a profit, and the sustainability of long-term cash burn remains in question.

## Second front: BYD — a scale-crushing behemoth

### Where is BYD's threat?

Direct competition between BYD and Li Auto isn't yet intense — BYD's core market is RMB 100,000-250,000, while Li Auto's is RMB 250,000-500,000. But two trends deserve attention:

**Trend one: BYD's premiumization is accelerating**

| Brand | Positioning | Price range | Overlap with Li Auto |
|------|------|---------|-----------|
| Denza | Upper-mid-tier | RMB 220,000-450,000 | Medium-high |
| Fang Cheng Bao | Premium off-road/SUV | RMB 280,000-600,000 | High |
| Yangwang | Ultra-luxury | RMB 800,000-1,500,000 | Low |

**(Source: BYD product lineup)**

The Denza D9 is already competing with the Li Auto L8/L9 for family MPV/SUV buyers; the Fang Cheng Bao Bao 5/Bao 8 EREV SUVs highly overlap with the Li Auto L7/L8 in positioning.

**Trend two: BYD's cost advantage is insurmountable**

BYD's 2025 global sales exceeded **4 million units** (source: BYD announcement) — **10 times** Li Auto's volume. This scale gap means:

- Component procurement costs 20-30% lower
- Lower battery costs (self-supplied via FinDreams Battery)
- Less exposure to lithium carbonate price swings

When BYD decides to attack the RMB 250,000-400,000 market with a premium brand, it can offer an equal or better product at a cost structure far below Li Auto's.

**Counter-argument**: BYD's premiumization push hasn't been very successful so far. Denza's brand recognition trails Li Auto's, and Fang Cheng Bao skews more toward off-road than family use. In the "family SUV" sub-segment specifically, Li Auto's brand mindshare remains the strongest. BYD's forte is the value-for-money market; premiumization takes time to build brand character.

## Third front: Xiaomi — the most unpredictable variable

### Why is Xiaomi frightening?

Xiaomi Auto only began deliveries in the second half of 2024, yet 2025 full-year deliveries already reached about **410,000 units** (source: 36Kr), up **204%** year over year. The 2026 target is **550,000 units** (source: CnEVPost) — the same as Li Auto's target.

Xiaomi's threat to Li Auto isn't in the present — the SU7 is a sports sedan and doesn't directly compete with Li Auto's SUVs. The threat lies in the future:

**Xiaomi is planning to launch an EREV SUV.** If Xiaomi releases an EREV/battery-electric SUV in the RMB 250,000-350,000 range, leveraging Xiaomi's brand traffic, ecosystem connectivity, and extreme value pricing, it would directly hit the Li Auto L6/L7 market.

| Xiaomi's cards | Li Auto's cards |
|---------|---------|
| **170 million monthly active MIUI users** (source: Xiaomi annual report) | ~1.6 million cumulative owners |
| Phone-home-car ecosystem | Standalone auto brand |
| Five-year R&D budget of **RMB 200 billion** | Annual R&D of RMB 11.3 billion |
| Three-year AI investment of **RMB 60 billion** | AI at 50% of R&D (~RMB 5.7 billion) |

**Counter-argument**: Xiaomi's SUV hasn't even launched yet — it's all expectation. And Xiaomi Auto isn't profitable today either — per-vehicle profit on the SU7 is razor-thin, and scale expansion relies entirely on cash burn. For Xiaomi to challenge Li Auto in the SUV market, it first has to prove it can build a good SUV — a completely different category capability from a sports sedan.

## Smart driving: Li Auto's biggest weakness

On the dimension of smart driving, Li Auto faces an awkward position: **it is neither the strongest, nor does it have a distinctive label.**

### China's three tiers of smart-driving capability

| Tier | Brands | Core capability | User perception |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| **Tier one** | Huawei ADS, XPeng XNGP | Full-scenario highway/urban NOA, L3-level capability | Consumers proactively mention it |
| **Tier two** | Li Auto AD Max, NIO NAD | Highway NOA mature, urban NOA advancing | Consumers passively aware |
| **Tier three** | Xiaomi, Leapmotor, BYD | Highway NOA, urban capability catching up | Lower awareness |

**(Source: composite industry-media assessment, not a strict ranking)**

Li Auto's biggest problem in smart driving isn't that the technology itself is inadequate — it's **the continuity risk created by team turmoil**.

From August 2025 through early 2026, the "three pillars" of Li Auto's smart-driving team departed one after another — including CTO Wang Kai and smart-driving head Lang Xianpeng. The entire smart-driving team has undergone something close to a rebuild (sources: TMTPost, 21st Century Business Herald).

The M100 chip (1,280 TOPS, 5nm automotive-grade), if successfully mass-produced, could partially close the smart-driving hardware gap. But chips typically require a 1-2 year validation and integration cycle from announcement to large-scale deployment in vehicles.

**Counter-argument**: The smart-driving gap is narrowing quickly. As L4-level autonomous driving gradually becomes widespread, smart driving will eventually commoditize — much like phone cameras, initially a key differentiator that later became an industry standard. Li Auto doesn't need to be first in smart driving, only "good enough."

**Our view**: This argument may hold true over the long run (5-10 years), but in the short run (1-3 years), smart driving remains a core selling point for Chinese NEVs. Huawei and XPeng have already established smart-driving brand recognition; if Li Auto cannot catch up within this 1-3 year window, user attrition will become difficult to reverse.

## Moat depth assessment

Taking the pressure from all three fronts together, Li Auto's moat currently looks like this:

| Moat dimension | 2023 (peak) | 2026 (current) | Change |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------|------|
| Brand recognition | Strong (family SUV = Li Auto) | Medium-strong (still recognized but premium declining) | ↓ |
| Technology barrier | Medium (EREV exclusivity) | **Weak (EREV now standard)** | ↓↓ |
| Switching cost | Weak (buying a car is a one-time decision) | Weak | → |
| Economies of scale | Medium (most cost-efficient among new forces) | Weak (crushed by BYD/Huawei) | ↓ |
| Network effects | None | Weak (charging network in early form) | ↑ |
| Smart-driving barrier | None (not a selling point) | **Weak (team being rebuilt)** | ↓ |

**Overall moat rating: medium-weak, and the trend is worsening.**

An analogy: Li Auto in 2023 was like a car that had a highway lane all to itself — running fast and steady. Its 2026 situation: that lane is now crowded with Huawei, Xiaomi, and BYD, and their cars are bigger and more powerful.

**Counter-argument**: Moat assessment should consider "combined effects" — individually, none of these run very deep, but "brand recognition + product-definition ability + tens of billions in cash + user word of mouth" combined still form a defensive line that isn't easily fully breached. Among the new-force automakers, Li Auto is currently the only one that simultaneously achieves high brand recognition, high user satisfaction, and ample cash reserves.

## Industry backdrop: the era of stock-market competition has arrived

All the competitive pressure Li Auto faces is unfolding against a larger industry backdrop:

**China's NEV penetration rate crossed 50% in 2025** (source: China Association of Automobile Manufacturers). This means competition is shifting in nature — from "taking share from gasoline cars" (incremental competition) to "NEVs fighting each other for the same pool" (stock competition).

In the incremental-competition era, all NEV brands could grow together — the market pie was expanding.
In the stock-competition era, one brand's growth must come at the expense of another's — a zero-sum game.

In stock competition, winners typically share these characteristics:

| Trait | BYD | Huawei | Xiaomi | Li Auto |
|------|--------|------|------|------|
| Cost advantage | ✓ (extremely strong) | ✓ (Seres manufacturing) | ✓ (strong supply chain) | ✗ |
| Brand power | ✓ (national brand) | ✓ (extremely strong) | ✓ (extremely strong) | △ (strong in the family segment) |
| Smart-driving capability | △ (catching up) | ✓ (leading) | △ | △ (rebuilding) |
| Ecosystem connectivity | △ | ✓ (HarmonyOS ecosystem) | ✓ (Xiaomi ecosystem) | ✗ |
| Financial strength | ✓ (extremely strong) | ✓ (Huawei parent) | ✓ (extremely strong) | ✓ |

Of five key competitive factors, financial strength is the only clear advantage Li Auto holds. That wasn't a problem in the incremental-competition era — a good product alone could drive growth; but in the stock-competition era, weaknesses in overall competitiveness get magnified.

## Core conclusions of this piece

| Competitor | Threat level | Core threat |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Huawei/AITO | **Extremely high (5/5)** | Fully overlapping product positioning + smart-driving dominance + brand pull |
| BYD | **Medium-high (3.5/5)** | Premiumization push + cost dominance + scale advantage |
| Xiaomi | **High (4/5)** | Planned EREV SUV + traffic + ecosystem connectivity + massive cash |
| XPeng | **Medium (3/5)** | Leading smart-driving VLA 2.0 + partial product overlap |
| Leapmotor | **Medium (3/5)** | Value-for-money + extremely fast scale growth |

In one line: **Li Auto is sliding from a niche player with a clear differentiated advantage toward an ordinary participant fighting giants in a red ocean. The deterioration in the competitive landscape is structural, and won't automatically reverse just because of a product-generation refresh.**
