## I. Industry Background

### 1.1 Global Cross-Border E-commerce Market Size

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 (est.) | 2031 forecast |
|------|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Global cross-border e-commerce market size | ~$798 billion | ~$950 billion | $1.74 trillion (Verified Market Research, CAGR 13.84%) |

**Note:** Different institutions use vastly different methodologies and scope definitions. This report uses Verified Market Research's more conservative figure ($1.74 trillion) as the baseline.

### 1.2 Share of Chinese Cross-Border ("Going Global") E-commerce

- In 2025, among Chinese cross-border e-commerce platforms, the combined GMV of the four major players — Temu, Shein, TikTok Shop, and AliExpress — was approximately $250-300 billion, accounting for roughly 25%-30% of global cross-border e-commerce
- By 2031, the share of Chinese "going global" e-commerce is projected to reach 30%-35% of global cross-border e-commerce, corresponding to a market space of $500-600 billion

### 1.3 Tariff/Regulatory Environment Assumptions

| Scenario | Assumption |
|------|------|
| **Optimistic** | A substantive China-US trade agreement eases tensions, with tariffs on Chinese goods falling below 20%; the de minimis exemption is partially restored or its threshold raised; the EU does not impose large-scale additional tariffs |
| **Neutral** | The current state persists — US comprehensive tariffs on China remain in the 31%-48% range; the de minimis exemption elimination stays in place; the EU rolls out new e-commerce rules, but with moderate intensity |
| **Pessimistic** | Tariffs escalate further to 60%+; the EU follows the US in eliminating de minimis and imposes a carbon border tax; Southeast Asian and Latin American markets also introduce protective measures |

**Current real-world baseline (May 2026):**
- US average effective tariff on China: 31.6%-47.5% (multiple layers stacked)
- The de minimis exemption was formally eliminated in August 2025
- Leaders of China and the US are in negotiations; market expectations point to "stability but not a thaw"
- The EU is reviewing new cross-border e-commerce rules, which have not yet taken effect

## III. Temu's Three 2031 Scenarios

### 3.1 Optimistic Scenario

**Core assumptions:**
- Tariffs ease somewhat (US falls into the 20% range), de minimis partially restored
- The semi-managed model succeeds globally, covering 50+ countries
- Advertising monetization matures, with ad revenue reaching 3%+ of GMV
- Europe remains the leader, with emerging markets (Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East) growing rapidly
- CAGR: GMV grows 20% annually from 2026-2031

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$95 billion | $280 billion |
| Platform revenue (commission + advertising + service fees) | ~$16 billion | $56 billion |
| Revenue/GMV ratio (take rate) | ~17% | ~20% (lifted by advertising) |
| Operating profit | ~0 (near breakeven) | $5.6-7.0 billion (operating margin 10-12.5%) |
| Net profit | ~negative to marginally profitable | $4.5-5.5 billion |

**Reasoning chain:**
- GMV of $95 billion -> compounding at 20% -> approximately $280 billion by 2031
- Take rate rises from 17% to 20% (advertising 3% + commission 15% + other 2%)
- Operating margin benchmarked against Pinduoduo's domestic business (~25%), but given higher overseas operating costs, estimated at 10-12.5%

### 3.2 Neutral Scenario (Base Case)

**Core assumptions:**
- Tariffs remain at current levels (US in the 30-50% range), no substantive trade breakthrough
- The semi-managed model succeeds in Europe and Latin America, but is constrained in the US
- Advertising monetization reaches 2%
- CAGR: GMV grows 12-15% annually from 2026-2031

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$95 billion | $180-200 billion |
| Platform revenue | ~$16 billion | $31-36 billion |
| Revenue/GMV ratio (take rate) | ~17% | ~18% |
| Operating profit | ~0 | $2.5-3.6 billion (operating margin 8-10%) |
| Net profit | ~negative to marginally profitable | $2.0-2.8 billion |

**Reasoning chain:**
- GMV compounding at 14% -> approximately $190 billion by 2031
- Take rate rises slowly to 18%
- Operating margin of 8-10%, reflecting ongoing logistics subsidies and new-market expansion investment

### 3.3 Pessimistic Scenario

**Core assumptions:**
- Tariffs tighten further to 60%+, the US market shrinks sharply to <15% of GMV
- The EU introduces strict cross-border e-commerce regulations, hampering growth
- Growth is sustained only in emerging markets (Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa)
- The semi-managed model advances slowly, ad monetization stays below 1%
- CAGR: 5-8%

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$95 billion | $120-135 billion |
| Platform revenue | ~$16 billion | $19-22 billion |
| Revenue/GMV ratio (take rate) | ~17% | ~16% (take rate declines under competitive pressure) |
| Operating profit | ~0 | $0.5-1.0 billion (operating margin 2.5-5%) |
| Net profit | ~negative to marginally profitable | $0.3-0.8 billion |

**Reasoning chain:**
- The US market shrinks from 31% to 15% of GMV, Europe's growth slows to single digits
- Emerging markets grow fast but have poor unit economics (expensive logistics, low order values)
- Forced to keep subsidizing to defend market share, leaving razor-thin margins

## V. Shein's Three 2031 Scenarios

### 5.1 Optimistic Scenario

**Core assumptions:**
- Successful Hong Kong IPO (2026-2027), valued at $50 billion+, securing ample capital
- Successful brand upgrade, shifting from "ultra-low-price fast fashion" to a "value-for-money fashion-tech platform"
- The marketplace scales up, with 3P share rising to 40-50%, generating substantial platform commission and advertising revenue
- The tariff environment eases somewhat
- CAGR: revenue growth of 15-18%

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$68 billion | $150-170 billion |
| Revenue (1P + 3P commission + advertising) | ~$48 billion | $100-110 billion |
| Operating profit | ~$2.5-3.0 billion | $8.0-10.0 billion (operating margin 8-9%) |
| Net profit | ~$2.0 billion | $6.0-8.0 billion (net margin 6-7%) |

**Reasoning chain:**
- IPO proceeds fund brand-building and localized supply chain expansion (factory capacity expansion in Turkey and Brazil)
- Rising 3P share means a shift toward an asset-light model, structurally improving margins
- Gross margin holds at 55%+, expense ratio declines, and net margin rises from 3.5% to 6-7%

### 5.2 Neutral Scenario (Base Case)

**Core assumptions:**
- IPO completes but is valued at $30-40 billion, with a lukewarm market reception
- Maintains its position as the fast-fashion leader, but growth slows to 10-12%
- US market share is eroded (tariffs plus competition), but Europe and emerging markets pick up the slack
- 3P share rises to 35%
- Brand negatives (ESG/labor/environmental controversies) persist but remain manageable

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$68 billion | $110-130 billion |
| Revenue | ~$48 billion | $75-85 billion |
| Operating profit | ~$2.5-3.0 billion | $4.5-6.0 billion (operating margin 6-7%) |
| Net profit | ~$2.0 billion | $3.5-4.5 billion (net margin 4.5-5.5%) |

**Reasoning chain:**
- CAGR of 11% -> GMV grows from $68 billion to $120 billion
- Take rate rises slightly (higher 3P share)
- Margins improve moderately, though brand-upgrade investment and compliance costs offset part of the gain

### 5.3 Pessimistic Scenario

**Core assumptions:**
- IPO fails or is severely delayed (regulatory/geopolitical obstacles)
- Tariffs continue to hit hard, with US market revenue falling 30-40%
- ESG/environmental/labor controversies escalate, with Europe introducing mandatory regulations (France already has restrictive precedent)
- Brand negatives cause user growth to stall
- CAGR: 3-6%

| Metric | 2025 baseline | 2031 forecast |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| GMV | ~$68 billion | $80-95 billion |
| Revenue | ~$48 billion | $55-65 billion |
| Operating profit | ~$2.5-3.0 billion | $2.0-3.0 billion (operating margin 3.5-4.5%) |
| Net profit | ~$2.0 billion | $1.5-2.2 billion (net margin 2.5-3.5%) |

**Reasoning chain:**
- No IPO funds -> brand upgrade constrained -> locked into the low-price segment
- Squeezed by both the US and Europe -> can only expand slowly in emerging markets
- Gross margin gets compressed (difficulty passing tariffs through, price wars), and expense ratio is hard to bring down

## VI. Comparative Summary

### 6.1 Who's Bigger in Five Years? Who's More Profitable?

| Dimension | Temu (Neutral) | Shein (Neutral) | Verdict |
|------|------------|-------------|------|
| 2031 GMV | $180-200 billion | $110-130 billion | **Temu is bigger** |
| 2031 revenue | $31-36 billion | $75-85 billion | **Shein has higher revenue** (under the 1P model, revenue = GMV) |
| 2031 net profit | $2.0-2.8 billion | $3.5-4.5 billion | **Shein is more profitable** |
| Net margin | 6.5-8% | 4.5-5.5% | **Temu has the higher margin** (pure platform model) |
| User scale | 600-800 million MAU | 300-400 million MAU | **Temu has more users** (all categories) |

**Core conclusions:**
- **On GMV: Temu wins.** As an all-category platform, Temu's ceiling is far higher than Shein's fashion-focused category
- **On profit: Shein is more profitable.** Shein's private-label brand and flexible supply chain deliver a 60% gross margin — something Temu's pure platform model struggles to match
- **But Temu's margin is higher** — because Temu runs a platform model, and once it crosses the breakeven point, marginal profitability rises quickly

### 6.2 Growth Ceiling

| Dimension | Temu | Shein |
|------|------|-------|
| **Category ceiling** | Extremely high — all categories, from general merchandise to appliances to food | Medium — apparel and footwear at its core, expanding into beauty and home goods |
| **Geographic ceiling** | High — already in 70+ countries, with strong emerging-market potential | Medium-high — 150+ countries/regions, but depth lags breadth |
| **Model ceiling** | Medium — the platform model's take rate has a cap (benchmarked against Amazon at ~15%, Pinduoduo's domestic business at ~5%) | Medium-high — hybrid 1P+3P model, with room for brand premium |
| **Policy ceiling** | High risk — heavily dependent on direct shipping out of China's supply chain, policy-sensitive | Medium risk — building out local supply chains (Turkey, Brazil), which can partly hedge exposure |
| **Ultimate scale estimate** | GMV of $300-500 billion (benchmarked against Amazon's third-party GMV) | GMV of $200-300 billion (benchmarked against the combined online share of Inditex, H&M, and Uniqlo) |

| Dimension | Temu (via PDD Holdings) | Shein (post-IPO or private market) |

## VIII. Final Judgment

**If you could only invest in one:**

- **For certainty -> invest indirectly in Temu via PDD Holdings.** PDD currently trades at roughly 10x PE; even if Temu only achieves the neutral scenario, PDD's overall (domestic + Temu) profit growth can support a 15-20% annualized return. The key is that Pinduoduo's domestic business provides a profit cushion.

- **For higher upside -> wait for the Shein IPO.** If it prices at a $30 billion valuation and 2031 net profit reaches $4.0 billion — implying 7.5x PE at entry — and the market later assigns a 15-20x PE multiple, market cap could reach $60-80 billion, doubling over five years. But the uncertainty is extremely high.

**Ranking of core variables (applicable to both):**
1. The direction of China-US tariffs (the decisive factor)
2. How strictly the EU's cross-border e-commerce regulations are implemented
3. The pace of advertising monetization / take-rate improvement
4. The degree to which growth materializes in emerging markets (Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East)
5. Progress on building out localized supply chains
