## I. Podcast Content Summary

### Show Background

This is Episode 16 of Luo Yonghao's in-depth podcast *Crossroads*. The guest, Chen Mian, is the founder of LiblibAI / Lovart. Born in 1992, he spent ten years collecting stints at major tech companies including Tencent, 360, Didi, Mobike, Missfresh, and ByteDance, becoming the youngest-ever Product 4-1 (equivalent to Alibaba's P9 level) in ByteDance's history at age 28, before quitting in 2023 to start an AI company.

The title "Let Me Raise Hell, Then Quietly Slip Away" hints at Chen Mian's entrepreneurial posture — restless with the status quo, willing to bet big on major trends, and ready to move on once the job is done.

### Chapter Structure and Core Content

**Part One: Formative Years (00:03 - 00:38)**

- Born in Chongqing; frequent moves during childhood; a personality marked by a strong "will to win"
- Attended Southeast University; attempted a startup during college that failed
- These early experiences shaped his underlying character of "not afraid to lose, willing to take risks"

**Part Two: Ten Years of "Collecting Dragon Balls" at Major Tech Companies (00:38 - 01:33)**

Chen Mian defines working for others as "preparation for entrepreneurship" — every stint was a deliberate learning exercise:

| Company | Period | Role / Takeaway |
|------|--------|----------|
| Tencent | Early career | Introduction to product methodology |
| 360 | Early career | Internet security sector |
| Mobike | 2016-2018 (estimate) | Head of Product, lived through the bike-sharing wars firsthand |
| Didi | Brief stint | Mobility sector experience |
| Missfresh | Brief stint | Fresh-produce e-commerce operations |
| ByteDance | 2019-2023 (estimate) | Global commercialization lead for CapCut (Jianying), promoted to 4-1 at age 28 |

**Key reflections**:
- The Mobike and Missfresh experiences made him realize: "Some business models simply don't work. However sexy a business model looks, if the unit economics don't add up, it will end up nothing more than a servant of capital."
- His time at ByteDance was his career peak. He personally built out the commercialization of CapCut (Jianying), but ran into difficulties after the "double reduction" policy took effect.

**Part Three: Brutal Competition and Preparing to Start a Business (01:18 - 02:06)**

- In early 2023, when GPT-3.5 took off, Chen Mian judged that AI would bring revolutionary opportunities
- Core judgment: "Applying past experience is completely useless in the AI era" and "the AI revolution will first hit the B2B professional creative field rather than C2C entertainment"
- Why he chose the image-design sector: Stable Diffusion became usable in 2022; the design industry has "high demand, low supply, and high cost," and AI could boost efficiency by 10-100x
- Focused on the "gap market" — vertical segments where big companies are unwilling to commit heavy resources but where user value is clear
- Formally founded LiblibAI in May 2023, and secured angel-round funding as early as July (GSR Ventures, Source Code Capital, and Banyan Capital, $3.5 million, at a $15 million valuation)

**Darkest hour**:
- In September 2023, the product was forced offline for failing to complete large-model registration/filing
- Early-stage subsidy wars burned through more than $3 million
- **In March 2024, the company had only 4,000 yuan left in the bank**
- Every investor backed away — citing regulatory risk, an unclear business model, and fierce market competition
- Chen Mian met three to four rounds of investors every day, cutting the team while hunting for money at the same time
- Ultimately secured life-saving strategic investment from an internet company and survived

**Part Four: Lovart and Achievements (02:06 - 03:04)**

- Strategic pivot: extended from LiblibAI (an AI image community) into Lovart (a design-agent product)
- Lovart's core philosophy: **"democratizing design"** — enabling people with no design skills to produce professional-grade designs
- Product innovation: the ChatCanvas interaction tool — "conversing with AI directly on the canvas" rather than traditional prompt input
- Lovart Beta launched in May 2025; **over 100,000 people joined the waitlist within 5 days**, and Elon Musk publicly praised it
- Named "Best AI Application of the Year in Graphics and Imaging"
- Fundraising accelerated: closed a $130 million Series B in October 2025 (led by HongShan (Sequoia China) and CMC Capital), at a valuation of about $1 billion

**Part Five: Industry Observations and Future Outlook (03:04 - 03:43)**

- Chen Mian and Luo Yonghao debated the concept of "AI native"
- His view of the industry: "People who understand AI can't create, and people who can create don't use AI well" — that gap is the opportunity
- Creators need three things: the best tools, a sustainable incentive system, and a community ecosystem that fosters belonging
- The ultimate risk discussed: AI technology iterates extremely fast, and a product's moat could be washed away at any time
- Goal: to become "the Adobe of the AI era," a world-class AI application company
- Recommended the guest for the next episode

## III. Character Analysis: What Kind of Person Is Chen Mian

### 1. Extremely Strong Pattern Recognition

Chen Mian is not someone "loyal to a company," but someone "loyal to learning." Over ten years he changed jobs six times, deliberately absorbing the methodologies of companies at different stages and of different types each time. He explicitly defined working for others as a "preparation period for entrepreneurship," which shows he had an extremely strong sense of purpose from the very start.

The failures at Mobike and Missfresh were not wasted — he distilled from them the iron law that "a business model whose unit economics don't work will eventually collapse." This insight directly shaped his later choice of the AI tools sector (high gross margin, scalable) over a cash-burning subsidy model.

### 2. Precise Timing Judgment, Willing to Go All-In

In early 2023, when GPT was taking off and a great many people were sitting on the sidelines, Chen Mian went straight ahead and quit his 4-1 executive position at ByteDance (with an annual salary most likely in the 2-4 million yuan range). His judgment was not based on a vague feeling that "AI is hot," but on a concrete assessment of a technological inflection point: Stable Diffusion had become usable in 2022, and the design industry had a clear efficiency gap.

### 3. Extremely Strong Survival Instincts

Surviving with only 4,000 yuan left in the bank was not luck. His approach: shrink the team (cutting unnecessary headcount), meet investors intensively every day (high-intensity execution), and find strategic investment to keep the lights on (flexible and pragmatic). This shows he has both an entrepreneur's resilience and a businessman's pragmatism — he wouldn't stubbornly hold on just to save face.

### 4. Sharp Product Instincts

From leading CapCut's commercialization to building AI design tools, Chen Mian has always stayed on the "creator tools" thread. His core insight — "people who understand AI can't create, and people who can create don't use AI well" — directly defines the product's value proposition. The interaction innovation of ChatCanvas, "conversing with AI on the canvas," also shows that rather than simply applying existing paradigms, he is rethinking human-computer interaction from first principles.

### 5. A Global Perspective

Choosing to prioritize overseas markets (Lovart) sidestepped domestic red-ocean competition and targeted the global designer community directly. Elon Musk's praise is fundamentally proof of product strength rather than a marketing outcome. His statement that "agent products should be global by nature" is a view not commonly held among Chinese AI entrepreneurs.

### 6. Risks to Watch

- **Moat depth is questionable**: Technology in the AI tools sector iterates extremely fast, and today's lead could be matched tomorrow. Is Lovart's core barrier its community ecosystem or its product experience? If it is the latter, big companies catching up is only a matter of time.
- **Management experience remains unproven**: Changing companies six times over ten years suggests he is better at the "0 to 1" sprint than at "1 to 100" long-term operations.
- **Valuation pressure**: The revenue scale and growth expectations implied by the $1 billion valuation still need ongoing validation.

### Core Portrait

> Chen Mian is a new-generation AI entrepreneur who is **extremely purposeful, an extremely fast learner, and remarkably resilient in survival.** He is not a technical-genius founder, but a hands-on operator combining **business instinct, product ability, and execution** in one. His ten years at major tech companies were not just resume padding, but a deliberate accumulation of capability. His biggest test: whether he can evolve from a "blitzscaling entrepreneur" into a "long-term-oriented operator."
