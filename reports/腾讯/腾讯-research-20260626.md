## Step Two: Business Nature Analysis — Duan Yongping's "Right Business"

### One-Sentence Definition

**Tencent is the "operating system" of China's digital life — through WeChat's super-portal serving 1.4 billion users, it connects social networking, payments, gaming, advertising, business services, and AI, with flywheel effects compounding at every layer.**

### Business Model Canvas

| Dimension | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Revenue type | Composite: in-game purchases (recurring) + advertising (recurring) + payment commissions (transaction take-rate) + cloud subscriptions |
| Customer relationship | 1.43 billion MAU deeply embedded in daily life — not "users" but "infrastructure dependents" |
| Monetization efficiency | ARPU is far below Meta's and ByteDance's, implying substantial room for monetization upside |
| Core resources | WeChat's social graph (irreplicable) + gaming IP portfolio (Honor of Kings, League of Legends, VALORANT) + a 4.3-million-strong mini-program ecosystem |

### Gross Margin vs. Peers

| Company | Gross margin (FY2025) | Note |
|------|-----------------|------|
| Tencent | 56% | Up steadily from 43% in 2022 — structural improvement |
| Meta | ~81% | Pure advertising model, structurally higher margin |
| NetEase | ~63% | Higher games mix |
| Alibaba | ~38% | E-commerce + cloud; hardware costs drag the margin down |

Tencent's gross margin rose from 43% in 2022 to 56% in 2025, a 13-percentage-point improvement over three years. Drivers: (1) a growing mix of high-margin games and advertising; (2) cost efficiencies in the FinTech business; (3) shrinkage of low-margin content businesses (such as livestreaming).

### Operating Leverage

Non-IFRS operating margin rose from 26% in 2022 to 37% in 2025. Over the same period, revenue grew 35% while Non-IFRS net profit grew 125% — profit growth was 3.6 times revenue growth, demonstrating powerful operating leverage.

### Duan Yongping-Style Question

> **What makes this a good business? If you could describe it in one sentence, what would it be?**
>
> 1.4 billion people cannot live without WeChat, and every interaction on WeChat — chatting, paying, hailing a ride, ordering food, watching video, playing games — generates revenue. This is a "tax-collector" business: WeChat is the infrastructure of the digital world, and every participant is effectively "paying tax" to Tencent.

## Step Three: Moat Assessment — Buffett's "Economic Moat"

### Five Moat Types Verified One by One

| Moat type | Strength | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Network effects** | ★★★★★ | WeChat has 1.43 billion MAU with a social graph that cannot be migrated. Its 4.3 million mini-programs form a self-reinforcing loop — merchants join to acquire customers, more categories increase user stickiness, and more merchants keep joining. This is one of the strongest network effects anywhere in global consumer tech |
| **Switching costs** | ★★★★★ | The highest among global consumer tech products. Leaving WeChat means giving up your entire social network, payment habits, mini-program data, and Enterprise WeChat work communications. This is an unsolvable collective-action problem — you cannot leave alone |
| **Brand / pricing power** | ★★★★☆ | Its gaming IP portfolio (Honor of Kings, evergreen for 10 years and still growing, ranked No. 1 in global mobile game revenue in FY2025). Advertising pricing power is more constrained — ad load is far below ByteDance's, leaving room to raise prices that has not yet been fully exercised |
| **Scale effects** | ★★★★☆ | R&D expenses are spread across 1.4 billion users, with marginal cost approaching zero. Game distribution is close to a duopoly — developers must go through Tencent or NetEase to reach China's 1 billion consumers, and Tencent typically captures up to 70% of distribution revenue |
| **Technology barriers** | ★★★☆☆ | Its AI model capability is not the leader (Hunyuan ranks second in China, behind ByteDance's Doubao). But its distribution capability is unmatched — WeChat is the world's largest deployment platform for AI agents |

**Morningstar rating**: Wide Moat, based on network effects.

### Moat Trend: Widening or Narrowing Over the Past 5 Years?

**Narrowing factors**:
- Antitrust enforcement forced Tencent Music to give up exclusive licensing rights
- WeChat was required to open up links (Taobao and Douyin can now jump directly into WeChat)
- ByteDance's rise proves that a global-class consumer product can succeed without relying on the Tencent platform
- miHoYo's Genshin Impact proves that global success is achievable without Tencent's distribution

**Widening factors**:
- Channels (Video Accounts) has opened a new monetization layer on top of the existing 1.4-billion-user base, without needing a new app to compete with Douyin
- Mini-programs expanded from 3.2 million to 4.3 million, with GMV exceeding RMB 2 trillion
- Enterprise WeChat (WeCom) has 65 million enterprise users plus AI agents, adding new enterprise-side switching costs
- International games revenue exceeded USD 10 billion for the first time, reducing reliance on any single market
- AI integration (Yuanbao, the WeChat "QClaw" AI agent) is adding a new layer of ecosystem stickiness

**Overall assessment**: Over the past five years the moat has been broadly stable, with a slight widening. Specific moats have weakened (exclusive licensing, walled gardens), but these have been offset by deepening of the WeChat ecosystem and international diversification.

### Buffett-Style Question

> **Will this moat still be there in 10 years? What could destroy it?**
>
> WeChat's social-graph moat is extremely hard to destroy — it would require 1.4 billion people to migrate to another platform simultaneously, something that has never happened in the history of consumer tech (even WhatsApp/Telegram have not been able to unseat the dominant messaging app in their respective markets).
>
> **The most likely threat path**: not "another WeChat," but a paradigm shift toward AI-native interaction — if future AI assistants no longer need an app as the entry point (an evolved version of Siri/Alexa), WeChat's gateway value could be eroded. But Tencent is proactively addressing this threat via QClaw (a WeChat AI agent), transforming WeChat from an "app gateway" into an "AI agent gateway."

### Civilizational-Level Trend Assessment

**Trends where Tencent is on the right side of history**:

1. **AI agents + super apps**: WeChat is shifting from a "social gateway" to a "smart task hub." QClaw lets users execute cross-app services across 4.3 million mini-programs through natural language. **Uncertain trends**:

4. **AI model competition**: Tencent is not a leader in foundation models (behind ByteDance and Alibaba). Whether the "distribution-first" strategy pays off depends on whether model capability commoditizes quickly — if it does, Tencent's distribution advantage becomes hugely valuable; if model differentiation continues to matter, Tencent could be at a disadvantage

**Tencent most resembles a hybrid of "Microsoft + Berkshire Hathaway"**:

- An investment holding company (827 portfolio companies, an investment portfolio worth roughly RMB 1.04 trillion, or approximately USD 144 billion)
- But more growth-oriented than Berkshire — the core business itself is growing rapidly

**Compared with Meta**: Tencent's revenue is far more diversified than Meta's (advertising is only 20% of revenue versus 98% at Meta), yet Tencent trades at roughly half of Meta's valuation (PE 14.6x vs. ~25-28x).
