## An Anomalous Curve

From 2021 to 2025, Chinese internet companies went through their toughest four years — the regulatory storm, the "double reduction" education crackdown, the freeze on game licenses, weak macro consumption... margins at almost every internet giant were falling.

But Tencent's gross margin traced out a curve running **in the opposite direction**:

| Year | Gross margin |
|------|--------|
| 2021 | 42% |
| 2022 | 43% |
| 2023 | 48% |
| 2024 | 53% |
| 2025 | **56% (an all-time high)** |

**Up from 42% to 56% in four years** — a 14 percentage point improvement. This wasn't the product of a macro tailwind; it was achieved precisely during a period of macro headwinds.

A rising gross margin is never an accident — it is one of the most important signals of genuine improvement in a company's operating quality.

In this episode we use the classic value-investing method of "three statements plus one test" to take Tencent's financial statements apart.

## I. The Income Statement: The Story Behind a 56% Gross Margin

### Revenue mix (full year 2025)

| Segment | Revenue (RMB 100M) | Share | YoY growth | Gross margin (estimate) |
|------|-------------|------|---------|--------------|
| Value-added services (games + social subscriptions) | 3,693 | 49% | +16% | **60-65%** |
| Online advertising | 1,450 | 19% | **+19%** | **55-60%** |
| Fintech and business services | 2,294 | 31% | +8% | **30-35%** |
| Other | 81 | 1% | — | — |
| **Total** | **7,518** | 100% | **+14%** | **56%** |

Note: RMB 100M = 100 million yuan. So total revenue was 751.8 billion yuan.

Why did gross margin rise so much? Three reasons:

### Reason 1: High-margin businesses (games, advertising) are growing faster

In 2025, games grew +22% and advertising grew +19% — both segments carry gross margins of around 60%.
Meanwhile the low-margin business (fintech and business services) grew only +8%, with a gross margin of just 30-35%.

**Structurally, Tencent's revenue mix is shifting toward higher-margin businesses.**

### Reason 2: AI-driven efficiency gains in advertising

The advertising business grew +19% in 2025, with gross margin up 3-5 percentage points — driven mainly by:
- Miaosi AIGC sharply cutting advertisers' creative production costs
- Release of Video Accounts (Weixin Channels) ad inventory
- AI-optimized ad delivery lifting ROAS

Every 1 percentage point improvement in advertising gross margin adds roughly 1.5 billion yuan to Tencent's profit.

### Reason 3: The "evergreen effect" in gaming

The marginal cost of the 14 evergreen games is extremely low — the fixed costs of servers, operations, and R&D have already been largely amortized, so the marginal profit from each additional paying user is very high.

Honor of Kings is now 11 years old, and its annual "maintenance development cost" is far below what it was in its early years.

## II. The Income Statement: Non-IFRS Net Profit Is the "Gold Standard" for Core Operations

Tencent discloses two net profit figures every quarter:

- **GAAP net profit** (IFRS basis)
- **Non-IFRS net profit** (basis that excludes one-off items)

Where does the difference come from? Mainly these items:

| Item | Deducted under GAAP? | Deducted under Non-IFRS? |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Core operating profit | Yes, fully included | Yes, fully included |
| Consolidated subsidiary profit | Yes, fully included | Yes, fully included |
| Fair-value changes on investments (e.g., Pinduoduo share price swings) | Yes, included | **No, excluded** |
| Investment impairment losses | Yes, included | **No, excluded** |
| Gains on disposal of investments (profit from selling stakes) | Yes, included | **No, excluded** |
| Share-based compensation expense | Yes, included | **No, excluded** |

### Why is Non-IFRS the better lens for core operations?

An example: in 2024, the share price of Pinduoduo, which Tencent holds, rose, adding 20 billion yuan to the book fair value. That 20 billion flows into GAAP net profit, but **it wasn't earned by Tencent's core business — it is paper wealth handed out by market sentiment**.

If you use GAAP net profit to compute a P/E ratio, Tencent will look "very profitable" — but this kind of "profit" can vanish the following year (if Pinduoduo's share price falls).

**Non-IFRS strips out this volatility, showing the true state of operations.**

### Tencent's Non-IFRS net profit over the past 5 years

| Year | Non-IFRS net profit (RMB 100M) | YoY |
|------|----------------------|------|
| 2021 | 1,237 | +1% |
| 2022 | 1,156 | -7% |
| 2023 | 1,577 | +36% |
| 2024 | 2,227 | +41% |
| 2025 | **2,596** | **+17%** |

**From 2022 to 2025, Non-IFRS net profit rose from 115.6 billion to 259.6 billion yuan — more than doubling in 3 years.**

This isn't an industry tailwind; it's the result of a sustained improvement in the company's operating quality.

## III. Non-IFRS EPS: Compounding Accelerated by Buybacks

EPS (earnings per share) is one of the most central metrics for measuring company value in value investing — it corresponds directly to how much each shareholder gets to claim.

Tencent's EPS has grown faster than its net profit — because **buybacks keep cancelling share capital**.

| Year | Non-IFRS net profit (RMB 100M) | Total shares outstanding at year-end (100M shares) | Non-IFRS EPS (RMB) |
|------|---------------------|--------------|------------------|
| 2021 | 1,237 | 96.0 | 12.9 |
| 2022 | 1,156 | 96.0 | 12.0 |
| 2023 | 1,577 | 95.0 | 16.6 |
| 2024 | 2,227 | 92.5 | 24.1 |
| 2025 | 2,596 | 90.8 | **28.6** |

Over 5 years, Non-IFRS EPS rose from **12.9 to 28.6 yuan — a CAGR of 22%**.

Total shares outstanding fell from 9.6 billion to 9.08 billion shares by the end of 2025 — **a net cancellation of 5.4% of shares outstanding over 5 years**.

> **An important detail — share count is not monotonically declining**: as of May 2026, Tencent's total shares outstanding have actually **rebounded to approximately 9.126 billion shares** from 9.08 billion at the end of 2025 — this is because a new batch of share-based compensation (SBC) grants in early 2026 issued about 45 million new shares, which **temporarily outpaced the concurrent buyback rate**. SBC issuance tends to be concentrated in the beginning/middle of the year, while buybacks proceed at a steady pace through the year, so the quarterly time series of share count will show +/- fluctuations. But looked at over the full year, Tencent has posted net cancellations in each of the past 4 years.

If Tencent sustains buybacks of HKD 80-100 billion per year for 10 years, the **gross buyback** could cancel 16-22% of shares outstanding — but after netting out new shares issued annually through equity incentives (estimated at roughly 0.5-1% of shares outstanding per year based on the recent pace), the **net cancellation** is more likely to fall in the **10-15% range**.

**What does a net cancellation of 10-15% mean? Tencent shareholders who do nothing but hold their shares will quietly see their per-share equity stake rise** — this is the "passive compounding" that buybacks deliver. **This point should be assessed using the net figure — don't be misled by the scale of gross buybacks.**

## IV. The Balance Sheet: Financial Discipline Behind RMB 107.1 Billion in Net Cash

### Key figures (year-end 2025)

| Item | Figure |
|------|------|
| Total assets | approximately RMB 1.6 trillion |
| **Net cash** | **RMB 107.1 billion** (+311% YoY) |
| Investment portfolio (fair value + associates/joint ventures) | **RMB 1,015.8 billion** |
| Total liabilities | approximately RMB 640 billion |
| Debt-to-assets ratio | approximately 40% |

### Why is "net cash" a core metric?

Net cash = cash + short-term investments - interest-bearing debt.

This figure tells you: **after paying off all interest-bearing debt, how much money the company has left on the books to deploy freely.**

Tencent's net cash rose from RMB 26 billion in 2024 to **RMB 107.1 billion** in 2025 — **an increase of 311% in a single year** (basis: cash + short-term investments - interest-bearing debt).

The increase came from three drivers stacking together: (1) operating cash flow of RMB 303.1 billion (+17%) continuing to flow in; (2) some interest-bearing debt maturing and being paid down without refinancing, shrinking the denominator; (3) some short-term investments being reclassified back into cash equivalents. AI investment is substantial (CAPEX +57%), yet FCF still grew +18%, so it hasn't eroded financial health.

**The financial significance of this figure is**: Tencent's "safety cushion" is very thick — even if its core business suddenly ran into trouble tomorrow, the company has ample cash to respond.

## V. Cash Flow: A "Money Printer" Generating RMB 182.6 Billion a Year

### 2025 cash flow picture

| Item | Amount (RMB 100M) | YoY |
|------|------------|------|
| Operating cash flow | 3,031 | +17% |
| Capital expenditure (CAPEX) | 792 | +57% (heavy AI investment) |
| Free cash flow (FCF) | **1,826** | **+18%** |

**What does RMB 182.6 billion in free cash flow amount to?**

- On average, Tencent generates 500 million yuan in free cash flow **every day**
- On average, 20.8 million yuan **every hour**
- On average, 350 thousand yuan **every minute**
- On average, 5,800 yuan **every second**

**Note a counterintuitive fact**: Tencent's capital expenditure grew sharply by +57% in 2025 (to RMB 79.2 billion, mainly AI infrastructure), yet free cash flow still grew +18%.

This shows that: **the growth rate of cash flow from Tencent's core business exceeds the growth rate of AI investment costs.** Spending on AI hasn't damaged the fundamentals — it's being layered on top of growth, and accelerating it.

### FCF Yield (free cash flow return)

- 2025 FCF: RMB 182.6 billion ≈ HKD 208.0 billion
- Current market cap: approximately HKD 4.29 trillion (9.126 billion shares x HKD 470)
- **FCF Yield ≈ 4.85%**

What does that mean? **At the current share price, Tencent is effectively returning about 4.85% of its market cap in actual cash flow each year to shareholders (if all of it were distributed as dividends).**

Compared with a risk-free rate of about 3%, **Tencent's FCF Yield is already meaningfully above the risk-free rate** — a classic value-investing buy signal.

## VI. RMB 121.0 Billion in Shareholder Returns: Assessed by the "One-Dollar Test"

Value investing has a classic standard for judging whether a company "should pay dividends / buy back shares" — the **one-dollar test**: for every dollar of profit a company retains, it should create at least one dollar of market value; otherwise, that dollar should be returned to shareholders.

Tencent's 2025 shareholder return figures:

| Item | Amount |
|------|------|
| Buyback amount (HKD) | HKD 80 billion |
| Dividend amount (HKD) | HKD 41 billion |
| **Total shareholder return (HKD)** | **HKD 121.0 billion** |
| Share of current market cap | **~2.8%** |

### The "opportunistic" character of Tencent's buybacks

Value investing also has a hard-and-fast rule about buybacks — **only do them under two conditions**: (1) the company has surplus capital, and (2) the share price is clearly below a conservative estimate of intrinsic value.

In other words: **"buy back more at low prices, buy back less at high prices" — this is the key test of management's capital allocation discipline.**

Tencent's buyback history:

| Year | Buyback amount (HKD) | Average buyback price (estimate) | Assessment |
|------|----------------|----------------|------|
| 2021 | HKD 2.6 billion | ~HKD 466 | Relatively small scale |
| **2022** | **HKD 33.8 billion** | **~HKD 316** | **Heavy buying at low prices** |
| **2023** | **HKD 49.4 billion** | **~HKD 332** | **Continued buying at low prices** |
| 2024 | HKD 112.0 billion | ~HKD 365 | Stepped up the pace |
| 2025 | HKD 80 billion | ~HKD 523 | Price level higher, but persisted |

Note that when the share price fell to HKD 316 in 2022, Tencent bought back a massive HKD 33.8 billion. **This is a textbook case of "opportunistic buybacks"** — buying heavily when the share price has been panic-driven to a low point.

The data confirms this: the correlation coefficient between Tencent's buyback amount and its P/E ratio is approximately **-0.65** — **the harder the drop, the harder the buying.**

This kind of discipline is **rare among Chinese listed companies.**

## VII. ROE: From 30% Down to 17% — Good News or Bad News?

ROE (return on equity) is the core metric for measuring how efficiently a company "makes money with shareholders' capital." Tencent's ROE has been declining in recent years:

| Year | ROE |
|------|-----|
| 2018 | ~30% |
| 2021 | ~25% |
| 2024 | ~20% |
| 2025 | ~24% (normalized) |

The decline in ROE is driven mainly by **the expansion of net assets**; breaking down the sources of the growing denominator, roughly:

- **Continued appreciation of the investment portfolio's fair value** (Pinduoduo, Spotify, Sea, UMG, etc. have risen sharply in recent years): accounts for roughly 40-50% of the increase in net assets
- **Accumulation of undistributed retained earnings** (free cash flow has stayed above RMB 180 billion; buybacks offset part of this but tens of billions are still retained net each year): accounts for roughly 30-40% of the increase
- **Other comprehensive income** (currency translation, foreign-currency investment revaluation, etc.): accounts for roughly 10-20% of the increase

Is a falling ROE a bad thing? It depends on your perspective:

- **In absolute terms**: 24% still far exceeds the traditional value-investing ROE threshold of 15%
- **In relative terms**: the drop from 30% to 24% is a genuine regression, reflecting the industry-wide decline in returns
- **From a structural standpoint**: the "denominator inflation" from investment-portfolio appreciation is essentially asset accumulation, not operational deterioration — but this portion of the ROE contribution from appreciation won't recur (high base effect), and future ROE will most likely stabilize in the 20-25% range

The decline in ROE is a Tencent financial metric worth continuing to monitor, but its absolute level remains excellent.

| # | Check item | Pass? | Notes |

## X. A Financial Portrait of "Hard Metrics All Pass, Soft Metrics Bear Watching"

Putting all the metrics together, Tencent's financial portrait looks like this:

```
Pass Gross margin: 56% (an all-time high)
Pass Non-IFRS net profit: RMB 259.6 billion (+17%)
Pass Non-IFRS EPS: RMB 28.6 (5-year CAGR 22%)
Pass Free cash flow: RMB 182.6 billion (+18%)
Pass Net cash: RMB 107.1 billion (+311% YoY)
Pass ROE: 24% (normalized)
Pass FCF Yield: 4.85% (above the 3% risk-free rate)
Pass Shareholder return: HKD 121.0 billion (2.8% of market cap)
Pass P/E: 14.5x (10-year median 27x, a discount of about 46%)
Pass RMB 107.1 billion net cash + RMB 1.04 trillion investment portfolio = a 22% hard-asset safety cushion
Watch ROE down from 30% to 24% (denominator inflation from investment-portfolio appreciation; expected to normalize to a 20-25% range)
Watch AI capex sharply higher (2025 +57%); ROIC needs 2-3 years to be validated
Watch Non-IFRS net profit growth slowing from +41% to +17%, entering a normalized growth range
```

On the hard metrics, **this is a financial report showing strong profitability, strong cash flow, a strong balance sheet, strong capital-allocation discipline, and reasonable valuation.** On the soft metrics, the ROE decline, the return on AI investment, and the normalization of net profit growth are all items that need continued tracking.

Under the classic value-investing framework, hard metrics all passing while soft metrics remain to be watched is a fairly positive portrait — but not an unconditional "safe house."

## XI. Compressing the Financial Report Into Three Sentences

We've covered 10 sections of financial detail. If we had to compress Tencent's financial report into three sentences, these are the three that matter most:

1. **Is the business good?** — Yes. Gross margin hit a record 56%, FCF of RMB 182.6 billion keeps growing, and the moat is undamaged.
2. **Is management good?** — Yes. HKD 121.0 billion in shareholder returns, opportunistic buybacks, and a 5-year commitment fulfillment rate near 100%.
3. **Is the price expensive?** — Reasonable. Non-IFRS P/E of 14.5x, FCF Yield of 4.85%, a 46% discount to the 10-year median.

All three sentences pass — this is the simplest yet hardest judgment in value investing. It's simple because there are only three sentences; it's hard because most companies fail to clear all three hurdles. Tencent cleared them.
