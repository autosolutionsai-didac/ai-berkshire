## A fact that should make you reconsider Tencent

Over the past 10 years, Tencent's share price has traced a curve that makes you question your own judgment:

- Surged to **HK$475** in early 2018, mocked for "rising too much";
- Crashed to **HK$252** by late 2018, mocked as "gaming is finished";
- Soared to **HK$775** in early 2021, hailed as "China's FAANG";
- Crashed to **HK$199** in October 2022, decried as "the end of the internet era";
- Climbed back above **HK$400** in 2024, lamented as "I should have bought the bottom";
- Fell again to **HK$467** in April 2026, blamed for "falling behind ByteDance in AI."

**The same company has been re-categorized by the market at least 5 times in 10 years. And nearly every single time, the market turned out to be wrong in hindsight.**

Tencent isn't an under-researched company — it's one of the most thoroughly covered Chinese companies in the world, with dozens of sell-side reports every quarter. But more research doesn't equal genuine understanding. This company is so badly misunderstood precisely because it's **genuinely too complex** — a 5,000-word sell-side report can't hold all of it, and forcing it in leaves only a binary "bullish/bearish" conclusion.

So what this series sets out to do is **peel Tencent apart layer by layer**, so you can see its true structure.

## Three common misconceptions about Tencent

Before we start peeling, let's debunk the three most widespread misconceptions.

### Misconception one: "Tencent is a social-media company"

This is the most intuitive impression — and also the least accurate.

Open the 2025 annual report, and Tencent's RMB 751.8 billion revenue breaks down like this:

| Segment | Revenue (RMB billion) | Share |
|---------|------------------------|-------|
| Value-added services (gaming + social subscriptions) | 369.3 | 49% |
| Fintech and business services | 229.4 | 31% |
| Online advertising | 145.0 | 19% |
| Other | 8.1 | 1% |

Within that revenue mix, purely "social" products (music/video memberships) contribute only 17%. **Tencent's real business uses the social relationship graph as its foundation, and builds four towers on top of it: gaming, advertising, payments, and cloud services.**

And the real money is made by the towers, not the foundation.

### Misconception two: "Tencent is a gaming company"

Gaming contributed RMB 241.6 billion in revenue in 2025 with a 60-70% gross margin — indeed Tencent's biggest profit engine. But if you treat Tencent purely as a "gaming company," you'll seriously underestimate **another company hiding on Tencent's balance sheet — a RMB 1.04 trillion external investment portfolio**.

One thing needs clarifying up front to avoid confusion: Tencent's equity holdings fall into two categories —

- **Consolidated subsidiaries** (e.g., Riot Games at 100%, Miniclip, China Literature) — their revenue and profit are already consolidated into Tencent's core financials, so when valuing the company **they should not be added again separately**, or that would be double-counting.
- **Non-consolidated external investments** (financial investments / associates) — these are the truly "additional" assets.

Tencent's financial statements show "financial assets at fair value + investments in associates/joint ventures" totaling approximately **RMB 1,015.8 billion** (about RMB 1.04 trillion, per Tencent's 2025 annual report), **counting only the second category**. Looking through to specific holdings:

- Pinduoduo: about **13-14%** (continually diluted by PDD's ESOP share issuances; the 2024 annual report cites 15.6%, while more recent data from channels like Futu put it around 13.76%), an equity stake worth about **$20 billion**
- ByteDance: Tencent is one of its early investors, **its exact stake is not publicly disclosed**; after being diluted across multiple funding rounds, industry estimates put it in the single-digit percentage range, so the equity value **is far smaller than the widely circulated $50 billion figure**
- Spotify: 9.1%, an equity stake worth about **$9.3 billion**
- Sea Limited: 18.7%, an equity stake worth about **$8.6 billion**
- Universal Music, WeBank, Epic Games (28%), Kuaishou, Supercell, Meituan, Krafton, and more...

**Buy one share of Tencent, and you effectively own a slice of the equity in all of the companies listed above.** Valuing Tencent as a pure gaming company means throwing away this RMB 1 trillion "external investment portfolio" for free — this is precisely the mathematical root cause of the market's long-standing valuation discount on Tencent.

(Note: the revenue and profit from Riot Games' 100%-owned League of Legends and Valorant franchises are already reflected in Tencent's "gaming" segment results, and are not part of the RMB 1 trillion "investment portfolio" figure above — that's a separate, additional layer of value, which Parts 03 and 04 will unpack respectively.)

### Misconception three: "Tencent has 'lost' the AI era"

This is the hottest narrative of 2026. The reasoning goes:

- Yuanbao (Tencent's AI assistant) has 57.35 million MAU (per QuestMobile and other third-party estimates, as of early 2026);
- Doubao (ByteDance's AI assistant) has 345 million MAU (same period, same methodology);
- **A roughly 6x gap in scale**.

The data is accurate, but the conclusion is debatable.

If you believe the AI era equals "a competition of AI chatbot-assistant MAU," then Tencent has indeed fallen behind. But if you believe the AI era equals "**whoever can turn AI into a multiplier for their existing businesses**," the conclusion flips entirely:

- Tencent's advertising business grew **+19%** in 2025 (per its annual report), with its AIGC tool "Miaosi" reaching a relatively high penetration rate among clients (third-party research estimates around 65%);
- WeChat Stores' GMV grew **+192%** YoY in 2024 (disclosed at the WeChat Open Class), and in 2025 Tencent shifted to disclosing structural metrics instead (branded-goods GMV growth at 4.3x the platform's overall growth rate, and monthly active-selling merchants up 1.7x — see Part 02 for details) — AI customer service, AI product selection, and AI translation are practically standard features now;
- Weixin Search's average daily search volume rose from 333 million to **over 1 billion** (per The Paper's reporting);
- Weixin Channels' ad monetization rate is significantly lower than Douyin's — third-party estimates put Tencent's side at roughly 1.8% versus Douyin's roughly 6-7% (note: monetization rate is an unofficial metric, and estimates vary widely across research sources), but even on a conservative estimate, **this still implies 2-3x room for improvement in Weixin Channels ad monetization**.

**Tencent isn't trying to build "China's ChatGPT" — Tencent's goal is "AI in everything"**: folding AI into every dish on the menu — WeChat, advertising, gaming, payments. Which approach ultimately wins out remains to be seen (see Part 05 for a detailed pros-and-cons breakdown), but judging Tencent purely by the ChatGPT yardstick misses its AI leverage within its existing businesses.

## Understanding Tencent requires wearing several pairs of glasses at once

If you had to define Tencent in one sentence, the closest version might be:

> **Tencent = an operating company that locks in 1.4 billion users through WeChat's social relationship graph + an investment fund worth roughly RMB 1 trillion + a cash-flow machine that prints RMB 230 billion a year.**

Each of these three things has been written about separately, but rarely has anyone evaluated them together. This is one common explanation for why the market often discounts Tencent's valuation — its current P/E is down roughly 45% from its 10-year median (about 27x), and valuation models aren't good at handling companies with "three stacked layers."

To truly understand this company, you need to wear at least these pairs of glasses simultaneously:

## A small teaser: is Tencent actually expensive right now?

Let's leave a hook and close with a set of numbers. These will be explained in detail in Parts 06 and 08 — for now, just remember the order of magnitude:

| Metric | 2025 figure |
|--------|-------------|
| Non-IFRS net profit | **RMB 259.6 billion** (+17%) |
| Non-IFRS EPS | ~RMB 28.6 / ~HK$32.5 |
| Free cash flow | **RMB 182.6 billion** (+18% even after heavy AI investment) |
| Shareholder returns (HK$80 billion buybacks + HK$41 billion dividends) | **HK$121 billion** |
| Net cash | **RMB 107.1 billion** |
| Gross margin | **56%** (up from 42% over 4 years, an all-time high) |
| Current share price (2026-05-04) | ~HK$470 |
| Current P/E (Non-IFRS basis) | **~14.5x** (HK$470 ÷ Non-IFRS EPS of HK$32.5) |
| Current P/E (IFRS/GAAP TTM basis) | **~17.3x** (real-time data via Futu) |
| 10-year median P/E | ~27x |
| Investment portfolio fair value | **~RMB 1,015.8 billion** |

> **Data sources**: Tencent's 2025 annual report (released 2026-03-18), HKEX disclosures.
> AI-related data (Miaosi penetration of 65%, Weixin Search daily volume of 1 billion+, Weixin Channels monetization rate of 1.8%, AI comic-drama figures, etc.) come from third-party research estimates (CBNData, Tech Planet, QuestMobile, etc.); figures vary somewhat across sources and should be taken only as an order-of-magnitude reference.

Put it all together, and the Tencent you see is:

- A company with an extremely deep moat and gross margin at an all-time high
- Currently valued at **45% below** its 10-year median
- Sitting on RMB 1 trillion in equity holdings
- Still returning HK$121 billion a year in cash via buybacks + dividends
- Yet with persistently bearish market sentiment, because the AI narrative has been seized by ByteDance

What do these facts add up to? **Different lenses give different answers.** That's exactly what the next 7 parts of this series will answer.
