## I. Valuation Method: SOTP (Sum-of-the-Parts)

Tencent isn't a single business — valuing it on a blended P/E blurs the differences between its parts. A more sensible approach is to break it apart:

**Core business (FY2025 annual report data):**

| Business | Revenue | YoY | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| Value-added services (games + social) | RMB 369.3 billion | +16% | Of which domestic games RMB 164.2 billion, overseas games RMB 77.4 billion |
| Advertising | RMB 145.0 billion | +19% | Video Accounts + Moments + Mini Programs + Search |
| FinTech and Business Services | RMB 229.4 billion | +8% | CSIG achieved scaled profitability in Q4 2025 |
| **Total revenue** | **RMB 751.8 billion** | **+14%** | |
| **Non-IFRS net profit** | **RMB 259.6 billion** | **+17%** | Gross margin 56% |
| **Free cash flow** | **RMB 182.6 billion** | | |

**Investment portfolio (unconsolidated, ~RMB 1.04 trillion):**
- Listed holdings: Pinduoduo (largest single holding, equity value ~USD 20 billion), Sea Limited (18.7%), Kuaishou (15.8%), Spotify, Universal Music, and others
- Unlisted holdings: ByteDance, Epic Games, Supercell, WeBank, and others
- Note: Meituan was spun off via dividend distribution down to ~1.5% by end-2022; Riot Games is now consolidated and excluded from this figure
- Fair value of listed investments RMB 672.7 billion + book value of unlisted investments RMB 363.1 billion, together ~RMB 1.04 trillion

**Net cash:** ~RMB 107.1 billion (cash and equivalents minus interest-bearing debt)

The point of SOTP isn't to arrive at one precise number — it's to help you understand what you're actually buying when you buy Tencent. How much comes from the core business, how much from the investment portfolio, which parts are certain, and which aren't.

## II. Historical Valuation Percentile — A Rearview Mirror, Not a GPS

| Metric | Current | 10-Year Median | Historical Low (Oct 2022) |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| P/E (Non-IFRS) | ~14.5x | ~27x | ~10x |
| P/B | ~3.4x | ~5x | ~2.7x |
| FCF Yield | ~4.9% | ~2.5% | ~8% |

The current P/E sits at the low end of the past 10 years.

But this must be stressed: **the historical percentile is a rearview mirror**. A 10-year median of 27x doesn't mean the multiple will return to 27x in the future. Hong Kong equities' valuation center of gravity may have shifted structurally lower (geopolitics, changes in the investor base, liquidity discount). Anchoring on the historical median risks overestimating fair value.

What the historical percentile can tell you is this: at the current level of profit, the market is willing to pay a price for Tencent that sits toward the low end of its own history. That's all.

## III. What Really Determines Valuation Is Future Profit

Historical P/E is a rearview mirror. What actually determines your return after buying is: **how much money Tencent will make in the future.**

2025 Non-IFRS net profit was RMB 259.6 billion. The variables driving future profit:

- **Video Accounts**: current monetization rate is ~1.8%, versus ~6-7% for Douyin — a mathematical gap of 3-4x, but whether it's realized depends on how mature the e-commerce closed loop becomes
- **AI**: 2025 capital expenditure hit a record RMB 79.2 billion; its contribution to cloud revenue and group operating efficiency remains to be validated
- **Overseas games**: 2025 overseas game revenue broke USD 10 billion for the first time, up 33% YoY; whether this continues depends on the product cycle
- **Buybacks**: share count has shrunk 1-2% net per year for the past three years, directly boosting EPS
- **FinTech and Business Services**: CSIG achieved scaled profitability in Q4 2025, and profit contribution is starting to show through

Based on these variables, **here is a forecast range for future profit** (note: this is a forecast, not a fact — actual results may deviate):

| | Non-IFRS net profit 3 years out (2028) | Implied CAGR |
|---|---|---|
| Optimistic | ~RMB 395.0 billion | ~15% |
| Neutral | ~RMB 320.0 billion | ~7% |
| Conservative | ~RMB 280.0 billion | ~3% |

Current P/E is ~14.5x. Future returns depend on two things: where actual profit lands within this range, and what multiple the market is willing to pay at that time. Both are unknowns.

## IV. Red Lines

Re-evaluation is warranted if any of the following occur:

1. WeChat MAU declines quarter-over-quarter
2. AI-native applications materially cannibalize WeChat usage time
3. Pony Ma or Martin Lau departs abruptly with no clear successor
4. Gross margin persistently falls below 50%

A trigger doesn't automatically mean "sell," but it does mean the thesis for holding must be re-examined.

---

## V. Summary

- **SOTP view**: what you're buying is the core business (games + advertising + fintech) + a trillion-yuan-scale investment portfolio + net cash
- **Historical percentile**: current P/E sits at a 10-year low, but historical percentile is a rearview mirror and cannot be directly extrapolated
- **Core variable**: profit growth over the next 3-5 years determines everything. At today's 14.5x P/E, the market isn't pricing in much growth — if profit keeps growing, returns will come from profit growth itself plus potential valuation re-rating
- **Risk**: Hong Kong's valuation center of gravity may never revert to its historical median; geopolitics and AI competition are real sources of uncertainty

Valuation isn't about computing one precise answer to "what is it worth." It's about helping you understand how much you might make or lose under different future scenarios. Then you decide for yourself whether those odds are worth betting on.
