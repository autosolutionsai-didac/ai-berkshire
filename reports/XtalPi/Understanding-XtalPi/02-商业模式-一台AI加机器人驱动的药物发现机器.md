## Start with the Revenue Structure

If a company has only two business lines, understanding those two lines is equivalent to understanding the whole company. XtalPi has exactly this structure — **803 million yuan** in revenue, all coming from two directions:

| Business segment | FY2025 revenue (RMB millions) | FY2024 revenue (RMB millions) | YoY growth | Share |
|---------|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|

Two numbers deserve special attention:

1. **Drug Discovery's +419% growth** was mainly due to the one-time recognition of DoveTree's $51 million upfront payment (approximately **360 million RMB**). Excluding this payment, Drug Discovery revenue was approximately 180 million yuan, up roughly 74% YoY — still fast, but a completely different order of magnitude.

2. **AI4S's +63% growth** is XtalPi's "second curve" — extending its AI + robotics capabilities from pharma into new materials, new energy, and other fields. Representative customers include JinkoSolar (photovoltaics) and BASF (chemicals).

## What Exactly Are the Two Business Lines

### Line One: Drug Discovery Solutions (67% of revenue)

This is XtalPi's core business. In simple terms: **pharma companies outsource "drug hunting" to XtalPi.**

The pain point of traditional drug R&D is finding a needle in a haystack — from billions of possible molecules, finding the handful that can treat disease, become a viable drug, and be manufactured at scale. Traditional methods rely on chemists testing candidates one by one, taking **3-5 years** and costing hundreds of millions of dollars.

XtalPi's approach:

```
Step 1: An AI model screens out the few thousand most promising candidates from billions of molecules
Step 2: Automated robots run experimental validation on those few thousand molecules
Step 3: Experimental data feeds back into the AI model, and the model iterates to become more accurate
Step 4: Narrow the few thousand down to a handful of drug candidates, delivered to the customer
```

The core platform behind this closed loop is called **ID4** — integrating an AI model cluster, robotic labs, and a data system.

**The pricing model is upgrading**:

| Stage | Pricing method | Characteristics | XtalPi's current status |
|------|---------|------|------|
| 1.0 | Service fees (charged per project) | Similar to traditional CRO, one-off deals | Still present, but declining share |
| 2.0 | Upfront payment + milestone payments | Staged payments tied to drug R&D progress | DoveTree, Eli Lilly partnership model |
| 3.0 | Sales royalties | A cut of sales revenue after a drug launches | Not yet realized, but stipulated in contracts |

The DoveTree partnership is a textbook example of the 2.0 model: the $51 million upfront payment has been received, with up to **$5.89 billion** more contingent on whether the drug R&D succeeds.

**Here's a fundamental judgment**: if the pricing model can upgrade from 1.0 to 3.0 (sales royalties), XtalPi's revenue ceiling would leap from "service fees" to "a percentage of drug sales revenue" — an entirely different order of magnitude.

Counter-argument: upgrading the pricing model is contingent on the drug actually reaching the market. The historical success rate of drug R&D is below 10%. Until any partnered drug successfully launches, so-called "sales royalties" are paper wealth.

### Line Two: AI4S Smart Solutions (33% of revenue)

AI4S = AI for Science. This line is XtalPi extending its "AI + robotics for experiments" capability from pharma into other scientific fields.

| Application field | Representative customer | What it does |
|---------|---------|--------|
| New energy | JinkoSolar | AI-optimized photovoltaic cell materials |
| Chemicals | BASF | AI-accelerated new materials R&D |
| Agrochemicals | — | AI-assisted pesticide molecule design |
| Synthetic biology | — | AI-optimized biosynthesis pathways |

This line generated **265 million yuan** in FY2025 revenue, up 63%. That looks solid, but two points are worth noting:

1. **Revenue scale is still small** — 265 million yuan is negligible in any downstream industry
2. **Gross margin may be lower than Drug Discovery** — hardware (robotic workstations) makes up a larger share of costs

**Strategic significance outweighs short-term financial significance**: the value of this line lies in proving that the "AI + robotics" platform has cross-industry reusability. If it can open up one more major industry beyond pharma, the company's valuation logic would upgrade from "AI pharma company" to "science intelligence platform" — an entirely different narrative.

Counter-argument: companies that try to do everything often end up doing nothing well. Pharma itself hasn't fully proven out yet, and rushing to expand into new fields risks spreading management attention and R&D resources too thin.

## Where Does the 70% Gross Margin Come From

This is the key number for understanding XtalPi's business model.

| Comparison | Gross margin | Model |
|------|:--:|------|
| WuXi AppTec (traditional CRO leader) | **35-40%** | Selling headcount: hiring chemists to run experiments |
| Schrodinger (computational chemistry) | **~70%** | Selling software: computational simulation tools |
| XtalPi | **~70%** | AI + robotics: a compute + do + data closed loop |

**Data source**: each company's annual report, StockAnalysis

XtalPi's 70% gross margin can be broken down into two layers:

**Layer one: the marginal cost of an AI model approaches zero.** Once a model is trained, there's almost no cost difference between serving the 1st customer and the 100th. This is the same logic as software companies — R&D investment is a fixed cost, while usage carries near-zero marginal cost.

**Layer two: DoveTree's upfront payment inflated the base.** The $51 million upfront payment is "pure-profit-type revenue" — XtalPi doesn't need to invest much additional new cost for this money. Excluding the DoveTree contribution, the regular business's gross margin might be in the **50-60%** range (an estimate, not an official figure).

**An honest question**: is a 70% gross margin sustainable?

- If large deals continue to flow in (more DoveTree-level partnerships), gross margin can hold or even rise
- If large deals dry up and revenue reverts to the regular service-fee model, gross margin could fall back to 50-60%
- If AI4S's hardware business grows as a share of revenue, gross margin overall could be dragged down given robots' higher costs

## Operating Leverage: The Inflection Point from "Burning Cash" to "Profitable"

It took XtalPi a **full 10 years** to go from its founding in 2015 to its first profit in 2025.

| Year | Revenue (RMB millions) | Growth | Adjusted net profit (RMB millions) | Cumulative loss status |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|------|
| 2021 | 63 | — | Substantial loss | Still burning cash |
| 2022 | 133 | +111% | Substantial loss | Still burning cash |
| 2023 | 174 (estimate) | +31% | -522 | Cumulative losses deepening |
| 2024 | 266 | +53% | -457 | Losses narrowing |
| 2025 | 803 | **+201%** | **+258** | **First profit** |

**Data sources**: XtalPi Holdings' historical annual reports, Huxiu, Jiemian News

This table shows an operating-leverage effect: revenue grew 201%, but costs grew far less — because the fixed investments in AI models and robotic workstations were already made in prior years, so revenue growth flowed directly into profit.

**But note one fact**: if the 360 million yuan DoveTree upfront payment is excluded from FY2025 revenue, revenue is approximately 440 million yuan, up roughly 65% YoY. Based on this, whether the business excluding the large deal **can be independently profitable** remains an open question.

This is the key to understanding the substance of XtalPi's "first profit": **is it a structural inflection point of platform economics, or a brief burst of sunshine propped up by a one-off large deal? It will take another 2-3 quarters to tell.**

## Customer Structure: Pfizer in Front, Eli Lilly to the Left, DoveTree to the Right

XtalPi's customer roster is impressive:

> Of the world's top 20 pharma companies, **17** have partnered with XtalPi at some point.

But "having partnered" and "continuing to pay" are two different things. Here's who is actually paying big money:

| Customer | Deal value | Time | Nature |
|------|---------|------|------|
| DoveTree | Up to **$6 billion** | August 2025 | Multi-pipeline partnership, upfront payment received |
| Eli Lilly | **$345 million** (second deal) | November 2025 | AI bispecific antibody R&D |
| Eli Lilly | $250 million (first deal) | 2023 | AI small-molecule drug partnership |
| Pfizer | Undisclosed | Long-term | Co-building an AI molecular simulation platform |
| Sunshine Lake Pharma | Several hundred million RMB | January 2026 | Co-building an AI + robotics joint lab |

**Customer concentration is one of the biggest risks**: in FY2025, DoveTree alone contributed approximately **45%** of revenue. This means that if anything goes wrong with the DoveTree partnership, XtalPi's revenue could see a cliff-edge decline.

Counter-argument: customer concentration is common in the early stages of platform development — Amazon AWS also relied heavily on a handful of major customers early on. What matters is whether the "funnel" is widening. XtalPi's customer count and deal values are indeed continuing to grow, which is a positive signal.

**A metric worth tracking continuously**: revenue growth from non-top-3 customers. If this segment can maintain 50%+ growth, it shows the platform's appeal is spreading; if growth slows, it shows dependence on large deals is deepening.

## Fundamental Difference from Traditional CROs

This is the single most important chart for understanding XtalPi:

| Dimension | Traditional CRO (e.g., WuXi AppTec) | XtalPi |
|------|---------------------|---------|
| Core resource | Chemists + labs | AI models + robots + data |
| Marginal cost | High (one more project needs one more team) | Low (AI model reuse cost trends to zero) |
| Gross margin | 35-40% | ~70% |
| Growth bottleneck | Hiring speed | Compute + data + customer acquisition |
| Moat | Talent + management + customer relationships | Data flywheel + algorithms + hardware closed loop |
| Fee ceiling | Service fees (linear growth) | Milestones + royalties (exponential growth potential) |
| Risk | Talent attrition + policy risk | Technology being matched + large deals underdelivering |

**If XtalPi's "platform-style CRO" narrative holds up**, its long-term value far exceeds that of a traditional CRO — because a business model with near-zero marginal cost should, in theory, see margins keep rising as scale grows.

**If the narrative doesn't hold up**, it's just a small CRO whose gross margin has been temporarily inflated by a large deal — revenue of 800 million yuan, not even a rounding error compared to WuXi AppTec (WuXi AppTec's FY2024 revenue was approximately **40 billion yuan**).

## R&D Investment: Still in the "Build-Out Phase"

| Year | R&D spend (RMB millions) | Revenue (RMB millions) | R&D intensity |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 2024 | 418 | 266 | **157%** |
| 2025 | ~400 (estimate) | 803 | ~50% (estimate) |

**R&D intensity fell from 157% to approximately 50%**, indicating the company is transitioning from "pure cash-burning to build the platform" to "the platform starting to generate output." But 50% R&D intensity is still very high — by comparison, WuXi AppTec's R&D intensity is approximately 8-10%.

In January 2026, XtalPi issued a **HK$2.866 billion zero-coupon convertible bond** (maturing 2027) at a conversion price of HK$13.85 per share; if fully converted, it would dilute shares by approximately **4.81%**. Combined with the roughly HK$6 billion raised through three share placements in 2025, the company has raised nearly **HK$9 billion** from capital markets in under two years.

**What this shows**: management believes the company is still at a stage requiring substantial capital investment. The good news is ample ammunition (cash of 7 billion yuan+); the bad news is shareholders' equity keeps being diluted.

## Core Judgment: Is the Business Model Actually Good?

Back to a Duan Yongping-style question: **What's good about this business? Describe it in one sentence.**

> The good part is that the "AI + data flywheel" can, in theory, form a positive feedback loop — the more projects done, the more data accumulated, the more accurate the models become, and the more willing customers are to partner. If the flywheel truly spins, marginal costs trend to zero, gross margin keeps rising, and customer stickiness keeps increasing.

**But there's one precondition**: is the flywheel actually spinning.

Current evidence:
- **Positive**: DoveTree's $6 billion mega-deal, two Eli Lilly renewals (totaling approximately $600 million), 70% gross margin, 26 million data points and growing
- **Negative**: the first profit is highly dependent on a single large deal, profitability excluding the large deal is unproven, revenue base is still small (800 million yuan vs. WuXi AppTec's 40 billion yuan)

**Conclusion**: the business model's direction is correct (AI + robotics replacing manual CRO work), but the degree of validation is insufficient — it will take another 2-3 years to observe whether recurring revenue, excluding large deals, can independently sustain profitability.
