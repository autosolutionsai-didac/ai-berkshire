## The Scariest Rival: Google's Isomorphic Labs

This $2.1 billion Series B round set a new record for a single funding round in the AI pharma industry, pushing Isomorphic Labs' total funding to **$2.7 billion**. For comparison:

| Dimension | Isomorphic Labs | XtalPi |
|------|:--:|:--:|
| Parent company | Alphabet (Google) | Independent listed company |
| Founders | Demis Hassabis (creator of AlphaFold / Nobel laureate) | Wen Shuhao, Ma Jian, Lai Lipeng (MIT postdocs) |
| Total funding | **Series B $2.1 billion + Series A $600 million = $2.7 billion** | IPO + placements + convertible bonds ~HK$9 billion (~$1.2 billion) |
| Core technology | AlphaFold → IsoDDE drug design engine | Quantum physics + AI + robotics closed loop |
| Clinical progress | Preclinical (originally planned to enter clinical trials by end of 2025, now delayed to end of 2026) | Incubated project SIGX1094 already in Phase I clinical trials |
| Major pharma partnerships | Eli Lilly + Novartis (combined $3 billion+) | DoveTree + Eli Lilly + Pfizer (combined $6.5 billion+) |

**Isomorphic Labs is the source of the "dimensionality-reducing strike" risk XtalPi faces.** The reasons are simple:

1. **Unlimited capital**: parent company Alphabet has a market cap of approximately $2 trillion; Isomorphic Labs is not short of money
2. **Top-tier talent**: Demis Hassabis is the creator of AlphaFold and winner of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry
3. **Overwhelming compute**: Google has one of the world's top TPU clusters and cloud computing infrastructure
4. **IsoDDE's accuracy**: in protein-ligand structure prediction, IsoDDE's accuracy is **more than double that of AlphaFold 3**

Counter-argument (in XtalPi's defense):

1. **Isomorphic Labs still has no drug in clinical trials**, whereas XtalPi's incubated company Xige Biosciences already has SIGX1094 in Phase I clinical trials. "Calculating accurately" and "making a successful drug" are two different things.
2. **XtalPi has robotic labs; Isomorphic does not.** AI drug discovery isn't just computation — it requires "wet-lab" validation. Pure-algorithm companies must rely on external CROs for experiments, which slows things down.
3. **Google's track record making drugs is not glowing.** Alphabet's Calico (founded 2013, focused on aging research) has invested over a decade with no significant output to date.
4. **The clinical delay is a negative signal.** Hassabis's 2025 prediction of "entering clinical trials by year-end" has already slipped to end of 2026, showing that the leap from algorithm to drug is harder than imagined.

**Core judgment**: Isomorphic Labs is XtalPi's biggest long-term threat, but won't directly poach XtalPi's customers in the short term (2-3 years). The reason is that pharma industry partnerships have long cycles and high switching costs, and Isomorphic itself still has no drug in clinical trials. **But on a 5-year horizon, if Isomorphic Labs achieves full commercialization, XtalPi's technological moat could be significantly eroded.**

## A Mess of Feathers: The Painful Lessons of AI Pharma Peers

Before discussing XtalPi's competitive advantages, we must first look at what happened to its peers — **this industry's list of "fallen pioneers" is long.**

| Company | Peak | Trough/current | Decline | Lesson |
|------|:--:|:--:|:--:|------|
| Schrodinger | $100 (2021) | ~$17 (2025) | **-83%** | Computational chemistry software has value, but the market isn't willing to pay a high valuation for it |
| Recursion | $30 (2021) | ~$5 (2025) | **-83%** | Forced to merge with Exscientia to "huddle for warmth" |
| Exscientia | $27 (2022) | Acquired by Recursion at a low price | — | The independent path didn't work |
| Relay Therapeutics | $75 (2021) | ~$5 (2025) | **-93%** | Clinical failure caused the valuation to collapse |

**A common pattern**: these companies were pushed to high valuations during the 2020-2021 AI craze, then were harshly punished by the market as "commercialization fell short of expectations."

**Why is XtalPi still standing?** Two reasons:

1. **It's one of the few genuinely profitable AI pharma companies.** In 2025, adjusted net profit was **258 million yuan** — though dependent on a large deal, it's at least positive. Schrodinger and Recursion are still loss-making.
2. **The special nature of a Hong Kong Chapter 18C listing.** XtalPi only listed in 2024, missing the peak of the 2021 valuation bubble. Market expectations for it were never that frenzied to begin with.

Counter-argument: XtalPi "still standing" could simply be because "it hasn't reached its moment of reckoning yet." Schrodinger and Recursion also didn't start crashing until 2-3 years after their IPOs. XtalPi has been listed for less than two years — the real test may still lie ahead.

## XtalPi's Differentiated Advantage: Physics + AI + Robotics as a Unified Whole

Among AI pharma companies, XtalPi holds a unique position — **it's one of the very few companies that possess both "algorithmic capability" and "wet-lab capability" at the same time.**

| Capability dimension | Pure-algorithm companies (Isomorphic, etc.) | Pure CROs (WuXi AppTec, etc.) | XtalPi |
|---------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| AI models | **Strong** | Weak | **Strong** (200+ vertical models) |
| Physics-based computation | Weak/medium | None | **Strong** (quantum physics methodology) |
| Robotic experimentation | None | None (manual experiments) | **Strong** (200+ workstations) |
| Data closed loop | None (relies on external experimental data) | Yes (but manual efficiency is low) | **Yes** (automated closed loop) |
| Clinical development | Weak/none | **Strong** | Weak (indirect participation via incubated companies) |

**"Physics + AI + robotics as a unified whole" is XtalPi's most core differentiated narrative.** Its logic is:

- Pure-algorithm companies (like Isomorphic Labs) lack experimental validation capability and must outsource experiments to CROs, so the data doesn't stay in their own hands
- Pure CROs (like WuXi AppTec) have experimental capability but lack AI, making them low-efficiency and low-margin
- XtalPi has both, forming a "compute → do → verify → iterate" closed loop, with data staying on the platform and continuously accumulating

**If this closed loop is effective**, the longer it runs, the deeper XtalPi's data moat becomes — because **26 million** pieces of experimental data can't simply be bought with money; it takes time to accumulate.

**But one must ask**: is this "data flywheel" actually spinning?

Validation signals already present:
- Eli Lilly's two renewals (from $250 million to $345 million) show the platform is indeed creating value for customers
- 17 of the world's top 20 pharma companies have partnership records
- PharmaEngine's SIGX1094 moved from discovery to Phase I clinical trials faster than the industry average

Aspects not yet validated:
- Specific customer repeat-purchase-rate data has not been disclosed
- There's no quantified evidence that data accumulation is actually improving model accuracy over time
- Non-major-customer willingness to pay, and trends in deal amounts, are unclear

## China Market vs. Global Market

One of XtalPi's unique advantages: **it's one of the few AI pharma companies with major customers in both the Chinese and global markets simultaneously.**

| Market | Representative customers | Advantage | Risk |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Global | Eli Lilly, Pfizer, DoveTree | Big pharma has strong paying power; large contract values | Geopolitical risk (US-China relations) |
| China | Sunshine Lake Pharma | Policy support, geographic proximity | Domestic pharma companies' willingness to pay is relatively low |

**One potential risk**: if US-China tech decoupling accelerates, XtalPi's cooperation with US pharma companies could face restrictions. This isn't a present-day threat, but it's worth watching on a 5-year horizon.

Counter-argument: the pharma industry is far more globalized than sensitive sectors like semiconductors. Companies like Eli Lilly are unlikely to abandon an AI partner they've already validated as effective for political reasons. And what XtalPi does is R&D tooling, which doesn't involve military or sensitive technology.

## Core Judgment: Three Conclusions on the Competitive Landscape

### Conclusion One: XtalPi Sits in the First Tier of the AI Pharma Industry

Fewer than 5 AI pharma companies globally can simultaneously land major deals with top-tier pharma like Eli Lilly and Pfizer. XtalPi is one of them, and the **only profitable one**.

### Conclusion Two: Isomorphic Labs Is the Biggest Long-Term Threat, but Its Near-Term Impact Is Limited

Google has money, people, and compute, but making drugs is much harder than making algorithms. Isomorphic Labs' clinical delay already demonstrates this. **It doesn't pose direct competition within 2-3 years; the picture beyond 5 years is uncertain.**

### Conclusion Three: The AI Pharma Industry Is Still in a "Validation Phase," Not a "Harvest Phase"

No AI drug has launched globally, most companies are still loss-making, and valuations are broadly elevated — this is a sector that needs "faith" to sustain it. If you don't believe AI will ultimately transform drug R&D, then no company in this sector is worth investing in, XtalPi included.
