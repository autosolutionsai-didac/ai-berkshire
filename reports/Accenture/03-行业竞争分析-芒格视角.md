# Accenture (ACN) Industry Competition Analysis

> Analytical framework: industry ecological niche, competitive strengths/weaknesses, moat type and width
> Data as of: May 2026

---

## I. Industry Panorama: What Kind of Battlefield Is IT Consulting?

The global IT consulting and services market is roughly $70 billion in size (narrow definition of consulting), while the broader IT services market exceeds $1.2 trillion. Industry growth is about 6-7% (CAGR), with AI transformation demand as the largest current source of incremental growth.

Characteristics of this industry:

1. **Highly fragmented**: Even the largest player, Accenture, holds only about 12% of global enterprise consulting contracts
2. **Moderate barriers to entry**: Technical barriers are not high, but barriers of scale, brand, and client relationships are very high
3. **Intense price competition**: Especially in offshore outsourcing, where Indian firms undercut the high end of the market with low costs
4. **Moderate cyclicality**: Enterprise IT spending gets delayed in economic downturns but does not disappear

## II. Comparison With Major Competitors

### Comparison With the Big Four Consulting Firms (Deloitte/PwC/EY/KPMG)

| Dimension | Accenture | Deloitte |
|------|-----------|----------|
| IT services revenue | ~$70 billion | ~$30 billion (IT portion) |
| Core strength | Technology implementation + operations | Audit + tax + consulting |
| Headcount | 786,000 | 450,000+ |
| Listing status | NYSE-listed | Private partnership |
| AI investment | GenAI bookings $5.9 billion | No detailed data disclosed |

Deloitte is stronger in strategy consulting and audit, but is not as strong as Accenture in large-scale technology implementation. Accenture's advantage is being "end-to-end" — from strategy to execution to operations, a full-service pipeline. Deloitte holds about 10% of the global IT consulting market.

### Comparison With Indian IT Giants (TCS/Infosys/Cognizant)

| Dimension | Accenture | TCS | Infosys | Cognizant |
|------|-----------|-----|---------|-----------|
| Revenue | $69.7 billion | ~$30 billion | ~$19 billion | ~$20 billion |
| Net margin | 11% | ~20% | ~17% | ~12% |
| Revenue per employee | ~$89,000 | ~$50,000 | ~$55,000 | ~$65,000 |
| Positioning | High-end full-stack | Large-scale delivery | Digital transformation | U.S. market |
| GenAI bookings | $5.9 billion | Not disclosed | Several hundred million | Not disclosed |

Key findings:
- **TCS has already surpassed Accenture in market capitalization**, becoming the world's largest IT services company by market cap — unthinkable two years ago
- Indian firms' net margins are far higher than Accenture's, because their labor cost structure is 30-50% lower
- But Accenture's revenue per employee is higher, indicating each employee creates more value
- In GenAI, Accenture's first-mover advantage is clear — the $5.9 billion in bookings is the largest figure disclosed in the entire industry

### Comparison With Management Consulting Firms (McKinsey/BCG/Bain)

These three firms sit above Accenture at the strategy level, but fall far short of Accenture in technology implementation and delivery at scale. In the AI era they are also ramping up technical capabilities, but the scale gap is enormous. They do not constitute a core competitive threat.

## III. Moat Analysis

### Moat 1: Economies of Scale ★★★★☆

- A global delivery network of 786,000 people that no new entrant can replicate in the short term
- Scale brings purchasing leverage, global delivery flexibility, and accumulated cross-industry expertise
- But scale also brings management complexity and organizational inertia

### Moat 2: Client Switching Costs ★★★★☆

- Systems-integration projects naturally lock in clients: you don't switch vendors halfway through an SAP implementation
- Managed services contracts run 3-7 years, forming a stable revenue base
- On-site consultants create knowledge lock-in and relationship lock-in
- But every new project is a fresh "re-bid" — the lock-in is not absolute

### Moat 3: Brand and Trust ★★★★☆

- Brand value of $41.5 billion, the No. 1 IT services brand globally
- The CIO's psychological safety of "you won't get fired for choosing Accenture"
- Deep partnerships with every mainstream technology platform

### Moat 4: Intangible Assets (Methodology and Tools) ★★★☆☆

- Proprietary platforms such as myWizard and SynOps improve delivery efficiency
- Accumulated industry-specific solutions and methodologies
- But unlike patents, these have no legal protection — competitors can study and imitate them

### Overall Moat Rating: Moderate-to-Wide (3.5/5)

Accenture has genuine competitive advantages, but not the kind of "nearly impossible to disrupt" moat that Coca-Cola has. AI development may simultaneously widen and erode the moat — widening it because demand for AI transformation is surging; eroding it because AI tools may let smaller competitors' delivery capability approach that of large firms.

## IV. Key Shifts in the Competitive Landscape

### The AI-Era "Reshuffle"

The traditional competitive landscape is being reshaped by AI:

1. **Accenture currently leads in AI consulting**: its $5.9 billion in GenAI bookings far exceeds the figures publicly disclosed by competitors
2. **Indian IT firms are catching up**: TCS and Infosys are also investing heavily in AI, but their booking scale lags
3. **New-style competitors are emerging**: AI-native firms (such as Palantir) are grabbing share in specific verticals
4. **Direct competition from tech giants**: Microsoft and Google are reducing clients' reliance on third-party consulting through their own AI services

### The DOGE Shockwave

Cuts to U.S. federal business have hit not only Accenture but also Deloitte, IBM, and other government IT vendors. But Accenture's share of federal business (~8% of global revenue) means it is hit harder. The Department of Defense terminated a $5.1 billion IT contract, and the GSA has required agencies to review consulting contracts.

## V. Core Conclusion

Viewed through Munger's multiple-mental-models lens:

**Ecological niche**: Accenture occupies the most favorable niche in the IT services industry — high-end full-stack provider. But "most favorable" does not mean "irreplaceable."

**Competitive dynamics**: In the short term, no competitor can displace Accenture's position. But AI is changing the rules of competition — previously winning meant having more people; in the future it may mean using AI well. Accenture currently leads in this transition, but the game is far from over.

**The biggest risk**: not any specific competitor, but technology itself — if AI tools drastically lower the barrier for enterprises to build their own capabilities in-house, the value proposition of the entire consulting industry will be redefined.

Munger would say: "This is a good business, but not a great business. A good business can still make money in a headwind; a great business can widen its moat in a headwind. Whether Accenture can widen its moat amid the AI wave is still unclear."
