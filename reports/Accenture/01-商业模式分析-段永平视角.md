# Accenture (ACN) Business Model Analysis

> Analytical framework: the nature of the business, customer value, competitive barriers, whether management is "doing the right things"
> Data as of: May 2026 | Latest earnings: Q2 FY2026 (period ended February 2026)

---

## I. What Kind of Business Is This?

Accenture is the world's largest IT consulting and outsourcing services company, with FY2025 revenue of $69.7 billion, roughly 786,000 employees, and service coverage in 120+ countries worldwide. The business has two main segments:

- **Consulting services (about 55%)**: helping enterprises with digital transformation, cloud migration, AI deployment, and the like, charged on a project basis
- **Managed services (about 45%)**: long-term outsourced operations, charged over the contract cycle, with stronger stickiness

The essence of this business is **selling a combination of "brainpower + manpower."** The consulting portion sells solutions and judgment; the managed-services portion sells execution capability and delivery at scale.

## II. Customer Value: Why Do Clients Choose Accenture?

Its core clients are Global 500 enterprises and large government agencies. The reasons clients choose Accenture over building an in-house team or hiring a smaller firm are clear:

1. **Delivery capability at scale**: a team of 786,000 people means resources can be mobilized globally and quickly, with large projects advancing simultaneously across multiple countries
2. **Full-stack capability**: from strategy consulting to systems integration to ongoing operations, all handled by one provider, reducing the coordination cost of managing multiple vendors
3. **Technology partner network**: deep partnerships with Microsoft, AWS, Google, SAP, Salesforce, and virtually every mainstream technology vendor, acting as a "translation layer" between clients and the technology ecosystem
4. **Brand trust**: a CIO at a large enterprise who chooses Accenture won't be questioned by the boss — it's "the safe choice"

Q2 FY2026 new bookings reached $22.1 billion, with 32 clients each signing contracts of over $100 million in the quarter, showing continued spending increases from large clients.

## III. Customer Lock-In Effects

The degree of client lock-in in IT consulting is moderate to high:

- **Systems integration projects**: once Accenture has helped a client deploy an ERP system or cloud architecture, switching vendors means starting over, so switching costs are extremely high
- **Managed services contracts**: typically 3-7 year terms, with a relatively high renewal rate at expiration
- **Embedded personnel**: Accenture consultants are stationed long-term at client offices, forming a "quasi-employee" relationship
- **Accumulated knowledge**: years of accumulated industry knowledge and systems understanding built through the partnership cannot be replicated

But it should also be noted that this is not the kind of "consumer mindshare lock-in" that Coca-Cola enjoys. Clients retain a choice every time a new project goes out to bid, and price pressure is ever-present.

## IV. Is AI an Incremental Opportunity or a Substitute?

This is the most critical question right now. AI's impact on Accenture cuts both ways.

**Upside (incremental)**:
- FY2025 full-year GenAI bookings reached $5.9 billion, doubling year over year; Q1 FY2026 reached $2.2 billion (up 76% year over year)
- FY2025 GenAI revenue reached $2.7 billion, up threefold year over year
- Every large enterprise needs help with AI transformation — this is a massive wave of consulting demand
- CEO Julie Sweet has already had 550,000 employees complete GenAI training

**Downside (substitution)**:
- AI coding tools (Cursor, GitHub Copilot) may let clients accomplish development with fewer people, compressing outsourcing demand
- In the long run, agentic AI may replace some low-end consulting and operations work
- Consulting firms' core revenue model is "selling headcount" — if AI devalues "headcount," per-unit pricing will be compressed

**In the short term, AI is a net positive** — clients don't know how to use AI and need Accenture's help. But 5-10 years from now, if AI tools mature enough, some consulting demand may be automated away. This is a "sweet now, bitter later" structure.

## V. Is It "Doing the Right Things"?

Applying Duan Yongping's framework — **do the right things, then do things right**:

**Things done right**:
- Made an early, heavy bet on AI, beginning large-scale investment in GenAI capability building as far back as FY2024
- Over $3 billion in acquisitions each year, continuously supplementing its capability map
- Had 550,000 people complete AI training — the strongest execution of any global consulting firm
- Deep partnership with Anthropic (Claude), positioning itself in agentic AI

**Things worth watching**:
- Federal government business is being hit by DOGE cuts, expected to drag down FY2026 revenue by 1-1.5 percentage points
- The fundamental contradiction of the "selling headcount" model — the better AI gets, the fewer people are needed per deal
- Organizational inertia at a large consulting firm — whether it can truly transform into an "AI-native" company

## VI. Core Conclusion

Accenture is a **decent but not top-tier business**. Its strengths are high barriers of scale, strong client stickiness, and excellent cash flow; its weaknesses are that it is fundamentally a "labor-intensive" business with a limited margin ceiling (net margin about 11%) and faces long-term risk of AI substitution.

In Duan Yongping's words: "This is a business you can run, but it's not the kind of business where you make money without thinking." Management is currently doing the right things, but the business model itself is not as "simple" as consumer goods or platform businesses.
