# Accenture (ACN) Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analytical framework: financial quality, capital allocation, valuation margin of safety
> Data as of: May 2026 | Latest earnings: Q2 FY2026 (period ended February 2026)

## II. Financial Quality Analysis

### Revenue Quality: Stable but Unspectacular

- 3-year revenue CAGR of about 6%, an upper-middle level within the global IT services industry
- Fairly balanced revenue mix: North America ~48%, Europe ~34%, growth markets ~18%
- Top 10 clients account for about 15% of revenue — client concentration is not high
- The book-to-bill ratio (new bookings/revenue) has continuously stayed above 1.0, indicating a growing backlog

### Margins: Stable but with a Ceiling

- A net margin of about 11% may not look high, but for a 700,000-person services company it's already excellent
- Room for margin improvement is limited — labor costs account for about 55-60% of revenue, a rigid cost base
- AI may boost efficiency in the short term, but clients will also demand price cuts, so the extent of margin improvement is uncertain
- The good news: operating margin has expanded 30bps YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating management is controlling costs

### Cash Flow: The Real Highlight

- FY2025 free cash flow of $10.9 billion, with FCF/net income above 130% — excellent cash conversion
- Capital expenditures of only about $600 million (under 1% of revenue) — the advantage of an asset-light model
- This means nearly all profit can be returned to shareholders or used for acquisitions

### Return on Invested Capital

- ROIC of about 27%, far above the estimated cost of capital of 8-10%, meaning every additional dollar reinvested is creating value
- The sources of high ROIC: an asset-light model, high utilization, and economies of scale

## III. Capital Allocation

FY2025 total shareholder returns were about $8.3 billion, or 72% of operating cash flow:

| Item | Amount |
|------|------|
| Share buybacks | $4.62 billion |
| Dividends | ~$3.7 billion |
| Total returned | ~$8.3 billion |

In addition, the company invests more than $3 billion per year in acquisitions, completing several strategic acquisitions in FY2025 to supplement its AI, cloud, and industry capabilities.

- Quarterly dividend of $1.63/share (up 15% in FY2025, and up another 10% in FY2026)
- Dividend has been raised for 20-plus consecutive years; the current payout ratio is about 54%, safe and sustainable
- Current dividend yield is about 3.4% (based on an estimated ~$190 share price), near the historical high

This is the kind of capital allocation Buffett likes: money earned is either bought back, paid out as dividends, or reinvested at a high ROIC. Accenture does all three, which is a point in its favor.

## IV. Valuation Analysis: Is a 14x P/E a Panic Price?

### Historical Valuation Comparison

| Period | P/E (TTM) |
|------|-----------|
| 2021 peak | ~38x |
| 2023 average | ~28x |
| 2024 average | ~25x |
| Current (5/2026) | ~14x |
| 10-year historical average | ~26x |

The current P/E of 14x represents a discount of about 47% to the 10-year average. This is one of the lowest valuation ranges since Accenture went public.

### Why Has the Stock Fallen to This Level?

1. **DOGE cutting federal contracts**: Accenture has been labeled a poster child for "government waste," with the Department of Defense terminating a $5.1 billion IT contract
2. **Fear of AI replacement**: the market worries that AI tools will reduce enterprise demand for external consulting
3. **Macro uncertainty**: tariff policy has led enterprises to delay IT investment decisions
4. **A roughly 46.5% decline over 52 weeks**, significantly underperforming the S&P 500

### Valuation Reasonableness Modeling

Assuming a conservative scenario:
- FY2026 adjusted EPS of about $13.70 (the midpoint of company guidance)
- Applying an 18x P/E (well below the historical average of 26x, reflecting an AI-risk discount)
- Fair value = $13.70 x 18 = **$247**
- At a current price of about $190, potential upside of ~30%

Assuming a pessimistic scenario:
- AI causes long-term growth to fall to 2-3%, with margins declining by 1 percentage point
- Applying a 15x P/E
- Floor value = $12.50 x 15 = **$188**
- The current stock price is close to this pessimistic floor

### Free Cash Flow Yield

- Current market cap of about $119.5 billion
- FY2025 FCF of $10.9 billion
- FCF yield = 10.9 / 119.5 = **9.1%**
- This level is extremely rare among large tech-services companies, indicating the market is pricing in a great deal of pessimism

## V. Core Conclusion

Viewed from Buffett's perspective:

**What's appealing**:
- Extremely strong cash flow, with an FCF conversion rate above 130%
- ROIC of 27% indicates a genuine competitive advantage
- Disciplined capital allocation — ongoing buybacks plus dividends
- A 14x P/E provides a considerable margin of safety

**What's concerning**:
- An 11% net margin isn't typical of a "wide-moat" company
- Long-term AI-replacement risk makes the timeline for a valuation recovery uncertain
- Federal business is exposed to policy risk, which may persist

**Overall assessment**: a 14x P/E is pricing in a considerable amount of panic. If the fear of AI replacement proves overdone (which is likely in the short term), there is substantial room for a valuation recovery. But this is not a "buy with your eyes closed" price — it requires forming your own judgment on AI's long-term impact on the consulting industry.
