# The Twin Engines of Consulting Plus Outsourcing

> Understanding Accenture series · Part 02 · Business Model
> Reading time: approx. 10 minutes

## Net margin is only 11% — so how does ROIC hit 27%?

An 11% net margin looks mediocre by tech-company standards. But in that same year, Accenture's ROIC was 27%, free cash flow was $10.9 billion, and capital expenditure was under 1% of revenue. When these three numbers appear together at one company, it tells you the true nature of this business is being seriously understated by its net margin.

**Accenture's money-making logic, in one line:**

> Large global enterprises don't know how to use new technology → they pay Accenture to design a solution (consulting) → they pay Accenture even more to help implement and run it (managed services) → the deeper the system embeds itself, the harder it is to leave → and when the next wave of new technology arrives, they come back for another round.

This is a **"consulting attracts clients, outsourcing locks them in" flywheel.** The consulting business is like the key that opens the door; the managed-services business is like the long-term lease behind that door.

## Twin engines: how the two revenue lines work together

| Business line | Share of revenue | Contract characteristics | Profit characteristics | Core role |
|--------|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Consulting services | **~55%** | Project-based, 6-18 months | Higher gross margin but more volatile | Client acquisition + capability showcase |
| Managed services | **~45%** | Long-term contracts, 3-7 years | Stable, predictable margin | Client lock-in + cash-flow foundation |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 annual report)

**Consulting is like the forward line, managed services is like the defense.** Forwards score the goals that grab the headlines, but what actually wins the league is a steady defense.

The Q2 FY2026 numbers illustrate this: consulting revenue grew **7%** year-over-year, while managed-services revenue grew **10%** year-over-year. Managed services growing faster indicates clients are "renewing and expanding," not just making one-off purchases.

(Data source: Accenture Q2 FY2026 earnings report)

## Why do clients stay stuck?

IT consulting's client-lock-in mechanism has four layers, each deeper than the last:

### Layer 1: Systems-integration lock-in

Once Accenture has helped a bank deploy an SAP system, or migrated an entire IT architecture to AWS — that setup runs bone-deep. Switching vendors isn't "swap in a different person to do the work," it's "tear down the house you already built and rebuild it."

**How high are switching costs?** A large ERP implementation project typically costs hundreds of millions of dollars and takes 2-3 years. Switching providers midway through? Nearly impossible.

### Layer 2: Embedded-personnel lock-in

Accenture's consultants are stationed on-site at client offices for extended periods, learning the client's business processes, system architecture, and internal politics. Over time, these consultants become "people who understand the system better than the client's own IT department." You can terminate Accenture's contract, but you can't take away the experience inside those people's heads.

### Layer 3: Contract-cycle lock-in

Managed-services contracts typically run **3-7 years**, with relatively high renewal rates at expiration. This means Accenture has 1-2 years of revenue visibility even amid severe market volatility. Q2 FY2026 new bookings hit a record **$22.1 billion**, with **41 clients** each signing over $100 million in a single quarter — major clients are doubling down, not pulling back.

(Data source: Accenture Q2 FY2026 earnings report)

### Layer 4: Technology-ecosystem lock-in

Accenture is a top-tier partner across all the major platforms — Microsoft, AWS, Google, SAP, Salesforce. It functions essentially as a **"translation layer" between clients and the technology ecosystem.**

A large-enterprise CIO facing five cloud platforms, three AI frameworks, and a dozen-plus SaaS products can't figure it all out alone, and doesn't dare bet on the wrong technology path. Accenture's value proposition is: "you don't have to choose — I'll choose for you, implement it for you, and if something goes wrong, I've got you covered."

**Counterargument: this lock-in isn't absolute.** Every new project put out to bid gives the client a choice. Price competition is constant, especially from Indian firms attacking the high end of the market with low costs. The lock-in is "moderate strength," not an "iron wall."

## Financial profile: a company that "looks unprofitable" but is actually very profitable

At first glance at Accenture's profit margins, you might think this is an ordinary business:

| Metric | FY2025 | Assessment |
|------|--------|------|
| Revenue | **$69.7 billion** | Number one in global IT services |
| GAAP operating margin | **14.7%** | Not particularly high |
| Net margin | **~11%** | Looks mediocre |
| ROIC | **~27%** | Far exceeds cost of capital |
| Free cash flow | **$10.9 billion** | FCF/net income > 130% |
| Capital expenditure | **~$600 million** | Under 1% of revenue |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 annual report)

**Core finding: an 11% net margin doesn't look high, but a 27% ROIC is exceptional. How can these two numbers coexist?**

The answer is **asset-light operations**. Accenture doesn't need to build factories, buy equipment, or hold inventory. Capital expenditure is only $600 million (under 1% of revenue), so even though it keeps only $11 of profit for every $100 earned, it needs almost no additional capital to sustain operations.

The result: **$10.9 billion in free cash flow, with FCF/net income exceeding 130%** — every dollar earned is "real cash," not paper profit eaten up by depreciation and capex.

A direct comparison makes this clearer:

| Company | Net margin | ROIC | Capex as % of revenue |
|------|--------|------|--------------|
| Accenture | 11% | 27% | <1% |
| TSMC | 41% | 28% | ~35% |
| Amazon | 8% | 18% | ~12% |

TSMC's net margin is nearly 4x Accenture's, but its capital expenditure is also more than 35x higher as a share of revenue. Once you get to ROIC, the two are similar. **Net margin is the appearance; ROIC is the substance.**

## Capital allocation: what does the company do with the money it earns?

Total shareholder returns in FY2025 were roughly **$8.3 billion**, or **72%** of operating cash flow:

| Item | Amount |
|------|------|
| Share buybacks | **$4.62 billion** |
| Dividends | **~$3.7 billion** |
| Total returned | **~$8.3 billion** |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 annual report)

In addition, Accenture invests **over $3 billion** per year in acquisitions to build out AI, cloud, and industry-vertical capabilities.

- Quarterly dividend of **$1.63/share**, up 15% in FY2025 and up another 10% in FY2026
- **20+ consecutive years** of dividend increases
- Current payout ratio of roughly **54%**, safe and sustainable
- Current dividend yield of roughly **3.6%**, at the high end of its historical range

**This is a company with a highly disciplined approach to capital allocation: buybacks, dividends, and strategic acquisitions all firing at once.** The money it earns isn't burned on vanity projects — the vast majority is returned to shareholders or invested in building capability. Returning 72% of operating cash flow to shareholders is among the highest ratios for large tech-services companies.

## AI transformation: not a slogan, real revenue

This may be the most important development to watch at Accenture right now.

| AI metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---------|--------|--------|------|
| GenAI bookings | ~$3 billion | **$5.9 billion** | **Doubled** |
| GenAI revenue | ~$900 million | **$2.7 billion** | **Tripled** |
| Employees trained on AI | — | **550,000** | Largest scale in the global consulting industry |

(Data sources: Accenture FY2025 annual report, Q1 FY2026 earnings call)

By Q1 FY2026, the company said AI already touches **80% of large deals** and stopped disclosing AI bookings as a separate line item — because AI is no longer a standalone business line, but has become woven into nearly every project.

**What does training 550,000 people on AI actually mean?** From 30 people when ChatGPT launched in November 2022, to 550,000 by the end of FY2025. That pace of execution is itself a competitive barrier — no other company has completed a talent transformation of this scale in this time window.

The scale of the organizational restructuring driven by CEO Julie Sweet is rare: overturning the company's **50-year-old organizational structure**, tying employee promotions to AI capability, and building a deep partnership with Anthropic (Claude) to position for autonomous agents (agentic AI).

**Counterargument: AI bookings are growing fast, but they still represent a small share of total revenue (roughly 4%). If AI tools ultimately mean clients need far fewer consultants, today's AI booking growth might just be a "last hurrah."**

This is a reasonable concern, and Part 03 will dig into it in detail.

## Global footprint: not putting all the eggs in one basket

| Region | Share of revenue | Characteristics |
|------|---------|------|
| North America | **~48%** | Largest market, hit by DOGE impact |
| Europe | **~34%** | Stable growth, strong digital-transformation demand |
| Growth markets | **~18%** | Fast-growing but small in scale |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 annual report)

The top 10 clients account for roughly **15%** of revenue — client concentration is low, so losing one or two big clients wouldn't do serious damage.

**Counterargument: with North America at 48%, if the U.S. economy enters a recession or policy risk (DOGE) continues to escalate, the impact won't be small. Federal business accounts for roughly 16% of Americas-region revenue, and that segment is shrinking.**

## Core conclusion: what kind of money machine is this?

If you had to define Accenture's business model in one sentence:

> **An asset-light, high-ROIC global cash-flow machine powered by a "consulting attracts clients, managed services locks them in" flywheel, currently turning AI from a threat into a new growth engine.**

**The strengths are clear**: high scale barriers, strong client stickiness, excellent cash flow, disciplined capital allocation, and a leading position in AI transformation.

**The weaknesses are equally clear**: at its core, this is still "selling heads," net margin has a limited ceiling, and over the long run AI could erode rather than reinforce this model.

This isn't the kind of "make money with your eyes closed" business that Coca-Cola is, but it is the best-run company of its kind.
