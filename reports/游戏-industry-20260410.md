## Step Two: Value Chain Panorama

### 2.1 Value Chain Structure

```
Upstream: content creation and technical support
├── Game engines: Unity, Unreal Engine (Epic)
├── Development tools/middleware: Havok, FMOD, SpeedTree
├── IP licensing: holders of anime/film/literary IP
└── Talent/studios: independent developers, outsourced art teams

Midstream: game development and publishing
├── In-house major studios: Tencent, NetEase, miHoYo, Capcom, Nintendo
├── Third-party publishers: 37 Interactive Entertainment, IGG, CMGE
├── Indie game publishers: Devolver Digital, Team17
└── Localization/QA testing services

Downstream: distribution channels and monetization
├── Mobile distribution: App Store, Google Play, Huawei/Xiaomi and other Android channels
├── PC distribution: Steam (Valve), Epic Games Store, WeGame (Tencent)
├── Console platforms: PlayStation Store, Xbox Store, Nintendo eShop
├── Mini-game platforms: WeChat Mini Games, Douyin Mini Games, Facebook Instant Games
└── Cloud gaming: GeForce NOW, Xbox Cloud Gaming, Tencent START

Supporting ecosystem
├── Ad monetization: AppLovin, Unity Ads, Google AdMob, Pangle (ByteDance)
├── Streaming/community: Twitch, YouTube Gaming, Bilibili, Douyu/Huya
├── Esports: tournament operations, teams, venues
├── Payments/anti-fraud: payment channels, risk-control services
└── Hardware: GPUs (NVIDIA/AMD), gaming phones, VR/AR devices
```

### 2.2 Business Characteristics by Segment

| Segment | Business model | Gross margin | Competitive landscape | Barrier type | Cyclicality |
|------|---------|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| Game engines | Licensing fees/revenue share | 80-90% | Duopoly (Unity + UE) | Technology + ecosystem lock-in | Weak |
| In-house major studios | IAP + advertising + subscription | 60-80% | Oligopoly (Tencent + NetEase hold 80%) | IP + social + brand | Medium (product cycle) |
| Third-party publishing | Paid UA + revenue share | 30-50% | Fully competitive | UA capability + capital | Strong |
| Mobile distribution (Apple/Google) | 30% platform cut | 90%+ | Duopoly | Platform monopoly | Weak |
| PC distribution (Steam) | 30% platform cut | 90%+ | Near-monopoly | Network effects + scale | Weak |
| Mini-game platforms | Revenue share + advertising | 70-80% | Tencent-dominated | Social relationship chain + traffic | Weak |
| Ad monetization | Ad-tech services | 50-70% | Oligopoly (AppLovin's rise) | AI algorithms + data | Medium |
| Esports | Tournaments + sponsorship + broadcast rights | 20-40% | Fully competitive | Brand + IP tie-in | Medium |
| Console hardware | Sell hardware (thin margin) + content ecosystem | 5-15% (hardware) | Triopoly (Sony/MS/Nintendo) | Massive R&D + IP | Strong (generational-refresh cycle) |

### 2.3 Chokepoint Segments

1. **Game engines**: Unity and Unreal Engine together hold 90%+ of the global game-development-engine market. Nearly all AAA and mid-size games depend on these two engines. Tencent holds roughly a 40% stake in Epic, giving it indirect leverage over engine-level decisions
2. **Mobile distribution channels**: Apple App Store/Google Play's 30% cut is the industry's biggest "tax." WeChat Mini Games bypasses this layer entirely — a structural advantage for Tencent
3. **Social relationship chain**: games' "social virality" user acquisition depends on social platforms. WeChat's 1.385 billion MAU is the strongest distribution pipeline for Chinese gaming, and it is irreplaceable

> **Duan Yongping's follow-up question**: Which segment of the value chain is a good, "toll-collecting" business? — **Platform distribution** (Steam, App Store, WeChat Mini Games) carries 90%+ gross margins, but Apple/Google are not pure-play gaming stocks. Among pure-play stocks, Tencent — via the WeChat Mini Games platform — comes closest to the "toll-collecting" model.

## Step Four: Deep-Dive on Tencent Gaming's Moat (Core Focus)

### 4.1 Business Essence (Duan Yongping's Lens)

**One-line definition**: Tencent Gaming is the world's largest gaming company. Driven by a "three-wheel" model of in-house development + licensed distribution + investment, it acquires users through the WeChat/QQ social relationship chain, monetizes via a free-to-play-plus-in-app-purchase model, and holds ~50% share of the China market.

**Revenue structure and growth rate**:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 3-yr CAGR |
|------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Domestic gaming | RMB 126.7 billion | RMB 139.7 billion (+10%) | RMB 164.2 billion (+18%) | +14% |
| Overseas gaming | RMB 53.2 billion | RMB 58.0 billion (+9%) | RMB 77.4 billion (+33%) | +21% |
| **Total gaming revenue** | **RMB 179.9 billion** | **RMB 197.7 billion (+10%)** | **RMB 241.6 billion (+22%)** | **+16%** |
| Gaming as % of group revenue | 30% | 30% | 32% | — |

**Gross margin/net margin**: group gross margin of 56% (a historical high in 2025); the gaming business's gross margin is estimated at 60-70% (above the group average).

**Cash-flow characteristics**:
- Operating cash flow of RMB 303.1 billion, FCF of RMB 182.6 billion
- Gaming runs on a "prepay-then-deliver" model, so cash flow leads revenue recognition
- 14 "evergreen games," each generating over RMB 4 billion in annual billings, provide a stable base

**Duan Yongping's "good business" follow-up**:

> **Is this a good business?** Yes. The essence of the gaming industry is "content monetization," but Tencent has turned it into "platform monetization" — the WeChat/QQ social relationship chain gives it a customer-acquisition cost far below its competitors, and that is "differentiation." More importantly, WeChat Mini Games is turning Tencent from a "gaming company" into a "gaming platform," and platform economics are far superior to those of a content company. Duan Yongping has said: "Do the right thing, and do the thing right." What Tencent has done right in gaming is this — **rather than rely on one hit game, it built a system that keeps producing hits.**

### 4.2 Moat Analysis (Buffett's Lens)

| Moat type | Strength | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Brand/pricing power** | ★★★★☆ | Honor of Kings is a national-level brand with DAU of 139 million; but brand loyalty in gaming runs lower than in consumer goods |
| **Switching costs** | ★★★★★ | Locked in by the social relationship chain: your friends are all playing Honor of Kings or PUBG Mobile, so you can't switch. Sunk costs in game-account assets (skins/levels) are high |
| **Network effects** | ★★★★★ | WeChat's 1.385 billion MAU → social virality in gaming → more players → better matchmaking → more retention. Network effects are especially powerful in competitive genres like MOBA/FPS |
| **Scale effects** | ★★★★☆ | R&D spend of RMB 85.7 billion a year, the highest in the world. The marginal-cost difference between serving 100 million users and 10 million users with the same game is minimal |
| **Technology/license barriers** | ★★★★☆ | Game licenses form a licensing-based barrier (unique to China); a ~40% stake in Epic gives Tencent indirect influence over Unreal Engine; deep reserves of AI + cloud-gaming technology |

**Composite moat rating: ★★★★½ (4.5/5)**

**Core thesis: Tencent Gaming's moat rests not on any single game, but on the systemic barrier formed by the trinity of "social relationship chain + platform distribution + global investment."**

Breaking it down:

**First layer of moat: the social relationship chain (nearly unbreakable)**
- WeChat's 1.385 billion MAU + QQ's hundreds of millions of MAU = the strongest game-distribution pipeline in China
- The cost for a competitor to acquire one gaming user is 3-5x Tencent's
- "All my friends play it" is the strongest retention mechanism — you might not personally like Honor of Kings, but your circle of friends is playing it

**Second layer of moat: the WeChat Mini Games platform (new, and rapidly strengthening)**
- 2025 market size of RMB 53.5 billion (+34%), MAU exceeding 500 million
- Bypasses Apple/Google's 30% cut, reaching users directly
- More than 65 mini-games with over 1 million DAU, and more than 300 with quarterly billings above RMB 10 million
- This is a **platform-level moat**, ten times stronger than the moat of any single game

**Third layer of moat: the global investment portfolio (one of a kind)**
- Wholly owned/controlling stakes: Riot Games (League of Legends/VALORANT), Supercell (Clash of Clans), Grinding Gear Games (Path of Exile)
- Major equity stakes: Epic Games (~40%, Unreal Engine), Ubisoft's new subsidiary (25%), FromSoftware (16%, Elden Ring)
- Minority stakes: Krafton, PlatinumGames, Larian Studios, Remedy, and dozens more
- The implication: **for every RMB 100 of revenue generated across the global gaming industry, Tencent directly or indirectly captures roughly RMB 15-20**

**Buffett's follow-up: will the moat still be there in 10 years?**

> WeChat's social-relationship-chain moat will very likely still be there in 10 years — human social needs will not disappear, and the relationship chain of 1.3 billion people will not migrate overnight. If WeChat Mini Games keeps growing, it will become an App-Store-like "toll-collecting" asset. But watch for: (1) AI may lower the barrier to game development, letting small teams produce AAA-quality output and eroding scale effects; (2) if Douyin/ByteDance succeeds in building a gaming-social ecosystem, it could erode Tencent's distribution edge; (3) if the U.S. government forces Tencent to divest its Epic/Riot/Supercell stakes, that would severely damage the investment-portfolio moat.
