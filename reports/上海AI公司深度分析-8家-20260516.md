## 1. MiniMax (Xiyu Jizhi) — Foundation Models

### Basic Information

- **City**: Shanghai
- **Founded**: December 2021
- **Founder**: Yan Junjie, 36, PhD from USTC, former SenseTime Vice President and Deputy Dean of its research institute, with over 6 years at SenseTime
- **Team size**: 385 people as of September 30, 2025, of whom 284 are R&D staff (74%); average employee age is just 29
- **Ticker**: 0100.HK (Hong Kong Stock Exchange)

### Business

MiniMax runs on a "full-stack foundation models + dual consumer/enterprise tracks" business model. Its consumer products, Hailuo AI and Talkie, have a combined cumulative global user base of over 2.12 billion, with paying users up 15x in two years. On the enterprise side, an open platform serves 13,000 corporate customers, processing over 1 trillion tokens of API requests per day. Core products span large language models, a music-generation model (supporting professional 5-minute songs), Hailuo Video generation (text-to-video and image-to-video), and the MiniMax Agent platform. In the revenue mix, consumer accounts for over 71%, overseas revenue is over 70%, and enterprise gross margin reaches 69.4%.

### Financing

Cumulative financing exceeds $1.5 billion. Strategic investors include miHoYo, Alibaba, Tencent, Xiaohongshu, Xiaomi, and Kingsoft. Institutional investors include Hillhouse, IDG, Sequoia, and MSA Capital, among others. It listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on January 9, 2026, with a global offering of about 33.58 million shares at HK$165 per share, raising about HK$5.54 billion; the public offering was oversubscribed 1,837 times.

### Competition

Main competitors are Zhipu AI (which listed in Hong Kong around the same time), Baichuan Intelligence, Moonshot AI, and ByteDance's Doubao. MiniMax's differentiation: (1) overseas revenue exceeds 70% of the total, far more internationalized than peers; (2) Talkie leads in the overseas social-AI category; (3) it took only 4 years from founding to IPO, making it the largest foundation-model IPO in history.

### Financials

Full-year 2025 revenue was $79.04 million (about RMB 570 million), up 158.9% year over year. Gross profit was $20.10 million, up 437.2% year over year. Adjusted net loss was $251 million. Cash reserves stood at $1.05 billion at the end of 2025. Analysts expect 2026 revenue to reach $219 million.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 4 stars** — MiniMax is one of the most commercially successful companies in China's foundation-model race. Its overseas revenue share of over 70% is extremely rare, and Talkie's global user base is a clear moat. But the current market cap of over HK$200 billion against less than RMB 600 million in revenue reflects a significant valuation bubble; the market needs to wait for revenue to catch up and losses to narrow.

## 2. Enflame — AI Chips

### Basic Information

- **City**: Shanghai (Lin-gang Special Area)
- **Founded**: March 2018
- **Founder**: Zhao Lidong (Chairman and CEO), a Tsinghua alumnus who worked in Silicon Valley for over 20 years, former Senior Director of AMD's Computing Business Unit, having managed R&D teams of over a thousand people; co-founder Zhang Yalin also came from AMD
- **Team size**: about 900+ people, 80% hold a master's or doctoral degree, 90% are R&D staff

### Business

Enflame focuses on AI training and inference chips, with its core product line being the "Cloudblazer" (Suisi) series. It has released 4 generations across 5 chips: Cloudblazer 1.0 (training), Cloudblazer 2.0/2.5 (combined training-inference), Cloudblazer 3.0 (inference), and Cloudblazer 400 (fourth-generation combined training-inference chip, launched July 2025, with native FP8 support). On the software side, Enflame built its own full-stack AI computing platform, "TopsRider," choosing an independent path rather than following the CUDA ecosystem. In 2024, revenue from intelligent-computing systems and clusters accounted for 56.24% of the business.

### Financing

Tencent and its concert-party affiliates hold a 20.258% stake and are the largest shareholder; Tencent has invested across 6 rounds. The valuation after the latest round is about RMB 18.2–20.0 billion. The company's Sci-Tech Innovation Board (STAR Market) IPO application was accepted on January 22, 2026, targeting RMB 6 billion in proceeds, with CITIC Securities as sponsor.

### Competition

Main competitors are Hygon Information Technology (AMD-licensed route), Cambricon (a listed AI-chip company), and Biren Technology (general-purpose GPUs). Enflame's differentiation: (1) deep ties with Tencent provide a stable anchor customer and ecosystem support; (2) a self-developed full-stack software platform independent of CUDA; (3) its combined training-inference chip roadmap covers broad use cases. However, customer concentration is extremely high — Tencent contributed 57.28% of revenue in the first three quarters of 2025.

### Financials

Core revenue grew from about RMB 90 million in 2022 to over RMB 720 million in 2024, a two-year CAGR of 183%. Revenue for January–September 2025 was RMB 540 million. Cumulative losses from 2022–2024 were about RMB 4 billion. The company could reach breakeven as early as 2026.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 3 stars** — Enflame benefits from strong Tencent backing and stable shipment guarantees, and its technology roadmap is solid, but customer concentration (Tencent at 57%) is the biggest risk. In substance it looks more like "Tencent's AI chip division" than an independent company — if Tencent shifts strategy or develops its own chips in-house, Enflame would face a major shock.

## 3. Iluvatar CoreX — GPU Chips

### Basic Information

- **City**: Shanghai/Nanjing (founded in Nanjing in 2015; the current main entity is Shanghai Iluvatar CoreX Semiconductor Co., Ltd.)
- **Founded**: December 2015
- **Founder**: Li Yunpeng (former R&D director at Oracle), who left management in 2019; the current Chairman and CEO is Gai Lujiang (a finance professional with 17 years of financial-investment experience), and the COO is Liu Zheng (a telecom-industry background)
- **Team size**: 685 employees as of June 2025, of whom 484 are R&D staff (70.6%)
- **Ticker**: Hong Kong Stock Exchange (listed January 8, 2026)

### Business

Iluvatar CoreX is a leading domestic provider of general-purpose GPU chips and computing systems, with products spanning the Tiankai series (training GPUs) and Zhikai series (inference GPUs). Tiankai Gen1 (mass production in 2021), Tiankai Gen2 (mass production in 2023), Tiankai Gen3 (announced 2024, mass production expected Q1 2026), and Zhikai Gen1/Gen1X (mass production in 2023). The company claims to be the first in China to achieve mass production of general-purpose GPU chips for both inference and training, and the first to adopt an advanced 7nm process. Cumulative shipments exceed 52,000 units.

### Financing

The company has gone through 10 rounds of financing since founding, totaling RMB 5.8 billion. Investors include DCP Capital, Yuanhe Partners, and Sequoia China. It completed Series D (over RMB 1.4 billion) and Series D+ (over RMB 2.05 billion) in 2025; the Series D+ pre-money valuation was RMB 12 billion, with a final valuation of about RMB 14.05 billion. It listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on January 8, 2026, at HK$144.6 per share, raising about HK$3.7 billion; its market cap approached HK$40 billion on its first day of trading.

### Competition

Main competitors are Moore Threads (listed on the STAR Market, market cap over RMB 300 billion), Biren Technology, Hygon Information Technology, and Cambricon. Iluvatar CoreX's differentiation lies in its "true general-purpose GPU" positioning, compatible with mainstream AI ecosystems and deep-learning frameworks. However, the company has been through a founder's departure, two changes of leadership, and strategic wavering — management stability remains a concern.

### Financials

Revenue for 2022–2024 was RMB 189 million, RMB 289 million, and RMB 540 million, respectively; full-year 2025 revenue surpassed RMB 1 billion (RMB 1.034 billion), up 91.6% year over year. Gross margin rose from 49.1% in 2024 to 54.0% in 2025. But net losses kept widening: RMB 553 million, RMB 817 million, and RMB 892 million in 2022–2024, respectively; the 2025 net loss was RMB 1.004 billion, up 12.5% year over year. Cumulative losses exceed RMB 2.8 billion.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 2.5 stars** — Iluvatar CoreX's revenue growth is decent, but losses keep widening. The founder's departure and frequent management changes are a deep-seated concern. With Moore Threads having already claimed the "first domestic GPU stock" halo and commanding a far higher market cap, Iluvatar CoreX's valuation headroom and competitive position are both at a disadvantage; it remains to be seen whether Gen3 mass production can deliver a qualitative breakthrough.

## 4. Moore Threads — GPU Chips

### Basic Information

- **City**: Beijing (note: not strictly a Shanghai company; headquartered in Haidian District, Beijing)
- **Founded**: June 2020
- **Founder**: Zhang Jianzhong, former Global Vice President and Greater China General Manager of Nvidia, with nearly 15 years at Nvidia
- **Team size**: 1,122 people (per prospectus data), 873 R&D staff (77.8%), 67% hold a master's degree or above
- **Ticker**: 688981.SH (STAR Market)

### Business

Moore Threads positions itself as a developer of "full-function GPUs," with products covering four areas: AI computing, graphics rendering, video processing, and scientific computing. In 2025, the core growth driver was the cloud-computing product business (intelligent-computing boards, intelligent-computing appliances, and intelligent-computing clusters), which generated RMB 1.461 billion in revenue, up 250.3% year over year. The company maintains a cadence of releasing one new GPU chip generation per year, and has built out a full product matrix from consumer-grade to data-center-grade chips.

### Financing

It completed a pre-IPO financing round at a post-money valuation of RMB 30 billion. It listed on the STAR Market on December 5, 2025, at RMB 114.28 per share, raising RMB 8 billion. Shares surged over 400% on the first day of trading, with an opening market cap exceeding RMB 305.5 billion. Early shareholders reaped returns of over 4,000x.

### Competition

Main competitors are Hygon Information Technology, Cambricon, Iluvatar CoreX, and Biren Technology. Moore Threads' core advantages: (1) founder Zhang Jianzhong's Nvidia background gives the team top-tier GPU R&D experience; (2) the "full-function GPU" positioning is clearly differentiated; (3) it was the first to list on the STAR Market, earning the highest capital-market recognition among peers.

### Financials

Full-year 2025 revenue was RMB 1.505 billion, up 243.37% year over year. Total gross profit was RMB 987 million, up 218.43% year over year. The company has not yet turned profitable; it expects to break even by 2027 when revenue reaches RMB 5.983 billion and gross margin reaches 61%. Post-IPO, the company holds an RMB 7.5 billion wealth-management allocation, which has drawn market controversy.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 3.5 stars** — Moore Threads is the benchmark company with the highest market cap in the domestic GPU race. The founder's background and technology roadmap are both highly compelling, and revenue growth is remarkable. But the current market cap of over RMB 300 billion against RMB 1.5 billion in revenue implies a P/S multiple over 200x — the valuation has already priced in years of future growth, and the post-IPO wealth-management allocation has also raised governance concerns. Worth watching, but wait for the valuation to normalize.

## 5. Fourier Intelligence — Humanoid/Rehabilitation Robots

### Basic Information

- **City**: Shanghai (Zhangjiang)
- **Founded**: 2015 (predecessor was Jinghe Robotics, formed in 2012)
- **Founder**: Gu Jie, a graduate of Shanghai Jiao Tong University's Department of Mechanical Engineering, who began developing rehabilitation robots in 2009 and is the company's largest shareholder (11.56% stake)
- **Team size**: headcount not disclosed; grown from 2 people at founding in 2015 to today

### Business

Fourier operates on a "rehabilitation robots + humanoid robots" dual-engine model. On the rehabilitation-robot side, its products are deployed in over 3,000 hospitals and institutions across more than 40 countries and regions; its upper-limb rehabilitation robot holds the No. 1 market share in China. Flagship products are priced at RMB 200,000–500,000 per unit, and the high-margin rehabilitation business "feeds" humanoid-robot R&D. On the humanoid-robot side, it launched GR-1 in 2023 and GR-2 in 2024, with over 100 GR-1 units delivered in a single year. As of Q1 2025, cumulative deliveries (including rehabilitation robots) exceeded 10,000 units; the 2025 overall shipment target is several thousand units, of which the humanoid-robot delivery target is 300 units. The company has completed its shareholding restructuring and is highly likely to pursue a STAR Market listing.

### Financing

Cumulative financing is nearly RMB 1 billion. Investors include SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Saudi Aramco's Prosperity7 Ventures, Guoxin Investment, Pudong Venture Capital, and Zhangjiang Science & Technology Venture Capital. It completed a nearly RMB 800 million Series E round in January 2025. In May 2025, its Series E3 pre-money valuation reached RMB 8 billion, putting it in unicorn territory.

### Competition

Main competitors are Unitree Robotics (quadruped/humanoid), AgiBot (founded by "Zhihui Jun"), UBTech (listed in Hong Kong), and Tesla Optimus. Fourier's differentiation: (1) its rehabilitation-robot business is already scaled and profitable, giving it self-funding capability rather than relying entirely on financing to burn cash; (2) rehabilitation is a natural landing scenario for humanoid robots, giving it a clear data-loop advantage; (3) its global footprint (40+ countries) came earlier than most competitors'.

### Financials

Specific revenue figures are not disclosed, but the rehabilitation-robot business is described as "high-margin" and already profitable, able to fund humanoid-robot R&D. Overall, the company remains in a period of heavy investment.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 4 stars** — Fourier is the rare company in the humanoid-robotics space that combines "self-funding capability + a proven landing scenario." Its rehabilitation-robot business provides real profit and a data moat, and its RMB 8 billion valuation is relatively reasonable within the humanoid-robotics space. The core risk is the high uncertainty around humanoid-robot commercialization timelines and intense competition in the general-purpose humanoid race.

## 6. Yitu Technology — Computer Vision

### Basic Information

- **City**: Shanghai
- **Founded**: 2012
- **Founders**: Zhu Long (CEO) and Lin Chenxi (co-founder), middle- and high-school classmates at Fujian Normal University Affiliated High School, both first-prize winners in informatics provincial competitions. Zhu Long is an MIT postdoc; Lin Chenxi previously served as Technical Director at Alibaba Cloud
- **Team size**: several thousand at its peak, down to about 500 after major layoffs in 2021

### Business

Yitu Technology is one of the "Four AI Dragons" (alongside SenseTime, Megvii, and CloudWalk), with core technology in computer vision and natural language processing. Its business spans smart security, smart cities, and smart finance. After withdrawing its STAR Market IPO application in 2021, the company underwent a major business restructuring, cutting its medical-AI business and focusing on the two main lines of security and smart cities. In 2025, co-founder Lin Chenxi revealed the company is now targeting frontier directions such as "spatial intelligence" and world models. Lin Chenxi has publicly criticized the idea that "the leading foundation-model makers face almost a dead end in consumer-facing commercialization," arguing that enterprise is the real way out.

### Financing

The company has completed 10 rounds of financing since founding, with disclosed cumulative proceeds of nearly $400 million; investors include Sequoia China, Hillhouse, and ZhenFund, among others. Its last publicly disclosed financing round was in June 2020 ($30 million). Its valuation once approached RMB 50 billion (during its attempted STAR Market IPO), and has shrunk considerably since. It still made Shanghai's 2025 Unicorn Top 20 list, but its outside valuation has fallen sharply from its peak.

### Competition

Among the "Four AI Dragons," SenseTime has listed in Hong Kong (its market cap fell sharply, then rebounded), CloudWalk has listed on the STAR Market (still loss-making), and Megvii remains unlisted. Yitu is the slowest of the four to list. Its differentiation is that the company claims it has achieved profitability and positive cash flow (while most peers remain loss-making), though revenue growth has been slow.

### Financials

In January 2025, Lin Chenxi disclosed that the company "has been profitable for the last three years (2022–2024), with positive operating cash flow," but "hasn't made much money, and revenue hasn't grown very fast either." Specific financial figures are undisclosed; estimated annual revenue is in the hundreds of millions of RMB. Its 2020 IPO prospectus showed cumulative losses of RMB 7.2 billion.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 1.5 stars** — Yitu Technology has missed the best window in the AI race and has progressively been marginalized from its "Four AI Dragons" halo. Its team has shrunk drastically, it has not raised financing in 6 years, and an IPO remains a distant prospect. While it claims profitability, growth has stalled, and amid the foundation-model wave it has neither a leading base model nor a strong consumer product. Its pivot toward "spatial intelligence" has yet to produce substantive output. Investment value is very low.

## 7. Whale — Retail AI Operations

### Basic Information

- **City**: Hangzhou (China headquarters) / Singapore (global headquarters) (note: not strictly a Shanghai company)
- **Founded**: December 2017
- **Founder**: Ye Shengxuan, a post-1980s "geek" entrepreneur with a Silicon Valley background
- **Team size**: headcount not disclosed; R&D staff make up nearly 70%

### Business

Whale provides a suite of AI-native enterprise products, with core offerings including: (1) Digital Intelligence Space — AI video analytics and space management, deployed in over 300,000 stores; (2) Voice Analytics — audio transcription and AI analysis, processing over 40 million minutes of speech data daily; (3) a content-marketing center and other supporting tools. Its customer base spans over 20 countries and more than 600 enterprises, covering industries such as smart mobility, dining, coffee and tea, home furnishings, footwear and apparel, luxury goods, consumer electronics, retail, healthcare, and finance. Annual growth exceeds 3x.

### Financing

Financing history: Angel round (2017, Alpha Startups) → Pre-A round (Linear Capital) → Series A/A+ (2020, several hundred million RMB) → Series B (led by Temasek, $50 million) → Series C1 (2023, Temasek + Linear Capital) → Series C2 (May 2025, over $60 million, with participation from Bosch Ventures, MTR Lab, Telkom Indonesia, Singtel, SM Prime, and others). Post-Series-C2 valuation exceeds $1 billion, making it a unicorn.

### Competition

Domestic competitors include Jirui Technology, Youyi Interactive, and Digital 100, among other retail-digitalization service providers. Whale's differentiation: (1) global reach (20+ countries), with several Southeast Asian conglomerates among its strategic investors; (2) an AI-native positioning, with products built around AI from the outset rather than retrofitted onto traditional SaaS; (3) the credibility of two funding rounds led by Temasek.

### Financials

Specific revenue figures are not disclosed, but annual growth exceeds 3x (company-reported). Its $1 billion valuation is on the higher end within the retail-AI segment.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 3 stars** — Whale has executed well on globalization within the niche retail-AI segment, and Temasek's repeated backing is a vote of confidence, but retail-AI operations is fundamentally an enterprise-services business, with a ceiling set by customer willingness to pay and renewal rates. Specific revenue figures are opaque, and the "3x growth" claim lacks a verifiable base. The $1 billion valuation requires sustained high growth to justify it. Worth watching as part of the SaaS+AI category.

## 8. LiblibAI — AI Image Generation Platform

### Basic Information

- **City**: Beijing (note: the main entity, Beijing Qidian Xingyu Technology Co., Ltd., is registered in Beijing)
- **Founded**: May 2023
- **Founder**: Chen Mian (born 1992), former head of global commercialization for ByteDance's CapCut; co-founder Zhang Zijie. The core team are graduates of Tsinghua and Peking University who previously worked at Tencent, Alibaba, and ByteDance
- **Team size**: 100–499 people

### Business

LiblibAI is a leading Chinese AI image-generation and creation platform, built on an "open-source model ecosystem + modular tool flow" architecture that brings professional-grade AI capability down to mass-market creation scenarios. Key metrics: 4 million daily active users, 25 million total users, over 500,000 user-trained original AI models and workflows, and over 500 million cumulative images generated. It released Version 2.0 in October 2025, strengthening video-generation capability. Its business model centers on a compute-credit subscription, with users paying to use its online image-generation tools. On the enterprise side, it serves clients such as Kingsoft Office and Wondershare. ARR surpassed $1.5 million within half a year of launching commercialization.

### Financing

The pace of financing has set an industry record — 4 rounds completed within a single year: Angel round (September 2023, $3.5 million, $15 million valuation, GSR Ventures/Banyan Capital/Source Code Capital) → Series A/A+ (2024, led by Vision Plus Capital/Shunwei Capital) → Series B (October 2025, $130 million, led by Sequoia China/CMC Capital). Series B was the largest single AI-application financing round in China in 2025. Its valuation is about $1 billion (unicorn).

### Competition

Main competitors are Midjourney (global), the Stable Diffusion ecosystem, Baidu's ERNIE-ViLG (Wenxin Yige), and Meitu AI, among others. LiblibAI's differentiation: (1) it operates as a platform rather than a single model, aggregating 500,000+ user-trained models into an ecosystem moat; (2) its 4-million-DAU community stickiness leads domestic AIGC platforms by a wide margin; (3) a platform strategy evolving from "tool" to "creation studio."

### Financials

Complete revenue figures have not been fully disclosed; ARR from commercialization surpassed $1.5 million (about RMB 10 million) within half a year, and the company is still in an early, fast-growing monetization phase. With a base of 25 million users, there is significant room for paid-conversion growth, but user willingness to pay and ARPU for AI image generation remain unverified.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: 3.5 stars** — LiblibAI is a community-driven platform player in the AIGC image race, with its 4 million DAU and 500,000 user-trained models forming a genuine network-effect moat. Four financing rounds within a single year, jointly led by Sequoia and CMC Capital, signal strong recognition from top-tier capital. The core risks are the ceiling on AI image generation's business model (user willingness to pay) and the pace of technology iteration (foundation-model makers could leapfrog it). Its $1 billion valuation will need substantial revenue growth in 2026–2027 to be justified.

## Key Conclusions

1. **Most favored**: Fourier Intelligence (self-funding capability + a proven landing scenario + reasonable valuation) and MiniMax (unmatched overseas revenue share + impressive user scale, though the valuation needs to correct)
2. **Least favored**: Yitu Technology (missed the era's window; the marginalization looks irreversible)
3. **The three GPU-chip companies** (Enflame, Iluvatar CoreX, Moore Threads) share a common problem: valuations are severely disconnected from fundamentals — essentially a "thematic investment" driven by policy and domestic-substitution sentiment. Approach with caution
