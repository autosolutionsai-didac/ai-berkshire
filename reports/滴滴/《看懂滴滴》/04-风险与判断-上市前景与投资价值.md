## Putting It All Together

After three installments, Didi's contradiction is already clear: the business is recovering (daily orders have passed 40 million, adjusted net profit is 7.9 billion yuan, and cash on hand is 55.7 billion yuan), yet its market cap is less than one-tenth of Uber's. Is this discount a market pricing failure, or a rational reflection of real risk?

This final chapter does just one thing: pull all the pieces together and unpack exactly what the current $16.5 billion price tag is pricing in — and under what conditions that price would be wrong.

## I. Hong Kong IPO: The Biggest Valuation Catalyst

### Current status

As of May 2026, Didi's listing status is as follows:

- Official position: **"There is currently no IPO timetable"** (official statement, June 2024)
- Market rumor: a Hong Kong Stock Exchange Main Board IPO, with a target valuation once reported at **HK$600 billion (~$77 billion)**
- Actual position: trading over-the-counter on the pink sheets, DIDIY at **~$3.55**, with a market cap of about **$16.5 billion**

(Sources: Didi official statement; Yahoo Finance, 2026-05-13; media reports)

### Five possible paths

| Path | Estimated probability | Notes |
|------|---------|------|
| **HKEX Main Board IPO** | **60%** | Most likely — the VIE structure is workable, and there is precedent for weighted voting rights |
| A-share STAR Market | 15% | Would require unwinding the VIE structure; an extremely high bar, 2-3 year timeline |
| Dual-track Hong Kong + A-share CDR | 10% | Slow to execute |
| SPAC reverse merger | 5% | Large valuation discount |
| Stay OTC / go private | 10% | Significant pressure from early investors (SoftBank, etc.) |

(Source: ai-berkshire research draft, 2026-04-24, based on an assessment of the regulatory environment and market precedent)

### Three regulatory approval gates

If Didi pursues the Hong Kong IPO path, it must clear the following gates in sequence:

| Gate | Approving body | Estimated timeline |
|------|---------|---------|
| 1. Data security review | Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) | 3-6 months (can partly run in parallel) |
| 2. Overseas listing filing | China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) | 6-12 months |
| 3. Listing hearing | HKEX | 3-6 months |
| **Combined** | — | **18-30 months from kickoff to listing** |

(Sources: Measures for Cybersecurity Review; Trial Administrative Measures for the Overseas Issuance and Listing of Securities by Domestic Enterprises, March 2023 version)

### Four timing scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Listing timing |
|------|------|---------|
| Fastest | 25% | Q4 2026 |
| **Base case** | **40%** | **H1 2027** |
| Delayed | 25% | H2 2027 ~ 2028 |
| No listing | 10% | 2028+ or remains OTC indefinitely |

### Disagreement over IPO pricing

There is a notable divergence worth flagging here:

| Basis | Valuation | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Financial valuation (four methods combined) | **$38-47 billion** | DCF + comparables + SOTP |
| Market rumor | **~$77 billion** (HK$600 billion) | Media reports |
| Current OTC market cap | **~$16.5 billion** | OTC trading price |

(Sources: ai-berkshire research draft valuation model; media reports; Yahoo Finance)

**The gap is enormous.** The rumored $77 billion valuation may embed a Robotaxi spin-off premium and "new-economy growth story" pricing. The $38-47 billion financial valuation is closer to the fundamentals.

**Reasonable estimate**: if the Hong Kong IPO succeeds, the real offering market cap will likely land in the **$35-50 billion** range (the middle ground between the rumored figure and the financial valuation); adding a typical 10-15% IPO discount, the offering price would correspond to a market cap of roughly **$30-45 billion**.

This implies: **the theoretical upside from the current OTC market cap of $16.5 billion to the IPO offering price is 80-170%.**

**But this upside is contingent on the IPO actually happening** — and there is still no confirmed timetable for that.

Didi's P/S multiple is **one-seventh** of Uber's and **one-tenth** of Grab's.

### What makes up the discount

This extreme discount can be broken down into several layers:

| Discount factor | Estimated impact | Notes |
|---------|---------|------|
| **OTC liquidity discount** | 30-40% | No institutional participation, no analyst coverage |
| **China-concept-stock discount** | 20-30% | Uncertainty in US-China relations |
| **Regulatory / data-security discount** | 10-20% | Risk premium for the "heart condition" |
| **IPO-uncertainty discount** | 10-15% | No visibility on when it can relist |
| **Cumulative** | **~60-75%** | Discount rate versus Uber |

**The key question**: how much of this discount would be removed by an IPO?

- OTC liquidity discount: **largely eliminated after an IPO**
- China-concept-stock discount: **partially eliminated** (a Hong Kong listing still trades at a discount to NYSE)
- Regulatory discount: **will not be eliminated** — the data-security risk is structural
- IPO-uncertainty discount: **eliminated after an IPO**

If the IPO succeeds, an estimated **40-55%** of the discount could be removed, corresponding to a market cap of **$27.5-37 billion** — close to the low end of the $38-47 billion financial valuation.

## III. Assessing the Three Major Risks

### Risk 1: The data-security "heart condition" (weight: 40%)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Probability | Low (annualized 5-10%) |
| Consequences if triggered | **Extreme** — app delisting, delayed IPO, valuation cut in half |
| Controllability | Low — depends on external events (hacking, employee leaks, US-China relations) |
| Mitigation | None — this is a structural risk that cannot be eliminated |

This is Didi's most distinctive risk. The regulatory risk facing Alibaba, Tencent, and Pinduoduo is mainly "antitrust plus tax" — quantifiable and predictable. Didi's risk is "data sovereignty" — **a single incident could be irreversible**.

### Risk 2: Driver social insurance (weight: 35%)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Probability | High (pilot expansion confirmed for July 2026) |
| Short-term impact | Manageable — estimated occupational injury insurance cost of 2-5 billion yuan/year |
| Long-term impact | **Potentially fatal** — if expanded to the full "five insurances," annual cost would rise by 40-50 billion yuan |
| Mitigation | Borne industry-wide, not unique to Didi |

(Sources: Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security announcement, 2026; industry cost estimates)

**The key uncertainty**: whether the policy ultimately lands at the "narrowest" or "broadest" scope — this determines whether the impact is "a bearable extra cost" or "one that swallows all profit."

### Risk 3: IPO approval dragging on or failing (weight: 25%)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Probability | Medium (30% chance of delay to 2028+) |
| Consequences | Persistently depressed share price, difficulty for early investors to exit |
| Controllability | Low — depends on regulatory pacing |

### Three-scenario synthesis

| Scenario | Probability | Market cap in 3 years | Corresponding share price |
|------|------|----------|---------|
| **Optimistic** (IPO succeeds + narrowest social-insurance scope + no incidents) | 25% | $40-55 billion | ~$8-12 |
| **Neutral** (IPO in 2027 + gradual social-insurance rollout + stable regulation) | **50%** | **$28-40 billion** | **~$6-9** |
| **Pessimistic** (IPO delayed + broadest social-insurance scope + data incident) | 25% | $8-15 billion | ~$2-3 |

(Note: the above are scenario estimates based on public data, not precise forecasts)

One notable signal: **none of the mainstream value-investing masters holds a significant position in Didi.**

| Investor | Holds Didi? | Major holdings |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Duan Yongping | **No** | Tencent, Kweichow Moutai, Apple, Pinduoduo |

| Buffett | **No** | Has never invested in Chinese internet companies |

(Sources: public SEC 13F holdings; public statements on Xueqiu)

Duan Yongping's only Didi-related comment on Xueqiu came on July 2, 2021 (the third day after Didi's listing):

> "How much Didi does Apple hold? They invested $1 billion back then, but I haven't seen the weighting."

(Source: https://xueqiu.com/1247347556/188105710)

This comment itself reveals Duan Yongping's attitude toward Didi — **he was tracking Apple's stake in Didi, not considering buying Didi himself**.

**Why don't the masters touch Didi?** There are likely three core reasons:

1. **The business model is mediocre** — a net take rate of 4% and thin margins don't meet the "good business" bar
2. **Regulation is unpredictable** — data-security risk cannot be quantified or hedged
3. **Management is not transparent enough** — disclosure has been limited since delisting

**Counterargument**: the masters not buying doesn't mean it isn't worth buying. Duan Yongping's and Buffett's circles of competence lean toward "high-certainty, high-margin" businesses — that's a preference, not a value judgment. Didi may fit better within a "special situation investing" framework.

## V. Frameworks for Different Types of Investors

### Investor A: OTC event-driven

- **Logic**: current $16.5 billion vs. a neutral-case IPO valuation of $30-40 billion → **80-140% upside**
- **Risk**: poor OTC liquidity, the IPO may not happen, a black swan can strike at any time
- **Suited to**: investors who can accept a 2-3 year lockup, keep position size small (under 5% of total portfolio), and have OTC trading experience

### Investor B: Wait-for-the-IPO-subscription

- **Logic**: wait until the Hong Kong IPO is confirmed before participating, in exchange for normal liquidity
- **Risk**: the IPO price may already reflect most of the upside
- **Suited to**: conservative investors unwilling to bear OTC risk

### Investor C: Long-term value investing

- **Judgment**: Didi is not "an undervalued great company" — it is "a discounted OK company"
- **Business quality** is not good enough (thin take rate, high regulatory risk)
- **Management transparency** is not high enough
- **Recommendation**: stay in watch-and-wait mode, and reassess once the core variables (IPO, social-insurance policy) are settled

### Investor D: Index allocation

- **Logic**: wait for passive buying once Didi is included in Stock Connect
- **Timing**: 6-12 months after the IPO

## VI. Key Signals to Track

If you decide to keep an eye on Didi, here are the signals worth tracking continuously:

### Strong signals (fate-determining)

1. **Filing of the Hong Kong IPO prospectus** (Form A1) — once this appears, the timetable becomes largely fixed
2. **Completion of CSRC overseas-listing filing** — clearance of one of the gates

6. **Quarterly earnings** — whether results keep delivering or start to slow
7. **International business profitability trend** — whether Brazil/Mexico keep improving
8. **Aggregator-platform regulatory developments** — pressure on Amap's take rate

### Weak signals (background environment)

9. **Evolution of US-China relations**
10. **The overall China-concept-stock IPO window**

## VII. Summary — Defining Didi in One Sentence

> **Didi is a "wounded super-incumbent" — a 70% share of China's ride-hailing market, 40 million daily orders, adjusted net profit of 7.9 billion yuan, and 55.7 billion yuan of cash on hand. But it is not "an undervalued great company" — it is "a discounted OK company." The discount goes by these names: OTC illiquidity, the regulatory heart condition, the driver social-insurance gray rhino, and IPO uncertainty. The current $16.5 billion market cap is less than one-tenth of Uber's — if the Hong Kong IPO lands in 2027, there is 80-140% of valuation-repair upside; if a black swan reappears, there is still downside. That the value-investing masters collectively avoid Didi is, in itself, a signal worth pondering.**
