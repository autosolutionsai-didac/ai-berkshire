# Competition and Regulation — The External Variables That Will Decide Its Fate

> *Understanding Didi* series · Part 03 · Competition and Regulation
> Estimated reading time: 12 minutes

## Why This Chapter Matters Most

The first two chapters built a "static portrait" of Didi: its business structure, how it makes money, and its moat. But a static portrait cannot answer the most critical question — **can this company's future get better?**

Answering it requires nailing down three dynamic variables: whether Amap's aggregation model can genuinely disrupt Didi's market share; whether regulatory risk has been fully lifted or has merely gone quiet for now; and how far away autonomous driving is from turning from a "long-dated option" into a "real threat." These three things will determine whether the next 3-5 years bring Didi "valuation repair" or "value destruction."

## I. The Competitive Landscape: One Dominant Player, Several Strong Followers, and an Aggregator Wildcard

### Current market share (2025)

| Platform | Average daily orders (millions) | Market share | Model |
|------|-------------|------|------|
| **Didi** | **~40-45** | **70-73%** | Self-operated |
| Amap Taxi | ~10-12 | 12-15% | **Aggregator** (does not provide rides directly) |
| Caocao Mobility | ~3 | 5.4% | Self-operated (Geely group) |
| T3 Chuxing | ~2.8 | 5.0-5.3% | Self-operated (FAW/Dongfeng/Changan) |
| Meituan Taxi | ~1 | ~2% | Primarily aggregator, strategically scaling back |
| Ruqi Mobility | ~0.8 | ~1.5% | Self-operated (GAC/Tencent); Hong Kong IPO in 2024 |
| Hello (Hellobike) | ~1.5 | 2-3% | Primarily two-wheelers |

(Sources: Didi 20-F filing; company financial reports/announcements; industry data; 2024-2025 basis)

**Assessment of the landscape**: **the "one dominant player, several strong followers" self-operated structure remains stable, but the aggregator model is changing the rules of the game.**

### Amap Aggregation: The Biggest Competitive Variable

Amap Taxi does not recruit its own drivers or run its own operations — what it does is act as an **"aggregator platform"**: it plugs in mid-sized and small fleet operators such as Caocao, T3, Enjoy, and Shouqi, and users hail a ride with one tap inside the Amap Maps app, with Amap taking a cut.

Where does this model's threat lie?

| Dimension | Didi (self-operated) | Amap (aggregator) |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Driver management | Recruits and manages its own drivers | Doesn't manage drivers; leaves it to fleet operators |
| Compliance cost | Bears it all itself | Passed on to fleet operators |
| User acquisition | Standalone app | Rides on Amap Maps (**600 million+** monthly active users) |
| Pricing power | Constrained by regulation | Multi-platform price comparison drives prices lower |
| Share of orders | ~70% of the entire industry | Aggregated orders already account for **~31%** of the industry |

(Sources: industry reports; public Amap Maps data)

**The core threat of the aggregator model is not stealing order volume** — Didi's daily orders are still growing. **The real threat is suppressing take rate and shifting bargaining power**:

1. Once users get used to "comparing prices on Amap and then hailing the cheapest ride," Didi's brand premium disappears
2. Once mid-sized and small fleet operators acquire users through Amap, their dependence on Didi declines
3. In August 2024, the industry collectively lowered its commission cap to **27%**, partly due to price-comparison pressure from aggregator platforms

**Counterargument**: the aggregator model has a natural ceiling — it doesn't manage service quality, so the user experience is uneven. Didi's self-operated model does a better job of controlling driver screening, training, and service standards. When users need "certainty" (catching a flight, late at night, bad weather), they tend to come back to Didi. What Amap's aggregation mainly captures is **price-sensitive, non-urgent use cases**.

### Meituan Taxi: Why Did It Retreat?

Meituan entered ride-hailing with much fanfare in 2017, burning through heavy subsidies in Shanghai and Nanjing. The result:

- **Gradually shifted to the aggregator model after 2023**, no longer recruiting its own drivers
- Average daily orders of roughly **1 million**, ~2% market share
- Its strategic positioning shifted from "disrupting Didi" to "a supporting entry point for local services"

The underlying reason for Meituan's retreat: **the two-sided network effect in ride-hailing is too strong, so once the subsidies were withdrawn, users flowed back**. Meituan succeeded with the same subsidy playbook in food delivery because restaurants' switching costs are low (they'll join whichever platform sends them traffic); but drivers' switching costs are high (order efficiency is highest on Didi).

**This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence for Didi's moat**: even a "war machine" like Meituan couldn't take it down.

### A New Variable in 2026: A Government Platform Enters

In May 2026, the Ministry of Transport's **95128** unified national cruising-taxi hailing platform officially launched in Kunming, with the first batch of **9,637** cruising taxis connected.

(Sources: Ministry of Transport announcement; Tencent News report, 2026-05-14)

This is a signal worth watching: **the government is trying to bypass commercial platforms and connect traditional taxis directly with riders**. The scale is currently minuscule, and the short-term impact on Didi is negligible, but it bears watching over the long run:

- If 95128 is successfully rolled out nationwide → traditional taxi orders get siphoned off
- If it fails (more likely) → it will confirm that "a platform without algorithmic and experience optimization struggles to gain traction"

## II. Regulatory History: The Harshest Lesson Among China-Concept Stocks

### Timeline

| Date | Event |
|------|------|
| **2021-06-30** | Didi lists on the NYSE at an offering price of $14, with a market cap of ~$67 billion |
| **2021-07-02** | On its third day of trading, the CAC announces a cybersecurity review of Didi |
| **2021-07-04** | The Didi app is pulled from app stores |
| **2021-07-09** | All 25 of Didi's apps are pulled |
| **2021-12** | Didi announces it will delist from the NYSE |
| **2022-06** | Formally delists from the NYSE and moves to OTC pink sheet trading |
| **2022-07-21** | The CAC announces its penalty decision: a fine of **8.026 billion yuan** |
| **2023-01-16** | The app is restored, and **the rectification is formally approved** |

(Sources: CAC announcements; SEC filings; public reporting)

### What Was the Real Nature of This Storm?

Many people interpret the Didi affair as "penalized for an illegal listing" — that's not accurate.

**At its core, this was about a data-sovereignty red line.** The data Didi holds includes:

- **High-precision surveying data for roads nationwide**
- **Vehicle entry and exit trajectories around militarily sensitive areas**
- **Travel patterns and location data for hundreds of millions of users**
- **Real-time locations of millions of drivers**

Against the backdrop of tense US-China relations in 2021, Didi listed in the US without CAC approval — meaning this data could potentially be reached by SEC review requirements.

**The CAC's response was not "opposing Didi making money" but "opposing strategic data leaving the country."** Article 7 of the Measures for Cybersecurity Review issued in 2022 — which states that "network platform operators holding the personal information of more than one million users must file for a cybersecurity review before listing abroad" — is known in the industry as the **"Didi clause."**

(Sources: Measures for Cybersecurity Review, February 2022 version; CAC administrative penalty decision)

### Evidence of Regulatory Warming

Since the penalty, policy signals have kept warming:

| Date | Signal |
|------|------|
| Jan 2023 | The app is restored |
| Jul 2023 | Premier Li Qiang chairs a platform-economy roundtable, affirming support for "the platform economy" |
| Dec 2023 | The Central Economic Work Conference includes "the sound and sustained development of the platform economy" |
| Jul 2024 | The Third Plenum calls for "promoting innovative development of the platform economy" |
| 2025 | Anti-"involution" enforcement focuses on food delivery and e-commerce, not ride-hailing |

(Sources: State Council announcements; Central Economic Work Conference documents; Third Plenum resolution)

**But one thing must be understood clearly**: the sensitivity of Didi's data is **structural** and will not disappear just because policy has warmed. As long as the core business involves nationwide road data and user location information, **any new data-leak incident could reopen the review**.

This is what the research draft calls the **"heart condition"** — not cured, just stabilized, and capable of flaring up again at any time.

### Comparing Penalties Across Platforms

| Company | Penalty date | Fine | Recovery speed |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| Alibaba | Apr 2021 | **18.2 billion yuan** | **Fast** — recovered within half a year |
| Meituan | Oct 2021 | 3.4 billion yuan | **Fast** |
| Tencent | Multiple times in 2021 | ~5.0 billion yuan cumulative | **Fast** |
| **Didi** | Jul 2022 | **8.026 billion yuan** | **Slow — app delisted for 18 months** |
| Ant Group | Nov 2020 | 7.1 billion yuan | **IPO still not restarted to this day** |

(Sources: announcements from the respective regulators)

**Didi's recovery speed sits between Alibaba's (fast recovery) and Ant's (still not recovered)**. This position itself indicates its risk level: higher than Alibaba's, lower than Ant's.

## III. Autonomous-Driving Competition: A 5-10 Year Perspective

The impact of autonomous driving on the ride-hailing industry needs to be examined on two levels:

### Level 1: Who Is Doing It?

| Company | Approach | Progress | Relationship to Didi |
|------|------|------|------------|
| **Didi Autonomous Driving** | L4 Robotaxi | Road-testing in Guangzhou/Beijing, valued at $5 billion | In-house subsidiary |
| Baidu Apollo (Apollo Go) | L4 Robotaxi | Operating in Wuhan and Beijing, ~5,000+ daily orders | **Direct competitor** |
| Pony.ai | L4 Robotaxi | Listed on Nasdaq in 2024 | Competitor |
| WeRide | L4 Robotaxi | Listed on Nasdaq in 2024 | Competitor |
| Tesla | FSD → Robotaxi | Launched in the US in June 2025 | Indirect competitor |

(Sources: company announcements; public reporting)

### Level 2: When Would It Truly Threaten Didi?

**Short term (1-3 years): almost no threat.**
- Robotaxi daily orders nationwide currently add up to fewer than **10,000**, versus Didi's **40 million** — a gap of roughly 4,000x
- Technology maturity, safety-driver costs, and licensing restrictions all limit scaling

**Medium term (3-5 years): starts to have an impact.**
- If Robotaxi commercializes in 5-10 cities, it could eat into 5-10% of Didi's orders
- **But Didi's response strategy is to do it too** — connecting Robotaxis to its own network

**Long term (5-10 years): could rewrite the rules of the game.**
- If L4 becomes widespread, driver costs (79% of GTV) would be eliminated, rewriting the industry's economics entirely
- Didi's advantage would shift from "driver network" to "demand network plus dispatch algorithm"
- But its competitors would also shift from "Amap, Meituan" to "Tesla, Waymo, Baidu"

**Counterargument**: the commercialization timeline for autonomous driving has been overestimated for 10 years running. The consensus in 2026 is "still another 5-10 years" — but the consensus 5 years ago was also "still another 5-10 years." Until Robotaxi truly scales, Didi's human driver network remains irreplaceable.

## IV. Driver Social Insurance: The Biggest Gray Rhino

This is the **most concrete, most quantifiable risk** Didi faces.

### Current status

- Didi has **~19 million** registered drivers on its platform
- Of these, full-time drivers (online for 8+ hours a day on average) are estimated at **3-4 million**
- Currently, most drivers work under **"flexible employment"** status, and the platform does not cover social insurance
- Starting July 2026, the occupational injury protection pilot for new forms of employment will expand to **all provinces nationwide**

(Sources: Didi 20-F filing; Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security announcement, 2026)

### Cost Estimate

| Scenario | Population covered | Additional annual cost (yuan) | Impact |
|------|---------|----------------|------|
| Narrowest (occupational injury insurance only) | 3-4 million full-time drivers | **2-5 billion** | Manageable |
| Moderate (partial "five insurances") | Full-time drivers | **20-30 billion** | **Would eat up most of adjusted net profit** |
| Broadest (full "five insurances plus housing fund") | Full-time drivers | **40-50 billion** | **Would far exceed full-year net profit** |

(Source: industry estimates, extrapolated from the social-insurance path taken for food-delivery riders)

**Key judgment**:

The pilot launching in July 2026 is **"occupational injury protection"** — the narrowest scope, with a manageable cost (on the order of a few hundred to about 1,000 yuan per person per year). But the question is: **is this the start of a slippery slope?**

If it gradually expands from "occupational injury insurance" to the "five insurances" or even "five insurances plus housing fund," costs would rise exponentially, directly wiping out Didi's full-year profit.

**Counterargument**: the government also understands that if full social insurance were made mandatory, large numbers of drivers would "leave the platform" and become unlicensed drivers — making regulation harder and safety worse. The most likely path is a **"gradual, tiered"** rollout, not an all-at-once move. Moreover, the social-insurance cost is borne industry-wide, not a disadvantage unique to Didi.

## V. An Integrated Assessment of Competition and Regulation

Taken together, the external environment Didi faces can be summarized as follows:

### Higher-confidence trends

1. **Aggregator platforms' share will keep rising** — possibly from 31% to 35-40%, but this will not dethrone Didi (Amap lacks operating capability)
2. **Driver social insurance will roll out gradually** — manageable in the short term, a gray rhino in the long term
3. **The broader regulatory environment is warming** — the "support the platform economy" policy tone has not changed since 2023
4. **Autonomous driving is not a near-term threat** — scaling up is still at least 3-5 years away

### High-uncertainty variables

1. **A data-security incident** — a single black swan could severely damage the valuation (low probability, extreme consequences)
2. **The Hong Kong IPO timetable** — officially denied to exist, with rumors persisting but unconfirmed
3. **US-China relations** — affects the valuation environment for China-concept stocks broadly
4. **The final scope of the social-insurance policy** — if it goes the "broadest" route, the profit structure changes entirely
